# Conditional Rephrase

Draw the complete rule on paper before you choose a diagram. This drill tests the translation, not whether you can guess from the answer choices.

## Before choosing

1. Read the whole sentence. Identify the complete sufficient condition and the complete necessary condition.
2. Write: **If [sufficient], then [necessary].** Keep every qualification and every part of an *and* or *or* condition.
3. Read your diagram back in ordinary language. Does the original actually require that result when the left side is met?
4. Choose the diagram that expresses the same complete rule. Your contrapositive is equivalent even when its wording looks different.

## The trap

A requirement is not automatically enough. “Entry requires a ticket” means **entry → must have a ticket**. A ticket alone does not guarantee entry. The event could be full.

A word you recognise is a clue to read carefully. It is not permission to ignore the sentence around it. *For* alone is not a conditional indicator.

## One habit

Before checking, say: **What would break this rule?** For entry requiring a ticket, entry without a ticket would break it. A ticket without entry would not.

If you use the contrapositive, reverse and negate the complete conditions. Negating “A and B” gives “not A or not B.” A translation that drops one condition says something different.
