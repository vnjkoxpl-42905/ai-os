### The head turn: how it is built
1. **Panorama, not a wider flat photo.** A flat outpaint cannot represent a 44-degree turn: the far edge of a planar image at 70 degrees is stretched 3.7x. The plate is mapped onto a cylinder at its own field of view (estimated ~65 deg horizontal). The room to the RIGHT (window wall, beyond the curtain) is generated as a second *view*, camera rotated ~45 deg right, by gemini-3-pro-image image_to_image with room-v3 as the reference. A 21:9 outpaint is generated too, as glue for the 31-45 deg overlap. All three are projected onto the cylinder and feather-blended in angular order: plate (untouched pixels, full res) -> outpaint wing -> rotated view. Backup if the still views are incoherent: kling-video-v3_0 image_to_video "camera pans right", frames stitched into the cylinder.
2. **Facing view is the same plate, centred.** Frame hFOV ~53 deg (matches the monitor size zoom 1.26 gave), yaw 0 aimed at the monitor centre. No left wing is needed because the frame is narrower than the plate. This is the Q3 "screen centred, straight in front" requirement, achieved without remaking the room.
3. **Turned view.** Yaw ~44 deg right, pitch -4, roll +1.5 (a head, not a tripod). The monitor's right edge sits ~2 deg inside the frame's left edge: a sliver of bezel, no text (Q2). Frame right edge reaches ~70 deg, all generated room.
4. **Renderer: Python (numpy/scipy), offline.** Rotation-only reprojection of a cylindrical panorama to a pinhole view is a per-pixel lookup; 4K frames in ~1s each on CPU. No WebGL in headless Chrome, no new npm dependencies, deterministic, debuggable as PNGs. The camera model lives in TypeScript (`src/pov/headTurn.ts`) and is EXPORTED per frame as JSON; Python consumes that JSON. One model, two consumers, verified by projecting the monitor corners in both and comparing.
5. **Only the moving frames are pre-rendered:** turn-away (~0.9s), turn-back (~0.8s), half-turn for card beats (~20 deg, both directions), plus the facing still and the turned still. Motion blur baked by averaging 4 sub-frame samples. Reused for all seven retells (Q4). The 20s+ holds use the stills with the existing 2D living drift.
6. **Parallax (phase 2, if the base works):** Depth Anything V2 on CPU gives a depth map for the panorama; the eye is offset ~6 cm during the turn because a head rotates about the neck, not the eye. That is the "3D effect" the flat still cannot give. Implemented as depth-guided inverse mapping in the same Python renderer.
7. **DOM overlays are world-anchored.** Every card, bubble, label and the "Look Away" text is a planar rectangle in room coordinates (cylinder angle, height, distance). Its four corners are projected through the same camera and applied with the existing `quadTransform` matrix3d. So the overlays rotate with the head exactly as the room does, and the monitor's Screen composite uses the identical path.

### Effects (his "coolest effects possible", each tied to something real)
- Gaze-shift blink at the start of the turn-away only (people blink when they shift gaze). Three frames, soft, masks the onset. Never on the return.
- Motion blur (baked, 4 samples), stronger at the fastest part of the turn.
- Peripheral softness: a blurred copy of the plate under a radial mask, strongest at the frame edge on the monitor side while turned. This is the Q3 "blurred to the side / corner of the eye" permission, and it is optical, not a mask on text.
- Exposure adaptation: brightness lifts as the window enters, then settles over ~0.6s. Turning toward a bright window is what a real eye does.
- Lens breathing: hFOV widens ~2 deg during the turn (focus relaxes), tightens on return.
- Ease profile: fast start, long settle with a 2% overshoot; return is quicker and settles square.
- Living drift on holds stays (existing), now in yaw/pitch terms so the overlays move with the room.

### "Look Away" and the bubbles
- "Look Away" is world-anchored in the free space in front of the curtain, large, Instrument Serif italic (HyperFrames type roster), chrome gradient, layered extrusion, local bloom. Appears once the turn passes ~60%, word-revealed, and leaves naturally when the head returns. His wording, verbatim.
- Speech bubbles (tell/colour) live in the free space under "Look Away", never on the monitor. Existing cue text (already short lines). Bubbly: rounder glass, tail toward the viewer, spring in.
- Step label + one-line cue while facing: a slim glass placard standing on the DESK, lower right, world-anchored on the desk plane. Off the passage entirely.
- Step intro cards (Q1 only): the head half-turns ~20 deg right and the card stands in the room beside the monitor. The dark full-frame dim is gone; the passage stays visible and unobstructed. This is the fix for "bubble in the way of the stimulus".
- The Recall: head stays turned; the rebuilt argument stacks in the free space beneath "Look Away"; the count sits under it. Head returns for the Space presses.
