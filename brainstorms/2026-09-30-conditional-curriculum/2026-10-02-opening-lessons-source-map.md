### An original anchor for the rebuild

**If an order is marked ready for pickup, it has been packed.**

- Opening: imagine an order with the ready label. Packing follows under the stated rule; the sentence hasn't said an actual order has the label.
- Sufficient: a ready label is enough to establish packing. Without the label, packing remains possible.
- Necessary: packing is required for an order carrying that label.
- Contrapositive: an order that hasn't been packed cannot be marked ready under this rule. Let the student work this out before naming it.
- Four situations: ready implies packed; not packed implies not ready; not ready leaves packing open; packed leaves the ready label open.
- Notation, introduced later: Ready → Packed; Not packed → Not ready. Abbreviate only after defining the complete labels.

This anchor is our own course example. Reuse it when a new view deepens the idea, and use fresh situations for transfer. A statement such as a published report requiring editor approval can provide a separate contrapositive prediction: no editor approval means it cannot be published under the rule. Do not treat an obligation or permission as proof that someone complied or acted.
