## Original analysis: prerequisites and learning demand

This compact lesson assumes the learner can handle abstract letters and elementary negation almost immediately. It introduces AND/OR contraposition approximately four minutes in, before detailed indicator translation. That makes it useful as a compressed overview or refresher; a ground-up independent pathway would benefit from more isolated practice before the combined tasks. This judgment concerns instructional demand, not evidence of learner outcomes.

The four fact-position cases teach an important boundary: from S→N, S licenses N and ¬N licenses ¬S; ¬S and N alone leave the other term undetermined. Students must interpret uncertainty as an answer rather than feel compelled to infer something.

The linking section requires a different output type from triggering. Rules alone yield another relationship. Adding a fact can yield a factual consequence. A fact satisfying part of an AND antecedent can also simplify the remaining conditional demand. This last operation merits its own assignable skill in LSAT U rather than being hidden under general linking.

Translation strategy is partly syntactic and partly semantic. The reference identifies arrow placement, but unlisted words and equivalent requirement readings require understanding necessity. The lesson therefore does not justify teaching indicator memorization as sufficient mastery.

The practice sequence shifts from guided cases to a mixed worksheet and delayed instructor review. This is feedback through worked explanation after the student pauses. Captions provide no evidence of answer entry, immediate adaptive feedback, a scored diagnostic, retries with fresh items, or gating. A ten-item mixed worksheet is one observed training event, not a complete inventory of LSAT Lab exercises.

## Original LSAT U examples and assignable skills

All examples below are new and are not paraphrases of source exercises.

- **Fact positions:** every licensed pilot has passed an exam. Test separately what follows from licensed, not licensed, passed, and not passed.
- **Compound contraposition:** inspected AND sealed → dispatchable. If not dispatchable, at least one prerequisite failed. Students must not infer both failed.
- **Rule versus fact:** regional → registered; registered → approved. Derive regional → approved. Only after a named applicant is regional may the student derive that applicant’s approval.
- **Residual condition:** code AND badge → entry; entry → attendance. Given the badge, code → attendance still remains conditional until a code fact is supplied.
- **Equivalent exception translations:** equipment operates only when a safety check has passed. Require both a necessity reading and its contrapositive.
- **Biconditional scope:** a display lights exactly when the circuit is active. Contrast an explicitly given two-way rule with an unsupported reversal of a one-way rule.

For Joshua's Beginner/Medium/Advanced model, start with a single operation, then mix operations, then vary language and compounds. Use a separate general mixed assignment at each level. These are design proposals, not LSAT Lab's observed level definitions.

## Nuances and source-fidelity limits

The short negative-quantifier aside includes few, most, and not-all transformations. In LSAT U, define the intended mathematical meaning of few before equating it with most-not; everyday usage can be vague. No existence assumption should be silently imported into a universal rule. The captions do not give a full quantifier inference lesson.

The certainty discussion includes guaranteed causal wording. LSAT U should distinguish the logical guarantee encoded by a sentence from proof of causation in an argument. The lesson's broad certainty framing is a recognition heuristic, not a complete account of every kind of statement.

Otherwise creates a biconditional in the discussed case because the other branch supplies the opposite outcome. It should not be taught as automatically biconditional in every sentence.

Native-track labeling does not establish transcription accuracy. Captions contain obvious substitutions involving contrapositive, flaw, letters, names, and ordinary nouns. One compound explanation labels the inferred side inconsistently; diagrams are necessary to verify its intended symbols. The item with alternative any/will readings requires the unseen sentence and subject scope to adjudicate fully. The slide's complete indicator list, displayed diagrams, and exact written worksheet were not recovered by reading captions. Do not fill these gaps from guesses.
