# Build one conditional pathway with independently assignable skills

Research updated 2026-10-01. This is a research synthesis and proposed curriculum, not a published course or an approved advancement policy. Source reports preserve the observed order, links, and evidence limits.

## What the research establishes

7Sage's Foundations schedule contains 54 resources under Conditional and Set Logic, then 34 under Logic of Intersecting Sets, followed by 12 under Formal Logic Flaws. These are resources, not 100 distinct skills. They include practice, reviews, a motivation break, and a feedback survey. The schedule is personalized; its calendar dates are not prerequisites.

The conditional written lessons and worksheet tasks show a deliberate progression: understand the relationship, represent it, translate varied language, combine rules, handle compound scope, then apply the operations to questions. Quantifiers extend this foundation with overlap, proportion, and new restrictions on inference. The flaw block contrasts valid forms with tempting invalid ones.

The live LR schedule returns to these operations inside inference, rule application, and assumption lessons. This makes transfer a repeated part of the broader course, rather than something accomplished by finishing the foundational chapter once.

LSAT Lab now has substantially stronger evidence: the entire available caption streams for its conditional primer, core conditional class, advanced conditional class, and quantifier class were reviewed. Its personalized Study Plan exposes flashcards, rephrasing, inference and negation activities, graded question sets, and displayed topic levels. The chronological reports below hold the source summaries; this synthesis uses them to propose the LSAT U design. Caption coverage does not establish audiovisual fidelity, displayed-board completeness or full interactive feedback.

For the three difficulties, tag each item by demands: number of operations, language/scope complexity, need to choose a method, scaffolding and independence. Map Beginner to isolated supported operations, Medium to independent selection and modest combinations, and Advanced to dense scope and coordinated operations. These are proposed rubrics. A given skill must still have practice at all three levels.

## How the conditional sequence builds

The examples in this table are original LSAT U examples. They are not competitor exercises. Dependencies describe our analysis of the teaching progression, not verified platform access restrictions.

| Ordered step | Content the student should learn | How to train it in LSAT U | Original example and dependency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Introduce the problem | Conditional reasoning solves practical inference problems. A concrete worked example shows the method's purpose. | Explain a worked inference, then guide a similar attempt and revisit later. This is teaching, not an approved placement test. | Every registered volunteer has an ID. Lina is registered. Therefore Lina has an ID. |
| 2. Understand sets and rules | A subset sits within a larger set; membership and nonmembership differ. Sufficiency and necessity are relative to a particular relationship. | Move a member inside/outside nested sets; explain which conclusions are forced and which remain open. | Every violinist in this orchestra is a musician. Being a musician alone does not establish being a violinist. |
| 3. Separate truth from validity | Evaluate whether the conclusion follows assuming the premises, rather than whether the premises match outside knowledge. | Compare arguments with the same form and different subject matter. Require a reason for validity. | All silver birds sing. K is a silver bird. Therefore K sings. The inference follows even if the first premise is fictional. |
| 4. Represent rules and facts | Symbols preserve complete meanings; a rule is a relationship, while a fact says a condition is satisfied. | Translate a rule and a fact separately, then derive the result. Keep symbol definitions visible. | R→I; Lina satisfies R; therefore Lina satisfies I. Builds on meaning, not arrows alone. |
| 5. Negate and contrapose | Negation covers every case outside the claim, including middle cases. A contrapositive expresses the same rule. | Negate concepts, cancel double negatives, derive the equivalent rule, and restate it in English. | Licensed→trained gives not trained→not licensed. Not expensive need not mean cheap. |
| 6. Learn Groups 1 and 2 | Identify the complete sufficient and necessary concepts despite word order, modifiers, and attribution. Distinguish only from the only. | Model indicator recognition, concept parsing, notation, and direction; then mix familiar and new phrasing. | Only trained staff may approve refunds: approve→trained. The only staff who approved were trained: same direction, different grammatical route. |
| 7. Learn Groups 3 and 4 | Unless/without and exclusion statements introduce negative relationships. Either valid contrapositive representation is acceptable. | Isolated translation, preexisting negation, meaning checks, and contrasts with unsupported reversals. | Unless the permit is renewed, the event is canceled: not renewed→canceled. No indoor events are unstaffed: indoor→staffed. |
| 8. Consolidate all groups | Recognize the same relationship across different expressions without mechanically diagramming every indicator word. | Mixed translation plus conditional/nonconditional contrasts; explain why the rule applies. | Whenever the alarm sounds, leave. Compare with a single historical statement about when an alarm sounded. |
| 9. Connect compatible rules | An intermediate result can trigger another rule. Contraposition can reveal a hidden bridge. A shared destination does not establish a chain. | Translate first; reorder; connect only licensed links; include branching and no-link examples. | A→B and B→C imply A→C. A→B and D→B do not establish A→D. |
| 10. Combine chains with facts | Distinguish a derived rule from a conclusion about a particular case. Track forward and backward consequences and what remains unknown. | Use the same chain with four different facts; ask for all supported results and a tempting unsupported one. | A→B→C and not B force not A, but leave C unresolved. |
| 11. Read meaning beyond indicator lists | Grammar, guarantees, requirements, and modified categories can express conditions without familiar indicators. | Active/passive rephrasing and full-concept parsing. Include statements that do not express a universal rule. | Approval requires inspection; inspection is required for approval. Both give approval→inspection. |
| 12. Preserve AND/OR scope | Joint sufficient conditions differ from separate necessary conditions; alternative sufficient conditions differ from alternative necessary outcomes. | Compare all four placements, trigger with partial facts, and explain when splitting is licensed. | A and B→C needs both. A→B and C gives both. A→B or C gives at least one without identifying which. |
| 13. Negate compounds | De Morgan's transformations operate on the whole expression. Use them when contraposing complex rules. | Parentheses first, transform, simplify, then test against cases. | A→(B and C) gives (not B or not C)→not A. Depends on negation and scope. |
| 14. Keep the domain explicit | A restricted rule applies only within its stated scope. Simpler notation must retain that boundary. | Identify domain conditions and check membership before using the reduced rule. | For registered students, attendance→credit. An attendee whose registration is unknown is not guaranteed credit. |
| 15. Distinguish one-way and two-way rules | A biconditional combines both directions. Exclusive alternatives relate one claim to the negation of the other. | Contrast if, only if, if and only if, and exactly one; examine allowed combinations. | Access↔membership permits both true or both false. Exactly one of A/B gives A↔not B. |
| 16. Transfer to LR tasks | Select the operation based on the question's demand, rather than repeating a familiar diagram. | Worked application, guided attempt, fresh independent application, then mixed tasks. | MBT: derive a result. SA: add a sufficient bridge. Rule application: establish every required trigger. |

This preserves the broad progression while adding explicit error contrasts and independent practice. It is not a plan to reproduce proprietary wording or exercise banks.

## How much emphasis is visible

Seven translation worksheets contain 35 visible prompts: one Group 1 set, four cumulative Group 1+2 sets, one Group 3 set, and one Group 4 set. Their schedule estimates total 210 minutes. The four sets following Group 2 include earlier indicators, attribution, modifiers, and scope. They establish substantial cumulative translation practice, not four times as much Group 2-only practice.

Two chaining worksheets contain ten visible prompts and 80 scheduled minutes. Their demands include contraposition, reordering, information that cannot join the chain, and semantic distinctions between similar phrases. This deserves a separate linking skill in our curriculum.

The five-task Draw Valid Conclusions worksheet moves from translating a sentence to coordinating premises and producing an inference. The compound worksheet contains six prompts and six inspected written explanations, adding implicit conjunctions, timing requirements, negation, compact notation and a contrast between possibility and guarantee. Short lessons are therefore followed by work that asks students to generate structure, not simply recognize a word.

The foundational conditional section also includes repeated You Try entries and two five-question adaptive entrances. Their pages promise attempts and explanations or tailored mixed practice. The runs were not completed, so exact feedback, selection algorithms, scoring, and mastery rules are unverified. Schedule estimates are study allocations, not measured video runtimes or validated recommended timings.

## Quantifiers form the following phase

The observed progression begins with overlap and boundaries, then some, most, and all; adds related quantity language; teaches translation and supported conclusions; then negates whole claims and combines quantified rules. It finishes with valid forms, applications, and contrasts against invalid forms.

Our next phase should cover separately assignable skills for quantity meaning, direction/reversal, whole-statement negation, valid linking, invalid linking, and mixed applications. Original examples:

- Some gardeners are cyclists; all cyclists wear helmets. Therefore some gardeners wear helmets. Follow the same witness through the universal rule.
- Most members volunteer; most members donate. At least some volunteers donate because both majorities share the same source population.
- Most members volunteer; most volunteers donate. This does not guarantee most members donate. The populations changed.
- Negating most members volunteer includes exactly half volunteering. Negating all members volunteer requires at least one member who does not.

Keep existence explicit: a universal rule alone does not establish that its subject group has members. Preserve contextual distinctions around only some, many, few, and overwhelming majority. Do not silently equate vague quantity language with an exact proportion.

## Scope and difficulty are independent assignment controls

Your confirmed design has three difficulties: Beginner, Medium, Advanced. Every specific skill should support these levels. A skill is not permanently assigned to a single stage. An advanced student can still need beginner work on one deficient skill.

| Assignment scope | Beginner | Medium | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific skill, such as Group 3 | Clear clauses, one relationship, explicit symbol definitions, guided explanation | Mixed phrasing, existing negatives, fewer prompts, independent meaning checks | Dense modifiers, embedded clauses, scope distinctions, unfamiliar contexts |
| Area, such as translation | Mix foundational skills within that area with support | Choose among learned procedures without announcing the group | Complex language and discriminating contrasts across the area |
| General conditionals | Mix skills already introduced in the beginner pathway | Mix translation, contraposition, linking, triggering, and compounds at medium complexity | Coordinate operations and discriminate supported/unsupported conclusions in demanding contexts |
| Full pathway | Introduce meaning and move through the ordered skills | Revisit skills with increasing independence and combinations | Extend precision and transfer across question demands |

Timing is an optional control for Medium and Advanced under your decision. Timed fluency should not silently redefine language or reasoning difficulty. Exact times, defaults, success criteria, reassessment, and advancement remain open decisions.

The archived LSAT Lab MBT teaching package supplies further explanations and contrasts. Item-by-item tagging is still needed before assigning these resources to precise skills and difficulties.

The largest gap is the connection among teaching, practice, assignment, and progress. Existing whole-resource assignments and separate saved/browser-local progress do not yet establish one independently guided course. These teaching details are proposed for review; no automatic placement or progression policy is approved.

For new formats, start with paper-first production and a deterministic reveal/self-comparison, matching the existing interaction model. Proposed additions include allowed-case cards, counterexample construction and error diagnosis. Automatic grading of those new interactions would require implementation and validation; no superiority or existing availability is claimed. A drill can be assigned without requiring the student to repeat the teaching sequence.

Alternating worked examples with student attempts, asking students to retrieve taught operations before reveal, and returning to them after a delay have a research basis. The [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) recommends worked-example/problem alternation, spaced learning and retrieval through quizzes. The [Dunlosky learning-techniques review](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/journals/pspi/learning-techniques.html) rates practice testing and distributed practice highly across varied learning contexts. These sources support those design choices; they do not establish that this LSAT curriculum is superior or determine our exact review intervals.

Prerequisite safeguards: teach basic negation before Groups 3–4; teach AND/OR before compound tasks within any group; teach whole-claim negation before rule-refutation tasks; and teach quantified existence before quantified applications. The later quantifier phase supplies the witness where an overlap inference needs one. Universal rules alone do not establish members. A contrapositive preserves the rule, while negating the whole rule asserts a counterexample. Group labels identify our translation taxonomy, not distinct laws of logic: Group 3 exception wording and Group 4 exclusions can have equivalent negative representations.

### Original Group 1 miniunit: learning what an if-clause guarantees

The [Group 1 teaching and drill draft](group-1-learning-and-drills.md) develops this example into a learning sequence and eighteen original standalone drill items, six per level, with answers, explanations and proposed optional hints. It supplements existing Activities; complete per-expression coverage and targeted platform packaging remain to be finished.

**Teach.** A sufficient condition is enough to establish the result in this rule. It need not be the only route. In an ordinary conditional statement, the complete if-clause goes before the arrow. Clause order in the sentence does not determine arrow direction.

**Worked example.** “If a parcel receives express service, it receives tracking.” Define E as express service and T as tracking. Write E→T. An express parcel must have tracking. A tracked parcel need not be express. Tracking could accompany another service. The forbidden case is E together with not T.

**Guided practice.** “A visitor receives an entry code if the visitor registers online.” First identify the if-clause: registers online. Then identify the guaranteed result: entry code. Write R→C. Explain why C→R would add a guarantee the sentence never supplied. Reveal these steps after the student attempts them.

**Independent practice.** Translate “If an exhibit is temporarily closed, its tickets remain valid.” Then answer separately what follows from a closed exhibit, and from valid tickets. Answer: closed→valid; closure establishes validity, while validity alone leaves closure unknown. Ask for a plain-language explanation before reveal.

**Error repair.** If the student reverses the rule, return to the two complete clauses and ask which fact the sentence guarantees a result from. If they infer that an open exhibit has invalid tickets, construct the allowed case of an open exhibit with valid tickets. Repair direction and unsupported inference separately.

**Fresh transfer.** “A refund is issued whenever a canceled reservation was prepaid.” Treat canceled-and-prepaid as the complete sufficient condition: (canceled AND prepaid)→refund. A prepaid reservation alone is insufficient. This is a Medium extension once AND prerequisites are taught, not a surprise Beginner requirement.

Advanced Group 1 work would add embedded modifiers and distractor rules while preserving the same operation. The separately assignable drill draws fresh items from the appropriate level; the sequence includes explanation and repair. Exact item counts, timing defaults and advancement criteria remain for Joshua to decide.
