## What the cards ask students to do

Find the complete sufficient or necessary condition, preserve its qualifications, explain its role, and place that condition on the appropriate side of the arrow. Controlled wording pairs cover initial and final if/when clauses, only versus only if, only versus the only, and a requirement appearing before required versus after requires. Backs explain the connection in ordinary English before giving notation. Source card indices are zero-based and identify the concept adapted, not a claim that our new example was generated by NotebookLM.

## Material corrections and exclusions

- **Always, only, and the only:** removed list-only memorization and mechanical next-word rules. Each expression is read in a whole sentence. Always does not automatically identify whatever follows it as the necessary target.
- **Must and compliance:** opening cards 47, 53, and 54 blur required attendance with actual attendance. The new must card keeps an instruction's requirement distinct from a report that someone followed it. Its diagram records required action, not completed action.
- **Roles:** opening card 57 describes necessary as required whenever the outcome occurs, which can obscure which condition requires it. Our foundation cards explicitly name what is enough and what is required for that sufficient condition.
- **Rule disappearance:** conditional card 15 says the rule disappears when its trigger is false. That wording was excluded. A rule remains available; a particular fact may simply leave the other condition unsettled.
- **Set sizes:** conditional cards 4 and 29 imply a necessarily smaller inner set and larger outer set. They were not included. Conditional containment does not require unequal sizes.
- **Scope:** excluded Group 3/4, exception language, quantities, compound negation, chains, biconditionals, assumption gaps, and source exercise sentences. The cards do not ask for a contrapositive.
- **Voice and examples:** replaced animal categories, source names, strained examples, bare word trivia, and LaTeX fragments with small, ordinary situations and reasoned feedback. No em dashes or unsupported learning claims.
