# Group 1: Find the sufficient idea

You already understand the relationship. Now we're going to recognize the different ways a sentence can express it.

Our Group 1 indicators are:

**if · when · where · all · the only · every · any**

These introduce the sufficient idea. That complete idea goes on the left of the arrow.

The important question is: **“What is the indicator referring to?”**

### Start with “if”

> If a student revises the essay, the student receives feedback.

If what? If the student revises the essay.

That's the sufficient idea:

**Revises the essay → Receives feedback**

Now move the same clause:

> A student receives feedback if the student revises the essay.

If what? Still, if the student revises the essay. The arrow stays the same. Sentence order changed; the relationship didn't.

### The same relationship in other words

| Sentence | What the indicator refers to | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| Every volunteer wears a badge. | Every volunteer. | Volunteer → Wears a badge |
| Any applicant who submits late pays a fee. | Any applicant who submits late. | Applicant submits late → Pays a fee |
| When the alarm sounds, the staff evacuate. | When the alarm sounds. | Alarm sounds → Staff evacuate |
| Where there is smoke, there is fire. | Where there is smoke. | Smoke → Fire |

Keep the complete idea. In the applicant sentence, “applicant” alone isn't enough. The rule applies to an applicant **who submits late**.

### Read “the only” carefully

> The only people admitted are members.

The only what? The only people admitted.

**Admitted → Member**

Everyone admitted must be a member. It doesn't tell us that every member is admitted.

Don't skip “the.” We'll compare “the only” with “only” in the next lesson.
