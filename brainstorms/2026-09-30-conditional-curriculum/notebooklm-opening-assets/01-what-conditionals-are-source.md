# Start with a guarantee

Take this statement:

> All dogs are mammals.

Now suppose I tell you that Luna is a dog. You know something else about Luna: she's a mammal.

You didn't need another fact about her. The rule already covers every dog, and Luna falls inside that group.

We can say the same rule another way:

> If something is a dog, then it is a mammal.

That's a conditional. It tells you that **when one condition is met, another must be met too.**

Notice the guarantee. The statement doesn't say dogs are usually mammals. It leaves no room for a dog that isn't a mammal.

We can write the relationship like this:

**Dog → Mammal**

Read the arrow as “if this is true, then that must be true.”

The rule doesn't tell us that a particular dog exists. It tells us what must be true of anything that is a dog. When we add the fact about Luna, we can apply the rule to her.

### See the relationship

Imagine a box containing every mammal. Every dog has to fit inside that box. Mammals can sit outside the dog box, but dogs can't sit outside the mammal box.

That's why knowing “dog” gives us “mammal.”
