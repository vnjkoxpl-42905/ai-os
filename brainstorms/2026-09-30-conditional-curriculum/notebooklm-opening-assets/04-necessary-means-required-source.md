# Necessary means required

Now look at the other side:

**Dog → Mammal**

Being a mammal is **necessary** for being a dog. A dog has to be a mammal.

Necessary means required. It doesn't mean enough.

Return to the assignment:

**Earns full credit → Passes**

Under this rule, passing is necessary for earning full credit. You can't earn full credit and fail. But knowing someone passed doesn't tell you they earned full credit.

Here's a different example:

> Entering the archive requires a pass.

**Enters the archive → Has a pass**

The pass is required. That's why it goes on the right. Having a pass doesn't, by itself, establish that someone enters the archive.

### Put the two sides together

| Sufficient | Necessary |
|---|---|
| Enough to guarantee the other condition. | Required whenever the sufficient condition is met. |
| Goes on the left. | Goes on the right. |
| If I have this, what must follow? | What must I have in order for the left side to be true? |

These are two roles in the same relationship. You don't decide that a condition is always sufficient or always necessary. You read the particular rule.
