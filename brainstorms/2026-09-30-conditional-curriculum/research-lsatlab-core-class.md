## Original examples, not provider exercises

- **Unless:** A shipment cannot depart unless it is labeled. Departure → labeled. Labeled alone does not prove departure. An unlabeled shipment cannot depart.
- **Missing bridge:** Delayed deliveries cause missed deadlines. Missed deadlines cause penalties. The argument concludes delayed deliveries lower supplier ratings. A bridge from penalties to lower ratings proves that conclusion; repeating that delays cause penalties adds no new bridge.
- **Refuting a rule:** To disprove “Every registered vehicle is inspected,” find a registered vehicle that is not inspected. An inspected vehicle that is unregistered does not disprove that rule. Negating the rule means such a counterexample exists; it does not mean every registered vehicle is uninspected.
- **Nested rule:** “If an application is complete, then if it is approved it receives funding” is equivalent to “Complete and approved → receives funding.” Neither condition alone suffices.
- **Compound conclusion:** The premises already prove that a qualifying project is documented. The conclusion says a qualifying project is documented and audited. The missing bridge must establish auditing; it need not re-establish documentation.

## Proposed adaptation for LSAT U

Turn each contrast into an individually assignable skill at Beginner, Medium and Advanced, then include the same skills in mixed practice. Preserve the committed first attempt and explanation before feedback. Follow correction with a fresh original item. Separate untimed understanding from optional Medium/Advanced timing. Treat selective diagramming as a choice supported by skill and task structure, rather than a universal speed rule.
