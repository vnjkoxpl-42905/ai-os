## 1. What a conditional says

Original rule: Every order marked ready has been packed.

Visual: Warm cream studio, two simple parcel stations. First an open parcel is packed and closed. A teal ready tag is applied only to that closed parcel. Show a second parcel being packed without a ready tag. No arrows suggesting that packing causes ready status. No generated text, logos or numbers.

Visible explanation: “An order marked ready has been packed. That is what our rule promises. We can have a packed order without knowing whether it is marked ready.”

Text alternative: An order marked ready is also packed. A second packed order has no known ready status. Ready implies packed; packed alone leaves ready unknown.

## 2. Why direction matters

Original rule: Every reserved locker displays a nameplate.

Visual: One locker receives a reservation tag and a nameplate. A different locker already has a nameplate but its reservation status is hidden, represented by an unfilled tag placeholder. Do not show the second locker definitely unreserved. End with the two nameplates visible while only the first reservation is known.

Visible explanation: “If the locker is reserved, the nameplate follows. If you only see a nameplate, you still need more information about its reservation.”

Text alternative: Reservation guarantees a nameplate. A nameplate alone does not establish reservation.

## 3. Sufficient means enough

Original rule: Every order marked ready has been packed.

Visual: A teal ready tag comes into focus on one closed parcel. The camera reveals that parcel in a larger group of packed parcels. Other closed parcels have no visible ready tag. Keep the relation geometric, not a mechanism that turns tags into packing.

Visible explanation: “Knowing the order is marked ready is enough to know it has been packed. Enough does not mean the only way. Another order could already be packed while waiting for its ready label.”

Text alternative: Ready status is sufficient for packed status under this rule. Packing may also be true of orders whose ready status is unknown.

## 4. Necessary means required

Original rule: Borrowing a camera requires a signed form.

Visual: A desk with a camera and signed form. A camera-borrowing case is shown with its signed form beside it. Then show a separate signed form on the desk with no camera being borrowed. Avoid a barrier animation that implies the form alone opens a gate.

Visible explanation: “If someone borrows the camera, a signed form is required. Someone could sign the form and never borrow the camera. Required does not mean enough.”

Text alternative: Camera borrowing implies a signed form. A signed form alone does not establish borrowing.

## 5. Group 1: find the complete sufficient idea

Original rule: A tray gets a green sticker if its lid is sealed.

Visual: A simple tray with a sealed lid and a green sticker. Show a close-up of the complete sealed-lid state, then the sticker. Keep animations in two steady panels for caption overlays authored in the app. No letter shapes or AI text. Do not show a sticker proving a sealed lid.

Visible explanation: “If what? Its lid is sealed. That is the sufficient idea. It comes second in the sentence, but it goes left in the diagram. Sealed lid implies green sticker.”

Text alternative: The clause after if identifies the complete sufficient condition even when that clause appears last in the sentence.

## 6. Group 2: find the complete requirement

Original rule: Borrowing a camera requires a signed form.

Visual: Camera-borrowing case with a signed form. Next to it, a signed form with borrowing status intentionally unknown. Two separate panels. Calm warm studio lighting, teal and copper accents, no text or arrow reversal.

Visible explanation: “Requires what? A signed form. The signed form goes right. Now check your direction: if the camera was borrowed, the form must have been signed. A signed form alone does not tell you the camera was borrowed.”

Text alternative: Borrowing implies signed form. Requires points to the necessary condition. Meeting it does not establish the sufficient condition.
