## Every worked example and instructional operation

1. **Group 1 p1: zombies attack NYC, real-estate market slips.** IF identifies the first event. Source assigns Z and RES, diagrams Z → RES, forms not-RES → not-Z, and reads it back. Diagram and contrapositive agree. Reuse as a later end-to-end worked sequence after negation is taught, not as a substitute for the already approved opening law-school example.
2. **Group 2 p1: lasting accomplishment only if hard work.** ALV → HW; not-HW → not-ALV. Both agree with the statement. Preserve “lasting value” rather than reducing the subject to any accomplishment. Useful side-by-side comparison with an IF rendering of the same relationship.
3. **Conditional intro p1: all dogs are unselfish.** D → not-S; contrapositive S → not-D. Correct if S means selfish, as the English establishes. An explicit symbol legend would make the lesson easier to read. This is a negative necessary condition, so place it after ordinary positive-condition translations.
4. **Conditional intro p2: breathing requires air.** Breathing present establishes air; air absent rules out breathing; breathing absent leaves air unresolved; air present leaves breathing unresolved. These are the four conditional inference states, not four new conditional rules. Use as a later four-case activity after original-arrow and missing-requirement teaching.

## Corrections and boundaries before integration

- **Definite source typo, intro p2:** the paragraph says “last two rows” are the productive cases. The table and the paragraph's final reference to “first two rows” show that it should say first two. Do not copy the typo.
- **Overbroad equivalence claim, intro p1:** a contrapositive is not literally the only logically equivalent formulation. For the pair of complete conditions, it is the other standard valid arrow form obtained by reversing and negating both. Teach that restricted claim, not a universal exclusivity claim.
- **Overbroad no-information wording, intro p2:** “Nothing” and “ignore the conditional” mean this rule plus that fact alone does not settle the other condition. They do not erase the rule or say no information exists. Preserve that limitation in feedback.
- **Inventory inconsistency, Group 2:** requires appears in the p2 review but not the p1 heading. The intro includes it. Current lesson inventory should retain requires.
- **Inventory difference, Group 1:** this source lists if, when, where, all, the only, every, any. Joshua's newer approved on-page families also include whenever, anytime, every time, in order to, people who, each. These are different inventories, not proof that either set of teaching material disappeared. Preserve provenance and contextual examples; do not silently replace the current approved list.
- **Meaning before speed:** both breakdowns p2 tell learners to run a mechanical machine and think very little. That is a fluency-stage teaching stance, not suitable opening copy under Joshua's current concept-first preferences. Keep the useful repeatable procedure, but require learners to check the meaning and full conditions.
- **Context matters:** “immediately following” is a useful shortcut for these demonstrated constructions, not a universal lexical guarantee. Preserve the entire phrase, distinguish only from the only, and preserve obligation wording when must states a requirement rather than observed compliance.
- References to unless/Group 3 on the breakdowns' second pages are motivational asides, not in-scope exercises. Do not expand the Group 1/2 unit because of them.
