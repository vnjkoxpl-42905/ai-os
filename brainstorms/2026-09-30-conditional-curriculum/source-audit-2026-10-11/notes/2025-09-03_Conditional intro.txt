LSAT Logical Reasoning: Contrapositives and
Conditionals
Overview of Conditional Statements
Group 1: Sufficient Condition Indicators
Keywords: IF, WHEN, WHERE, ALL, THE ONLY, EVERY, ANY
Rule: Introduces the sufficient condition.
Group 2: Necessary Condition Indicators
Keywords: ONLY IF, ONLY WHEN, ONLY WHERE, ONLY, ALWAYS, MUST, REQUIRES
Rule: Introduces the necessary condition.
Steps to Analyze Conditional Statements
1. Identify the logical operator.
2. Identify the two main concepts (groups, categories, events, ideas).
3. Assign symbols to the two main concepts.
4. Apply the translation rule.
5. Find the contrapositive.
6. Translate back to English.
Understanding Contrapositives
Definition: Contrapositives are statements that are logically equivalent to the
original conditional statement.
Properties: They are literally the same relationship expressed in reverse form,
the only thing logically equivalent to the original relationship.
Example:
English: All dogs are unselfish.
Lawgic: D → ¬S
Contrapositive in English: If something is selfish, then it’s not a dog.
Contrapositive in Lawgic: S → ¬D
Key Concept: Contrapositives represent the only other way in "Lawgic" to convey
a specific conditional relationship.
How to Formulate a Contrapositive(FLIP AND NEGATE )
1. Switch the symbols around the arrow.
2. Apply negation to each symbol.
3. There is no Step 3; it's a simple two-step process to ensure clarity.
Importance of Contrapositives in LSAT
Application: Essential for solving logical reasoning problems effectively.
Strategy: Often provides the correct perspective to answer tricky questions
correctly.
Highlights
Critical Insight: "Contrapositives will save your life on the LSAT."

Example of Logical Equivalence: The contrapositive S → ¬D is as valid as D →
¬S, The original statement and its contrapositive are all you know from your
conditional relationship. The LSAT will tempt you to erroneously assume other
things from your conditional. Don't fall for these ploys. For example, they
will give you a conditional statement and then say, "We know the necessary
condition is happening!" When they do this, you should be like, "Cool. So I can
ignore the conditional." The presence of the necessary condition doesn't make
anything happen.

The last two rows of this chart represent the only two situations that make anything

in the first two rows of this chart, the conditional might as well not exist.
