## Original analysis: what LSAT U should learn from the design

The chronology supports Joshua's decision to place quantity after conditionals: the class relies on arrow direction, contraposition, common terms, and valid chains before adding quantified relationships. It does not separately remediate these prerequisites.

Training demand progresses from identifying a statement's strength to testing whether a proposed connection is forced. Numerical or spatial countermodels provide a reason for failure, rather than merely teaching a list of permitted patterns. Live polls expose disagreement; students then explain their reasoning while the teacher diagnoses it. This creates richer feedback than displaying the correct letter alone.

The first application requires combining negative categories with a quantified existence statement. The second exposes errors in reversing strength, importing an unasserted region into a drawn set, comparing counts from percentages, and omitting the restrictive part of a subject. The third rewards reading the entire stimulus before drawing, because several relationships can be irrelevant to the required inference. These distinct demands should become independently assignable LSAT U skills, followed by mixed practice.

The observed worksheet request reveals a potential packaging gap in this class, not proof that the platform has no quantity activities. Independent learners need short translation and inference drills before complex question transfer, plus fresh feedback after an error. A class recording can demonstrate reasoning but does not itself supply that practice loop.

## Original accuracy checks before adaptation

- **Most means greater than 50%, not a minimum of 51%.** A group can have 50.1% possessing a property. Use exact thresholds in teaching and assessment.
- **Some means at least one, not at least 1%.** One member of a large group may be far below 1%. Keep counts and proportions distinct.
- **All does not by itself assert existence in ordinary formal conditional reasoning.** From all A are B and all A are C, a B/C overlap requires an A to exist. The class's infer-down and shared-antecedent all/all explanations omit that premise. In its first application, a separate some statement establishes relevant existence; that can repair an inference in that context, but not the unconditional general rule.
- **Most entails some; it is not equivalent to some.** A momentary equality-style caption should not become course wording.
- **No new cross-term inference is not no inference of any kind.** Premises can still yield restatements, existential weakening, or reversals of some. Scope the pattern rules to their intended new endpoint connection.
- **A some/most prohibition needs scope qualifications.** Nested subgroup statements can encode additional information. General pattern shortcuts assume the specific three-term arrangement under discussion; they must not override explicit subset structure or extra premises.
- **Few and context-sensitive adverbs need careful semantics.** A platform's heuristic classification is not proof of a universal lexical equivalence. Teach the weakest warranted claim and distinguish plurality from majority.
- **A drawing's blank region does not establish members.** Use marked witnesses or explicit cardinalities when proving overlap or building counterexamples.
- Reported test-frequency guidance is the instructor's estimate, not a verified distribution of current tests.

## Original examples for our version

1. **Same denominator:** of 100 festival volunteers, 60 speak French and 60 speak Arabic. At least 20 speak both. Change the numbers to 51 and 51; the guaranteed intersection becomes much smaller. This trains why the output need not be most.
2. **Different denominators:** most photographers are club members, and most club members are cyclists. Construct a larger club containing many cyclists outside the photographer group. This trains why apparent arrow alignment is insufficient.
3. **Existence plus universal:** at least one archivist is bilingual; all bilingual archivists can translate the catalog. Identify a witness and carry that witness through the universal rule.
4. **Universal without existence:** every purple badge holder is cleared and every purple badge holder is trained. An empty purple-badge group satisfies both. Add an explicit badge holder before concluding overlap exists.
5. **Scope:** most night-shift nurses are certified. Keep night-shift in the quantified subject; do not diagram this as most nurses are certified.
6. **Strength and quantities:** some river samples are contaminated and most lake samples are contaminated. Neither fact compares the number of contaminated samples without group sizes.

Beginner work can isolate meaning, direction, and marked witnesses. Medium can combine a quantified statement with a universal and contrast valid with invalid pairs. Advanced can add negative terms, scope, redundant information, countermodels, and LSAT transfer. General mixed assignments and targeted assignments should coexist at all three levels; Medium and Advanced can optionally be timed.
