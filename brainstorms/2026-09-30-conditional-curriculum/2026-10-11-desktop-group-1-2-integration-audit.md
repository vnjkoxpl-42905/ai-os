## Interaction designs justified by these worksheets

1. **Select the indicator, then both conditions.** Separate these selections so students cannot mistake a word such as always for a condition. Highlight only after an attempt.
2. **Repair a cropped condition.** Show a draft that omits registered, applicant, before noon or can. Ask what must be restored and explain why the shorter claim changes meaning.
3. **Rewrite before diagramming.** Student forms an if/then statement, then locates its two complete sides. Accept equivalent wording, not one exact string.
4. **Place both sides.** Use the same accessible controls for pointer, keyboard and touch. Give feedback for reversal separately from feedback for missing words.
5. **Distinguish permission from action.** For can attend, ask whether actual attendance follows. For must attend, distinguish required attendance from observed compliance.
6. **Return to the same sentence later.** An earlier indicator item can become a contrapositive item after that lesson; label it as a new operation on a familiar rule, not a new source question.
7. **Read the diagram back.** Check that the student's diagram preserves the original rule before adding a contrapositive. This follows the source's English-to-symbol-to-English sequence.

These are proposed adaptations, not features implemented by this audit.

## Correct or hold before student use

Highest-priority defects are documented per item in the linked ledgers:

- **Translation 20:** the key diagrams Sunny in Philadelphia → Always. Always is not a complete necessary condition. Hold or rewrite the item with an explicit domain and a corrected relation.
- **Translation 28:** the proposed arrow does not capture the exclusivity in pleasure is the only good. At least Good → Pleasure is needed; the ordinary-language assertion may carry an additional claim. Do not grade the existing one-way key as the sole correct interpretation.
- **Translation 33:** the key calls will a sufficient-condition indicator and loses the after-a-week restriction. Identify the covered people and preserve the full result.
- **Translation 10 / Indicator ID Part 1 item 4:** explanations or group labels conflict over the only. Inspect the complete phrase; separate a wrong label/explanation from a correctly directed diagram.
- **If/then rephrase items 1, 6, 7, 11, 20 and 25 with both indicator families:** examples combine all/any/every with must/requires/always. A forced single-label response can be underspecified. Define whether the task asks for one named expression or all expressions; do not mark a defensible response wrong. This does not reinstate then as a standalone indicator.
- **Indicator ID Part 2 item 6:** the key changes a collateral requirement into a claim that collateral secures a loan. That is an unsupported result.
- **Rephrase item 4:** the only key that opens a door does not rule out non-key ways of opening it. The key broadens the claim. Item 18 also needs a scoped negation: one evaluator not signing does not exclude another qualified signer.
- **Intro 2:** expect thunder becomes thunder will follow in the key. That changes the statement's strength.
- **Intro 4 and 9:** obligation wording becomes actual compliance. The source needs a derivative correction or a deliberately framed interpretation question.
- **Intro 5, 8, 13:** key abbreviations omit student, member or applicant restrictions; Intro 8 also loses can.
- **Reference intro p2:** last two rows should be first two for the productive inference cases. The table itself shows the correct distinction.
- **PDF strikethroughs:** extracted plain text can silently lose negation. Store explicit negation in the derived data and verify against the rendered source.

Some difficult items require prerequisites rather than correction: translation 25, 27 and 35 require negating a whole compound or quantified condition. Other items need clarity work, especially translation 31's unclear plant-soil wording. Preserve source wording separately; never silently repair the original evidence.
