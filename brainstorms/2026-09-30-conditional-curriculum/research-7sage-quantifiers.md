## Distinctions the original LSAT U course should retain

**Some is a lower bound.** It can include all unless context excludes that possibility. A learner who hears some as some-but-not-all will overinfer. Teach minimum guarantee separately from conversational implication.

**Most means more than half of the stated source group.** The denominator belongs to the claim's direction. An overlap with most members of one group does not establish that most members of the other group participate. Some relationships are reversible; majority claims generally are not.

**Some and most do not take ordinary conditional contrapositives.** The source's quantified arrow is not a universal guarantee. Teach valid quantity reasoning directly instead of importing every operation learned for all.

**Weak quantity terms are not automatically majorities.** Many does not establish most. A precise course should distinguish a determinate threshold from loose quantity language and avoid invented numerical bounds.

**Order changes whether a mixed inference works.** Some-before-all and most-before-all can support valid conclusions; all-before-some/most does not generally license the same extension. Students should explain the overlap or membership reasoning, not only memorize the order.

**Two majorities of the same source force overlap.** A successive most chain does not generally provide that guarantee. Keep the shared-source proof visible and contrast it with a chain whose populations change.

**Whole-claim negation is different from contraposition.** Not most means at most half, including exactly half. Do not silently replace it with fewer than half. Negating a relationship should deny its actual quantified scope rather than negate both terms and reverse them.

**Existence needs an explicit assumption.** A move from all to most to some requires a nonempty relevant domain. A universal rule can be true without establishing any members. Do not turn the source shorthand into an unconditional existence theorem. LSAT U should state the assumed domain or supplied existence fact before using such an inference.

## Original LSAT U adaptation

Build the quantifier phase after students can preserve a complete condition, identify direction and distinguish a rule from an existence fact. Preserve Beginner / Medium / Advanced while making each specific skill independently assignable.

Beginner should cover bounds, scope, denominator direction, all/none/some/most translation, existence and whole-claim negation. Medium should teach valid mixed inferences and paired invalid contrasts, including some/most with all and shared-source majorities. Advanced should mix language, changing populations, distractor quantities and real-question transfer without hiding unsupported existence assumptions.

For each subsection, provide one original worked explanation, a guided attempt, fresh independent practice and a short exit check. Ask students to justify what must follow and construct a situation showing why a tempting conclusion can fail. Record quantity meaning, direction, existence, negation and inference errors separately.
