# Conditional curriculum: Brainstorm / Discovery Notes

Date: 2026-09-30 · Goal: Define a complete conditional-reasoning curriculum that lets Joshua place a student, choose teaching and practice, and identify when they are ready to advance.

## Summary / key decisions

- Joshua has several existing pieces, but lacks a clear setup for each stage of a student's conditional expertise.
- Requested scope includes LSAT U Activities, drills, notes, and LSAT questions requiring conditional reasoning.
- Wants an interview to clarify the intended curriculum before implementation.
- The curriculum is intended for students to work through independently. Teacher guidance is not the primary workflow.
- Cover the full range from complete beginner through advanced/expert, with placement at the student's current proficiency.
- Each placement should lead to appropriate teaching and practice. Possible formats include Activities, drills, bootcamps, and flashcards; no particular format has been mandated yet.
- Cover every skill needed to understand and execute conditional reasoning. Concrete expert criteria and the skill map remain to be defined together.
- Joshua can identify a student's gaps and assign specific subsections, while students can also work through the general curriculum independently.
- The curriculum must support uneven proficiency: target a deficient skill without requiring already-mastered material again.
- Timing is selectable for suitable drills; universal timed completion has not been requested.
- Stage definitions, placement criteria, progression criteria, and the curriculum's home remain undecided.

- Asked: Should this curriculum primarily guide tutoring, or should students work through it independently?
- Captured: "I wanted to have it for students to work through independently."
- Decision: Design around independent student progression. The initial teacher-guided recommendation was not accepted.
- Interview method: Joshua invoked grill-with-docs. Its grilling and domain-modeling skills now govern the interview: ask independent unresolved decisions in rounds; checkpoint answers; clarify terms as they settle; create ADRs only for qualifying tradeoffs.

### Q2 and Q3: Entry audience and completion outcome
- Asked: Who should be able to start, and what should completion mean?
- Captured: "all the way from the very beginning to more advanced, like expert"; "all levels covered to know where they are"; "every skill that's required to understand conditional perfectly executed."
- Decisions: Full beginner-to-expert coverage; placement should route students to work suited to their level; comprehensive skill coverage is the objective.
- Format preference: Activities, drills, bootcamps, and possibly flashcards are candidate tools. Joshua wants help determining the structure rather than supplying a pre-existing level system.
- Still unresolved: Whether placement produces one overall level or a profile by skill; observable expert standard; boundaries of the conditional curriculum.

- Unclear transcript fragment: Final reference to players/video has no clear curriculum instruction; no requirement inferred from it.
