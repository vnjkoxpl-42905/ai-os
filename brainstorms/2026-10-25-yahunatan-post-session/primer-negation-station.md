# Negation Station

**30 sentences. Negate each sentence: write what would make it not true. Repeat the set until you can explain your answers.**

Do this before the necessary assumption questions. In those questions, you will take an answer you are considering and treat it as not true. Practising that separately lets you concentrate on the argument when you get there.

## Negate means treat it as not true

Suppose the sentence says **all the guests arrived.** What would make it false? Just one guest not arriving. You don't need every guest to be missing. So write: **at least one guest did not arrive.**

Now take **some guests arrived.** One missing guest would not make that false. Other guests could still have arrived. This time you need **no guests arrived.**

That is the habit: ask what would make this sentence false. Don't change more than you need to.

## Do not write the contrapositive instead

**If the café is busy, Sam works late.** To make that false, the café must be busy while Sam does not work late. A quiet café would not do it: the sentence never told us what Sam does when it is quiet.

The contrapositive says **if Sam does not work late, the café is not busy.** That says the same thing as the original rule. It doesn't make the original false.

Check your answer both ways. Could it and the original sentence both be true? Then you haven't contradicted the original. Could they both be false? Then your answer went too far.

For example, **all guests arrived** and **no guests arrived** can both be false: half the guests might arrive. That's why changing all to none isn't negation. **At least one guest did not arrive** gets it right: it's true exactly when all guests arrived is false.
