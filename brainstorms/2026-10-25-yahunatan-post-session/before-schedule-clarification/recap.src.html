Session recap · October 20

Necessary Assumptions
and Negation

A necessary assumption is something the argument needs for its reasoning to work. Start with the evidence and the exact conclusion. Then ask what connection the author is taking for granted.

Step zero

Split the argument in two

Conclusion on one side, evidence on the other. Then treat them completely differently.

The evidenceAccept every word as true. You don't fight it.
The conclusionAccept nothing yet. The author has to earn it.
Grant nothing that isn't written Common sense will fill in the gaps for you, and that's exactly what the test is counting on. Primary doesn't mean only. Most doesn't mean all. If it isn't on the page, the author doesn't get it.
Finding the gap

What the author never said

The town promised to repave Elm Street. Not one block has been finished this month. So the town has broken its promise.

No block finished this month → promise broken

Those aren't the same thing. To get from one to the other, the author has to believe something he never wrote down.

The assumption If the town were keeping its promise, at least one block would be finished within a month. Nobody said how long the promise was supposed to take. Maybe a block takes six weeks.
After we separated the plan from the evidence, you explained that the plan had no stated one-year deadline. Keep checking the time limit instead of supplying one.
The two tools

Negation is not the contrapositive

Both use a "not," so they get mixed up. They do different jobs.

Contrapositive

The same conditional rule said a different way. Reverse the two complete conditions and negate each one. Preserve all qualifications; negating an AND condition uses OR, and negating an OR condition uses AND.

Looks like If you're in New York, you're in the US. If you're not in the US, you're not in New York.
Use itTo read a rule from the other end. It says exactly what the original says.
Negation

A claim that is true exactly when the original is false. Contradict the complete idea, without inventing a stronger opposite.

Looks like If the café is busy, Sam works late. The café is busy, and Sam doesn't work late.
Use itTo test a candidate assumption. Negate it while keeping the premises true. Ask whether the reasoning can still support the conclusion.
A contrapositive, if trueSays the same thing as the rule.
A negation, if trueContradicts the original claim. It is not an equivalent rule.

For Elm Street, the negation is: the town is keeping its promise, and still no block was finished this month. That possibility preserves the evidence but defeats this inference. The conclusion might still be true for another reason; the evidence no longer gets us there.

You distinguished negation from the contrapositive in your own words. Keep that distinction, then work out what makes the complete claim false.
Test the actual claim

Strength is a reason to check, not a reason to reject

A necessary assumption is required for this argument's reasoning. A weak-looking answer can still be irrelevant. A strong answer can be required. Neither its tone nor one word decides.

Suppose the author concludes that every registered vehicle passed inspection. The argument might need a claim about every vehicle. Do not eliminate it merely because it says every. Ask whether this argument can work if that answer is false.

Keep modality and scope intactAlex will travel is a prediction. Alex must travel can express a requirement or a logical necessity, depending on context. They are not automatically interchangeable. Preserve words such as probably, must and only, and the complete meaning they express.

A sufficient assumption makes the conclusion follow when added to the premises. A necessary assumption is something the reasoning cannot do without. An answer can be one without being the other.

Two useful roles

Connect an idea or rule out a threat

An assumption can connect the evidence to a conclusion, or exclude a possibility that would undermine the connection. Read its meaning. Either role can be worded positively or negatively; the presence of not does not determine whether it is necessary.

Predict the missing connection if you can, then test the actual answers. Do not select by grammatical shape.

Recognition drill

Negate it

Say the negation out loud, then tap to check

The weakest claim that contradicts the original. Not the opposite.

All of the guests arrived.· Not all of the guests arrived Some of the guests arrived.· None of the guests arrived The shop is open on Monday.· The shop is not open on Monday If it rains, the game is off.· It rains, and the game is still on

What is actually required?

A club's only voting rule says that a person may vote only if registered. The author concludes that Maya may vote. What must be true?

Maya is registered.

Required. Negate it: Maya is not registered. The supplied rule then rules out permission to vote. Registration alone does not prove permission, but it is necessary.

Every registered person may vote.

Not required. Other registered people could be barred while Maya is allowed. The conclusion concerns Maya, not every registered person.

The finalist

On every necessary assumption question

  1. Split it. Accept the evidence, accept nothing in the conclusion.
  2. Find the gap. What does the conclusion need that the evidence never said?
  3. Read every complete answer. Ask what connection or protection it supplies.
  4. Check scope and strength. Does this argument require that much, about that group?
  5. Negate a finalist. Keep the premises true and test whether the reasoning still supports its conclusion.
