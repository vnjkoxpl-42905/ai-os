Facts,Rules,and What Follows

Every trained person has a badge. Start with that rule. Here are two different ways to use it.

Fact

Something established as true about a person or an object. “Mira is trained.”

Rule

What must be true if a condition is met. “Every trained person has a badge.”

1Apply a rule to a fact

Fact
Mira is trained.
Rule
Every trained person has a badge.
ConclusionMira has a badge.

Mira meets the rule's starting condition: she is trained. The rule tells us she has a badge.

A conclusion about Mira.

Every trained person has a badge.
Rule 2
Every badge holder has building access.
ConclusionEvery trained person has building access.

The first rule gets us from trained to badge. The second gets us from badge to access. The shared condition, has a badge, connects them.

A new rule. It does not establish that anyone is trained.

01Facts state what is true

A fact supplied by the question is your starting point. A new fact needs proof from what you were given.

“Mira is trained” establishes training for Mira. It does not establish anyone else's training, a badge for Mira, or a relationship between training and badges.

A fact can also tell us that a condition is absent: “Niko does not have a badge.” That is a fact about Niko. Whether it proves anything about his training depends on the rule we are given.

Worked answer

The east gate is not locked. Nothing here establishes whether the west gate is locked. The fact names one gate; we cannot transfer it to a different gate.

02Rules connect conditions

A conditional rule covers every person or object that meets its starting condition. It does not establish that anyone or anything meets that condition.

In “Everyone who is trained has a badge,” training is the starting condition. Having a badge is the result that must follow for that same person.

Write the conditions as short phrases: Is trained → Has a badge. The arrow connects the condition we start with to the result that must follow. If a person is trained, that same person must have a badge.

Being trained is sufficient to establish that someone has a badge: it is enough to trigger this rule. Having a badge is necessary for being trained under this rule: a trained person cannot lack a badge.

That does not mean training is the only way to get a badge. It also does not establish that anyone is trained. The rule could hold even if no one met its starting condition.

03Apply a rule to the same person or object

Definition

Applying a rule means finding an established fact that satisfies the rule's starting condition, then concluding the required result for that same person or object.

Apply the rule forward

Given fact

Mira is trained.

Rule

Everyone who is trained has a badge.

Match

“Mira is trained” satisfies the rule's condition, “is trained.”

Therefore

Mira has a badge.

Why

The rule covers every trained person, including Mira. We have proved a new fact about Mira. We have not made a claim about everyone who has a badge.

What follows?

Does the rule alone establish that a confidential file exists?

Worked answer

This file requires restricted storage. The fact establishes that this particular file is confidential. The rule alone establishes a requirement for any confidential file, without establishing that any such file exists.

04Link rules to produce a new rule

Definition

Linking rules means matching the result of one rule to the starting condition of another, so that together they establish a new conditional rule.

What two rules tell us on their own

First rule: Every late application requires special approval.

Second rule: Every application requiring special approval needs a supervisor's signature.

New rule

Every late application needs a supervisor's signature.

Match

“Requires special approval” is the result of the first rule and the starting condition of the second. The complete condition matches, not just the word “approval.”

Therefore

Every late application needs a supervisor's signature.

Why

Consider any application that is late. The first rule requires approval; that satisfies the second rule's condition, which requires a signature. This proves the general connection Submitted late → Needs a supervisor's signature. It does not establish that any application was late.

05Add a fact to the chain

Given fact

This application was submitted late.

Rules
Submitted late→Requires special approval→Needs a supervisor's signature
Therefore

This application requires special approval and needs a supervisor's signature.

The rules-only conclusion was a new if-then rule. The fact that this application was late lets us apply the chain to this application. If we have only the rules, we have not established that any application was late.

Try itSeparate a new rule from a new fact.
Given
  • Every trained person has a badge.
  • Every badge holder has access.
  • No fact identifies a trained person.

Can you conclude that Mira has access?

What new rule can you conclude?

Worked answer

You cannot conclude that Mira has access. Nothing establishes that Mira meets the chain's starting condition. You can conclude that every trained person has access. That is a new rule, not a fact about Mira.

06Recognize connections the rules do not prove

1Do not reverse the rule

Given rule

Every trained person has a badge. Is trained → Has a badge.

Proposed conclusion

Anyone with a badge is trained.

Possible example

An untrained visitor has a badge. The rule still holds: every trained person has a badge. But this badge holder is not trained.

What follows

A badge alone does not prove training. Knowing only that someone is not trained also leaves their badge status open. The rule makes no badge claim about untrained people.

2A shared starting condition does not connect the results

Use only the rules given in this new example. Rules from earlier examples do not carry over.

Given rules

Every late application requires special approval. Every late application needs a supervisor's signature.

Proposed conclusion

Every application requiring special approval needs a supervisor's signature.

Possible example

An on-time application requires approval for another reason, without needing a signature. Neither rule is broken. They only tell us what late applications require.

What follows

If an application is late, both rules apply to it. It requires approval and needs a signature. Approval alone does not prove that it needs a signature.

Pause and predict?

Late applications need approval and a signature. What does approval alone prove?

Neither is guaranteed. An on-time application could require approval without needing a signature. Both original rules still hold because neither is triggered by that application.

3A shared result does not connect the starting conditions

Given rules

Confidential files require restricted storage. Medical files require restricted storage.

Proposed conclusion

Every confidential file is a medical file.

Possible example

A confidential financial report requires restricted storage but is not a medical file. Restricted storage can cover both kinds of files separately.

What follows

Neither type of file has been shown to belong to the other. Ending at the same condition does not create a link between their starting conditions.

Check the whole condition before linking

Find the result of the first rule and the starting condition of the second. They must match, including any negation or qualifier. If they do not match, these rules do not form a chain as written. Keep them separate.

07Use the contrapositive when the result is absent

A different fact calls for a different move. Suppose Niko does not have a badge, and the same rule says everyone who is trained has a badge.

If Niko were trained, the rule would require Niko to have a badge. That would contradict the given fact. So Niko is not trained.

Given fact

Niko does not have a badge.

Rule
Is trained→Has a badge
Match

The fact denies the required result. Restate the rule as No badge → Not trained.

Therefore

Niko is not trained.

Why

Training cannot be present while the rule's required result is absent. This is the contrapositive: reverse the arrow and negate both complete conditions.

The contrapositive states the same rule. It does not deny the original rule, and it is not the unsupported reversal Has a badge → Is trained.

Four facts, one unchanged rule

The rule is Is trained → Has a badge. The rule stays the same. Change only what we know about the person.

Fact 1: The person is trained

Match

The original starting condition is met.

What follows

The person has a badge.

Why

Every trained person has a badge under this rule. Apply it to this person.

Fact 2: The person has no badge

Match

The required result is absent. This meets the contrapositive's starting condition.

What follows

The person is not trained.

Why

A trained person without a badge would contradict the rule.

Fact 3: The person has a badge

Match

We have only the original result. The rule does not say that everyone with a badge is trained.

What follows

Whether this person is trained remains unknown.

Why

An untrained visitor with a badge fits the rule. A trained person with a badge also fits it.

Fact 4: The person is not trained

Match

The original starting condition is absent. The rule only guarantees badges for trained people.

What follows

Whether this person has a badge remains unknown.

Why

An untrained person could have a badge or lack one. Both situations fit the rule.

Trained, but no badge contradicts the rule. The other three combinations fit.

What if all you know is that it does require restricted storage?

Worked answer

If it does not require restricted storage, it is not confidential. That is the contrapositive. If it does require restricted storage, its confidentiality remains unknown. A nonconfidential file could require restricted storage for another reason.

You do not have to write every contrapositive automatically. If the fact clearly meets the original starting condition, apply that rule. If the fact denies its result, or a connection is difficult to see, a contrapositive can make the supported move clear.

Carry a negative fact through a chain

Return to the two application rules: Submitted late → Requires special approval → Needs a supervisor's signature.

Now add a different fact: This application does not need a supervisor's signature. If this application required special approval, the second rule would require a signature. So it does not require approval. If it were late, the first rule would require approval. So it was not late.

Contrapositive chain
Does not need signature→Does not require approval→Was not late
Therefore

This application does not require special approval and was not late.

A contrapositive can uncover the match

Suppose these two rules are given:

Rule 1
Is trained→Has a badge
Rule 2
Not trained→Requires supervision

“Trained” and “not trained” are opposite ideas in the same position: both are starting conditions. They do not look like a chain yet. Take the contrapositive of the first rule:

Restated rule 1
No badge→Not trained

Now “not trained” is the result of that restatement and the starting condition of rule 2. We can link them:

New chain
No badge→Not trained→Requires supervision
New conditional

Anyone without a badge requires supervision.

Again, that is a general rule. It does not prove that someone lacks a badge. If a separate fact says Niko lacks a badge, then we can apply the chain and conclude that Niko requires supervision.

If the middle condition already matches, link the rules directly. You only need to restate a rule when doing so reveals a connection you could not use as written.

08Translate the complete claim

“Only if” introduces the required condition

“A visitor may enter only if the visitor has a badge” means permission to enter requires a badge. Write May enter → Has a badge. Put the requirement after the arrow. A badge alone does not prove permission to enter.

“Only badge holders may enter” states that same requirement. Translate the complete sentence before shortening its conditions.

Keep requirements separate from completed actions

“Requires special approval” and “has received special approval” are not the same condition. A requirement can remain unfulfilled. A rule ending in the first phrase cannot be linked to one starting in the second unless another statement connects them. Shared words are not enough; match the complete claim, including qualifiers and negation.

Rules can connect different kinds of claims

A rule can tell us what someone should do or what they may do. That is different from telling us what they actually did.

Suppose every damaged item should be inspected. If this item is damaged, it should be inspected. We cannot conclude it has been inspected. But “has not been inspected” is not the same as “it is not true that it should be inspected.” An inspection could be overdue.

For Damaged → Should be inspected, the contrapositive starts with “it is not true that it should be inspected” and concludes “not damaged.” Negate the complete claim about what should happen. Whether the item has actually been inspected is a different claim.

Suppose a visitor may enter the archive only if the visitor has a badge. A badge is necessary for permission to enter. The rule is May enter → Has a badge. Without a badge, the visitor does not have permission to enter under this rule. Having a badge alone does not establish permission; another requirement might still apply.

The rule concerns permission. It does not prove that someone physically cannot enter without permission. Keep the same kind of claim throughout your reasoning: a requirement stays a requirement, advice stays advice, and permission stays permission.

Negate the whole requirement

A condition can contain more than one part. Suppose a document may be released only if it has been reviewed AND authorized. Permission requires both parts. If either part is missing, the combined requirement is not satisfied.

Rule
May be released→Reviewed AND authorized
Contrapositive
Not reviewed OR not authorized→May not be released

To negate “reviewed AND authorized,” you only need at least one failure. You do not need both parts to fail. “Not both” does not mean “neither.”

Now suppose a visitor may enter only if the visitor has a badge OR an invitation. This requirement allows either route, including both. To fail the entire requirement, the visitor must have neither.

Rule
May enter→Has badge OR has invitation
Contrapositive
No badge AND no invitation→May not enter

Negating a compound condition changes AND to OR, or OR to AND, while negating each part. For the original AND requirement, one missing part is enough. For the original OR requirement, both routes must be missing.

09Choose the move and test the claim

Ask what you have: a fact that can activate a rule, or two rules that can connect? The matching condition determines the move.

What you haveThe moveWhat to check
A fact meeting a rule's starting conditionApply the rule forwardDoes the whole fact match the whole condition?
A fact denying a rule's required resultUse the contrapositiveDid you reverse and negate both complete ideas?
Two rules with a matching middle conditionLink the rulesDoes the first rule end with the exact condition the second starts with?
Only the required result, or only the absence of the starting conditionLeave the other side openCould another explanation fit the rule?

To disprove a proposed inference, keep every given statement true and make the proposed conclusion false. For example, an on-time application that requires approval fits Submitted late → Requires approval, but disproves Requires approval → Submitted late.

11Take this into your assigned work

Do the conditional-diagramming drills first, then move to the LSAT questions. In the drills, write the complete conditions before shortening them. Check your arrow and its contrapositive. When two rules appear, look for an exact match before combining them.

In the LSAT questions, identify the rule or chain that proves each answer. Check that the answer preserves the complete conditions. Requires approval does not become has received approval. If you cannot prove an answer, try a counterexample: can the given statements be true while the answer is false?
