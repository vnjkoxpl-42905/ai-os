Day 2 · Sufficient vs necessary · Note 1 of 2

Two words that do two different jobs

Read this before you open The Core Connection. This is the idea the rest of the week is built on.

Yesterday you were reading conditionals that were handed to you. Today the conditional is missing and you have to supply it. Same machinery, harder job.

Enough, versus required

Hold these two apart and most of the assumption questions on this test stop being hard.

Sufficient means enough.

Add it to the argument and the conclusion is forced. It leaves no room to argue. It is allowed to be more than you strictly needed.

Necessary means required.

The argument cannot survive without it. But having it does not get you to the conclusion. It is one plank, not the bridge.

Oxygen and a match. Oxygen is necessary for the fire and it lights nothing on its own. A lit match in a dry field is sufficient. You do not need to ask anything else. Same fire, two completely different roles, and answer choices are written to make you take one for the other.

The test that settles it

When you cannot tell which one a choice is, negate it. Say the opposite of the choice out loud and look at what happens to the argument.

If the argument falls apart, the choice was necessary. If the argument limps along but still stands, it was not. This one move decides more questions than any amount of rereading, and it works because "required" means exactly this: take it away and nothing is left.

Where the strength of the wording comes from

This is also why a sweeping answer choice is usually wrong on a necessary question and can be right on a sufficient one. Sufficient answers are allowed to be strong, because their job is to close the argument completely. Necessary answers are usually modest, because they only have to be true, not decisive. When a choice feels too big for the argument, that reaction is worth listening to. Ask which job the question is asking for.

While you drill

  1. Before the choices, name the conclusion and name the evidence. You are already good at the first part. Use it.
  2. Ask which job the question wants: enough on its own, or required.
  3. On anything you are torn between, negate it and watch what happens.

Five questions today, so there is time to do them slowly. Slowly is the point. Speed on this comes from having done it carefully enough times that the question stops needing to be asked.
