# Most Supported: pick the answer that asks the least

This drill gives you information and asks which answer that information supports best. There's no argument to judge. The answer is the conclusion the information reaches.

## The rule from our session

The stronger an answer is, the harder it is to support. Picture a weightlifter: the stimulus is the lifter, and each answer is a weight. Lean toward the answer that asks the least of what you've read.

A soft answer still has to match what the stimulus says. A strong word is fine when the stimulus says it just as strongly.

## The trap this drill catches

An answer that sounds close but swaps in a bigger or different idea. Pulling attention away is not losing it. Helping is not guaranteeing. Check the key words of each answer against the words on the page.

## One habit

Before you read the answers, say what kind of claim each sentence is: conditional, causal or quantity. Then ask which answers would stay inside those claims.

## What the drill asks

Eight Most Supported questions at levels 1 to 3. A few come in variations:

* Fill in the blank: pick a safely worded conclusion that pulls the thoughts together.
* Apply a principle: the evidence matches the principle's trigger, and the conclusion matches its outcome.
* Generalizations: watch for language that is too strong.

If you see a pivot, the right answer usually pulls the last idea together with what came before it. If you see a cause and effect chain, expect the right answer to reinforce it.
