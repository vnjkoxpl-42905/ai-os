## The six definitions to teach

- Fact: states what is true in the described situation. Check whether the fact activates a rule or its contrapositive.
- Conditional rule: guarantees a complete idea if another is true. The rule does not itself assert that the starting condition occurs.
- Causal claim: says one thing produces or influences another. It need not say the cause is the only cause.
- Correlation: reports an association without itself claiming that one thing causes the other. Association alone does not prove a particular causal explanation.
- Normative claim: says what should happen, or what is right, wrong, permitted or required. What should happen is different from what actually happens.
- Quantity claim: says how many/how much, using words such as some, most, all and none, a number or percentage. Keep the amount and group; some does not establish most or an identified individual.

An overlap example such as "All libraries should provide step-free access" has both quantity and judgment. A universal conditional can also contain quantity. Do not require one exclusive label for every sentence.
