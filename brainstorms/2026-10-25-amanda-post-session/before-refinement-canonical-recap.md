**Session Manual · October 21**

# Rules, Facts, and What Follows

Every conditional question asks you to do one of two jobs. You either link rules together, or you apply a rule to a fact. When one of these questions stalls you, it is almost always because you are doing the wrong job, or because you read a fact as filler.

**Step zero**

## Sort every sentence as you read

"Read actively" only helps if you know what to do. Here is what to do: as you finish each sentence, put it in a bucket.

fact · rule · causal · correlation · normative · quantity

The buckets tell you where the argument is headed before you finish reading it. A correlation followed by a causal conclusion? You already know where it breaks.

**A fact is not filler** A fact says what actually happened. A rule only says what follows **if** something happens. Facts are what set rules off, so a fact sitting next to a rule is usually the most important sentence in the stimulus.

**Watch the pivots.** When you see *but*, *yet* or *however* in a Must Be True question, the right answer usually brings together what comes before the pivot and what comes after it.

**The two jobs**

## Link rules, or apply them

Name the job before you do anything else. Everything after depends on it.

**Link rules**

Rules and no facts. Two rules share an idea, and you join them into a longer chain.

**Looks like**

- if A, then B
- if B, then C
- the same idea shows up twice

**Test** Find the start of the chain and the end. What runs from one to the other?

**Apply a rule**

A rule plus a fact. You check the fact against the rule, like a lawyer checks a client's situation against the law.

**Looks like**

- if A, then B
- A happened
- B didn't happen

**Test** Does the fact match the left side, or deny the right side? If neither, nothing follows.

- **The fact matches the left side** The right side must happen.
- **The fact matches the right side** Nothing follows. You can't work backwards.

The second box is the trap. A rule runs one way. Seeing the result tells you nothing about what caused it.

> When I tried to bait you with the outcome, you said nothing follows, and you were right. Don't switch off that answer when someone pushes. Keep using it.

**Job two, step by step**

## Applying a rule to a fact

Rule: *If the library card is valid, the holder can borrow books.*

1. **The card is valid.** That matches the left side, so the holder can borrow books.
2. **The holder can't borrow books.** That denies the right side, so the card isn't valid. This is the contrapositive at work, and it is what the test likes to use.
3. **The holder borrowed books.** That only matches the right side. Nothing follows about the card.

**Same pieces**

## Sufficient Assumption and Must Be True

Both question types are built from the same three pieces. The only difference is which piece is missing.

Burns down a fence → damaged public property

Fact: Mike burned down a fence. Conclusion: Mike damaged public property.

- **Hide the rule or the fact** Sufficient Assumption. You supply the missing piece of evidence.
- **Hide the conclusion** Must Be True. You supply where the evidence lands.

**The trap**

## When the left side has an "and"

Trail is dry + guide is certified → hike goes ahead

**"And" turns into "or"** The contrapositive: **if the hike doesn't go ahead, then the trail wasn't dry or the guide wasn't certified.** "Or" means one or the other, or both.

**The trap that costs the most** The guide is certified, and the hike went ahead, so the trail must have been dry. **That doesn't follow.** The rule only tells you what happens when both left-side ideas are true. One of them plus the result proves nothing about the other. Maybe they hiked in the mud.

> You named this exact structure on the book tour question: two ideas on the left, one of them given, the result given, and a conclusion that claims the other one. That's how you picked C.

**Recognition drill**

## Which bucket is it?

### Tap each sentence to check yourself

Say the bucket out loud first, then tap. You want this to be instant.

- If the pharmacy is closed, prescriptions go to the front desk.
  **Answer:** Rule

- The pharmacy closed at noon on Sunday.
  **Answer:** Fact

- Hospitals with more nurses tend to have shorter waits.
  **Answer:** Correlation

- The outage caused the game to be cancelled.
  **Answer:** Causal

- The city should run more night buses.
  **Answer:** Normative

- Some of the nurses work weekends.
  **Answer:** Quantity

### Does anything follow?

Rule: if the soup is salted and stirred, it's ready. Decide, then tap.

- It's salted and stirred.
  **Answer:** It's ready

- It isn't ready.
  **Answer:** Not salted or not stirred, or both

- It's salted, and it's ready.
  **Answer:** Nothing about stirring

- It isn't salted.
  **Answer:** Nothing follows

**The finalist**

## When a conditional question stalls you

1. **Sort the sentences.** Which are rules and which are facts?
2. **Name the job.** Rules only: link them. A rule and a fact: apply it.
3. **Applying:** does the fact match the left side, or deny the right side? Anything else, nothing follows.
4. **An "and" on the left?** Both have to be true. One plus the result proves nothing.
5. **Write one line** on why the loser lost.

# Two Jobs: Linking Rules and Applying Them to Facts

Every conditional question asks you to do one of two jobs. You either link rules together, or you apply a rule to a fact. That's the whole list.

## Rules and facts

A **rule** says what must happen **if** something is true. *If a library card is valid, the holder can borrow books.*

A **fact** says what actually happened. *Dana's card is valid.*

**Job 1: link rules.** When you see rules and no facts, look for an idea that shows up in two rules. Find the start of the chain and the end of the chain, then read what runs from one to the other.

**Job 2: apply a rule to a fact.** When you see a rule and a fact, put the fact against the rule. Does it match the left side? Then the right side must happen. Does it say the right side didn't happen? Then the left side didn't happen either. Anything else, and the rule tells you nothing.

Think like a lawyer. A client walks in with a situation. You open the rulebook and ask one question: how does this rule apply to what happened?

## Sort every sentence as you read

"Read actively" only helps if you know what to do. Here's what to do: put each sentence in a bucket as you read it.

| Bucket | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| Fact | The museum closed early on Friday. |
| Rule | If the museum closes early, the guards leave at five. |
| Causal | The storm caused the museum to close. |
| Correlation | Museums near the coast are more likely to close in winter. |
| Normative | The museum should post its hours online. |
| Quantity | Some of the guards work weekends. |

Buckets tell you where an argument will go wrong before you finish reading it. A correlation followed by a causal conclusion? That's the gap. A fact isn't filler, either. Facts are what set rules off.

Watch for **but**, **yet** and **however** too. In Must Be True questions, the right answer usually brings together what comes before the pivot and what comes after it.

## Sufficient Assumption and Must Be True use the same pieces

Three pieces: a rule, a fact, a conclusion.

- **Rule:** If someone burns down a fence, they've damaged public property.
- **Fact:** Mike burned down a fence.
- **Conclusion:** Mike damaged public property.

Hide the rule or the fact, and you have a **Sufficient Assumption** question: supply the missing piece of evidence. Hide the conclusion, and you have a **Must Be True** question: supply where the evidence lands.

## When the left side has an "and"

**If the trail is dry and the guide is certified, the hike goes ahead.**

Flip it for the contrapositive and the "and" becomes "or":

**If the hike doesn't go ahead, then the trail wasn't dry or the guide wasn't certified.** "Or" means one or the other, or both.

Here's the trap to watch for:

> The guide is certified, and the hike went ahead. So the trail must have been dry.

That doesn't follow. The rule only tells you what happens when **both** left-side ideas are true. One left-side idea plus the result never proves the other left-side idea. Maybe they hiked in the mud.

## Pop Quiz

1. Rule: *If the printer is out of paper, the warning light blinks.* Fact: *The warning light is blinking.* What must be true?
   **Answer: nothing follows.** If the printer were out of paper, the light would blink. A blinking light can't tell you why it's blinking.
2. Rule: *If the soup is salted and stirred, it's ready.* What's the contrapositive?
   **Answer: If the soup isn't ready, then it wasn't salted or it wasn't stirred, or both.**
3. Rule: *If a shop is open late and has two staff on, it takes delivery orders.* Fact: *The shop has two staff on, and it took delivery orders.* Was it open late?
   **Answer: you can't tell.** One left-side idea plus the result doesn't prove the other left-side idea.
