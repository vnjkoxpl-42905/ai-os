# Necessary Assumptions

A necessary assumption is something the author's reasoning depends on. Treat the answer as false and ask whether the author's evidence can still do the job it was supposed to do.

## Before you read the choices

1. **Find the conclusion.** What is the author trying to prove?
2. **Name the evidence.** What reason does the author give?
3. **Identify the reasoning.** A rule can leave a missing connection. A cause can leave another explanation open. Some arguments use both.
4. **Ask what could break the argument.** Imagine the evidence is true. What could still stop it from supporting the conclusion?

## Negate the whole answer

| If the choice says | Test this |
|---|---|
| All buses arrived late. | At least one bus did not arrive late. |
| Some buses arrived late. | No buses arrived late. |
| No buses arrived late. | At least one bus arrived late. |
| If the alarm sounds, the doors unlock. | The alarm can sound while the doors stay locked. |

## Choose the answer the argument needs

Keep the evidence true as you test the answer. If the author's reasoning breaks, the answer was required. If the argument still works, move on. Be cautious with <em>most</em> and <em>primarily</em>. Ask whether the argument needs that much.
