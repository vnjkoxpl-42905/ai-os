# Cappuccino replaces repeated memory recaps with text confirmation

This compares all thirteen Day Six caption captures and their two notes blocks with [Day One's Decaf](../Day%20One/comparison.md), [Day Two's RC process](../Day%20Two/comparison.md), [Day Three's question strategies](../Day%20Three/comparison.md), and the [existing method library](../../corpus/loophole-online/method-library.md). Captions are auto-generated. No listening verification or independent passage/answer-key validation was performed for this comparison. Instructor claims and demonstrated choices remain attributed.

## The two Cappuccino versions have different steps

[How and Why to Do Cappuccino](<How and Why to Do Cappuccino RC Translation/source.json>) and its [complete notes](<How and Why to Do Cappuccino RC Translation/ellens-notes.txt>) retain the same chunk-level read/translate/quip and full conceptual-recall standard as Decaf. Confirmation means checking a translation already held in memory, not rereading from scratch (2:46–4:07).

| Procedure | After each paragraph | Cumulative step |
|---|---|---|
| Day One Decaf | Look away and recap the newest paragraph from memory. | Recap all paragraphs so far from memory. |
| Full Cappuccino | Look away and recap the newest paragraph, then confirm against the text. | Confirm all paragraphs read so far while looking at them. Full-passage memory recap is recommended initially and periodically. |
| Cappuccino 35 Version | Proceed directly from RTQ to confirmation while viewing the text; omit the solo look-away recap. | Confirm previous paragraphs as new ones are added; notes also specify an approximately thirty-second whole-passage review. |

The notes say accommodated testers may use full Cappuccino if it better preserves retention. That is Cassidy's advice, not a newly assigned routine. [Progressing to Caffeinated](<How Do I Know When to Progress from Cappuccino to Caffeinated/source.json>), 0:00–2:47, describes progression as optional: compare retention and the value/time cost of confirmations across several passages or sections. This qualifies the simplified Decaf→Cappuccino→Caffeinated route in Day One's progression lesson; it is not mandatory graduation to the last variant.

## The comparative demo has an explicit demonstration limitation

[The demo warning](<A Note on the Cappuccino RC Translation Demo You're About to Watch/source.json>), 0:00–1:11, says speaking her confirmations aloud was awkward and only approximates simultaneous silent reading/thinking. She directs viewers to trust the strategy instructions over that imperfect demo. At 1:11–1:41 she permits confirming within each comparative passage or across A+B, provided full recall remains available. Its separate [written note](<A Note on the Cappuccino RC Translation Demo You're About to Watch/ellens-notes.txt>) tells the viewer to attempt the question before watching; preserve both messages.

[Passage A + Review](<Cappuccino RC Translation Demo - PT140.S4.P2 Passage A + Review/source.json>) moves from multiplayer-game activity to virtual goods, auctions and real-currency exchange. Reviews occur at approximately 0:29, 1:09, 2:00 and 3:15–3:28. [Passage B + Review](<Cappuccino RC Translation Demo - PT140.S4.P2 Passage B + Review/source.json>) distinguishes ordinary in-game activity, real-money sales and intentionally commodified worlds; its reviews stay within B. The [Full Passage Recap](<Cappuccino RC Translation Demo - PT140.S4.P2 Full Passage Recap/source.json>) combines A+B without those confirmation interruptions.

World of Warcraft, runes, wolf pelts, patent illustrations and hypothetical tax collection include the instructor's reactions/examples. They must not silently become literal passage text or current legal guidance. In particular, the recap's 2:20 shorthand about taxing things sold versus found needs checking against the specific virtual-world context; it is not a universal tax rule.

Keeping a doubtful candidate open is not proof it is supported; later comparison remains necessary. Playback durations do not establish silent completion time or a student's natural pace.
