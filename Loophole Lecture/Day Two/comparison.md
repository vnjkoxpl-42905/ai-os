# Day Two connects passage understanding to answer evaluation

This comparison reads all twelve captured `source.json` transcripts and all five separate notes blocks. Native captions are auto-generated. Audio and on-screen demonstrations were not checked for this comparison; suspected recognition errors below remain unresolved. These are Ellen Cassidy's explanations, anecdotes and recommendations, not adopted Joshua curriculum or verified results.

## Vocabulary instruction gives the existing advice a complete workflow

The [existing method library](../../corpus/loophole-online/method-library.md) already preserves contextual vocabulary guesses, a later tracker, roots and usages, and the distinction between ordinary comprehension and importing outside specialist facts. Day Two supplies a more detailed workflow:

- [Harvest Vocab](<How to Harvest Vocab Words/source.json>), 2:10–5:11: notice personally unfamiliar words across reading, practice and conversation; consolidate them in a spreadsheet; include partially understood words rather than only entirely unknown ones.
- [Learn Vocab Without AI](<How to Learn Vocab Without AI/source.json>), 0:53–2:24: combine a looked-up definition with its original sentence, write a short English definition, then supply an original example. Non-native speakers also consult their native-language definition. At 2:41–4:36 she recommends everyday usage and consistent review. The [written notes](<How to Learn Vocab Without AI/ellens-notes.txt>) specify ten minutes daily and one original sentence.
- [AI edition](<How to Learn Vocab AI Supercharged Edition/source.json>), 0:43–3:19: demonstrates giving definitions and sentences, receiving correction and memory aids, and returning to missed words. Its [notes](<How to Learn Vocab AI Supercharged Edition/ellens-notes.txt>) link the tutor and request two original sentences for non-native speakers. That differs from the non-AI notes' one-sentence instruction; preserve each version rather than silently standardizing them.
- [Word Roots](<How to Use Word Roots to Learn Vocab/source.json>), 0:30–3:44: notice recurring components and parts of speech, then practice roots actively. The [notes](<How to Use Word Roots to Learn Vocab/ellens-notes.html>) contain a Word Roots PDF link, not the PDF's contents. Tutor persistence, accuracy and spaced-repetition behavior are described by the instructor; this capture does not independently test those capabilities.

[Passage-type trends](<How to Fix Passage Type-Specific Trends/source.json>) connects vocabulary and subject familiarity to extracurricular reading instead of relying only on grouped LSAT passages. Its [notes](<How to Fix Passage Type-Specific Trends/ellens-notes.txt>) preserve the resource list, subject sections and three goals: vocabulary, academic familiarity and translation practice. These source recommendations complement the older foundation routine. Their current availability and efficacy were not researched here.

## Day Two clarifies the answer-selection boundary flagged in Day One

[Day One's comparison](../Day%20One/comparison.md) cautioned against turning confident recall and answer unfamiliarity into a complete elimination rule. Day Two explicitly rejects literal matching. [Learning to Love Vanilla](<RC Correct Answers Learning to Love Vanilla/source.json>), 3:32–4:18, says supported answers may use words absent from the passage; at 5:02–6:23 it directs evaluation by the actual question stem. Its mixed-correctness-answer discussion (1:19–3:12) also reinforces the existing requirement to judge the entire choice instead of averaging a supported portion with an unsupported one.

[Passage + Common Sense](<Passage + Common Sense = Correct Answer/source.json>), 0:35–2:19 and 3:47, warns against specialist assumptions changing the passage's meaning. At 4:33–6:45 it describes supported, modest inferences rather than exact word matches. This complements the existing library's context/evidence boundary. The burger illustration and claims about what an author probably believes remain source examples, not a general permission to add unprovided facts.

[What is RC?](<What is RC/source.json>) and [How to Get Better at RC](<How to Get Better at RC/source.json>) frame translation and Powerful-Provable as two skills, with familiarity and answer-evaluation habits as additional work. Their personal score changes, drill counts and law-school outcomes are anecdotes or instructor prescriptions, not causal proof or guarantees.

## The full process adds question-stage steps beyond the Day One drill

[The RC Process](<The RC Process/source.json>) extends passage translation into a sequence: translate the individual stem, answer it yourself in a Mission, then assess choices against the stem. It allows passage review during the Mission (2:52–3:00), discourages passage searches while evaluating individual choices (3:56–4:32), and places hyperskip return at the end of the section (5:26–6:50). Those passage-return limits and suggested five-to-seven hyperskips are Cassidy's strategy prescriptions, not rules established by the existing method library.

Its [notes](<The RC Process/ellens-notes.txt>) add concrete marking instructions absent from the spoken overview: highlight the first word when a choice meets the stem's standard, leave uncertainty open, cross out a known failure, then choose among remaining/open candidates. Preserving the notes therefore matters for complete source capture.

[RC Missions](<RC Missions/source.json>), 5:09–6:12, emphasizes that the self-generated answer is a demonstration of understanding, not a prediction to match against the choices. This supports the existing principle of evaluating against the actual task and passage. The claim that skipped Missions inevitably cost more time is an instructor argument, not an independently measured result.

[Notes and Diagramming](<Should I Diagram the Passage or Write Notes/source.json>), 1:01–1:36, permits notes while requiring recall independent of them. At 1:54–2:11 it describes short, targeted highlighting practice for missed qualifiers. That complements the existing library's qualifier repair; it does not establish that notes generally have no score effect merely because the instructor reports that experience.
