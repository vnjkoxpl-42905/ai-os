# Day Three applies the same process to different question tasks

This comparison reads all eleven captured lesson transcripts and ten separate notes blocks. It compares them with [Day One](../Day%20One/comparison.md), [Day Two](../Day%20Two/comparison.md), and the [existing method library](../../corpus/loophole-online/method-library.md). The native captions are auto-generated. No listening verification or independent validation of instructor efficacy claims is implied. These sources have not been adapted into Joshua's voice or adopted as his curriculum.

## The exact question still controls the task

[Why I Don't Question Type](<Why I Don't Question Type RC (but also how to question type 😛)/source.json>), 1:25–2:38, repeats Day Two's distinction: knowing a broad category does not replace translating the stem's specifying information. Its forest-fire policy example (3:39–4:36) illustrates retaining the particular policy rather than answering a generic author-agreement question. The instructor's claim that this explains half the misses near the top of the score curve (4:54) is her observation, not measured evidence supplied with this capture.

Across the strategy videos, Missions remain self-generated answers grounded in the text, not predictions to match against answer choices. This matches Day Two's RC Process and Missions lessons and the existing library's requirement to evaluate the whole choice against the actual task.

## The notes change what a complete capture contains

The ten notes blocks preserve actual stem examples, including comparative and EXCEPT requirements. They should be read with their respective lessons, not flattened into generic question-type labels.

The [organization notes](<Function & Organization Question Strategy/ellens-notes.txt>) give a complete incremental procedure: compare the first pieces of choices, fully inspect a suspect choice, then progress through later pieces of surviving choices while checking order. They explicitly prohibit choosing an answer before vetting all its text. This clarifies how partial reading during comparison coexists with the existing whole-choice evaluation rule.

The [specific-reference notes](<Specific Reference Question Strategy/ellens-notes.txt>) say the digital test format changed and the reference is no longer highlighted. The spoken video at 0:22–0:36 says it is highlighted. Preserve this source-generation discrepancy. The notes present themselves as a later caveat; this comparison does not verify the current LSAC interface.

The [Primer](<How and Why to Do the Answer Choice Translation Primer/source.json>), 1:45–3:30 and 6:38–7:31, fills a previously named-but-incomplete drill pointer: three written answer-choice sections, followed by three aloud RTQ sections. The [notes](<How and Why to Do the Answer Choice Translation Primer/ellens-notes.txt>) require different sections for the phases and prohibit combining it with other drills. In the written phase, copy each choice, then translate it; in the spoken phase, translate and quip each choice. It excludes stimuli and stems so that meaning is the target rather than answer selection. This differs from the existing three-column Method/Flaw exercise, whose third column is an invented fitting stimulus.
