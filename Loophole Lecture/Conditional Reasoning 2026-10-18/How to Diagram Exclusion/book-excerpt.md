<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 130</span></div><div><div><div><p><strong>THE EXCLUSION INDICATORS</strong></p>
<p><em>No, None, Nobody, Never</em></p>
<p>The exclusion indicators set up mutually exclusive relationships.</p>
<p>Exclusion sets restrictions. Just like <strong>EITHER/OR</strong>, they’re not traditional indicators, but they do result in a predictable Must Relationship.</p>
<p>Exclusion means you’re going to have to choose between the two variables introduced in the statement. You can’t have both.<strong>If you have one of them, you can’t have the other. Rephrasing mutual exclusivity as an if/then is essential.</strong></p>
<p>If you are ever confused with conclusion and exclusion. Think through what this statement means and translate it as a personalized if/then.</p>
<p><strong>Exclusion Diagramming Process</strong></p>
<p>1. Choose one side of the statement and put it in the sufficient condition.</p>
<p>2. Negate the other side and put it in the necessary condition.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
