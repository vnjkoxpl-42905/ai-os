<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 108</span></div><div><div><div><p>Conditional reasoning is the art of the if/then. If one thing happens, then another thing must happen. That’s it.</p>
<p><strong>The Sufficient Condition</strong></p>
<p>The “if” part of the conditional is called the sufficient condition. The “then” part of the conditional is called the necessary condition.</p>
<p>If the sufficient condition is absent, you can completely ignore the conditional statement. But if the sufficient condition is present, the conditional statement must be followed.</p>
<p>The sufficient condition extends beyond just the word “if.” There is a whole family of words that work just like “if ”; they let you know you have a sufficient condition on your hands. </p>
<p><strong>SUFFICIENT INDICATORS</strong></p>
<p><em>If, when(ever), any(time), all, every(time), in order to, people who, each</em></p>
<p><strong>The Necessary Condition</strong></p>
<p>The “then” part of the conditional is called the necessary condition.</p>
<p>The necessary condition slams the door that the sufficient condition opens. It is the place of certainty. I mean just look at its name: It’s called the necessary condition because it is literally necessary. It has to occur at some point if the conditional relationship is activated. You can prove things that live in the necessary condition.</p>
<p><strong>NECESSARY INDICATORS</strong></p>
<p><em>Then, must, necessary, required, only (if), depends, need (to), have to, essential, precondition</em></p></div></div><div></div></div></div></div>
