<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 132</span></div><div><div><div><p><strong>UNLESS</strong> is our conditional exception indicator</p>
<p>Exceptions are how we get out of the way things always are. <strong>UNLESS</strong> indicates that we’re about to see the only way around how things always are. That only should give you a hint about which condition <strong>UNLESS</strong> lives in.</p>
<p><strong><em>RULE!</em></strong> <em>If I go against the way things always are, I must have my UNLESS exception.</em></p>
<p><strong>Unless Diagramming Process</strong></p>
<p>1. The target of <strong>UNLESS</strong> is the exception. Throw it in the necessary condition.</p>
<p>2. The other half of the statement is the way things always are. Go against the grain. Negate the way things always are and put that in the sufficient.</p>
<p>3. You end up with: ~<strong>[THE WAY THINGS ALWAYS ARE] → EXCEPTION</strong></p>
<p><strong>THE UNLESS FAMILY</strong></p>
<p><em>except, until, without</em></p>
<p>Whenever you see any of these words, act as though you saw <strong>UNLESS</strong>. They all target exceptions, so you can do the exact same thing as you do with <strong>UNLESS</strong>.</p></div></div><div></div></div></div></div>
