<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 201</span></div><div><div><div><p><strong>Bad Conditional Reasoning</strong></p>
<p>Bad Conditional Reasoning occurs when the author reads the conditionals supplied in the premises incorrectly.</p>
<p><strong>The Play-By-Play</strong></p>
<p>1. There are conditional premises</p>
<p>2. Crazy person concludes something by reading the conditional premises backwards without negating.</p>
<p><strong>OR</strong></p>
<p>1. There are conditional premises</p>
<p>2. Crazy person concludes something by negating the conditional premises and reading it forwards.</p>
<p><strong>Bad Causal Reasoning</strong></p>
<p>The vast majority of causal conclusions on the LSAT are bad. Looking for Bad Causal Reasoning is really just looking for causal reasoning — as soon as you see it, the Omitted Options should start popping into your head.</p>
<p><strong>The Play-By-Play</strong></p>
<p>1. Crazy person sees that two things are correlated.</p>
<p>2. Crazy person concludes that one of those things is causing the other.</p></div></div><div></div></div></div></div>
