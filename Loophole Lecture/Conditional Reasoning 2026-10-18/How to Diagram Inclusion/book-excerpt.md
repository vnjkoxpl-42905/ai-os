<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 128</span></div><div><div><div><p><strong>EITHER/OR</strong> forces us to include at least one of the two things it targets.</p>
<p>Let’s just think through what <strong>EITHER/OR</strong> means in LSAT terms. It’s slightly different from its real world meaning. I call <strong>EITHER/OR</strong> an inclusion indicator because it forces us to include at least one option. It does not imply that you can only have one of the pair. <strong>EITHER/OR</strong> implies that you must have at least one of the pair.</p>
<p><strong>Inclusion Diagramming Process</strong></p>
<p>1. Negate one half of the statement and put it in the sufficient condition.</p>
<p>2. Place the other half of the statement in the necessary condition.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
