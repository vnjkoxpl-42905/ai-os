<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 126</span></div><div><div><div><p><strong>IF AND ONLY IF</strong> is a double indicator. It is a marriage between the sufficient and necessary families. A Romeo and Juliet situation, but with less murder. As soon as you see <strong>IF AND ONLY IF</strong>, throw down a Double Must Arrow: ↔</p>
<p><strong>IF AND ONLY IF</strong> just means the two variables always go together, hand in hand. You should rejoice when you see it; it vastly simplifies the conditional relationship <strong>. IF AND ONLY IF</strong> is one conditional indicator, but it is made up of a member of the sufficient family and the necessary family.</p>
<p>It doesn’t matter who goes on the left or right because each term is both sufficient and necessary. Whenever you have a double arrow, you can switch who’s on the left and right at will. This is why you don’t have to flip the conditional when you negate it in the contrapositive.</p>
