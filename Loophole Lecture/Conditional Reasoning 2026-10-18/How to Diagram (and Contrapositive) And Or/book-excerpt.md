<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 124</span></div><div><div><div><p><strong>AND/OR</strong> makes sufficient and necessary conditions more complex. They are mirror images of one another.</p>
<p>When you diagram <strong>AND/OR</strong>:</p>
<p>Use + for <strong>AND</strong>. Just to shorten it up.</p>
<p>Write out or. It’s already pretty short.</p>
<p>When you read a conditional for the contrapositive, you negate terms as you read backwards. You should also switch the <strong>AND/OR</strong> as you go. Think of <strong>AND</strong> as the negated version of OR and vice versa. Any time you&#x27;re negating a conditional and it contains an <strong>AND/OR</strong>, you must negate the <strong>AND/ OR</strong>.</p>
<p>Remenber! Always diagram <strong>AND/ OR</strong> conditionals vertically.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
