<div><div><div><h4>Book Excerpt</h4><span>Page 366</span></div><div><div><div><p>How’s this necessary condition trick work?</p>
<p>Imagine a stimulus containing solely this conditional: <strong>Q → R</strong>. This stimulus can only prove <strong>R or ~Q</strong>. Those are the only two things we can get into the provable necessary condition.</p>
<p><strong>If a provable question follows, R and ~Q are the only provable correct answers</strong>. This is because <strong>R</strong> and <strong>~Q</strong> are the only things we can get into the necessary condition.</p></div></div></div></div></div>
