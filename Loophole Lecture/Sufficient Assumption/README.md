## What is Sufficient Assumption?

In Logical Reasoning, a Sufficient Assumption question stem asks you to supply an unstated assumption that, when added to the argument premises, forces the conclusion to be one hundred percent logically valid. 

Unlike Necessary Assumption questions (which demand minimalist, weak assumptions), Sufficient Assumption questions demand powerful, binding rules (either a bridge between mismatched terms or an existential trigger activating a contrapositive chain).

### Core Mathematical Archetypes

1. **The Chain Bridge (Form A):**
   - Premise 1: $A \rightarrow B$
   - Premise 2: $B \rightarrow C$
   - Conclusion: $A \rightarrow D$
   - **Missing Sufficient Assumption:** $C \rightarrow D$ (or contrapositive: $\sim D \rightarrow \sim C$).

2. **The Existential Trigger (Form B):**
   - Premise Chain: $A \rightarrow B \rightarrow C \rightarrow D$
   - Conclusion: $\sim A$ (factual denial)
   - **Missing Sufficient Assumption:** A factual claim establishing $\sim D$ (which cascades through the contrapositives to prove $\sim A$).
