# Day One demonstrates the cumulative recall method

This compares the eleven Day One lesson captures with the [existing method library](../../corpus/loophole-online/method-library.md), [reading lecture edition](../../corpus/loophole-online/reading-lectures.generated.md), and [RC cumulative-translation reference](<../../corpus/Curriculum/Reading Comprehension/Notes/cumulative-translation-review.md>). The new evidence read here is each lesson's `source.json` transcript. Native captions are auto-generated. This is a text comparison, with no claim that audio, passage visuals, or every spoken word has been checked. Nothing here adopts the vendor's targets as Joshua's curriculum.

## The instruction matches the existing drill

The existing references distinguish full conceptual translation from a gist, require reactions to the actual content, and test recall with the passage covered. The new [Decaf explanation](<How and Why to Do Decaf RC Translation/source.json>) makes the same requirements explicit: use the LR RTQ standard without removing details (2:12–3:32), look away (3:38), tell a connected story rather than a shopping list (4:00–4:46), and retain names, dates and lists.

The sequence is also consistent: RTQ paragraph 1, recap 1; RTQ paragraph 2, recap 2, then recap 1+2; repeat with each new paragraph. The explanation explicitly warns against skipping the newest paragraph's solo recap and going straight to the cumulative recap (6:29–6:53). That is a useful clarification of the already captured procedure.

## The new progression lessons add details and a timing discrepancy

[Decaf to Cappuccino](<How Do I Know When to Progress from Decaf to Cappuccino/source.json>) requires both dependable full recall with less strain and roughly twelve minutes per passage, with recaps on the clock, demonstrated across a whole section rather than once (0:00–1:24). It allows recaps to be silent or aloud at that twelve-minute goal. The existing method library and RC reference preserve historical goals of twelve minutes aloud and eight minutes silent. Keep both source generations visible; the new lesson does not establish whether the earlier silent goal was superseded.

[RC workout progression](<How Do People Progress Through the RC Workouts/source.json>) adds a fuller route: establish LR RTQ first (0:07–0:42), Decaf, Cappuccino, then caffeinated translation (3:27–4:32). Question practice begins after Decaf or later depending on demonstrated translation confidence (2:29–3:21). It gives approximately four minutes, with a thirty-second range either way, for its 35-minute Cappuccino/caffeinated versions (5:30–5:53). It also separates passage and question timing (8:05–8:29) and requires periodic recall checks while reducing passage time (8:35–9:04). These are Cassidy's prescriptions and observations, not verified learning outcomes or student assignments.

## Keep the AMA rationale attributed

[The goal lesson](<What's the Goal of RC Translation And Why/source.json>) explains Cassidy's “ask me anything” standard through her own preparation experience and claims that full retention improves speed and accuracy. At 3:31–3:52 and 5:49–6:14, she connects conceptual unfamiliarity in an answer to its absence from the passage. The existing method library requires evaluating the whole answer against the actual task and evidence. Memory confidence alone does not establish that an inference is unsupported: a valid inference can be expressed in unfamiliar terms. Treat the new lesson as a rationale for detailed retention, not an independently sufficient answer-elimination rule or a guarantee of perfect RC.
