01 The kitchen sink session EASY
THE MISTAKE You keep one chat going all day. A client email, then research on some topic, then the copy on your website, all
in the same session.
WHY IT MATTERSClaude re-reads the whole conversation every time you send a message. The prompt cache, Claude's short-
term memory, makes that re-read much cheaper, but you still pay for it and it still counts toward your limit.
And all that unrelated stuff distracts Claude, so the answers get worse over time. This habit is so common that
Anthropic has a name for it: the kitchen sink session.
THE FIX One task, one session. When you switch to a new topic, start a new chat. Or, to use the fewest tokens, type
/clear in Claude Code: the chat empties, and your CLAUDE md and other system prompts, like your skill
descriptions, don't need to load again.
“The kitchen sink session ... Context is full of irrelevant inform ation. Fix: /clear between unrelated tasks.”

02 Switching models mid-chat EASY
THE MISTAKE You are deep in a session on Opus, and you flip to Haiku to save money on a quick question.
WHY IT MATTERSEvery model has its own prompt cache, so switching means the whole conversation gets re-read with no

03 Arguing instead of rewinding EASY
THE MISTAKE Claude gets something wrong, you correct it, it is still wrong, you correct it again, and now you are five rounds
deep giving feedback one prompt after another.
WHY IT MATTERSEvery one of those failed attempts stays in the chat, and it pulls the next answer toward the same mistakes.
And the whole conversation gets re-read with every prompt you send. The prompt cache gives you a discount,
but it still drains your usage.
THE FIX Anthropic's own rule: after two failed corrections, stop and rewind. Press Esc twice, or type /rewind. That
brings up a list of your earlier messages, so you can pick the point just before it went wrong and start again
from there.

failed approaches.”

04 Side questions in your main chat EASY
THE MISTAKE You are in the middle of a task and you have a quick question. What is a webhook, which Claude just
mentioned? What was the answer from a few prompts back? How far along is the long build Claude is working
on? So you send it as a new prompt.
WHY IT MATTERSThat little detour now stays in the conversation, and it can confuse your agent later on. It also gets re-sent with
every message for the rest of the task, so it drains your limits even faster.
THE FIX Type /btw (by the way), then your question. The answer pops up in a side panel, but it never goes into the
chat history, so your main task stays clean.

05 Using max effort more than you should EASY
THE MISTAKE Putting effort on max for every single task, because more thinking must be better.
WHY IT MATTERSThinking counts as output, so it eats your usage, and Anthropic itself says max “may show diminishing returns
and is prone to overthinking.” The independent tests agree. Artificial Analysis, an independent company that
runs every AI model through the same tests, found that for Opus 5.5, max scores about four points higher than
high, so only a bit, but costs over three times as much per task.
THE FIX Unless you have an unlimited budget, leave effort on medium for most normal work, go high or xhigh (extra
high) when it really matters, and save max for the rare big job.

06 One-liner vague prompts EASY
THE MISTAKE One-line vague prompts, like “make me a website”, “write a newsletter” or “write an article”.
WHY IT MATTERSClaude has to guess, so it falls back to the most average answer, and then you spend five follow-ups fixing it.
Anthropic says so in its guide to prompting Opus 5.5: ask for a design without a direction, and it falls back on a
few default styles. That is where most AI slop comes from.
THE FIX Name the output you want, and give the context: who it is for, the goal, and what done looks like. And for
anything you do often, give Claude a harness, which at its core is just a set of instructions: a design system for
anything visual, and skills (step-by-step guides your agent follows) for the jobs you repeat. That way it starts
from your standards instead of from scratch every single time.
“Asked for frontend work without design direction, Claude Opus 5.5 falls back on a few default styles.”

07 Giving Claude rules without a reason EASY
THE MISTAKE Giving your agent bare rules, like “never use bullet points” or “don't mention the price”.
WHY IT MATTERSWithout the why, Claude mostly follows the rule blindly. Sometimes it misses the point, or uses the rule where it
makes zero sense.
THE FIX Add the reason in one short clause. With today's models, Claude is smart enough to work out the rest from the
reason. And as models keep getting smarter, explaining your intention, instead of hemming your agent in with
rules that are too specific, only matters more.

email and price comes on the onboarding call.
NEVER use ellipses → Your response will be read aloud by a text-to-
speech engine, so never use ellipses.

12 Walking away mid-session INTERMEDIATE
THE MISTAKE You have a big conversation going, you leave Claude Code open over lunch, then come back and type
“continue”.
WHY IT MATTERSClaude keeps your chat in a cache, which is basically a discount on the conversation it has to re-read. On a
subscription that cache goes cold after about an hour, and on an API key or extra usage credits, after just five
minutes. Then your first message back re-reads the whole thing at full price. In one community example, a big
session cost $0.15 to continue warm, and $11.72 cold.
THE FIX When you come back, /clear it if you are done with that conversation. If saving tokens is your priority,
/compact it before the break, while it is still warm. And if you really need to keep it warm, a trick a lot of
power users use is to have Claude ping itself with /loop, say every 30 minutes, so each ping keeps the cache
warm.

14 Turning off all safety INTERMEDIATE
THE MISTAKE Permission prompts get annoying, so you start Claude Code with the permissions skipped, or click yes to
everything without reading.
WHY IT MATTERSThis one is serious. On 7 October 2026, someone reported that Claude deleted his entire C drive while running

15 Letting Claude edit important files with no backup INTERMEDIATE
THE MISTAKE Letting Claude loose on important files with no copy saved anywhere.
WHY IT MATTERSBecause of the way AI agents work, there is no real undo button for a lot of what Claude does to your files:
rewind only covers Claude's own file edits. There is a report from July where Claude read five lines of a file,

THE FIX For important files, make a dated backup first: just ask Claude to copy the folder with today's date before any
big change. And if you are a bit more advanced, ask Claude to set up git. Think of it as a save button for your
whole project: every time you save, it keeps a snapshot, and you can jump back to any snapshot later. Then
you can experiment freely, because you can always roll back.
“Checkpoints only track changes m ade through Claude's file editing tools ... This isn't a replacem ent for

16 Having everything connected all the time INTERMEDIATE
THE MISTAKE Gmail, Notion, Google Drive, Slack, plus five MCP servers, which are add-ons that plug Claude into other
apps, all switched on for every chat.
WHY IT MATTERSIt drains your usage, and it is riskier, because your agent has more access than it probably needs. Claude Code
is smarter about this now, and only loads a tool's full details when it needs it. But every server still adds its list
of tools to every chat, and in the Claude app, Anthropic says connectors are token-intensive, so they still eat
into your limit.
THE FIX Every so often, have Claude audit them with the prompt below. And prefer a CLI (command line interface)
where there is one. A CLI is just a way to control an app by typing text, and AI models are very good at text.
Claude already knows how to use most of them, they cost nothing until they run, and Claude can trim the
