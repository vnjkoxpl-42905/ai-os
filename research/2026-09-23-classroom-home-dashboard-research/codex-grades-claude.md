Claude’s strongest contribution is making coaching visible inside the work. Its weakest is turning blind-review score differences into a confident diagnosis.

**Grades for Claude’s five ideas**

1. **Today’s session — B:** A finishable sequence could reduce indecision, but WHOOP is an analogy rather than learning evidence, and prescribing “Smart Drill on Necessary Assumption” conflicts with LSAT U’s distinction between adaptive Smart Drill and filtered practice.
2. **Speed or understanding? — D:** Relevant question, unjustified verdict: a score gap alone cannot establish the cause of mistakes, the thresholds lack support, and LSAT U already has a richer question-level diagnosis engine.
3. **Sure but wrong — B+:** Useful, well matched to existing data, and directionally supported by calibration research, but “overconfidence is the most common study failure” is unsupported and the proposal leaves rating definitions and collection timing unresolved.
4. **The coach is on the page — A−:** Signed pin notes and evidence-attached questions fit paid tutoring exceptionally well, but automatic agenda collection needs student control and the claimed competitive exclusivity is unsubstantiated.
5. **Craft you can feel — B−:** Fast, stable loading and keyboard access matter, but the evidence does not establish that count-ups or shared-element transitions improve studying, and “instant” promises more than caching guarantees.

**Claude’s blind spots**

- **Test-day phases — C:** Calendar context is real; automatic “14 days = timed sections” and “3 days = no new material” are unsupported prescriptions that could override the student’s actual needs. Make this coach-controlled.
- **Color-only meaning — B:** Valid audit requirement, overstated as a discovery: v3 already includes numeric changes, type labels, and counts. Check every state rather than assume the entire design relies on colour.
- **Freshness and trust — A−:** Update timestamps and stale-state handling are real omissions; denominators and coverage labels are already explicit in the plan.
- **Phone first — B+:** A deliberate phone composition matters, but responsive/container-width work is already planned. The useful addition is prioritising what appears before the first scroll.
- **Joshua’s mirror — A:** A strong addition: Joshua needs the student’s actual selected insight, evidence, and pin state, not merely a similarly styled teacher dashboard.

**What I would steal—and reject**

I would steal **the signed note on a pinned insight, plus Ask with attached evidence**. It gives Joshua’s judgment a precise location and lets students ask about the actual finding without reconstructing it in a message.

I reject **the blind-review verdict as written**. “You know more than the clock lets you show” overstates what the score difference proves. Additional time, reconsideration, and question-specific errors need examination. Three sections reduce noise; they do not validate the causal inference.

**Where we overlap**

- **Practice prescription:** Mine is better grounded: route a saved diagnosis to the appropriate learning activity. Claude’s sequence is better at defining a stopping point. Combine those strengths without forcing assignments into step two.
- **Confidence calibration:** Mine is stronger on evidence and data integrity: explicit buckets, missing ratings, initial attempts, and cautious interpretation. Claude’s headline is shorter and more immediately actionable.
- **Coaching:** Claude wins on bringing Joshua into the insight itself; mine wins on deliberate agenda selection, draft/share states, and workload boundaries.
- **Craft and trust:** Claude gives performance deserved prominence. Mine better addresses stale evidence, misleading charts, and layout stability. Neither list demonstrates student outcomes yet.
