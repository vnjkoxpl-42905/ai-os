# Instructions for the Firecrawl agent

Research LSAT Lab's own teaching on the following topics:

1. The distinction LSAT Lab draws between facts and rules in Logical Reasoning.
2. How that distinction is used in Must Be True questions.
3. How LSAT Lab teaches conditional reasoning, including sufficient and necessary
   conditions, chaining, contrapositives, and common invalid reversals or negations.
4. The practical procedure a student should follow while solving these questions.

Search LSAT Lab's website, help or lesson pages, and LSAT Lab's official YouTube
channel. Find the most relevant official YouTube videos about Must Be True questions
and conditional logic. Use video transcripts or captions, not titles and descriptions
alone.

Return:

- Exact source URLs and titles.
- A faithful explanation of LSAT Lab's definitions of facts and rules.
- The step-by-step method LSAT Lab recommends for Must Be True questions.
- The conditional-logic rules and warnings LSAT Lab emphasizes.
- Short quotations only when the exact wording matters, with the source URL and video
  timestamp when available.
- Any uncertainty or conflict between sources.
- A final section translating the source material into a concise, student-facing study
  note. Do not mention a tutoring session, what answer a student selected, or whether
  the student got a question right.

Use only official LSAT Lab sources for claims about LSAT Lab's method. Do not fill gaps
from generic LSAT-prep sites. If an official page or video cannot be accessed, report
that explicitly.
