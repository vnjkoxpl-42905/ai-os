Group 3: Basic Translation - Group 3
GROUP 3: UNLESS, UNTIL, OR, WITHOUT
These are often confusing for students. However, this difficulty can be easily 
overcome by sticking to your translation mechanism.
All the words in this group follow this translation rule:
You pick either idea, then negate that idea, and make that idea the sufficient 
condition.
Let’s Try It:
Example Statement:
"Don’t go to Hawaii, unless you want to have a good time."
Step 1: Identify the logical operator.
Here, it’s “unless.”
Step 2: Identify the two main concepts (or groups, categories, events, or 
ideas).
Here, it’s the two events “not going to Hawaii” and “having a good time.”
Step 3: Assign symbols to the two main concepts.
Here, “/H” for “not going to Hawaii” and “GT” for “having a good time.”
Step 4: Apply the translation rule.
Pick an idea—“/H”—negate it—“H”—and make it the sufficient condition.
H → GT
Step 5: Find the contrapositive.
/GT → /H
Step 6: Translate back to English.
If you don’t want to have a good time, then don’t go to Hawaii.

Explanation:
The thing about this group is that it indicates necessity, doesn’t it? "Unless" is 
emphasizing the necessity of what comes after it. But then it’s simultaneously 
warning you about the consequences of not following through with that 
necessity. For instance, with “Don’t go to Hawaii, unless you want to have a 
good time,” the “unless” is indicating that “wanting to have a good time” is the 
necessary condition. However, the sentence doesn’t explicitly state what it’s 
necessary for. It’s implied that it’s the necessary condition for “going to 
Hawaii.”
To avoid confusion, just straightforwardly follow the translation mechanism, 
and you’ll be golden.
LET’S REVIEW:
Group 3 Logical Indicators: unless, until, or, without.
Group 3 Rule: You pick either idea, negate that idea, and make that idea 
the sufficient condition.

"None of the Americans attended the dictator’s party."
Step 1: Identify the logical operator.
Here, it’s “none.”
Step 2: Identify the two main concepts (or groups, categories, events, or 
ideas).
Here, it’s the two events “being an American” and “attending the dictator’s 
party.”
Step 3: Assign symbols to the two main concepts.
Here, “A” for “being an American” and “ADP” for “attending the dictator’s 
party.”
Step 4: Apply the translation rule.
Pick an idea—“A”—negate it—“/A”—and make it the necessary condition.
ADP → /A
Step 5: Find the contrapositive.
A → /ADP
Step 6: Translate back to English.
If you’re an American, then you did not attend the dictator’s party.
Explanation:
Group 4 emphasizes negation. The logical indicators in this group make it clear 
that one of the ideas must not occur for the other to be true. For instance, 
“None of the Americans attended the dictator’s party” tells you that being an 
American is a necessary condition for not attending. By using negation 
correctly, you can handle these structures without confusion.
LET’S REVIEW:
Group 4 Logical Indicators: no, none, not both, never, cannot.
Group 4 Rule: You pick either idea, negate that idea, and make that idea
