 
         Conditional Logic Practice Workbook         📝 Instructions      These are the LR question types that most often would prompt us to consider diagramming the Conditional Logic:     • Must Be True     • Most Supported     • Sufficient Assumption     • Parallel / Parallel Flaw     • Principle-Strengthen / Principle-Conform        Look out for conditional trigger words.        Practice diagramming some of these problems, by writing conditionals on your paper. But also practice trusting your instinct not to diagram the conditional logic, when you feel like you’re having no trouble absorbing the ideas.        How does Conditional Logic usually show up, by type?        • Principle-Conform w/ Principle in Stimulus (makes a lot of sense to diagram these conditionals, because all five answers are testing us on whether we have an argument that establishes the left side of a rule and concludes the right side of a rule)        • Parallel (makes a lot of sense to diagram these arguments in the abstract, using generic variables like A, B, C. The more specific we are with seeing the abstract recipe, the more picky and decisive we can be with answers.)        • Sufficient Assumption (the conclusion gives us an A --&gt; Z logic path we want to prove. the evidence, combined with the correct answer choice, assemble together to create that A --&gt; Z path)        • Must Be True / Most Supported (if there are multiple conditionals, they will often chain together. otherwise, a fact provided will trigger the contrapositive of a conditional)        • Flaw / Parallel Flaw (if we're doing Flaw and see conditional logic, it usually means that the author is about to use that conditional rule in an illegal Backwards or Opposite fashion, which is the #1 Famous Flaw, known as Necessary vs. Sufficient)         ––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––––          📖 Question Set                      #           PrepTest           Section           Question             1           134           2           22             2           106           2           15             3           134           2           3             4           103           2           16             5           106           1           2             6           126           1           16             7           130           1           14             8           130           1           16             9           130           3           14             10           130           4           8
