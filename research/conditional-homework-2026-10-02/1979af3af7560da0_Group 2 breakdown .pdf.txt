Group 2: Basic Translation - Group 2
GROUP 2: ONLY, ONLY IF, ONLY WHEN, ONLY WHERE, ALWAYS, MUST
All the words in this group follow this translation rule:
The ideas introduced by (i.e., immediately following) these words are the 
necessary conditions.
Let’s Try It:
Example Statement:
"There will be an accomplishment of lasting value only if there is hard work."
Step 1: Identify the logical operator.
Here, it’s “only if.”
Step 2: Identify the two main concepts (or groups, categories, events, or 
ideas).
Here, it’s the two events “accomplishment of lasting value” and “there is 
hard work.”
Step 3: Assign symbols to the two main concepts.
Here, “ALV” for “accomplishment of lasting value” and “HW” for “there is 
hard work.”
Step 4: Apply the translation rule.
The ideas introduced by these words are the necessary conditions.
ALV → HW
Step 5: Find the contrapositive.
/HW → /ALV
Step 6: Translate back to English.
If there is no hard work, then there is not an accomplishment of lasting 
value.

Explanation:
You see why this is called a “machine”? Because it’s mechanical! You don’t 
really have to think very much about these sentences. It’s almost like a 
computer algorithm. You put in the data and it puts out the results. The 
computer doesn’t know what it’s doing. It’s simply following instructions. 
That’s what you should do for now. You have to be fast at this, really fast.
There’s an advantage to using a machine. It helps to elucidate the meaning of 
certain opaque sentences. A sentence using the “only if” construct, or really 
any of the constructions in Group 2, probably doesn’t need elucidation no 
matter how complicated the ideas may be. But a less clear construct, like 
“unless” (that’s Group 3), when coupled with more complicated ideas, may 
spell trouble. But as long as you coldly follow the machine, you’ll be in the 
clear.
LET’S REVIEW:
Group 2 Logical Indicators: only, only if, only when, only where, always, 
must, requires.
Group 2 Rule: The idea immediately following the logical indicator is the 
necessary condition.
