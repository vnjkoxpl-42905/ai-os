# LSAT platform UI audit: running log

Started 2026-09-27. This is the working comparison for LSAT U, LSAT Lab, and 7Sage. The competitor findings below come from direct interaction with Joshua's signed-in browser on 2026-09-27. LSAT U comparisons are from its current repository unless marked live. A visible control is evidence of an interface, not proof that its data or outcome is reliable. No student records or competitor question text are reproduced here.

## Decisions already made for LSAT U

Joshua accepted these navigation directions on 2026-09-27:

1. **My Work** is one student destination with To do, Completed, and Notes & recaps inside it. Assignment and recap records keep their separate meanings.
2. **Review** holds Wrong answers and Flagged. **Progress** holds Analytics and History. These are separate pages or top-level sections, each with internal tabs and deep links.
3. The teacher has a short, distinct workspace rail plus a clear **View student experience** shortcut. Bootcamps and Activities remain distinct internal teacher Library tabs rather than additional teacher rail items.

Joshua is not producing videos for this phase. Any idea that requires new video production is deferred. Existing videos can stay accessible.

## How to read the log

- **Adopt:** useful fit for Joshua's one-to-one teaching model. **Adapt:** sound behavior, but LSAT Lab's structure or visual treatment needs changing. **Keep:** LSAT U already has meaningful parity. **Defer:** weak fit or high cost now.
- **P0:** prerequisite to trustworthy workflows. **P1:** next product improvement. **P2:** worthwhile after core work. **Later:** requires new content, scale, or a separate product bet.
- **Verified:** seen in the browser or source. **Proposed:** design judgment that still needs a prototype or student test.

## Small interface details worth considering

1. **One primary action per card.** LSAT Lab often presents both Drill and Review on a priority. In LSAT U, place the recommended action first and the alternative as a text action, with the attempt count beside the claim.
2. **Show the size of the next step.** Estimated activity time, question count, and available pool help a student commit. Mark estimates clearly and hide them if unreliable.
3. **Use tabs within a named destination.** Tests, Practice Sets, subject overviews, and video library use tabs effectively. LSAT U's planned My Work, Review, and Progress should retain direct URLs for each tab so reminders and teacher links open the right state.
4. **Offer compact and expanded views.** A concise list should be the default for work and review; an optional table can expose PT/S/Q, result, date, time, source, and notes for power users and teachers.
5. **Use progressive disclosure for filters.** Practice Sets' visible essentials plus Customize are a better model than putting every type and feature into the side rail.
6. **Keep navigation labels visible.** LSAT Lab's compact rail recovers space but costs recognition. A short labeled LSAT U rail can fit fewer, stronger destinations and use icons as support.
7. **Place topic navigation near learning.** LSAT Lab's LR expansion lists 15 question types in the global rail. LSAT U should put such long taxonomies inside the topic or Library page with search, recent topics, and grouped families.
8. **Keep sidebar expansion from covering work.** LSAT Lab's expanded rail shifted or obscured the booking content in the observed viewport. Test LSAT U's flyouts at laptop widths, keyboard focus, and zoom.
9. **Make progress states explicit.** In progress, completed, blind review, hidden score, and flagged should have words or accessible labels, not color and tiny icons alone.
10. **Preserve source context.** A question found from Analytics or History should return to the originating filtered view. LSAT Lab's question finder includes a return URL; LSAT U should maintain the same continuity.
11. **Avoid false precision.** Frequency, average time, difficulty, and adaptive rating need definitions, sample sizes, and outlier handling. LSAT Lab showed several extreme times and zero-question adaptive sessions with long durations in this account.
12. **Avoid decorative emptiness.** Some LSAT Lab pages use a large banner or chart before the first useful action. LSAT U should put the next task or diagnostic evidence within the first screen.

## Visual and interaction details from 7Sage

1. **A short global vocabulary wins.** The Study menu groups tools by the student's intent, while each destination uses local tabs or a table of contents. LSAT U should keep its approved few rail destinations and put Bootcamps, Activities, topic lists, and review subtypes inside their parent pages.
2. **The best screens support two reading speeds.** A test result starts with score and section summary, then offers a dense question table. A study plan starts with the day and lets the student collapse completed work. Use a compact default plus Details for power users and Joshua.
3. **Persistent context reduces backtracking.** In the tester, the passage stays put as explanation, analytics, and notes appear beside it. A student's answer and reasoning should likewise remain visible when Joshua's explanation is opened.
4. **Resume belongs near Start.** Both platforms show in-process work at the launch point. LSAT U should make Continue the first action for unfinished drills and tests, rather than sending students to a separate history page.
5. **A one-click next step is only useful when its reason is visible.** 7Sage's priority row shows frequency, accuracy, goal, Review count, and Drill. LSAT U can provide a teacher-approved priority with attempt count and a plain-language reason before its actions.
6. **Small controls carry teaching intent.** Blind Review choices, Anticipation mode, answer-reveal control, bookmarks, and first-versus-blind answer state reinforce how to practice. Their labels and defaults matter more than visual polish.
7. **Question-level notes are a bridge between pages.** A journal entry should open from My Work when assigned, Review when revisiting the question, and Progress when explaining a pattern. Its source attempt and teacher feedback should remain attached.
8. **Time and source labels prevent misleading interpretation.** A 7Sage result distinguishes LawHub import, includes analytics state, and shows actual against target time. LSAT U should show whether a number came from an assigned drill, independent test, Activity, or bootcamp attempt.
9. **The visual style is calm but often too tall.** Off-white backgrounds, navy serif headings, teal actions, yellow active underlines, broad margins, and thin card borders make 7Sage readable. Large blank areas and long vertically stacked pages hide useful actions below the first screen. Borrow spacing hierarchy, not its exact colors or type.
10. **Settings explain consequences.** 7Sage labels what changes analytic inclusion or review prompts. LSAT U should explain any setting that affects scoring, recommendations, or attempt history in the control's own context.

## Cross-platform design for LSAT U

**Recommended information architecture, based on Joshua's accepted mergers:** a seven-item student rail holds Home, My Work, Practice, Review, Progress, Library, and Schedule. My Work contains To do, Completed, Notes & recaps. Practice contains Drills, Sections, PrepTests, and saved sets as internal tabs, so Testing does not need another global item. Review contains To review, Wrong answers, Flagged, and teacher-selected items as filters or tabs. Progress contains Overview, History, and topic detail. Library contains teaching material and activities. On the teacher side, use a four-item workspace rail with Students, Plans, Library, and Schedule, plus an obvious View student experience action; Library contains separate Bootcamps and Activities tabs. This is the proposed layout for a clickable prototype. Validate the final labels against the actual routes and student tasks before removing destinations.

**One end-to-end student path to design first:** Home says what Joshua wants done next and why. My Work shows the assigned activity with time and completion state. The question workspace preserves first reasoning and optional blind review. Review queues the miss, flag, skip, or teacher-selected question with a reason. Progress shows the pattern and links to a suitable drill. A future session page ties the assigned prep to Joshua's recap and follow-up. This path would make the smaller navigation genuinely easier, instead of just hiding existing links.

**Where LSAT U can be better:** Joshua can make a priority personal and explain the reasoning behind it; competitors mostly rely on automatic ranks. A recap can connect the exact teaching moment to the student's original attempt, next drill, and next session. Written, focused Activities can teach one reasoning operation without a video catalog. Teacher and student can share the same question identity and context rather than exchange vague PT references. These advantages depend on the shared attempt/flag/assignment coverage contract described above.
