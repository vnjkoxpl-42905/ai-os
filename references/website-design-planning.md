# Plan the visitor's decisions before the visual treatment

Recovered method, adapted from the historical clip [I Built The Ultimate Claude Website Design Skill](<../raw/sources/old-obsidian-vault-2026-10-07/Clippings/I Built The Ultimate Claude Website Design Skill (steal this).md>). This is a planning reference, not a verified description of current software or an approved LSAT U redesign.

1. State who arrives, what they currently believe, and what evidence would let them take the next step. A section needs a job in that sequence.
2. Outline the scroll journey before selecting components. Put explanation and proof where the visitor needs them, rather than filling a standard template.
3. Inventory available assets. Use real product screenshots and real evidence for factual claims. Treat generated imagery as illustration, with its purpose specified.
4. Vary visual intensity to support attention. Quiet explanation and a deliberate focal point can coexist; animation needs a reason tied to the visitor's task.
5. Choose any signature interaction around useful information or feedback. It should help the visitor understand or decide.
6. Check screenshots at meaningful positions and viewport sizes. Compare the rendered result with the intended sequence, readability, evidence, and next action.

## What two later videos add (ingested 2026-10-12)

Nate Herk's [Fable 5.1](../wiki/sources/fable-5-1-kills-ai-website-slop.md) and [GPT-6 Astra](../wiki/sources/gpt-6-astra-kills-ai-website-slop.md) videos reach the same method from the build side. These are source claims, not tested here.

- **Fit the visitor before choosing effects.** His "three P's" (the pain, the person, the promise) come first. A site is slop when it feels made for someone else, however polished it looks. This is step 1 above, stated as a failure test.
- **Give the model references, not adjectives.** "Make it premium" fails. Name a reference site and say what you like about it, or borrow specific components. His sources: 21st.dev (components with prompts), godly.website (sites and sections), Awwwards (by industry), Motion Sites (backgrounds).
- **Depth through layering.** Separate planes that move at different rates on scroll add depth without a busy page. Subtle scroll feedback, such as text revealing as it enters, often does more than heavy 3D.
- **The verification loop extends step 6.** Check mobile, and have the agent screenshot frame by frame while scrolling, then fix what looks wrong. His one-shot sites still had visible flaws, so a first pass is a draft.
- **Method outweighs model.** With the same prompts, assets and skill, different models produced similar sites in his tests.
