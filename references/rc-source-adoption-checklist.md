# Use the source to address a specific reading problem

Private teacher reference implemented on 2026-10-26 under Joshua's request to adopt useful source insights. These are reusable local preparation tools, not approved changes to LSAT U or evidence of learner improvement. Loophole procedures remain Ellen Cassidy's methods. Joshua's selected teaching voice and existing RC Training direction govern any future learner surface.

## Preserve the recall task before choosing a target

Use the existing [cumulative translation reference](<../corpus/Curriculum/Reading Comprehension/Notes/cumulative-translation-review.md>) for the historical teacher protocol. Do not convert that protocol into a replacement RC Training mode: the settled app direction remains paragraph recall, silent by default, optional elapsed time, no forced cutoff and no grading of reading or room speech.

| Captured formulation | What actually differs | Local adoption decision |
|---|---|---|
| [Decaf workout](../corpus/loophole-online/workouts/decaf-rc-translation-drill/README.md), instructions and timing | Ten steps include all remaining passages; opening note says start aloud although numbered recap steps say silently; 12-minute aloud and 8-minute silent goals. | Keep historical source intact. Use a teacher-observed recall check only where a specific omission is suspected; do not adopt these timing or workload targets. |
| [Decaf video notes](<../Loophole Lecture/Day One/How and Why to Do Decaf RC Translation/ellens-notes.txt>) | Nine-step paragraph pattern; aloud is permitted rather than required initially. | Preserve as a separate formulation, not a correction of the workout. |
| [Full and 35-version Cappuccino notes](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Six/How and Why to Do Cappuccino RC Translation/ellens-notes.txt>) | Full version includes separate look-away paragraph recall then cumulative text confirmation; 35 version omits separate look-away recall and proposes a roughly 30-second final confirmation. | Adopt the distinction between recalling and checking against text for teacher diagnosis. Do not score silent confidence as demonstrated full recall. If a teacher needs observable evidence, ask for an explanation of the relevant idea with the learner's knowledge and agreement. Keep that coaching observation separate from app grading.

## Keep vocabulary tied to the sentence that caused difficulty

Adopt contextual meaning, a plain definition and a new sentence from the [without-AI notes](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Two/How to Learn Vocab Without AI/ellens-notes.txt>). A definition can be longer than the source's one-to-four-word preference when scope would otherwise be lost. Paper or the table below works immediately; no new spaced-review subscription is needed.

| Word | Original source and sentence location | Meaning here in plain English | Own new sentence | Definition checked against | Revisit date | Meaning recalled before reveal? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qualified | Original practice: The researcher offered qualified support because the sample was small. | Support with reservations or limits. | The editor gave qualified approval, asking for a clearer example first. | Original context; teacher-reviewed example, not dictionary verification | Choose after first attempt | |

Select unfamiliar topics because observed topic knowledge is impeding comprehension, not because boredom itself proves a weakness. [The outside-reading notes](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Two/How to Fix Passage Type-Specific Trends/ellens-notes.txt>) name ScienceDaily, Aeon, Arts & Letters Daily, the NYT knowledge guide and Crash Course. Their current access, pedagogical quality and any particular article's suitability have not been verified here. They remain candidates rather than endorsed assignments. The [roots note](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Two/How to Use Word Roots to Learn Vocab/ellens-notes.txt>) only points to a PDF; its contents were not reviewed. The [AI-tutor notes](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Two/How to Learn Vocab AI Supercharged Edition/ellens-notes.txt>) differ on one versus two example sentences. Neither difference warrants a mandatory student workload or a new external tutor connection.

## Evaluate the whole answer against the exact question

Adopt task identification, whole-choice evaluation and the option to leave a genuinely uncertain choice open from [The RC Process](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Two/The RC Process/ellens-notes.txt>). Modify the passage-return rule: return when the needed evidence is missing or uncertain, including after reading a choice. Do not import a fixed five-to-seven-question skip quota or a ban on returning to the text during choice evaluation.

Keep question tasks distinct: a true detail may not state the main point; a description of subject matter may not explain a mention's function; an attitude answer must preserve its strength. The [Day Three comparison](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Three/comparison.md>) maps source-specific exceptions and caption problems. Its organization notes require vetting all text and order before selection. For strengthen, weaken and resolution questions, evaluate the effect of the stated hypothetical information rather than demanding it already be asserted in the passage. For EXCEPT/LEAST tasks, explicitly reverse the selection criterion.

Original worked example: A passage says a survey found more library visits after evening hours expanded, but the author warns that the survey cannot establish why visits increased. Asked for the function of the warning, answer: it limits what the survey supports. Reject “it proves the longer hours caused the increase”: that reverses the warning. Reject “it describes a library survey”: related subject matter without the warning's role. An answer naming a different possible cause may still overstate what the author actually supplied. Check the text before rejecting or accepting it.

For answer-paraphrase diagnosis use this original practice row: “Some reviews of the new edition overlooked its revised introduction.” A faithful paraphrase is “At least one review missed the revised introduction.” “Most reviewers disliked the introduction” adds quantity and attitude. This is a local diagnostic example, not Cassidy's full Answer Choice Translation Primer. Her [written instructions](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Three/How and Why to Do the Answer Choice Translation Primer/ellens-notes.txt>) require three written sections then three different spoken sections, without stems/stimuli or combination with other drills. Preserve that full vendor routine separately; do not call this small example completion of it.

## Compare authors without assuming disagreement

Adopt separate account of A before B, conceptual comparison and evidence-backed relationship labels from [the comparative notes](<../Loophole Lecture/Day Seven/How to Translate Comparative Passages/ellens-notes.txt>). Modify mandatory highlighting and one quip per B paragraph into optional annotations where useful. A missing highlight is not evidence a topic is absent. Read and represent each author fully before deciding how they relate.

| Comparison prompt | A | B | Relationship and evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central claim and limits | | | |
| Shared question/topic | | | |
| Same term, different meaning? | | | |
| What can we establish, and what remains unstated? | | | |

Original dry run: A says evening opening could help shift workers but does not establish demand. B reports a pilot survey in which shift workers used evening hours. B supplies relevant evidence for A's proposal; it does not refute A. B does not establish that every library should expand hours. This demonstrates the local sheet with authored content, not a learner trial or an audit of an official comparative passage.

Before any assignment, choose an actual student need, source rights and passage, then observe whether the chosen task helps. LQ-001 and LQ-004 still lack the requested observed student-task evidence; no timing target or learning-effect claim was validated by this document.
