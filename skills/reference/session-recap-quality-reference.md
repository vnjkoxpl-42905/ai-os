# Post-session note quality reference

Canonical source: `students/sofia/recaps/2026-09-11-recap.md`.

Use that note as the quality bar for post-session notes. Preserve its method, not its
topic-specific wording. A strong note:

- reads as lecture notes, not an email or a transcript summary;
- opens directly with the governing concept;
- separates ideas with descriptive headings and generous spacing;
- defines the important distinction before asking the student to use it;
- turns Joshua's explanation into a reusable method;
- uses compact tables, numbered steps, and diagrams only when they reduce cognitive load;
- includes valid moves, invalid moves, boundary cases, and a way to test the answer;
- gives examples that stand alone without requiring memory of a question from days ago;
- ends with concrete practice supported by the session; and
- keeps private teaching judgments below the `Notes for me (not posted)` divider.

## Reference structure

The Sofia note uses this content-shaped progression:

1. **Concept title.** `Conditional Must Be True: Facts, Rules, and Bridges`
2. **Direct orientation.** The two moves are stated immediately: connect rules or use a
   fact to activate a rule.
3. **Method at a glance.** A small table distinguishes chaining from triggering.
4. **Foundational distinction.** Facts and rules are defined before either is applied.
5. **First method.** Chaining is taught through a definition, positional test, diagram,
   valid example, invalid example, hidden case, and longer-chain consequence.
6. **Second method.** Triggering is taught through its two legal moves and the two facts
   that produce no inference.
7. **Decision aid.** A four-row table makes valid and invalid directions visible.
8. **Execution advice.** The student learns when to contrapose, when to diagram, and how
   to test a conditional answer from its trigger.
9. **Repeatable process.** A numbered procedure turns the lesson into something usable
   on the next question.
10. **Practice.** Checkboxes state the work actually assigned.
11. **Private continuation.** Weak spots, Joshua's follow-ups, and teaching judgments are
    separated from the student-facing note.

## What to copy and what to vary

Copy the depth, hierarchy, and independence from remembered question context. Vary the
sections to fit the lesson. Do not force every recap into the same headings. A causation
session, an RC mapping session, and a conditional-logic session should not look identical.

The note is complete when the student can return several days later and use it to solve
the same kind of problem without reconstructing the original session.
