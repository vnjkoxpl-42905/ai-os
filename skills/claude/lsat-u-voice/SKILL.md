# Write LSAT material in Joshua's selected teaching voice

Resolve the canonical ai-os vault from the workspace instructions. This project-owned skill takes precedence over older vendor skills with the same name that prescribe a writer blend.

Before drafting or revising, read:

1. [Canonical teaching voice](../../../wiki/teaching-voice.md), especially the teaching-content register.
2. [LSAT teaching voice standard](../../../references/lsat-teaching-voice-standard.md). Read it in full when calibrating the voice; for subsequent revisions, revisit the relevant sections and examples.
3. [Demonstrated copy corrections](../../../wiki/COPY-CORRECTIONS.md).

The detailed standard is maintained in one place. Do not copy it into this skill or replace it with generic brevity rules, required catchphrases, humor quotas, or a fixed number of sentences per paragraph.

Establish the actual lesson topic, prerequisites, evidence, and Joshua's approved method. The conditional reference teaches how to explain; it does not add conditional content to another topic. Preserve complete ideas, logical strength, modality, and the selected scope.

Use original intuitive examples. Explain the ordinary meaning before notation, show the intermediate reasoning, and give questions a real instructional job. Keep the teacher and reader thinking together. When useful, show a tempting interpretation and how its meaning fails. Fresh practice should require the taught skill and provide reasoned feedback.

Read the draft aloud, check its inferences and boundaries, and apply the standard's review rubric. Review actual prompt/reveal states when writing for an interactive surface. Do not claim an identical voice has been objectively established; use Joshua's corrections to refine calibration.

This skill governs local authoring. Posting, sending, assigning, deploying, or generating paid media requires the authorization and workflow appropriate to that action.
