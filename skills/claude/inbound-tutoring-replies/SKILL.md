# Inbound Tutoring Replies

Use this skill for direct inbound leads who have already shown interest in LSAT tutoring.

## Default Rules

- Be warm, direct, and human.
- Keep the reply short. Do not sound like a sales page.
- Do not mention price unless the person asks about pricing, rates, or cost.
- Mention the free first session when useful.
- Prefer moving the lead toward a consultation link rather than over-explaining.
- Include `contact@aspiringattorneys.com` as an alternate contact path when helpful.
- Use `LSAT`, not `lsat`, unless quoting the lead.
- Do not overuse Joshua's `138 -> 173` story in first direct-message replies unless the lead asks about background or credibility.

## Core Reply

Use this as the default response to: "Are you still offering LSAT tutoring?"

```text
Hi [Name], yes, I am still offering LSAT tutoring.

I'd be happy to talk and see if I can help. I usually start with a free first session so we can go over where you are scoring, your timeline, and what has been giving you the most trouble.

You can book a time here if you want to chat: https://cal.com/joshua-jtx5ud/consultation

You can also email me at contact@aspiringattorneys.com if you have any further questions.
```

## If They Ask About Price

Only include this if they ask about price, rates, cost, or affordability.

```text
After the free first session, I charge $40/h.
```

## If They Give Score And Test Date

Add one tailored sentence before the booking link. Keep it specific but not diagnostic-heavy.

Examples:

- `If you are around the mid-160s, the work is usually less about relearning the basics and more about tightening the few decisions that keep repeating.`
- `If timing is the main issue, I would want to look at where the time is actually going: stimulus read, answer choice comparison, or review habits.`
- `If you are still building foundations, I would focus first on question stems, stimulus structure, and a simple review system.`
