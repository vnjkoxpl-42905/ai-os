# Sandra Kooh teaching notes

## 2026-09-23 | Argument structure and intermediate conclusions

Source: [[transcript-downloads/2026-09-23-sandra-notes-by-gemini]].

- Sandra said the earlier structure drill helped her identify main conclusions faster. She reported missing roughly two of ten questions when she did not read closely enough. That is her report, not a measured accuracy rate.
- She could define premise as support and conclusion as the author's claim, but said the test of how one statement supports another had not fully settled for her. She leaned on conclusion and premise indicator words.
- Joshua taught that an intermediate conclusion is both supported by a premise and used to support the main conclusion. He used the question “Why should I believe this?” to test which statement supports which. Sandra applied this to the personal conflicts stimulus and correctly identified the intermediate conclusion after checking the support chain.
- The reason to locate an intermediate conclusion extends beyond main conclusion questions: a reasoning gap may lie between premise and intermediate conclusion or between intermediate and main conclusion. She asked about that purpose directly.
- Joshua recommended using indicator words when present, while retaining the support test when they are absent or incomplete. He also recommended highlighting the main conclusion before comparing answer choices so she need not hold its wording in memory.
- Joshua promised intermediate conclusion question drills and said he would add a replacement session on Monday, with other moved sessions reconciled. Verify assignment and schedule in their live systems before closing those loops.
