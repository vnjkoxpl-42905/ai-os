Core structure and definition of conditionals
* Joshua introduced conditionals as strict rules or laws rather than simple sentences.
* Conditionals consist of a sufficient condition trigger and a necessary condition outcome.


Contrapositive and logical equivalence
* The contrapositive serves as the only other logically equivalent deduction for any conditional statement.
* Finding the contrapositive requires flipping the term positions and negating both elements.


Conditional indicator words and 5-step solving process
* Words like if, every, and all indicate sufficient conditions while must and required indicate necessary conditions.
* Sandra practiced a 5-step process involving indicator identification, concept labeling, rule application, and contrapositive formation.


Conditional logic fundamentals
* Mastering conditional logic requires automatic muscle memory, similar to learning multiplication tables.
* Conditionals represent the most common concept tested on exam day.


Logical fallacies and reasoning errors
* Students frequently struggle with conditional logic because the core concepts are counterintuitive.
* Confusing necessary conditions with sufficient conditions leads to invalid reasoning fallacies.
