## 2026-09-17 | Diagnose the argument before choosing

Source: [[transcript-downloads/2026-09-17-gianna-notes-by-gemini]]; [[sessions/2026-09-17-session/transcript]].

- Gianna reported a 150 on her August LSAT, stronger Reading Comprehension, and roughly five correct out of twelve in recent Logical Reasoning drills. These are her self-reported figures. She said answer choices often narrow to two and she chooses the wrong one.
- On a campaign-mailing question, she first selected B. Joshua had her restate each claim, distinguish a factual report from a claim about the campaign's purpose, and ask what explicitly supported the author's claim. Once she identified the supported conclusion, she changed to the correct answer. The useful diagnosis is that a pivot word or plausible wording had been substituting for a support check.
- Joshua taught a repeatable sequence: form a provisional hypothesis about the argument's direction, update it while reading, identify the author's opinion, then test its support with “Why should I believe this?” The core identification step carries across question types; the later operation changes for weaken, strengthen, and other tasks.
