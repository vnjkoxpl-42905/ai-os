* **Reading Comprehension Challenges**: Joshua Fisseha notes that students often struggle with Reading Comprehension due to difficulty remembering details and nuanced content, particularly when the subject matter is unfamiliar. They advise students to focus on identifying the purpose of the passage, as every passage is written to convey a specific main point.

* **Active Reading Strategies**: Adriana Varea describes their method for active reading, which involves rephrasing sentences in their own words and ensuring full comprehension before proceeding to the next sentence ([00:02:16](#00:02:16)). They mention utilizing the "ViewStamp" method (Viewpoint, Structure, Tone, Argument, Main Point), but note that they find the distinctions between argument and main point to be repetitive ([00:03:34](#00:03:34)).

* **Limitations of the ViewStamp Method**: Joshua Fisseha critiques the ViewStamp method as potentially overcomplicating the reading process. They offer a simpler framework for reading: every sentence either builds on the preceding sentence or pivots away from it ([00:04:54](#00:04:54)).

* **Hypothesis-Driven Reading**: Joshua Fisseha emphasizes the importance of developing a hypothesis regarding the author's purpose early in the passage ([00:06:54](#00:06:54)). Adriana Varea confirms that it is acceptable to adjust this hypothesis if subsequent information invalidates the initial assumption, as this process forces deeper engagement with the material ([00:07:45](#00:07:45)).

* **The SER Method**: Joshua Fisseha introduces the "SER" method (Comparison, Explanation, Evaluation, Recommendation) as a more effective framework for analyzing Reading Comprehension passages ([00:09:23](#00:09:23)). They define these four rhetorical moves, explaining that every sentence and paragraph will fall into one of these categories ([00:10:08](#00:10:08)) ([00:17:04](#00:17:04)).

* **Nuance Between Evaluation and Recommendation**: Adriana Varea and Joshua Fisseha discuss the distinctions between evaluation and recommendation ([00:14:19](#00:14:19)). They clarify that evaluation involves describing or criticizing a position, whereas recommendation involves asserting a solution or prescribing what one should do ([00:15:15](#00:15:15)).

* **Categorizing Paragraphs for Efficiency**: Joshua Fisseha explains that categorizing paragraphs using the SER method allows a reader to retain the structure of a passage without the need to memorize every minor detail ([00:18:37](#00:18:37)).
