### Summary

The session debriefed student bias on wrong answers and analyzed 2 complex logical reasoning questions with a focus on conclusion identification and premise definition.

**Initial Bias and Logic Analysis**  
Discussion addressed the "wrong answer bias" and suggested a shift from finding the most correct answer to finding the "least wrong" answer by identifying flaws in each choice. Analysis of the 1st logical reasoning question focused on identifying the main conclusion, which was determined to be the first sentence, and defining the role of supporting premises.

**Strict Definition Application**  
The analysis of the 1st question utilized the strict definition of "generalization" in the test context, determining the stimulus statement was not a generalization because it lacked a specific individual finding to support the broader claim. A major decision was made to revise the conclusion for the 1st question from the final sentence to the first sentence after applying the “Why should I believe that?” test.

**Complex Rebuttal and Assumption**  
The 2nd question focused on a consumer advocate's rebuttal to an economist's reasoning about price gouging. The correct complex answer choice required identifying that the key statement, "willingness to pay is not proportional to need," directly denies an unstated assumption in the economist's rejected reasoning.
