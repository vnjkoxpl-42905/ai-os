* **Most Strongly Supported Questions (Q23)** Malaya Jules found Most Strongly Supported (MSS) questions challenging, admitting they sometimes choose answers based on "vibes" and go against their gut feeling, which often proves incorrect ([00:15:40](#00:15:40)). * **Developing Instinct for MSS Questions** Joshua Fisseha's Presentation advised Malaya Jules to train their instinct for MSS questions by developing a "reactionary reflex" to identify specific wording in the stimulus ([00:16:41](#00:16:41)). They suggested looking for causal, comparative, quantifiable (e.g., "many," "most," "some"), or conditional wording ([00:19:12](#00:19:12)). * **Applying MSS Strategy to a Specific Question** Malaya Jules applied the MSS strategy to a specific question (Q23), identifying quantifiable words like "most," "many," and "sometimes," as well as causal relationships like "arise out of" and "as a result" ([00:19:59](#00:19:59)). * **Prioritizing Answers in Strengthening Questions** Joshua Fisseha's Presentation outlined a priority order for strengthening questions: first, address potential gaps in the argument (assumptions); second, find an answer that makes the conclusion more likely; and third, find an answer that makes the premise more likely ([00:46:38](#00:46:38)). * **Analyzing Answer Choices in Strengthen Questions** Joshua Fisseha's Presentation explained that when choosing between two answer choices in strengthen questions, negating one of them can reveal which one has a higher impact on the argument. They suggested that the answer choice that "moves the needle more" or creates the biggest impact when negated is likely the correct one, even if it doesn't completely destroy the argument. Malaya Jules agreed that this approach makes a lot of sense, especially for harder strengthen questions where a necessary assumption might be presented as a strengthening factor ([00:56:51](#00:56:51)).

* **Prioritizing Answer Choices and Identifying Impact** Joshua Fisseha's Presentation outlined a hierarchy for evaluating answer choices in strengthen questions: first, look for answers that address the assumption, then the conclusion, and finally the premise ([00:57:31](#00:57:31)). They emphasized that strengthening questions are harder than weakening questions because it's more challenging to naturally perceive what would be beneficial to an argument ([00:58:59](#00:58:59)). They reiterated that the key trick is to assess the impact of each answer choice by negating it, as this helps differentiate between two tempting options ([00:58:20](#00:58:20)).
