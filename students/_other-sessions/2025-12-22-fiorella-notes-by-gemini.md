* **Reading Conditional Statements and Drawing Inferences** Joshua Fisseha's Presentation emphasized the importance of taking time to understand a stimulus and building a tangible illustration of what is being read to make the process easier. They highlighted that when reading a conditional statement, one must identify what is known to be true and its contrapositive ([00:00:00](#00:00:00)). By connecting "morally wrong" with "offenses against humanity" and then with "equally bad," they inferred that all morally wrong actions are equally bad ([00:04:09](#00:04:09)). This upfront solving allows them to avoid the nightmare of sorting through information later and helps prevent being biased by later information ([00:05:19](#00:05:19)).

Using the analogy of a hockey team, they explained that concluding the team is amazing because one player is a superstar (part-to-whole) is invalid, as is concluding that every player is phenomenal because the team is phenomenal (whole-to-part) ([00:20:05](#00:20:05)).
