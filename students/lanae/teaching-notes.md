# Lanae Teaching Notes

Durable learning judgments only, each tied to a dated session and a transcript timestamp. These are working observations from three sessions, not a diagnosis. Method links: [tutoring diagnosis](../../corpus/tutoring-diagnosis.md) and the [corpus](../../corpus/index.md).

## Pattern: conditionals are the load-bearing skill

- **Contrapositive mechanics are solid.** She flips and negates correctly and unprompted (2026-11-14 00:04:11; 2026-11-17 00:13:36 and 00:46:41). She also finds the conditional keywords on her own once asked (2026-11-14 00:44:22).
- **The weak spot is what a conditional does and does not let her infer.** Twice she moved from "not Nebraska" to "not US" (2026-11-17 00:11:25) and from "not decreased" to "increased" (2026-11-14 00:29:29, 00:31:27). In each case one prompt (the bank account example, the "nothing else follows" point) fixed it, and she then answered the follow-ups correctly (2026-11-14 00:32:54; 2026-11-17 00:17:19).
- **Keyword conversion needs reps.** On "only" she first put "Americans" on the sufficient side (2026-11-17 00:25:53) and kept "only" in the contrapositive (00:36:38). On "unless" she reversed the negation (00:44:32). All three corrected within the session, but each needed Joshua's walk-through. Her own remark that the pattern is "always not on the sufficient side after unless" (00:46:41) shows she is building the rule.
- **Open question from her:** groups 3 and 4 of the keyword groups were already confusing for her on 2026-10-29 and she raised them again (2026-11-17 00:41:18). Joshua held it until the basics are locked.

## Pattern: question-type identification

- On 2026-11-17 (00:04:23, 00:05:45) she called a sufficient assumption question "must be true", then "main conclusion", and defined sufficient assumption as "negate and then the arrow pointing to sufficient". Reading: she has fragments of the negation test (a necessary assumption tool) attached to the wrong question type. Worth a direct question-type pass once conditionals settle.
- On 2026-11-14 (00:44:22) she correctly stated that must-be-true stimuli have no conclusion, only facts and rules.

## Pattern: reading what the stimulus actually says

- Missed the only conditional in a stimulus on first read ("I don't think so", 2026-11-14 00:27:46), found it when pointed to the last sentence. Joshua's fix: underline the support (00:43:26).
- Tense: first read "from now on" against past-tense stimulus language, and needed a prompt to see the stimulus was about the past (2026-11-14 00:39:05). She did reach B by her own inference that the regulations "were not followed" (00:41:21), which is a strength worth keeping: her first instinct was right and the answer-choice work confirmed it.
- Asked Joshua to repeat himself once and said she zoned out (2026-11-14 00:31:27). One data point, not a pattern.

## Pattern: quantity statements

- Entered 2026-11-14 thinking "some" means "not all" or "all but one" and "most" means "at least two" (00:12:18, 00:16:32). After the cookie examples she gave "three out of five" for most and agreed that some and most can include all. Combination rules were assigned for self-study and have not been checked in session.

## Study habits and constraints (as she stated them)

- Goal of daily LR study except weekends, but school projects crowded it out before Thanksgiving break (2026-11-17 00:00:00). Planned to use the week off and winter break for LR.
- Had not done drills before the 2026-11-17 session.
- Joshua's standing advice to her: write full words rather than one or two letter abbreviations in diagrams (2026-11-17 00:22:13).
