*Before you start · Main Conclusion Lab*

# Main Conclusion Lab

**Tap the piece of the argument the author is trying to prove.**

Each question shows a full Logical Reasoning argument broken into pieces. A piece is sometimes a whole sentence and sometimes part of one. Tap the one you think is the conclusion, then press Analyze selection. You get a hint whether you're right or wrong, and you have to land on the right piece to go on. The lab lists 20 questions.

When you finish, it scores how many you got right on the first try. Eighty percent or better counts as finding the conclusion on the first read.

This is the two-question check from our session, run on new arguments. For every piece you consider, ask two things. Is it an opinion? And does another piece give the reason to believe it?

The trap this drill catches is **tapping the piece after a pivot word like *but* without checking it.** A pivot tells you the author is pushing back. The piece that follows can be a fact the author uses as proof.

One habit: before you press Analyze selection, point to the piece that gives the reason for your pick. If you can't find one, you haven't found the conclusion yet.

When you miss, read the hint and look for the check you skipped. How you got to the piece matters more than the score.
