LSAT question types and tutoring strategy
* Argument part questions are overrated because underlying skills apply universally across question types.
* Necessary assumption questions were selected as the primary focus for the tutoring session.


Definition and identification of assumptions
* An assumption on the LSAT is defined strictly as an unstated premise.
* Arguments on the LSAT are evaluated based on binary validity rather than real-world reasonableness.


Necessary versus sufficient assumptions
* Necessary assumptions act as required minimum thresholds similar to a minimum payment requirement.
* Sufficient assumptions provide more than enough information to guarantee the truth of the conclusion.
* Necessary assumption questions require weaker answer choices while sufficient assumption questions require stronger ones.


Flavors of necessary assumptions
* Necessary assumption questions comprise 2 flavors consisting of statements the author must agree with or reject.
* Defender assumptions require the author to reject potential objections that could destroy the argument.


Methods of reasoning and argument validity
* Logical reasoning methods on the LSAT rely on conditional, causal, or comparative frameworks.
* Treating a required condition as a sufficient guarantee renders an argument invalid.


LSAT logical reasoning methodology and conditional statements
* Eliminating ambiguity establishes a systematic and repeatable approach for the LSAT.
* Recognizing conditional statements early helps predict argument flaws and logical paths.
* Relying purely on intuition frequently penalizes high-achieving students on test day.


Necessary assumption and negation concepts
* Necessary assumption questions require evaluating answer choice strength and conceptual requirements carefully.
* Correct necessary assumption choices avoid overly restrictive terms like only.
* Negating conditional, quantity, and correlation statements correctly requires specific methodological techniques.


Argument parts and role question equivalence
* Argument parts and role questions serve the exact same function in evaluating statement roles.
* Different prep platforms use distinct terminology for identical logical concepts.
