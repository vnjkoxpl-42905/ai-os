* **Defining Conditional Statements (If/Then)**: The core of a conditional statement is the art of the "if then," which seeks to determine what must be true based on a given state of affairs ([00:03:19](#00:03:19)). The statement essentially asks, "If I know this to be true, what else must be true?". A basic example was provided: "If I'm breathing, then air is present" ([00:05:40](#00:05:40)).

* **Rule of Necessity in Conditionals**: Conditionals establish an absolute, certain relationship where the second thing "must happen" if the first thing occurs, with no way around the relationship. The focus of conditional reasoning is determining what "has to be true," rather than what "could be true" ([00:06:44](#00:06:44)) ([00:14:03](#00:14:03)).

* **The Sufficient Condition**: The "if" part of the conditional relationship is called the sufficient condition, which functions as the "door opener" ([00:07:43](#00:07:43)). A sufficient condition is "enough" to know about another condition; for example, knowing "I am breathing" is sufficient to know "air is present" ([00:08:37](#00:08:37)). The sufficient condition must be activated (known to be true) for the necessary condition to follow ([00:09:46](#00:09:46)).

* **Sufficient Condition and Uncertainty**: If the sufficient condition is not present (e.g., "if you're not in Ohio"), the corresponding conclusion about the necessary condition is unknown ([00:10:55](#00:10:55)) ([00:14:42](#00:14:42)). The sufficient condition guarantees the outcome but is not necessarily the only way to achieve it ([00:13:06](#00:13:06)).

* **Indicator Words for Sufficient Condition**: Certain words serve as placeholders for "if" and indicate the sufficient condition, including "if," "when," "whenever," "every," and "all" ([00:15:15](#00:15:15)). Whatever follows these words is the sufficient condition ([00:16:06](#00:16:06)).

* **The Necessary Condition**: The "then" part of the conditional is the necessary condition, which is straightforwardly defined as the "must" or "required" condition ([00:17:12](#00:17:12)). The necessary condition is a requirement that must be met if the sufficient condition occurs ([00:18:10](#00:18:10)).

* **Necessary Condition and Diagramming**: Meeting a requirement (the necessary condition) does not automatically mean the outcome (the sufficient condition) was activated ([00:19:22](#00:19:22)) ([00:23:14](#00:23:14)). The diagramming process uses an arrow to visualize the relationship, which dakota brown may do on a piece of paper during the test ([00:24:30](#00:24:30)). The relationship is read from left to right, meaning that if A happens, B must happen, but reversing the relationship (B means A) is incorrect ([00:26:17](#00:26:17)).

* **The Contrapositive (Logical Inference)**: Every conditional statement has only one logical inference, which must be true, known as the contrapositive ([00:27:16](#00:27:16)). The contrapositive is logically equivalent and interchangeable with the original statement ([00:28:09](#00:28:09)).

* **Generating the Contrapositive**: To generate the contrapositive, one must perform two steps: "flip and negate" both the sufficient and necessary conditions ([00:30:12](#00:30:12)). For example, if the statement is "If air is not present, then I am not breathing" ([00:33:41](#00:33:41)).

* **Negation and Logical Force**: Negation involves adding a "not" to an idea, which is not the same as thinking of the extreme opposite (e.g., "not rich" does not mean "poor") ([00:31:09](#00:31:09)). Negation must be thought of in minimal terms, focusing on the least amount of force required to push out of the original category ([00:32:51](#00:32:51)).

* **Applying Conditionals to LSAT Questions**: Conditional statements can serve as either evidence (premises) or the conclusion in an argument ([00:35:44](#00:35:44)). The most common LSAT flaw involves misapplying conditionals, often by assuming an outcome based on a non-activated sufficient condition ([00:36:30](#00:36:30)). In non-argument questions, a conclusion that logically follows must be supported by the premises ([00:37:47](#00:37:47)).

* **Indicator Words for Necessary Condition**: Words that signal a necessary condition (the outcome or requirement) include "must," "require," "only when," "only where," and "only if" ([00:39:45](#00:39:45)). If a sufficient indicator is present, a necessary condition must be attached, and vice versa ([00:41:16](#00:41:16)).
