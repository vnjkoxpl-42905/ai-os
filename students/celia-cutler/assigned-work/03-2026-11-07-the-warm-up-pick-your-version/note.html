Before test day · The warm-up

The Warm-Up. Pick the version that gets you in fastest.

You told me the hard part is getting out of your head and into LSAT mode. Once you're in, you're strong. A warm-up gets you there before Section 1 starts. There are four versions, each in its own note. Try each one, see which works for you, and keep it.

The moves

Four moves you already know

Every version is built from these. The first three are the steps from Read, Recall, Colour. The fourth comes from our last session. You can do any of them out loud or silently, whichever you can do where you are. Then do what each step says.

Read it.One sentence at a time, silently.Read, Recall, Colour
Tell it back.Look away and say it in your own words, as simply as you can. Out loud or in your head.Read, Recall, Colour
Colour it.React: a doubt, a question, an example, a joke. Out loud or in your head.Read, Recall, Colour
Find the spot.Before the answers, say what the right answer has to do for this argument.Our last session
The rules

Three rules for every version

  1. Warm up on sections you've already done. Use a section you have taken before, so none of the questions are new to you. Save new sections for real timed practice.
  2. Don't look at the answers before you start. That includes the answer key and your notes on the spots. Looking pulls you out of the flow you just built.
  3. Don't take a break between the warm-up and Section 1. Go straight in. Treat the warm-up as part of the test.
What "already done" means. It is a section from a PrepTest you have already worked through. You have seen the questions and reviewed them. Pick one that is far enough back that you don't remember the answers. LR is Logical Reasoning and RC is Reading Comprehension. A stimulus is the short argument above an LR question.
