Conditional Logic, The Breakdown

The Breakdown

Eleven parts, one at a time. The cheat sheet tells you what to write. This tells you why it is right.

The arrow is certainty. If the left side is true, the right side has to be true. Everything below is a consequence of that one sentence.

01

Words that put it on the left

These are rules, universals and guarantees. All three say the same thing: when this, then that. Whatever the word attaches to is the trigger.

It goes on the left, exactly as written, and nothing gets negated.

It shows up like this
If A, then B.
B whenever A.
Every A is B.
All A are B.
Ask yourself
"If what?" or "every who or what?" Whatever answers it goes on the left.
Which gives you
If left, right. right if left.
If it rains, practice is cancelled.
rain → practice cancelled

03

Words that negate the left

All four are "if not" in disguise. The attached idea is what is required, so it goes right, and the other side picks up a NOT.

If you would rather not negate anything, swap the word for "if not" and put that on the left instead. Both routes give the same rule, one as the flip of the other.

It shows up like this
A unless B.
No A without B.
Not A until B.
Ask yourself
Read the word as "if not", then put that on the left and negate it.
Which gives you
left unless right. Or: if not right, then not left.
You cannot enter unless you have a pass.
enter → pass
All four, each in a sentence
unless
You cannot enter unless you have a pass.
enter → pass
until
You cannot leave until the bell rings.
leave → bell rang
without
No one enters without a badge.
enter → badge
except
Everyone may enter except minors.
enter → NOT a minor
Diagram this. You cannot submit the form without a signature.
Check it
submit → signature
Which is the same rule as NOT signature → NOT submit. The two routes always give you a rule and its flip, so if yours came out as the other one, you are still right.
04

Words that negate the right

The attached idea is the trigger and goes left, but the negative belongs to the second half of the sentence.

Rewrite it as a positive before you draw and it stops being confusing: "no A are B" is "all A are not B."

It shows up like this
No A is B.
None of the A are B.
A is never B.
A cannot be B.
Ask yourself
Rewrite it as a positive first, then draw what you rewrote.
Which gives you
No left is right. Which is left → NOT right.
No freshman may apply.
freshman → NOT apply
All five, each in a sentence
no
No freshman may apply.
freshman → NOT apply
none
None of the drafts were saved.
draft → NOT saved
never
A draft is never final.
draft → NOT final
cannot
A member cannot vote twice.
member → NOT vote twice
not
Paper applications are not accepted.
paper application → NOT accepted
Diagram this. No expired badge opens the door.
Check it
expired badge → NOT opens door
The NOT sits on the right. Putting it on the left is the most common way this group goes wrong.
05

Words that are on no list

Some words have a home in the lists above. Others you will meet cold. Either way the same question settles it: what is guaranteed, and what is required.

Whatever sets the rule off goes left. Whatever has to be there goes right. That works whether the word made a list or not.

It shows up like this
prerequisite → right
depends on → right
will → right
the moment that → left
Ask yourself
What is guaranteed here, and what is required?
Which gives you
The requirement goes right, even when it happens first.
Spotting the gap is a prerequisite to picking the assumption.
pick assumption → spot gap
Diagram this. Learning to walk is a prerequisite to running.
Check it
running → learned to walk
Learning to walk is the requirement, so it goes on the right, even though it happens first.
06

And, or, and the flip

A rule and its flip are two sides of the same coin. The flip says: when the right side is not true, the left side cannot be true either. Flipping a rule is what the test is really checking.

Three steps, and the third is where the points are.

A and B → C NOT C → NOT A or NOT B
01
Swap
What was on the right goes on the left.
02
Negate
Positive becomes negative, negative becomes positive.
03
Switch the connector
Every and becomes an or, every or becomes an and.

If C did not happen, at least one of A or B is missing, and you cannot say which. Writing and there claims both are missing, which is more than the rule ever gave you. Going the other way, A → B or C becomes NOT B and NOT C → NOT A.

07

Both directions at once

Some sentences hand you the rule going both ways. Two words do it, and they do not work the same way.

if and only if
The if puts it left and the only if puts it right, so it is telling you to do both.
Also then and only then, when and only when.
otherwise
It hands you a second rule covering the other case. Write both down, flip both, and the arrow runs both ways.
Either both happen or neither does.
Why otherwise runs both ways

Jeff takes a vacation if he wins the lottery. Otherwise, he does not. That is two rules, not one.

lottery → vacation
NOT lottery → NOT vacation

Flip both and they collapse into a double arrow. The trap: one rule plus its own flip is not this. The deposit clears, otherwise the seat is released gives you only NOT deposit → released. Flipping that is the same rule said backwards, not a second direction. The sentence has to state both cases.

08

Not conditionals at all

These sit next to no and behave nothing like it. They count. They do not guarantee, so do not draw an arrow.

Few A are B
is
most A are not B
Not all A are B
is
some A are not B

Not all the essays were graded tells you at least one was not graded. An arrow would say every essay behaves the same way, which is more than the sentence gave you. Flip these to the positive and leave them as statements.

09

The two moves that are never allowed

Given A → B, four things can happen and only two of them prove anything. When the trigger is missing the rule simply disappears. It has no power over you, so it tells you nothing.

A is true
B has to be true.
B is false
A cannot be true.
A is false
Nothing follows. Saying B is false is the illegal move.
B is true
Nothing follows. Saying A is true is reading the arrow backwards.
Try it. The rule is if the alarm sounds, the doors lock. You are told the doors are locked. What follows?
Check it
Nothing follows.
The doors could be locked for any number of reasons. You were handed the right side and asked to claim the left. When a flaw answer says the author confuses a necessary condition with a sufficient one, this is what it is describing.
10

The two things you do with a rule

Everything above gets the arrow onto the page. From there the test only ever asks for two things. Chain it to another rule and you get a new rule. Trigger it with a fact and you get a new fact.

Chaining

Whenever you have more than one conditional, ask one question: do these connect? If the right side of one rule is the left side of another, they link, and the link is the inference.

A → B → C → D
flips whole
NOT D → NOT C → NOT B → NOT A

Start at the far end and walk it backwards. The test usually wants the front joined to the back, so A → D is the headline answer. But every shorter run is an inference too: A → C, B → D, NOT C → NOT A.

Triggering

A rule sits there doing nothing until a fact sets it off. Hand it the left side and the right side has to follow. Hand it a missing piece of the right side and the rest has to carry the load.

rule
A → B or C
fact
A
fact
NOT C
so
B

Which one is being asked for? Look at the shape of the answers. If they read if this, then that, you are chaining and the answer is a rule. If they read as a flat statement, you are triggering and the answer is a fact.

11

When to draw at all

Diagramming is setup, not the answer. Draw only when there is a reason to.

DRAW
Two or more conditionals that could link. Chain them and look for what connects the two ends.
DRAW
The conclusion is itself a conditional. The author is claiming one thing guarantees another.
DO NOT
One conditional in an ordinary argument. Hear it as a requirement and keep reading.

The missing link: an argument gives you A → B and concludes A → C. There is a hole in the chain, and the piece that fills it is B → C, or its flip, and that piece is the answer.

Test yourself

Say the answer out loud before you open each one, and you will know which part still needs work.

unless
Negate left. The attached idea goes right, the other side takes a NOT. Or read it as if not and put that on the left.
only if
Right. Whatever follows it goes on the right, exactly as written.
The three steps of the flip
Swap the sides, negate both, and switch every and to an or.
You are given the right side of a rule. What follows?
Nothing. Claiming the left from the right is reading the arrow backwards, and it is the most commonly tested flaw on the test.
few A are B
Not a conditional. It means most A are not B. It counts, it does not guarantee, so no arrow.
A → B, B → C, C → D. What is the flip of the whole chain?
NOT D → NOT C → NOT B → NOT A. Start at the far end and walk it backwards. The headline inference is A → D, but every shorter run counts too.
Rule plus rule, and rule plus fact. What does each give you?
Rule plus rule chains into a new rule. Rule plus fact triggers a new fact. The shape of the answer choices tells you which one is being asked for.
When is it worth drawing?
When two or more rules could link, or when the conclusion is itself a conditional. A single conditional in an ordinary argument is something you hear, not something you write down.

requirement

Read every rule as the left requires the right, and the ones that link get easy to spot.

There are only four moves

Every word on this page does one of them. Find the word, do what it says.

A → B

03Negate left
B → A, and a NOT
The word's idea goes right, and the other side picks up a NOT.

A → NOT B
The word's idea goes left, and the second half flips.
