Conditional Logic

Every conditional is one sentence with a trigger and a requirement. One word tells you which is which.

trigger
what sets the rule off
requirement
what has to be there

Read every rule as the left requires the right, and the ones that link get easy to spot.

There are only four moves

Every word on this page does one of them. Find the word, do what it says.

01Left
A → B
The word's idea is the trigger. It goes on the left, exactly as written.
02Right
B → A
The word's idea is what is required. It goes on the right, exactly as written.
03Negate left
B → A, and a NOT
The word's idea goes right, and the other side picks up a NOT.
04Negate right
A → NOT B
The word's idea goes left, and the second half flips.

LEFT19 words

Words that put it on the left

These are rules, universals and guarantees. All three say the same thing: when this, then that.

Ask "if what?" or "every who or what?" Whatever answers it goes on the left, exactly as written, and nothing gets negated.

If it rains, practice is cancelled.
rain → practice cancelled

NEGATE LEFT4 words

Words that negate the left

All four are "if not" in disguise. The attached idea is what is required, so it goes right, and the other side picks up a NOT.

If you would rather not negate anything, swap the word for "if not" and put that on the left instead. Both routes give the same rule, one as the flip of the other.

You cannot enter unless you have a pass.
enter → pass
All four, each in a sentence
unless
You cannot enter unless you have a pass.
enter → pass
until
You cannot leave until the bell rings.
leave → bell rang
without
No one enters without a badge.
enter → badge
except
Everyone may enter except minors.
enter → NOT a minor

Words that negate the right

The attached idea is the trigger and goes left, but the negative belongs to the second half of the sentence.

Rewrite it as a positive before you draw and it stops being confusing: "no A are B" is "all A are not B."

No freshman may apply.
freshman → NOT apply

Words that are on no list

Some words have a home in the lists above. Others you will meet cold. Either way the same question settles it: what is guaranteed, and what is required.

Whatever sets the rule off goes left. Whatever has to be there goes right. That works whether the word made a list or not.

Spotting the gap is a prerequisite to picking the assumption.
pick assumption → spot gap
Four that come up
prerequisite
A requirement, so it goes right.
physics → calculus

A requirement, so it goes right.
picnic → good weather
will
An outcome, so it goes right too.
deadline passes → form locks
the moment that
A trigger, so it goes left.
the moment it ships → billing starts

Flipping a rule

A rule and its flip are two sides of the same coin. Flipping is what the test is really checking, and the third step is where the points are.

A and B → C NOT C → NOT A or NOT B
Swap
What was on the right goes on the left.
Negate
Positive becomes negative, negative becomes positive.
Switch the connector
Every and becomes an or, every or becomes an and.

If C did not happen, at least one of A or B is missing, and you cannot say which. Writing and there claims both are missing, which is more than the rule ever gave you.

The two things you do with a rule

Everything above gets the arrow onto the page. From there the test only ever asks for two things.

Chain it
rule + rule = a new rule
If the right side of one rule is the left side of another, they link, and the link is the inference.
A → B → C → D
Trigger it
rule + fact = a new fact
A rule sits there doing nothing until a fact sets it off. Hand it the left side and the right side has to follow.
A → B or C, plus A, plus NOT C

The whole thing, in five steps

01
Find the word. One word tells you which side is the trigger and which side is required.
02
Do what it says. Left, right, negate left, or negate right.
03
Flip it when the question wants the other direction. Swap, negate, and switch every and to an or.
04
Chain it or trigger it. Rule plus rule gives a new rule. Rule plus fact gives a new fact.
05
Only draw when the rules could link. A single conditional inside an ordinary argument is something you hear, not something you write down.
