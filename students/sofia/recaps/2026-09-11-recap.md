# Session recap · 2026-09-11

Hey Sofia,

Today’s focus was conditional Must Be True questions: knowing when to apply a rule to a fact, when to chain rules, and how to keep every arrow moving in the right direction.

## Start by sorting the stimulus

### If you have rules and a fact

A rule tells you what must happen when its trigger occurs. A fact tells you whether that trigger has occurred.

Your job is to apply the rule to the fact. The question will often hide the answer in the contrapositive.

### If you have only rules

Your job is to chain them. Look for the idea that appears in more than one rule. That repeated idea is the bridge.

```text
A → B
B → C

Therefore: A → C
```

The test is usually interested in the two loose ends, A and C. The repeated idea, B, is there to help you connect them.

## Keep the arrow honest

Write the trigger first and the guaranteed result second. Then test the answer against that exact direction.

```text
Burning a large amount of trash in an incinerator → wasting resources

Valid contrapositive:
Avoid wasting resources → do not burn a large amount of trash in an incinerator

Invalid reversal:
Do not burn a large amount of trash in an incinerator → avoid wasting resources
```

The invalid version goes too far. Avoiding one way of wasting resources does not prove that every other way has disappeared.

When your reading and your diagram seem to disagree, return to the sentence that created the arrow. Ask two questions:

1. What is the trigger?
2. What result does the sentence guarantee?

That check is more reliable than rebuilding the whole stimulus from memory.
