Anti-Causal Set 3: make an excuse for the evidence

This drill gives you six Weaken questions at difficulty 2 to 4, untimed. In each one, the author says one thing does not cause another, because a fact seems to go against it. Read this once, then start.

What these arguments look like

The author points to a fact and says it shows there is no cause. Here is the idea in a new setting. Two cafes ran the same promotion, but only one played music. Their sales ended up about equal, so the author says music does nothing for sales.

Keep the cause, explain the fact

Your job is to accept the fact and still argue that the cause is real. Finish this sentence for every question before you read the answers: the cause still works, because ...?

What the author points to An excuse that keeps the cause
The two cafes ended up with equal sales The cafe with music started with far fewer customers, so music did help
Sales did not change Music takes months to build a regular crowd, so it is too early to see it

A study has the same shape. If the group that never got the treatment did just as well, ask how that group was reached anyway. That is the study question we worked through in the session.

One trap, one habit

An answer that agrees with the fact but never explains it away does not weaken. An answer about a group the author did not use almost never helps either. When you cut a wrong answer, say what it leaves untouched. Then go back to the sentence you finished and check the next answer against it.
