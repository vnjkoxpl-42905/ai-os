Day 2 · Sufficient vs necessary · Note 2 of 2

Find the gap before you read a single answer

Read this before you open Missing Links: First Taste. Four questions, and each one is the same move.

Arguments leak in a predictable place

An argument gives you evidence about one thing and draws a conclusion about something slightly different. The gap sits right at that seam. It is almost never subtle once you look for it, and it is almost impossible to see if you start reading answer choices first.

Evidence: this fertilizer produced larger tomatoes in the trial.

Conclusion: so the farm should buy it to raise profits.

The evidence is about size. The conclusion is about profit. Nobody connected those. That is the link, and you found it without reading one option.

Predict, then read

Say the missing link out loud in your own plain words before you look. It does not have to be phrased the way the test would phrase it. It has to be right. Then the answer choices become a matching exercise instead of five arguments you have to referee, and the ones written to sound impressive lose most of their pull.

Same gap, two sizes of answer

Once you have the gap, yesterday's distinction tells you what the right answer looks like. A sufficient answer builds the whole bridge and lets you walk across: bigger tomatoes always mean more profit here. A necessary answer lays down one plank you cannot do without: bigger tomatoes do not cost more to produce than they earn. Both are about the same gap. They differ in how much they are trying to do.

Bring the habit from our session

When a choice says "it" or "they" or "this result," stop and put the actual words back in. You talked yourself out of correct answers on the role questions by reading the fancy phrasing whole instead of in pieces. Same fix here: break the choice apart, substitute the real claim for the pronoun, and then decide. The choices on these are written to reward exactly that.

While you drill

  1. Underline the thing the evidence is about. Underline the thing the conclusion is about. The gap is between them.
  2. Say the link in your own words before you read the options.
  3. Translate every choice, including the ones you are sure are wrong. Knowing why four fail is how you stop second-guessing the one that works.
