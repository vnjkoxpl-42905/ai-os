If, then, and nothing else

Read this before you open Conditional Logic (Easier) Set 4. Four minutes, then go.

What a conditional actually claims

A conditional is a promise. It says: when this is true, that has to be true as well. That is the whole claim. It is not a claim about causes, about how often, or about what usually happens. It is a guarantee, and guarantees are easy to test.

Every conditional has two halves doing two different jobs.

sufficient → necessary

The sufficient half is enough on its own. Have it, and the promise fires. The necessary half is what the promise buys you. It has to be there, but having it proves nothing on its own.

How to spot which is which

Certain words tell you the sufficient half is coming. Whatever follows them is the trigger:

if · when · whenever · all · every · any · each

So in "every senator voted," being a senator is the trigger and voting is what it guarantees. Write the arrow before you look at a single answer choice. On these questions the diagram is not showing your work. It is the work.

The one thing it never says

The arrow points one way. It does not run backwards, and the test makes most of its money on people who let it.

rain → the ground is wet

Wet ground does not tell you it rained. Someone could have washed a car. The promise was only ever made in one direction, and an answer choice that walks it back the other way is wrong no matter how sensible it sounds.

While you drill

  1. Find the indicator word first. It tells you which half is the trigger.
  2. Write the arrow before reading the choices. Every time, even when it feels obvious.
  3. When a choice feels right, check the direction it runs. That is where these go wrong.

One habit for this week

After you finish, go back through the ones you got wrong before you look at anything else, and work out what the right answer was without timing yourself. You have fifteen tests and fifty-five sections logged and no blind review on any of them. Until that changes, neither of us can tell whether a miss was the concept or the clock. Start here, on six questions. It is a small enough set to actually do it.
