The hard part is the answer choices

Read this before you open Flaw - First Taste. Last four questions of the week.

You find flaws. That is not the thing standing between you and these points. The wrong answers on flaw questions are written to be attractive to someone who already understands the argument, which is exactly where you sit.

The trap that catches most people here

By a wide margin, the most chosen wrong answer in this question pool is a real, genuine, textbook flaw. It is simply not the flaw this argument made.

It reads as correct because everything in the sentence is a legitimate reasoning error you have heard of. The only question that matters is whether this argument committed it. That is why naming the flaw yourself first is not a stylistic preference. It is the defense.

Two more worth knowing by name. Out of scope: the choice describes an error about something the argument never discussed. Too strong: the choice is right in spirit but overstates what the argument actually did.

Read the choices in pieces

These answers are written in a compressed, abstract register: "presumes, without warrant, that a condition sufficient for a result is also required for it." Read a sentence like that whole and it turns into a fog. Break it at the commas. Take one clause at a time. For every pronoun, put the real claim back in and check that piece against the argument before moving on.

Do this for all five, including the ones you have already dismissed. Knowing exactly why four are wrong is what lets you commit to the fifth instead of circling back to it, and circling back is what has been costing you.

While you drill

  1. Name the flaw in your own words. Write it down so you cannot quietly revise it later.
  2. For each choice, ask the narrow question: did this argument do that? Not: is that a flaw.
  3. Substitute real words for every pronoun before you judge a clause.

Before our next session

Blind review all three days: go back to everything you missed and work out the right answer untimed, before looking at any explanation. Bring me the ones that still do not sit right. That conversation is worth more than another twenty questions, and this week was built to be small enough that you can actually do it. Stay on these six drills and do not run ahead. You cannot negotiate with this skill.
