Quantity words: what each one lets you say

Keep this open beside any set that uses quantity words. Find the word in the table, then read what it says and what it leaves open.

The words, from needing the least to needing the most

Word It says It leaves open
some, sometimes, can At least one has it Whether it is most, or all
some are not, not all At least one does not have it Whether most lack it
many, commonly, often At least a handful have it Whether it is more than half
most, majority, usually, typically More than half have it, so at least one does Whether it is all
a minority, few, rarely 50% or less have it, so most do not How small the group is
all, each, every, any Every one has it. A rule: in the group means has it Whether anyone outside the group has it

A minority claim needs about as much from the passage as a most claim. It points the other way.

Weaker needs less

A weaker claim needs less from the passage, so on Must Be True the weaker answer is the safer one. Must Be False asks the same kind of question turned around: which answer has to be not true.

Words that ask for the same thing as must be true

"Inferred," "properly inferred" and "must be true" are the stems we treated as the same question. In Reading Comprehension, an inference question asks for it too.
