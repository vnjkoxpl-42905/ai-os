Conditional Chains in Parallel Questions

When the stimulus runs on if-then claims, the thing to match is the chain. Find where the claims link, and where they do not.

When do two claims link?

A chain forms when the outcome of one claim is the trigger of another. That repeated idea is the hinge. Where the repeat sits decides everything.

Where the repeated idea sitsWritten outDoes it link?
Outcome, then triggerfountain on → crowded
crowded → out of cups		Yes. Fountain on leads to out of cups.
Trigger, then triggerfountain on → crowded
fountain on → out of cups		No. Both outcomes follow from the same trigger. They tell you nothing about each other.
Outcome, then outcomefountain on → crowded
street fair → crowded		No. Two different things lead to crowded. Crowded does not tell you which.

This is the copycat step from your Parallel Reasoning note, with one addition: in a conditional, position tells you something.

Match the chain

If the fountain is on, the plaza is crowded. If the plaza is crowded, the café runs out of cups. So if the fountain is on, the café runs out of cups.

Requirement: two conditionals joined at the outcome of the first and the trigger of the second, and a conclusion from the first trigger to the last outcome.

Matches. If the lamp is lit, the room is warm. If the room is warm, the window is open. So if the lamp is lit, the window is open.
Link is broken. If the lamp is lit, the room is warm. If the lamp is lit, the window is open. So if the room is warm, the window is open. Both claims start from the same trigger. The conclusion joins two outcomes that were never connected.
Chain read backwards. If the lamp is lit, the room is warm. If the room is warm, the window is open. So if the window is open, the lamp is lit. The chain runs one way. The conclusion runs against it.

Writing the contrapositive

Flip the two sides, then negate both. A conditional and its contrapositive say the same thing.

fountain on → out of cups
out of cups → fountain on

In words: if the café still has cups, the fountain was off.

The right answer is often the chain written backwards. If an answer reads like a mismatch and you cannot name the rule it broke, flip it before you cut it.

Words that hide a conditional

WordingWritten as
Whenever the alarm sounds, the doors lock.alarm sounds → doors lock
The doors lock only if the alarm sounds.doors lock → alarm sounds
The doors stay unlocked unless the alarm sounds.alarm sounds → doors unlocked
None of the guests who arrived late got a seat.arrived late → got a seat

Whenever and only if point opposite ways. That one swap decides whether a chain links, so slow down on them.

Try it before the drill

1

Write this as an arrow: "A visitor may enter the archive only if a librarian signs them in."

Open the answer

may enter → librarian signs in. Only if puts the thing you need on the outcome side.

2

If the bridge is closed, traffic uses the tunnel. If traffic uses the tunnel, the toll booth backs up. Which conclusion follows?

Open the answer

If the bridge is closed, the toll booth backs up. Its contrapositive also follows: if the toll booth does not back up, the bridge is not closed.
