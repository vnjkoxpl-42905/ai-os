Parallel Flaw: Name the Mistake First

In a parallel flaw question you are not matching the structure. You are matching the mistake. Say the mistake in one sentence before you look at a single answer.

The job

The stimulus is a broken argument. The right answer is a different argument, about a different subject, that breaks in the same place. Two arguments can both be broken and still not be the same broken argument, so "this one also looks bad" is never enough.

One example, start to finish

Everyone who works the night shift at the bakery gets free coffee. Dana gets free coffee. So Dana works the night shift at the bakery.

night shift → free coffee
Dana: free coffee
So: Dana → night shift

The mistake, in one sentence: it treats the outcome as proof of the trigger. Other people could get free coffee too, so free coffee does not tell you who works nights.

Now hold three answers to that sentence.

Same mistake. All trains that stop at Elm station are late. This train is late. So it stops at Elm station. The outcome is used as proof of the trigger. Keep it.
No mistake at all. All trains that stop at Elm station are late. This train stops at Elm station. So it is late. This one is valid. A flawed stimulus can never be matched by a valid answer. Cut it.
A different mistake. If a train stops at Elm station, it is late. This train does not stop at Elm station. So it is not late. This one denies the trigger and concludes the outcome is false. That is a real flaw, but it is not yours. Cut it.

Notice what you did not need: anything about trains or bakeries.

Five mistakes to name on sight

If you cannot name the flaw, you will not recognise it when it shows up in a new subject. These five cover most of what you will meet in this set.

MistakeWhat went wrongSounds like
ReversalOutcome used as proof of the trigger.Every winner trained daily. Sam trained daily. So Sam won.
Denying the triggerThe trigger is missing, so the outcome is declared missing too.If you study, you pass. Lee did not study. So Lee did not pass.
Two somes that do not connectTwo "some" claims joined as if they pointed at the same people.Some choir members play piano. Some piano players also play drums. So some choir members play drums.
Correlation to causeTwo things go together, so one is said to produce the other.Households with a treadmill report better sleep. So treadmills improve sleep.
Part to wholeWhat is true of each piece is claimed for the group.Each musician in the band is skilled, so the band will sound great.

Check for validity first. If the stimulus has no flaw, then the right answer has none either. Test the stimulus first: can you reach the conclusion by following the arrows forward? If you can, you are in a valid-argument question and the flaw list does not apply.

How to run it

  1. Say the mistake in one sentence. Use the words from the table. If it will not fit in a sentence, you have not found it yet.
  2. Cut every answer that has no flaw. This is fast. If you can follow the arrows to the conclusion, the answer is valid and it is gone.
  3. Cut the answers with a different flaw. Reversal does not match denying the trigger, even though both start with "if".
  4. If two answers survive, compare the details. Strength words (all against most), a not that appears in one and not the other, and any extra feature the stimulus never had.

On the hardest questions the flaw outranks the structure. If one answer commits your exact mistake with a slightly different shape, and another has the exact shape but a different mistake, take the first.

Try it before the drill

Name the mistake, then open the answer. One of these has no mistake.

1

Every employee who passed the safety course may enter the lab. Priya may enter the lab. So Priya passed the safety course.

Open the answer

Reversal. Passing the course is enough to enter the lab, and Priya may enter the lab, but that does not show how she got in. Someone else could have been allowed in another way.

2

If the shop is open, the lights are on. The lights are off. So the shop is closed.

Open the answer

No mistake. This is the contrapositive. If open leads to lights on, then lights off leads to not open. Do not hunt for a flaw that is not there.

3

Each chapter of the book is short, so the book is short.

Open the answer

Part to whole. Many short chapters can make a long book.
