## What the source says

- **ARMS is an operating-system inventory:** Applications, Routines, Memory and Skills (1:52 onward). Applications expose both automation reach and granted trust. Find frequently used disconnected tools, review unused connections, and review forgotten routines for possible retirement. The graph links routines and skills and lets the user read skills and open local files. The narrator reports mapping 35,466 files; that is his reported workspace, not a measured ai-os inventory.
- **Retrieval is the main value:** at 6:53 he assigns the graph roughly 30% and day-to-day retrieval 70%. Those percentages express his judgment. He demonstrates two sessions and reports about 30,000 versus 50,000 message tokens, roughly 40% savings (8:46). The clip supplies neither a reproducible benchmark nor the complete questions, raw traces, repeated results or correctness grades. This is a demonstration claim, not a proven saving for Joshua.
- **Use deterministic routing before model reading:** his custom `brain.js` removes filler words, ranks candidate files from indexes/reference maps, reads a likely answer section, and follows pointers before handing evidence to the model (9:26 to 10:49). The described technique is transferable; its code is not included.
- **Research before rebuilding:** he says he used a Matt Van Horn `/last 30 days` skill for recent cross-platform research and supplied QMD, G Brain and Graphify as repositories to consider (10:49 to 12:14). These names, creator/funding claims and source transcriptions are unverified; exact repository identities are not captured. None is installed by this ingestion.
- **Make a graph operational:** show department size and ARMS relationships, expand folders, open actual files and read skill text; use it to explain a client workspace (1:08 to 6:53). If building that interface, set measurable no-lag interaction and under-ten-second reload goals, then verify them (12:14 to 12:38).
- **Resources and promotion:** the description offers a free R65 PDF in the RoboNuggets Skool community, a commercial import-ready system and RUBRIC. It also promotes affiliate trials for Blotato, n8n, Make, ElevenLabs and Apify. The PDF, implementation, exact prompts and commercial tools are not present in the supplied file. Subscription urgency and the named model's market/pricing claims at 0:00 and 13:00 are dated promotional assertions, not verified current product facts.
