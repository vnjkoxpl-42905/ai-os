## What the source actually teaches

The six main sections proceed in this order:

1. **The Art of the If/Then:** ordinary meaning; one recurring breathing/air example; sufficient and necessary conditions; indicator families; relationship before wording.
2. **The Contrapositive:** recap; coffee/walking thought experiment; attempt before reveal; missing requirement rules out the sufficient condition; four fact situations and their inference limits.
3. **How to Diagram Conditionals:** meaning of the arrow; four construction steps; incremental worked diagram; negation and four contrapositive sign patterns; five FAQ entries about use, confidence, timing, contrapositives, and abbreviation.
4. **Figuring Out the Language:** identify an indicator's target; common target placement; counterexamples to a positional shortcut; the same indicator can appear on either side of its target.
5. **Your Friend, the Personalized If/Then:** four checks for testing a translation against ordinary meaning; one correct and one deliberately reversed interpretation of an orchid/conversation statement.
6. **Basic Conditional Drill:** six themed translation items. Original and contrapositive notations are displayed in the supplied HTML. The source attributes its adapted exercise sentences to Shakespeare; these are not evidence of the narrator's own prose style.

The source explicitly distinguishes training from eventual performance: students write contrapositives in its drills to demonstrate the skill, even though the author does not recommend mechanically writing every contrapositive during a test.

## What Joshua chose and what remains interpretation

**Chosen by Joshua:** this source's teaching voice is the calibration standard across LSAT material, with new examples and independently written lessons. For the conditional bootcamp specifically, his 2026-10-03 instruction also selects the entire supplied concept sequence as its content backbone.

**Analysis:** the strongest mechanism is audible reasoning. The narrator states a small idea, works it through familiar language, lets the learner make a prediction, explains the result, tests a tempting wrong interpretation, and only then formalizes it. Conversational diction, variable sentence lengths, controlled repetition, and questions support that mechanism.

**Limits:** personal experience claims belong to the source narrator. Its learning and timing assertions are not independently established research. Its four-step diagramming routine, two indicator families, letter limits, and legacy Logic Games advice do not automatically replace Joshua's methods or current course scope. “Ignore the conditional” is local advice about an immediate inference from one rule; it does not permit discarding that rule from later reasoning.
