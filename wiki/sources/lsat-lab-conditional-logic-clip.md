## Keyword inventory is now observed, with scope limits

The last board's **left** inventory is: if, when, whenever, the only; all, each, any, every, no. Its **right** inventory is: ensures, implies, leads to, will, always; only, only if, requires, must, necessitates, depends on, relies on, unless, until, without. These are the vendor's observed labels, not an unconditional parsing algorithm and not Joshua's complete group taxonomy.

For the ordinary Group 1/2 work, adapt the rule/universal and requirement/guarantee meanings, preserving the full qualified idea attached to an expression. Sentence location does not determine diagram side. In an if sentence, the if-clause may come first or last. In a requirement sentence, the goal can come before the required thing or after it. Teach students to identify what is enough and what that situation requires, then read the whole relationship back.

Keep **no** and **unless/until/without** in their separately taught negative/exception lessons. Their appearance in the reference does not eliminate the additional transformations. **Only** and **the only** require separate grammatical explanations. **Always**, **will**, and **must** must be read within the actual sentence: a rule prescribing an action is not proof that someone complied. The source's equivalent-expression board makes these distinctions worth teaching with controlled contrasts.

## What to adapt and what to correct

Use original examples and wording. Useful design patterns are: stable rule plus one changed fact; full-idea labels; prompt before reveal; role labels next to terms; original and contrapositive side by side after the meaning is understood; equivalent sentences before a reference table; and a final meaning-based check rather than keyword recitation alone.

Do not copy the clip's introductory numerical prevalence claims into course copy. No supporting study is supplied. Do not repeat the caption's statement that the rule disappears when the sufficient condition is absent: the rule still holds, but that fact alone leaves the other condition unsettled. Do not imply this ten-minute overview proves mastery or teaches all conditional skills.
