## An explicit transcript discrepancy

At 6:06 the transcript criticizes deriving **¬X** from **¬Y** under **X → ¬Y**. At 6:34 it criticizes deriving **X** from **¬Y**. These are different proposed conclusions. Both are unsupported, but the transcript shifts between them. We cannot identify whether this was a speaker correction or transcription error without playback.

The logically correct teaching point is: given X → ¬Y and ¬Y, X could be true or false. Both X/¬Y and ¬X/¬Y satisfy the rule. Preserve the discrepancy as evidence; do not silently attribute a polished, consistent exercise to the source.

## Original adaptations for the bootcamp

Use the [existing source map](../../brainstorms/2026-09-30-conditional-curriculum/2026-10-02-opening-lessons-source-map.md) and [selected voice](../../references/lsat-teaching-voice-standard.md). These are newly authored examples inspired by the teaching functions above, not source quotations or published assignments.

1. **Familiar inclusion:** Every square is a rectangle. A shape is not a square. Can it still be a rectangle? Yes. A rectangle with unequal adjacent sides supplies a possible case. Use only if these shape meanings are clear; the logic must not turn into a geometry test.
2. **Stipulated rule:** Every ticket in Drawer R has a blue border. A ticket is in Drawer R. What follows? It has a blue border. The exercise does not need to explain why tickets were sorted this way.
3. **Wording comparison:** “Only blue-bordered tickets are in Drawer R” and “A ticket is in Drawer R only if it has a blue border” both express Drawer R → Blue border. Ask learners to choose the matching relationship and explain what the sentence requires.
4. **Negative-condition transfer:** If a room is showing a film, it is not hosting a workshop. The room is not hosting a workshop. Does that settle whether a film is showing? No. Ask the learner to build two possible rooms: film/no workshop and no film/no workshop. Both fit. This directly demonstrates why the reverse inference fails.
5. **Delayed abstract retrieval:** After the negative-condition lesson, show X → ¬Y and ask which fact rules out X. Y does, because X would require ¬Y. Ask separately what ¬Y establishes about X: nothing definite. Keep the two questions distinct.

## Do not import these source habits

- Saying a rule no longer matters when one trigger is absent. Keep it available for later facts and other inferences.
- Profanity, ridicule or dismissive characterizations of a learner's reasoning.
- Smoking as a universally sufficient condition for an undefined “not healthy.” That analogy introduces vague and contestable content unrelated to learning the inference.
- Legacy Logic Games framing, source promotion or unsupported learning guarantees.
- The source's hurried “the only”/“must” sentence as a clean indicator template. Use a grammatical original and preserve obligation versus compliance.
- Unless/without before their transformations have been taught. Keep all topic prerequisites in the existing 13-part progression and later-unit boundaries.
