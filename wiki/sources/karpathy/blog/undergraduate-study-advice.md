## What this source says

Karpathy recommends planning the scope of study, inspecting prior assessment styles, reconstructing important material with the book closed, doing exercises and teaching others. His longer advice also covers personal sleep/caffeine habits, exam strategy, using time on real projects and building relationships and documented contributions rather than optimizing grades alone.

## Interpretation and limits

The useful teaching transfer is to require independent reconstruction and exercise performance rather than passive recognition. Personal sleep, caffeine and grade prescriptions are anecdotes, not medical guidance or LSAT policy. Some recommendations presume handwritten, graded university exams and do not directly fit the LSAT. The companion HTML is a format counterpart, not an independent supporting article. Statements are attributed to the captured source; no changing external capability was verified live in this compilation.
