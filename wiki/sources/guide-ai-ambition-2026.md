# Choose the goal, the expert method, and the needed perspectives

RoboNuggets, *3 Types of AI Ambition*, 10 pages. The guide cites Felix Rieseberg's [2026-10-13 essay](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/); the PDF's own publication date is not stated. [Archived PDF](../../raw/sources/ai-guides-2026-10-29/ai-ambition/3%20Types%20of%20AI%20Ambition%20Guide.pdf) and [provenance](../../raw/sources/ai-guides-2026-10-29/ai-ambition/PROVENANCE.md).

## The three moves

- **Altitude** (pp. 2–3): ask what outcome a task serves, then select the level of autonomy and data needed. The example progresses from quarterly analysis to decisions to a trial of a better review process. The source says this suits a clear ultimate outcome more than some creative work.
- **Depth** (pp. 4–5): identify the professional method, underlying data, appropriate tools, and a test or outside judge. The guide warns that professional language alone does not establish professional methods. Ambitious performance targets remain hypotheses to measure, not guarantees.
- **Range** (pp. 6–7): describe what the artifact must accomplish using verbs; choose relevant disciplines and methods to address those functions. Distinguish actual independent review or domain methods from merely role-playing experts. Additional agents carry cost and depend on the runtime's delegation rules.
- **Motivation and limits** (p. 8): the guide connects hard goals to self-efficacy and advocates pacing and purpose. Its historical and psychological examples are attributed claims, not independently checked here.

> “a deeper version of this task, not a bigger one” (p. 5).
