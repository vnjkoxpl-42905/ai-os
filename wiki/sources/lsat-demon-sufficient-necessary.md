Original adaptation: **If a display lamp is lit, its battery is installed.** First give only the rule and ask whether it establishes that the lamp is lit. Then add the fact that it is lit and ask what follows. Later, after explaining the necessary role, ask what follows if the battery is absent. Keep the same lamp and the same stated rule so the changing fact is the learner's focus.

This is an authored hypothetical, not a technical claim about every real lamp. Introduce it explicitly as the rule for this exercise. Use fresh objects for independent practice after the walkthrough.

## Boundaries and corrections

- The source's phrasing that the rule is not “hard and fast” could suggest exceptions. Our conditional has no exception when its sufficient condition holds; what is conditional is its application to a case.
- A trigger need not cause an event, and a conditional need not describe a time sequence. The arrow means implication under the stated rule.
- Remove the source's weapon analogy and dismissive comparisons with schoolchildren. A learner's uncertainty deserves an explanation.
- The missing sufficient condition does not remove the rule. It leaves the other condition unsettled by that fact and rule alone.
- The clip is evidence of a teaching approach, not comparative learning-science evidence or proof of student outcomes.
