# Meghan Mullins, 2026-04-10

## LSAT concepts

- **Method of reasoning** — the session's core work: answer choices that describe what the author did, and how to verify them word by word against the stimulus.
- **Argument** — defined strictly and enforced from here on: "argument strictly means premise and conclusion." It does not require an opposing side. "Arguing against" is what requires an opposing viewpoint to exist in the stimulus.
- **Hypothesis** — defined as "potential causation, a potential cause." If an answer choice says hypothesis, you must be able to point at a cause and an effect in the stimulus. "This is one of those words that you would never know unless you got taught it."
- **Explanation** — "it strictly means causation. It does not mean justifying, it does not mean giving reasons. That's what we normally mean. It doesn't mean that at all." Closely linked to hypothesis.
- **Role / intermediate conclusion** — a claim that has its own premise supporting it and that in turn supports the main conclusion. Also covered: "provides a specific instance of the general principle the argument as a whole is directed toward establishing" points at the conclusion.
- **Main conclusion identification** — the "however / but they are wrong" pivot after an opposing view is presented is almost always the main conclusion, even when it is only three words long.
- **Quantifier precision** — "everyone" versus "many people" as an answer-killing distinction.

## Worked examples

- **Fraudulent medical products / method question.** Stimulus: many people are scammed by sellers of fraudulent medical products, easily explained by their yearning for easy solutions to complex medical problems plus lack of medical background. However, the same explanation cannot be given for a recent trend among medical professionals toward susceptibility to fraudulent claims, since they of course have no lack of medical knowledge. Meghan chose B, debating against E. E was eliminated because the author is not arguing that the explanation should be accepted, she is arguing it is inadequate for the new group. B was eliminated on a single word, "everyone," where the stimulus says only "many people." Correct answer D: since two groups are disanalogous in important respects (medical knowledge), there must be different explanations for similar behavior (being scammed). Joshua also ran his de-abstraction procedure on the earlier choice about "arguing against a hypothesis," establishing that it would require an opposing viewpoint plus an identifiable cause-and-effect the author is displacing.
- **Astrology and personality types / role question.** Stimulus: although some scientists believe there is no correlation between astrological sign and personality type, that claim is scientifically unjustified; since science does not have precise criteria for distinguishing one personality type from another, scientific studies cannot be used to disprove a correlation between personality type and any other phenomenon. Question asks the role of the second half of the last sentence. Run backwards: A requires a premise supporting an intermediate conclusion, B requires the intermediate conclusion itself, C the main conclusion, D an opposing viewpoint, E a specific instance of a general principle. Meghan stalled on the stimulus, then with prompting identified "although" as marking the opposing view, and found the buried three-word main conclusion ("is scientifically unjustified"). Once the structure was visible she named B immediately. Joshua: "you got it bang on. Once you solve for the stimulus, the answer choices are very easy."
