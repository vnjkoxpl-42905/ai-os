## LSAT concepts

- **Question-type approach beats section approach.** "There's about 13 types of question types... a section approach is not really how you want to. There is a place for that, you do want to have that general approach, but you want to be able to approach different questions with different tools." He named must be true vs parallel reasoning vs weaken as needing "almost different lenses on."
- **Efficiency is a per-type skill, not a general speed skill.** On parallel reasoning: "There's ways to be efficient with that, because if you waste your time reading — technically you're reading six arguments." The operative questions per type: "What do I do different? What do I look for? What do I automatically delete without even looking at them?"
- **Mental energy is a finite resource that the score depends on.** "The level of energy you have for focus in the beginning of a practice test, and even paying attention to detail, may not be the same at the end of the test... that all adds up to the score you get at the end of the day, because we're not robots." And: "That pressure of getting to question 16 and seeing the time running is a different kind of stress."
- **Practice tests are diagnostic, not instructional.** "You don't want to take practice tests prior to knowing... practice tests are where you look and you find what you need to do based off of your results, but having a technique to doing it in the first place — you don't want to do that while you're taking practice tests."
- **The test is learnable.** "It's a skills-based test. It's not a random set of questions that have completely nothing to do with each other and just disjointed. So you can learn the skill."

## Teaching moves

- **Name the student's own best insight back to her with a term.** Kristin said "I don't know what I don't know." Joshua: "You hit on something pretty profound. You were basically talking about your unknown unknowns... It's one thing to say I have an issue with conditional reasoning — address that issue. But the harder thing is that you don't even know that you're not aware of something, which is challenging, because you want to know the problem to be able to address it."
- **The free-throw analogy** — his standard opener on volume vs mechanics, and he asked what sport she played before choosing it (basketball, which he also plays). "If you just take as many free throws as you possibly can without learning the mechanics — lining up your feet, having your elbow tucked in... If I never shot a free throw in my life and I took a thousand free throws and never saw anybody else take a free throw, you're going to be seeing backwards shots, granny shots, hook shots, because they've never been exposed to it. The volume will only be useful once you have the technique."
- **The track-and-field analogy for timeline**: "You're not going to become a fast sprinter really working hard in two days. You need to practice, rest, come back."
- **Detach the score from the person, hard and early.** "Your score that you got has no indication or reflection of what you're capable of... Take it as just a random number that you got on a test day. You can improve your score by 20 points." And: "It's so premature to be able to say even remotely what you're able to do and what you're not able to do."
- **Say the blunt thing, then immediately check whether it landed as an insult.** He told her twice that she hasn't started ("you haven't even started... I don't mean that disrespectfully") and checked three separate times: "I hope I'm not coming off condescending. I don't mean to be." When she pushed back ("Well, I wouldn't say all that — I took practice tests, I had a strategy"), he conceded instantly: "Fair."
- **Pair every criticism with a genuine compliment about capacity.** "It's impressive that you were able to pull that off... You have the mental horsepower to be able to effectively get a high score." And the calibration that makes it credible: "I've had students I've worked with for six, seven months who've done this religiously — for you to come in and score in a decent score range is a very good sign. You started off better than I did."
- **Undercut his own authority to keep it honest.** "I'm speaking very prematurely. I haven't seen you approach a question. Take what I'm saying with a grain of salt. Don't take me as gospel." And: "I could be completely off the ball."
- **Warn against vibes-based work.** "If you're just going by vibes, you're going to get punished... You may have got a question right, but why did you get it right? And how would you do that again?"
- **Tell his own humbling story.** "I did pretty well in school. I expected to enroll in this test — I thought I would walk in and say what's up and get a good score. That was not the case. I got humbled. I started off in the 130s." He also flagged his own recovery as a cautionary tale, not a model: "I ended up going through every resource on the material. Not because I was any good — it was actually very counterproductive. I would never recommend you do that, because there's a lot of conflicting information, waste of your time. But I didn't know any better."
- **Reframe tuition as a controllable variable.** "It's unfortunate that the LSAT will be the great equalizer... whatever your score on the test will define whether you go to school for free or you pay full price. It's pretty ridiculous the system is set up like that, but that's the way it is." Then the mindset instruction: "You shouldn't really pay much for law school. You ideally want to have that mindset... If you don't want to pay for law school, just get enough questions right on a test and you'll never have to pay for law school."
- **Permission to aim high, framed as a question.** "If you're like, hey, I want to go to an Ivy League school — if not you, somebody's going to go. Why not you?"
- **Match the client's register.** She was proud of the one-week stunt; rather than lecture, he leaned into it: "You're built different. That's wild. You might be the first student — that might be the least amount of studying that I've ever come across." And: "You're giving me anxiety here, I won't lie to you." And: "One week is genuinely like you should take that as a trophy. I need some of that confidence."
- **No-pressure close.** "Take your time as well. Don't feel pressured to like, oh, I need to start. Look around, find what's good for you, and then if it's obviously a good match we can do that as well. Don't feel pressured by any means."
