# Abdullah Soliman, 2026-03-13

## Teaching moves

Consultation moves, not classroom teaching. This was a short 10-minute call the prospect had to leave early.

- **Validate the goal, then price it in commitment, not in doubt.** On the 140s to 170s ask: "It's definitely ambitious. Like it's definitely doable. I had similar goals and I was able to come to a similar outcome as to what you're aiming for. But it's going to require quite a bit of commitment."
- **Reframe a job as an asset rather than an obstacle.** Abdullah offers his 30 hours a week as the one thing in his way; Joshua flips it: "That's actually probably... you do want to work a job as well. You don't want to just do the LSAT, because you need to take time to be away from the LSAT, let it sink in, let it cool off, then come in. Versus sometimes people try to do eight hour, ten hour runs and that doesn't improve."
- **Answer the origin-story question with a failure narrative, not a success one.** Asked for his LSAT journey, Joshua leads with the wasted year: "I tried every single resource. I went through 7Sage, went through Loophole, went through PowerScore, also Demon. I was going through everything. Not because it was useful. It was actually very counterproductive. It was the biggest waste of your time. But I was just a person that didn't know better."
- **Preempt the arrogance read before self-praise.** "I was expecting to do pretty well because, without sounding arrogant, I was very inclined toward academics. I was just always good at school." Then immediately undercuts it: "That was not my experience. I expected to steamroll it."
- **The core value proposition, stated as a named problem.** "The biggest thing about people, especially that are going through the test earlier on, is that you don't know what you don't know. If you have an issue you can address that issue. But if you don't know that you don't know, that's the biggest problem, because it's not even on your radar." A tutor's job is to move unknown-unknowns onto the radar: "then you'll be like, wait, I didn't even know that was an issue that I had, then I can start working on it."
- **Sell direction over material.** "If you don't understand what the test is testing on, it's hard to put your head around the type of thinking it requires... You're not just looking at a sea of information. You want a specific repeatable process to have a repeatable outcome."
- **Meet a credential challenge with more transparency than was asked for.** Abdullah asks, apologetically, for proof of the 173. Joshua does not take offense: "Of course. That's a very reasonable question. You just want to be able to verify that." He offers both email and screen share, then does both, sharing his screen live and narrating: "I want to make sure I'm very transparent with you so you can see this... you see that it came from LSAC. In case people like to use photo, I don't know, there's people that do some weird stuff." Then still promises the emailed copy "just to be safe."
- **Accept the small order without upselling.** Abdullah caps himself at two sessions for financial reasons; Joshua's response is "That's great," with no push toward a package.
