# Sofia Minassian, 2026-06-16

Intake consultation, referred by an existing student. Goal setting, scope of work, rate, accommodations, and a first assignment. Roughly 18 minutes.

## LSAT concepts

- **Reading comprehension and logical reasoning are the same underlying skill.** "They're testing an underlying pattern recognition, an underlying ability to recognize structure, reading with structure. You're not reading for fun, you're reading with an intent." RC is the same thinking "fleshed out"; LR is it condensed. He put the retired logic games section in the same family: "it's just a difference in the way it manifests."
- **Question types are symptoms, not diagnoses.** Beneath every named type ("most strongly supported," "point at issue") sits a subskill, and the subskill varies by person: reading within the context of what is stated, reading critically, structural reading. "Usually it's not the question type, it's a symptom of something else that needs to be addressed."

## Teaching moves

- **Pushing back on her all-in study posture before she asks.** Sofia described the LSAT as having consumed her, with "every time I'm not studying, that's time wasted." Joshua did not reward it: "for you it's knowing when to put your foot on the gas and when to pull back. There's a dance there. It's not useful to spend all your time studying, it just becomes diminishing returns and you end up losing more than you gain."
- **Deferring her self-diagnosis plan without dismissing it.** She proposed building a strengths-and-weaknesses chart by question type and handing it to him. He accepted the instinct but reframed the unit of analysis, warning she may be wrong about what she is good at because the labels are semantic. Then he parked it: "that's something we don't have to worry about now."
- **Using his own score history to normalize a slow start.** "You started off at 145. I started off even lower than you did. I was naturally pretty good in school, but the LSAT kind of humbled me. It ended up being a long journey."
- **Reading effort as the predictor.** He kept coming back to her work habits rather than her current score: "if you're able to get a 4.0 in undergrad, the LSAT won't be, you have that ability to work hard, so that's not going to be my worry."
- **A note on how he usually handles accommodations.** "Sometimes I like to keep it from telling students so early on, to force them to prepare with the understanding of regular time, and then when they get an extra boost their score explodes." He explicitly opted out of that here because she is already self-motivated.
- **Removing the stigma around accommodations.** His standard pitch, which he noted he usually has to argue for: it is not counted against you, "it's not even visible to the law schools you're applying to, they don't even know." Sofia needed no convincing, which he flagged as unusual.
