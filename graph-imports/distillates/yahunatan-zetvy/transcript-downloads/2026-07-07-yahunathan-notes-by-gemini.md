## Teaching moves
- **Reframes the score target as a percentile fact, not a number.** "A 160 means you're beating out 75% of lawyers... everybody taking the test already is smart. So you're not just competing with the general public. You're taking the smartest people in a room and you're saying you're smarter than 75% of those people." Used to make an achievable goal feel worthy rather than modest.
- **Counters student self-deprecation about scores:** "People are very hard on themselves. 'I only got a 150.' It's like, 150 means that you're smarter than half a lawyer."
- **Readiness test, stated as a rule:** to know you are ready, take three practice tests and your *lowest* score should be your goal score. "You want to be comfortably over your goal. You don't want to just be at your goal," because "test day can also happen" (bad sleep, life). Aim 163-165 to safely land 160.
- **"Do today's work properly" framing** — his central anti-procrastination move: "Students are so concerned with the end... The work to be done for that score is today. Do today's work properly, and when you do that consistently, you will get to the score." He names the failure mode explicitly: "because they're thinking of themselves in three months, it gives them that level of comfort to say, oh, today doesn't matter... it's just a Tuesday."
- **Refuses to sell a formula.** Asked point-blank whether once or twice a week is better, he declines a generic answer three times: "It's not a factory... there isn't a one-size-fits-all. You have a certain way of learning that is different than others." Explicitly says he wants to read the student's analytics before prescribing.
- **The feedback-loop argument for twice-weekly sessions:** "We do something on Tuesday, you reinforce it Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, we touch on to see everything connected... it's almost like a feedback loop." Against once-weekly: "a lot can be forgotten from what we learned a week before."
- **Asks for the platform data before diagnosing.** Requests the student's Seven Sage URL mid-call, opens the section history live, and says "the more data the better. Numbers are always easy to work with with understanding what you're going to need to first address." Then confirms the read against the student's own account of it.
- **Separates ethic from outcome as a diagnostic:** "As long as the ethic is there, the ability to deliver on a score, that part will come."
- **Accommodations reframe, delivered without pressure.** Yahunatan refused accommodations on pride grounds ("I'm a warrior"). Joshua corrected the premise but respected the choice: accommodations are not reported to schools, "it's not a test you can cheat on with more time." His core argument: "The test is not testing time. As a lawyer, you're not racing to read contracts... I don't want a lawyer who's fast." And the equity point, unprompted: "Often black students and students from minority backgrounds don't know enough about the system to understand how the accommodation system works... you're not even getting accommodated, you're just getting to level ground."
- **Named a lost-money recovery he could have run:** on hearing Yahunatan paid for the June LSAT and did not sit it, "I wish you would have told me, I would have been able to help you get the money back." Also proactively asked about an LSAC fee waiver.
- **Money-barrier offer made explicitly, tied to his own background:** "If there's a financial need, let me know... I don't want a financial barrier at all to be the reason why we don't get to where you get to. I came from a lower socioeconomic background."
