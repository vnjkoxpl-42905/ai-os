## LSAT concepts

- **Weaken by alternative source** — where an author infers a specific origin from a marker, an answer supplying a different producer of that same marker weakens the inference.
- **Term shift / scope of a category** — David's own diagnosis on the oil question: the argument moves between "living organisms" and "plants and animals." "Living organisms" is the broader term and includes bacteria, so evidence of living-organism biomarkers does not establish plant and animal origin.
- **Correlation versus causation** — the flaw named on question two. A survey correlation between sleep duration and illness does not license a causal conclusion that mild sleep deprivation increases the body's defenses.
- **The third factor** — Joshua's framing of the correct Flaw answer: it names the possibility of another factor producing the observed correlation. "You cannot assume a single causal explanation for a correlation when other explanations are possible."
- **Correlation-implies-causation vs. causation-implies-correlation** — the trap on answer D was the reversal: it asserted that causality implies correlation, which is the opposite of the argument's error.
- **Conceding the conclusion** — an answer choice that grants the argument's conclusion rather than exposing its reasoning is not a flaw answer. Basis for eliminating B.
- **Strengthen by locality** — for an argument that two products share an origin, evidence constraining them to the same physical place adds plausibility to the shared-origin explanation.
- **Good enough versus perfect wording** — David wanted "same" and got "small" geographical radius. The lesson taken: an answer that meaningfully limits the alternative (production miles apart) does the strengthening job even when the wording is not what you predicted.

## Worked examples

- **LR, Weaken — the origin of oil.** The author refutes the carbon-deposit theory of oil's origin by pointing to petroleum biomarkers indicating living organisms, concluding oil comes from the remains of plants and animals. David spotted the gap as a term shift: living organisms is broader than plants and animals and could include bacteria. Correct answer **D**: certain strains of bacteria thrive deep inside Earth's crust. Bacteria are living organisms and can account for the biomarkers without plant or animal remains. A, B, C, and E were cut as not touching the core inference or as timeline plays. Joshua added that the bacteria could carry the oil toward the surface, which explains how the biomarkers get there.
- **LR, Flaw — sleep deprivation and illness.** A survey found more illness among people sleeping eight hours than among those sleeping less; the author concludes mild sleep deprivation can be healthy and increases the body's defenses against illness. David named the flaw as correlation-to-causation immediately on reading the stimulus. Correct answer **A**: the argument fails to address that the observed correlation could be caused by another factor. Eliminations: **D** reversed the relationship (causality implies correlation), **C** required a sufficient condition the argument never established, **B** conceded the conclusion rather than exposing the flaw, **E** out. David noted that recognizing the flaw in the stimulus before reading the choices made the elimination pass far more efficient, though he did not feel fully confident in the answer.
- **LR, Strengthen — "Hand Purple" and glass production.** Conclusion: Hand Purple was discovered by an accident of glass production, supported by the two sharing ingredients, process, temperature, and use of lead. David's prephrase was that the argument needs the production to be "united" — the accident has to have happened in the same place. Correct answer **A**: chemical analysis shows most known fragments of both Hand Purple and white glass were produced within a small geographical radius. David flagged that "small" is not "same" but accepted it as sufficient, since it rules out production miles apart. Joshua agreed: it adds plausibility to the author's explanation. Rejections: **B** (use comparison), **C** (technique), **D** (attainable ingredients), **E** (survival rate of artifacts, irrelevant because the argument concerns origins, not what society ended up using or what survived).
