Do not use "door" as a **unit of content** in student-facing copy. The live example is
the Conditional Logic cheat sheet note: "The same content in five doors instead of
eleven." Those five things are sections. The same page then calls them "five steps" and
"one part at a time", so the note contradicts itself and the plain word was already
there.

**Why:** it reads as an AI tell. It also buys nothing - a reader has to translate "door"
back into "section" before the sentence means anything, and the surrounding copy already
uses the plain word.

**How to apply:** say sections, parts, or steps, matching whatever the surrounding page
already calls them. On the LSAT specifically, prefer "parts" or "steps" over "sections"
where "section" could be read as a test section.

**This is not a blanket ban on the word.** Joshua corrected that reading on 2026-09-09:
"not necessarily any time it's being used" - it is about the way it is used. Idioms like
"opening the door to possibility" and adjacency lines like "the conclusion is right next
door" are a different use and are not covered. Developer comments and JSDoc are not
covered at all; the rule is about copy a student reads.
