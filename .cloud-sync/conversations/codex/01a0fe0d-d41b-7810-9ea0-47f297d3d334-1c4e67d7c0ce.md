### Argument Breakdown

**Situation:** Under an HCP, developers get to use land where endangered species live. In return, they protect critical habitat or provide replacement land. Environmentalists like it because they win concessions from developers who would otherwise ignore weak laws. Property owners like it because it beats stricter bans.

**Evaluate:** The last two sentences are the payoff: both sides are satisfied. Each gives something up and each gets something it wants. A law that leaves opposing parties both pleased is a law built to balance their interests.

**Goal:** A principle about laws that reconcile the interests of developers and environmentalists.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Not Described.** Nobody mentions legal battles. The compromise here comes from the design of a law, not from environmentalists trying to stay out of court.

**(B) Not Described.** Developers here give up land and make promises. Nothing says they get to skip laws they find burdensome.

**(C) Opposite.** The HCP serves the interests of both parties, and both are satisfied. This principle says laws shouldn't try to do that.

**(D) Correct.** Developers get to build, environmentalists get habitat protection, and both are pleased. That is a law fashioned to reconcile their interests.

**(E) Too Strong.** "The most effective means" ranks every option for saving species. The passage describes one arrangement that pleases everyone. It never compares it to anything else or says species fare best this way.
