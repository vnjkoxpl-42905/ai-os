**I recommend including these nine teaching points:**

1. **What a sufficient condition is.** A condition that, if true, is enough to establish another condition under the stated rule.

2. **How it fits into a conditional.** A conditional connects two conditions: whenever the sufficient condition holds, the other condition must hold.

3. **What “enough” means.** Enough to establish the particular result in that relationship. The learner should always be able to finish: “This is sufficient **for…**”

4. **Why the result follows.** A case where the sufficient condition holds but the stated result does not would violate the rule. Show this explicitly.

5. **A rule is different from a fact.** Knowing “every square has four sides” does not establish that the shape in front of us is a square. We need that additional fact to apply the rule to it.

6. **Sufficient does not mean the only way.** Other conditions may also establish the result. Explain this with a concrete alternative.

7. **Absence does not establish the opposite result.** If the shape isn’t a square, it could still have four sides. It could also have three. This rule alone doesn’t settle it.

8. **The result does not establish the sufficient condition.** Four sides do not prove square. A rectangle that isn’t a square shows why.

9. **Sufficiency is a relationship, not necessarily a cause.** Being square doesn’t cause a shape to acquire four sides. Knowing it is square tells us something that must already be true.
