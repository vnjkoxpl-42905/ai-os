# Sufficient condition: notes from the supplied Conditional Reasoning HTML (2026-10-12)

Source: `raw/sources/conditional-reasoning-voice-standard/conditional_reasoning_transcription.html`, section "The Sufficient Condition" (read in full). Backbone for content order. Wording and examples in the bootcamp stay original.

## How the source breaks it down, in order

1. **Name it by its place in the sentence.** "The 'if' part of the conditional is called the sufficient condition." Example: If I'm breathing, then air is present.
2. **It may or may not happen.** The sufficient condition could occur but doesn't have to. We only learn what follows if it does.
3. **Absent: ignore the rule.** If it isn't true, the rule says nothing. There might be air or there might not.
4. **Present: the rule must be followed.** If it is true, the second part is 100% certain. The source calls this "activating" the conditional.
5. **Side question, answered in one line:** what does it mean to activate a conditional? The sufficient condition is present.
6. **It is more than the word "if."** A family of words works the same way: if, when(ever), any(time), all, every(time), in order to, people who, each. Table: indicator plus the same example reworded.
7. **What the family has in common:** inclusive, open words. Use that when language gets complex.
8. **Warning:** the words are the hard part. Understand the relationship, do not rely on indicator words alone.
9. **Side question:** what does when(ever) mean? The parentheses mark an optional ending.
