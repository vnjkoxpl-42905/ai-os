## Proposed teaching copy

A necessary condition is a requirement for another condition to be true. To understand it, ask: what has to be true whenever the other condition is true?

If you are enrolled in law school, you are a student.

Being a student is necessary for being enrolled in law school. Every person enrolled in law school must be a student. Someone who is not a student cannot be enrolled in law school.

But being a student does not, by itself, establish law-school enrollment. A history student who is not enrolled in law school meets the requirement without being enrolled. Being a student is required for enrollment, but is not enough to establish it.

Connect to the earlier lesson: being enrolled is the sufficient condition, because it guarantees being a student. Being a student is the necessary condition, because enrollment requires it. They describe the same relationship from two sides.

Boundary: do not say every necessary condition is never sufficient. Some relationships work both ways. Do not teach then as a standalone necessary-condition indicator; infer roles from the whole conditional. Keep existing Group2 inventory separate.

142	<h2>The Art of the If/Then</h2>
143	
144	<p>Conditional reasoning is the art of the <em>if/then</em>. If one thing happens, then another thing must happen. That’s it.</p>
145	
146	<p>Let’s explore our first conditional relationship:</p>
147	
148	<p class="example">If I’m breathing, then air is present.</p>
149	
150	<p>Because you’re a thinking human, you can probably guess what this means at a literal level. If I’m breathing, there’s air around. The core of the relationship is that simple. If we have one thing, we <strong>must</strong> have the other thing. Just like with the Power Players, we have to take this <strong>MUST</strong> seriously. There is no way around this relationship. If you have the first thing, you have to have the second thing.</p>
151	
152	<p>For all the ballyhoo about conditional reasoning, you would think it’s a lot more complicated than that. The key to keeping conditional reasoning simple through its many applications, which we will examine in detail throughout this chapter, is understanding the concept behind it. I want you to intuitively understand the relationship at the heart of conditionals. Once you have that, the verbal idiosyncrasies that separate one conditional from another will be nothing more than blips on the radar.</p>
153	
154	<p>Let’s start by taking our conditional example apart piece by piece.</p>
155	
156	<h3>The Sufficient Condition</h3>
157	
158	<div class="callout">The “if” part of the conditional is called the sufficient condition.</div>
159	
160	<p class="example">If I’m breathing, then air is present.</p>
161	
162	<p>The sufficient condition is a door opener. It’s welcoming. It could occur, but it doesn’t have to. Just like an open door, someone may walk in, but they don’t have to. In the example, I might be breathing or I might not: I could be holding my breath trying to get rid of hiccups or I could be breathing. We don’t know. We just know what will happen if I <em>am</em> breathing.</p>
163	
164	<p><strong>If the sufficient condition is absent, you can completely ignore the conditional statement.</strong> If I’m not breathing, this conditional doesn’t matter at all. There might be air; there might not be air. The conditional wasn’t activated, so who cares? I’m not breathing, so it doesn’t matter.</p>
165	
166	<p><strong>But if the sufficient condition is present, the conditional statement must be followed.</strong> If I am breathing, there absolutely 100% has to be air. The conditional statement gets activated and everything goes according to plan.</p>
167	
168	<div class="note">
169	  <strong>What does it mean to activate a conditional?</strong>
170	  <p>It means the sufficient condition is present! So the conditional must be followed.</p>
171	</div>
172	
173	<p>The sufficient condition extends beyond just the word “if.” There is a whole family of words that work just like “if”; they let you know you have a sufficient condition on your hands.</p>
