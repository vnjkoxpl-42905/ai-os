118	- **Must Be True / Most Supported (462):** Statements > Evaluate > Goal (45%); Statements only (16%). Statements is often a diagram. Evaluate scans for conditional, causal, math and pivot wording and names the inference to make.
119	- **Necessary Assumption (352):** Conclusion > Evidence > Evaluate > Goal (66%). Goal typically offers a linking answer and a defender answer, and says "negate it".
120	- **Weaken (335) / Strengthen (277):** Conclusion > Evidence > Evaluate > Goal (59% / 55%). Evaluate uses "Given that ... how could we argue that ..." or Curious Fact / Alternate Explanation / Plausibility.
121	- **Paradox (213):** "Apparent Paradox > Evaluate" (22%, older tests) or "Given that ... > How can it be that / Why is it that ... > Evaluate > Goal" (about 30% combined, often with Evaluate / Goal merged).
122	- **Sufficient Assumption (193):** Conclusion > Evidence > Evaluate > Goal (53%); Evidence > Conclusion > Evaluate (21%). Goal usually diagrams the missing link as the logic path.
123	- **Principle-Strengthen (181):** Conclusion > Evidence > Evaluate > Goal (48%). Goal is "If evidence, then conclusion" in general terms.
124	- **Principle-Conform (99):** mixed. Conclusion > Evidence > Evaluate > Goal (24%) when the principle is in the answers; Principle > Evaluate > Goal (9%) or Principle > Goal (7%) when it is in the stimulus.
125	- **Main Conclusion (144) / Role (126):** Conclusion > Evidence > Evaluate > Goal (42% / 53%), with an Intermediate Conclusion step in about 8%. Evaluate almost always opens with the reminder that the conclusion usually comes early (first sentence or after a pivot).
