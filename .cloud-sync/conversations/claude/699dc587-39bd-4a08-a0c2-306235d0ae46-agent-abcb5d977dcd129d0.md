65	## How the teacher relates to the reader
66	
67	### The reader is beside the teacher
68	
69	The narrator talks directly to someone doing the work. “Let's,” “you,” and “we” keep the reader involved. The writing seldom steps back to describe what students generally do. It asks this reader to notice, try, decide, or check something now.
70	
71	The reader is treated as capable of understanding ordinary meaning. The teacher supplies support when a technical step is new. In the opening, the source first gives the literal relationship, then asks the reader to take its certainty seriously. Later, it encourages a guess before revealing the contrapositive. These are expectations of participation, not claims that everything should feel easy.
72

76
