97	yields two spans: `working` (her Reading Recalls and Colouring In, text on

99	per ADR 0013; dead air reads only `recalling`.
100	
101	**Before anything else is trusted, prove the clocks agree.** `startMs` is
102	measured from the recorder starting, Deepgram's times from the first sample in
103	the file, and Chrome's WebM duration metadata is unreliable. Assert that the
104	first stimulus's `working` span contains speech and that the last `recalling`
105	span ends before the file does. If they disagree the whole grade is wrong by a
106	constant offset and every stimulus is scored against the wrong words.
107	
108	**3 — Judge.** One model call per stimulus through `gemini-proxy`. All of that
109	stimulus's Details in one call, which is 22 to 26 calls per section rather than
110	426 individual ones. Contract in §5.
111	
112	**4 — Measure.** The code-graded criteria in §6. No model involved.
113	
114	**5 — Write.** One `activity_section_grades` row, service role, plus
115	`status = 'completed'` and `completed_at` on the assignment, which nothing sets
116	today.
117	
118	## 5. The judge contract
119	
120	**Input per call:** the stimulus text, the Details with their `kind`, and what
121	she said (the `working` and `recalling` spans concatenated, in order).
122	
123	**Three questions, not one.** ADR 0018 classifies each Detail `content` (1,133),
124	`structure` (137) or `modality` (24), and the judge is asked a different thing
125	for each:
126	
127	- `content` — did she state this fact? Her own words, any order, any phrasing.
128	- `structure` — is this relationship present in how she told it? "This is a
129	  contrast." is carried by "Glass is different from steel, though". It does not
130	  need naming.
131	- `modality` — did her qualifier survive? "This is hedged as probable." is
132	  carried by a "probably" and lost by a flat assertion.
133	
134	The classification is produced by
135	`system/scripts/activity-centre/classify_details.py` and travels with the
136	content, so the grader reads a `kind` rather than deciding one.
137	
138	**The stimulus is context, not evidence.** The judge sees it so it knows what a
139	Detail refers to, and is told explicitly that the verdict is about her words
140	alone. The failure mode is the judge reasoning "the stimulus says this, so she
141	must have meant it", and it is the only way this call can be wrong in the
142	direction that flatters her.
143	
144	**Decoys catch that failure per run.** Two Details from a different stimulus in
145	the same section are mixed into each call and must come back missed. A carried
146	decoy flags the stimulus for review rather than silently inflating Coverage.
147	Two extra judgements on twenty is a rounding error against the call's cost.
148	
149	**Output:** a verdict per Detail, plus the two named failure modes from ADR
150	0012 — Reciting and Listing — as booleans with a one-line reason, and whether
151	she coloured the stimulus and whether the colour was specific to it.
152	
153	**Model:** `google/gemini-2.5-flash` through the proxy, matching
154	`activity-coach`. The calls are independent and should run concurrently.
155	
156	## 6. What code grades, not the model
157	
158	**Reciting.** `longestSharedRun` and `bigramOverlap` already exist in
159	`recall/overlap.ts`, written for exactly this and currently used only by the v1
160	runner. Compare what she said against the stimulus text. High overlap is the
161	failure. Its own header makes the point that own-words comparison is the one
162	text comparison in RRC that means what it looks like, because the vocabulary
163	genuinely is shared.
164

174	abandoned midway writes nothing today, so a partial record means something went

# 0009 — Read, Recall, Colour is a runner, not a world

Date: 2026-08-29
Status: accepted

## Context

Read, Recall, Colour is an Activity: it has a slug, a bank, an `ACTIVITIES`
entry, a generated JSON and an `activities` row like the other eight. The
obvious place to put it is therefore the world registry, where every other
Activity gets its surface, and the spec said so
(`docs/specs/2026-08-29-read-recall-colour.md`, step 3: "the RRC world").

Reading the registry's own contract says otherwise. `registry.tsx` states what a
world is and is not:

> What a world replaces is the LEFT column of the running screen, the cover
> rail. The answer box, the Coach and the verdict loop on the right are
> untouched: a world is the surface an Activity is worked on, not a different
> way of working it.

`WorldBinding.render` takes one `ActivityItem` plus the response the student has
typed for it, and returns one element. `ActivityRunner` puts that beside
`ActivityQuestionStack`, which owns the textarea, the attempt counter, the Coach
call and the verdict.

RRC breaks every assumption in that sentence:

- **There is no typed answer.** She speaks. The textarea, its label, its
  placeholder and its draft persistence have nothing to hold.
- **The unit is not the item.** One RRC item is a whole stimulus, worked as a
  sequence of chunks, so a single item produces many captures, many Coach calls
  and many feedback moments. `render(item, response)` has no way to express
  "chunk 3 of 5, recall phase".
- **The screen is not two columns.** During Reading Recall the screen must carry
  nothing readable at all. A world that hid the answer column would be reaching
  outside itself to do it.
- **The run has phases the stack does not model**: read, hide, recall, feedback,
  colour, and periodic cumulative rounds over everything so far.
- **Advancing is gated on something other than a verdict.** Colouring In must
  happen and is never judged, which is not a state `Verdict` can carry.

Forcing this through `WorldBinding` would mean a `render` that ignores its
`response` argument, a question stack rendered and then hidden, and a phase
machine smuggled into a component whose contract says it draws a cover rail.

## Decision

**RRC gets its own runner.** `ActivityRunner` selects it on
`item_format === "spoken-recall"` and hands off the whole running screen. The
world registry is not touched and gains no RRC entry.

What is reused, deliberately:

- the assignment record, its snapshot, its RLS and its resume behaviour
- `activity_item_responses` for persistence, extended rather than replaced
- the `activity-coach` edge function and its `ideas`/`covered` checklist, which
  is already the per-detail mechanism RRC needs
- the Activity Centre's assignment-gated entry, so a teacher assigns RRC the
  same way as anything else

What is not reused: `ActivityQuestionStack`, `AnswerPanel`, the world registry,
and the item-grained attempt loop.

The session itself is modelled as a pure module (`recall/session.ts`) with no
React in it: the round sequence, the phase machine, the advance rules and the
union-of-details scoring for cumulative rounds are decided there and tested
there. The components render a session; they do not compute one.

## Consequences

**`item_format` becomes load-bearing.** It was a display string
(`TeacherActivities` prints it beside the item count) and is now the switch that
picks a runner. The column is a plain `string` in the database, so this is
additive, but a typo in a seed now shows a student the wrong player rather than
the wrong label.

**RRC stays out of the seed library until the runner exists.** Its slug is
deliberately absent from `ORDER` in `build_seed_migration.py` and from
`SEED_ACTIVITIES` in `content/index.ts`. An Activity in the teacher library can
be assigned, and an assignable Activity with no runner is a student stuck on a
blank screen.

**A second runner is now a real possibility rather than a hack.** Anything whose
answering unit is smaller than an item, or whose medium is not text, has a
precedent to follow instead of a world contract to bend.

**The cost is duplication.** Progress, exit, resume and the completion summary
exist once for the item-grained path and will need equivalents here. That is
accepted: the alternative was one component serving two genuinely different
shapes, which is how `AnswerPanel` came to ask Conditional Rephrase students for
a negation.

## Alternatives considered

**Make it a world and let it hide the answer column.** Rejected. A world would
have to reach outside its own frame to suppress a sibling, and `render`'s
signature still could not say which chunk the student is on.

**Widen `WorldBinding` to carry a phase and a sub-index.** Rejected. That widens
a contract eight working worlds depend on, to serve one Activity that shares
none of the rest of it.

**Ship RRC as a standalone route outside the Activity Centre.** Rejected. It
would lose assignment, the roster, the snapshot and the teacher's view of
results, which is most of the reason it lives in the platform at all.

## Amendment — 2026-09-04 — the launch surface is a component, not a world either

The runner needed a page in front of it: what the section is, what the method
asks, how her last few Attempts went, and the control that starts the run. The

It is not, for two reasons. The first is this ADR: a world replaces the cover
rail of a *running* screen, and this page exists before anything is running.
The second is plainer — `worlds/chamber/` already exists and is a different
Activity's surface. Shipping a second Chamber would have put the word on two
unrelated things in one module.

**The launch surface is `SectionLaunch`**, a component chosen inside
`ActivityIntro`, namespaced `.sl-` in CSS. The name "Chamber" stays in
`docs/design-previews/rrc-section-launch-chamber.html` as the mockup's name and
is used nowhere in code.

`ActivityIntro` became a hook-free wrapper to do this, not an early return.
It called four hooks near the top, so a `mode === "section"` return above them
would have been a conditional hook. The old body moved verbatim into
`ActivityEditorialIntro`; the wrapper picks between the two. This is scoped to
section mode: the eight typed Activities and the section-library door still get
the editorial intro, unchanged.

**Consequence.** `ActivityIntro` is now a two-line switch and carries no logic
of its own. Anything added to "the intro" has to pick a side, which is the
point: the two surfaces share a route and a prop, and nothing else.

# 0010 — A Read, Recall, Colour run is a section, not a stimulus

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: accepted
Supersedes nothing. Extends [0009](./0009-read-recall-colour-is-a-runner-not-a-world.md).

## Context

ADR 0009 gave RRC its own runner because its answering unit is smaller than an
Item: one Item is a whole stimulus worked as a sequence of chunks. That was
correct and is unchanged.

It was also incomplete. The Activity was built around a single stimulus because
the calibration fixture had one, and the v1 spec's content pipeline says
"stimuli come from the existing bank" without saying how many constitute a run.

The source method has an answer, and it is not one. Its drill card and its
foundation routine are written about **a section**:

- the time goal is 20 minutes, and it is a goal for a section
- the goal is valid only if *every* stimulus in the section was recalled,
  coloured and given a Read
- the recording is one file, started before the first stimulus and never paused
  or stopped until the last
- the prescribed volume is three or more consecutive sections a day
- review happens in a separate sitting, never between stimuli

Every one of those is a statement about a unit larger than the Item the runner
currently models. A run of one stimulus cannot be timed against a 20-minute
goal, cannot carry an unbroken recording that spans anything, and cannot be
reviewed "afterwards" in any meaningful sense.

There is a second forcing fact. Two questions frequently share one stimulus —
PT105.S2 has two such pairs, so 25 questions are 23 stimuli. If the unit were
the question, she would recall the same dialogue twice in one run.

## Decision

**The unit of an RRC run is one Logical Reasoning section.** An RRC Activity is
a section; its items are that section's distinct stimuli, collapsed so a shared
stimulus appears once; and the run's contract — timer, recording, completion,
scoring — belongs to the section rather than to any item inside it.

Three things follow, and each is a real change:

**The coach leaves the loop.** Nothing is judged or shown until the section
ends. This reverses the behaviour `useRecallCoach` implements today. It is not
a performance concession: a live verdict teaches her to vet an utterance before
speaking it, which is precisely the habit the colour stage exists to break, and
it cannot coexist with a recording that never pauses.

**`cumulative_after` and `credited_answers` go empty.** The drill has no
cumulative rounds; The Read at the end of each stimulus is the synthesis, and a
cumulative round would be a second one. `credited_answers` exists only to be the
cumulative round's reference, so with no round it references nothing. The Read
is scored against the captured key instead.

**Scoring The Read becomes possible.** v1 deferred it as "content work that
would block shipping." The key was captured on 2026-08-30: 5,015 typed Reads,
one per LR question. The blocker is gone, not worked around.

## Consequences

**A second content shape.** Section content carries `mode`, `time_goal_minutes`,
`source_section`, and per item `the_read`, `model_recording`, `questions[]`.
Single-stimulus RRC content stays valid, which matters because the existing
fixture is what the runner's tests are written against.

**Section mode is gated on recording coverage.** Each stimulus offers a model
recording in review, so a section missing one shows a dead play button. Only 5
captured sections have complete coverage, so those are the ones that ship. This
is a content constraint, not a code one, and it lifts as coverage grows.

**The dead-air check needs windowing.** She reads silently and speaks only the
recall and the colour, so silence during a read is correct behaviour. A naive
silence detector would fail every chunk. The windowing is not optional polish;
without it the check is worse than absent.

**Two runners' worth of surface, still one runner.** Section mode is the same
`spoken-recall` runner with a section-scoped session, not a third path. ADR 0009
warned that duplication was the accepted cost of a second runner; this decision
declines to pay it a second time.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep the stimulus as the unit and let a teacher assign 23 of them.** Rejected.
The timer, the unbroken recording and the "every stimulus" condition are all
section-scoped; 23 assignments would produce 23 recordings and 23 clocks, which
is the opposite of what the drill requires.

**Model the section in the Drill engine instead.** Rejected, and the glossary
rejects it first: a Drill is a timed run over multiple-choice questions pulled
by `qid`, and an Activity is not a Drill. This run shows no answer choices and
never asks her to pick one. Joshua was explicit that regular Drills and regular
sections stay untouched.

**Score The Read with an authored key per stimulus.** Rejected as unnecessary
now. It was the v1 plan only because no key existed; a captured key covering
every LR question is both cheaper and more faithful than anything we would
write.

# 0011 — An oval is a question, not a verdict

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: Accepted

## Context

A student who builds their own LR set could only move forward through it. A full
section has always had the LawHub bottom strip — one oval per question, click any
of them to jump — and a built set had nothing equivalent, so the questions the
student chose could only be walked in the order they came out.

Two things were actually wrong, and only one of them was missing work:

1. `Drill.tsx` had been passing `onPrev` and `onAdvance` to `OfficialSmartDrillBar`
   for some time, but the bar never rendered those pills. The handlers were
   received and dropped. This was a bug, not a gap.
2. The oval strip genuinely did not exist for a built set.

Fixing (2) raised a question a section never had to answer. A section never
reveals, so its ovals could only ever mean "answered". A built set *does* reveal:
the moment a question is revealed the app knows whether the student got it right,
and that information could be shown on the oval — green for correct, red for
missed, jump straight to your misses.

## Decision

The strip stays exactly as rich as the section's. An oval carries three states and
no more: **current**, **answered**, **jumpable**. It never shows whether the answer
was right, in a set or anywhere else.

The reason is what an oval *is*. One oval stands for one **question** — a place to
go. It does not stand for an answer choice, even though on paper the bubble you
fill in is precisely your answer. Colouring ovals by correctness quietly recasts a
navigation control as a scorecard, and then the strip is doing two jobs and reads
as neither.

The strip is extracted into `AnswerOvals` so the section and the built set render
one component rather than two copies that drift apart.

Jumping commits any pending selection before it moves, for the same reason
Previous does: leaving a question must never be the thing that loses the answer on
it.

## Consequences

- A built set now navigates like a section: ovals, Previous, Next, free movement.
- Wanting a right/wrong overview after a set is a real want, and it now has an
  obvious home that is *not* the ovals — the post-set review screen, where being a
  scorecard is the job.
- `AnswerOvals` is shared, so a change to oval appearance lands in both surfaces at
  once. That is the point, and it also means neither surface can quietly diverge.
- Rejected: colouring on reveal, and colouring only after the set ends. Both were
  offered and both were declined on the grounds above.

# 0012 — The Recall is a recap from memory, not a takeaway

Date: 2026-09-01
Status: accepted
Extends [0010](./0010-rrc-section-is-the-unit-of-work.md).

## Context

ADR 0010 decided that scoring The Read had become possible, because the key was
captured on 2026-08-30: 5,015 typed analytical takeaways, one per LR question.
The runner was built to match. `SectionPhase` is `read | speak | the-read`, all
four chunks stay on screen through the final phase, and she says one sentence
against that key.

Working the drill through from the student's side says that is the wrong end of
a stimulus to test. Nothing in the run ever checks whether she still holds the
argument. She can see every chunk while she produces her takeaway, so the one
faculty the method is built to train — carrying a stimulus without the text in
front of her — is the one faculty the activity never exercises.

The source method has the missing step. Proto-Stimulus Rebound's step 2 is
"cover the stimulus and say the full Translation from memory," and its recap
standard is explicit: own words are fine, reordering is encouraged, and
"efficiency is never a licence to leave information out." Names, dates and lists
are never dropped.

## Decision

**At the end of a stimulus the text is removed and she says the whole argument
back from memory. That is The Recall, renamed here from The Read.** No analytical takeaway is spoken and none
is scored.

The stimulus does not return once she has recapped it, she is shown nothing
about how she did, and the next stimulus follows automatically.

The Recall is graded on **Coverage**: how many of the stimulus's authored Details
she carried. ([0013](./0013-she-chunks-freely-and-the-app-does-not-track-it.md)
later widened this to count everything she said about the stimulus, not the
recap alone.) PT102.S3.Q1 has 21 across its four chunks. Grading applies
the source's standard rather than a similarity threshold — the only failure is
omission — and names the two ways a full-Coverage recap can still be wrong:
**Reciting** it close to word-for-word, and **Listing** it as flat facts rather
than a story.

## Consequences

**The captured key goes unused.** 5,015 rows and `the_read` / `the_read_type` on
every item stay in the content and are read by nothing. They are the whole cost
of this decision, and they are why it is worth recording: a future reader will
find a fully captured answer key wired to nothing and assume it was an
oversight.

**The takeaway is deferred, not rejected.** Joshua was explicit that analysis is
a later level. Turning it on needs no content work and no new capture; the key
is already there and already typed.

**Step 4 is renamed.** "The Read" was named after the takeaway it no longer
contains, and it sat one definite article away from step 1's **Read**. Step 4 is
now **The Recall**, which says what it is and pairs with **Reading Recall**, the
per-chunk version of the same act. The `the-read` phase in `section.ts` and the
`the_read` content fields still carry the old name and are the rename's cost.

**Section goals move.** A ~15-second recap on every stimulus replaces a
~3-second sentence, so the standard goal rises from 20 to 25 minutes and scales
by the student's real accommodation multiplier (50% extra, double time) rather
than by a smaller bump of its own.

**Each stimulus yields one number.** With no takeaway there is no second score
to blend or show, so the Report Card carries a Coverage percentage per stimulus
plus tips.

**Grading is a post-section job.** Roughly 92 Detail judgements per section, all
of them after she finishes, run server-side once the section ends rather than in
her tab.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep the takeaway and score it against the key.** Rejected. The recap has a
checkable rule and the takeaway has a judgement call, and mixing them makes the
memory drill's score depend on an analysis verdict Joshua does not want yet.

**Keep the takeaway and capture it unscored.** Offered and declined. It would
have cost nothing to store, but it puts an ungraded artifact on the Report Card
in a version whose whole subject is memory.

**Do both acts: takeaway with the text up, then a recap with it hidden.**
Rejected. Two acts per stimulus pushes a 22-stimulus section past 30 minutes
before any thinking time, and the takeaway half is the half being deferred.

# 0013 — She chunks freely; the app does not track where she is

Date: 2026-09-01
Status: accepted
Extends [0012](./0012-the-recall-is-a-recap-from-memory.md).

## Context

The section runner carries a cursor that walks chunk by chunk through a
`read` then `speak` phase, and a rail that highlights the chunk she is on.
`chunkStates` exists to render it. The grain is ours: PT102.S3.Q1 is cut into
four chunks holding 8, 5, 4 and 4 Details.

Two things are wrong with holding her to that grain.

She may not chunk the way we cut. Taking the first two sentences together is a
legitimate way to work the drill, and a cursor that expects otherwise scores her
for disagreeing with our line breaks rather than for what she retained.

And the cursor cannot be driven without her telling it where she is. She is
silent through every Read, so silence carries no information — a pause while
reading and a pause after finishing are the same signal. That leaves tapping
once per chunk, which puts roughly 90 taps in a 25-minute section and desyncs
the whole recording if one is mistimed.

## Decision

**The app does not track which chunk she is on.** The full stimulus is on screen
throughout, she reads, recalls and colours in whatever units she likes, and the
recording runs unbroken. Which parts she covered is worked out afterwards from
what she said, not from where a cursor was pointing.

**Coverage is therefore a per-stimulus number.** All 21 of PT102.S3.Q1's Details,
counted across everything she said about that stimulus — her Reading Recalls,
her Colouring In and her Recall together — rather than per Chunk. A Chunk stays
the unit content is authored in and a guide for her eye; it is not a boundary
she is graded against.

**One key, pressed twice per stimulus.** She presses **Recall Full Stimulus**
when she is ready, and the text vanishes. She presses it again when her Recall
is finished, and the next stimulus loads. Those two moments are the only ones
not recoverable from the audio, and stimulus-level segmentation comes from
knowing what was on screen when.

**Nothing is revisitable.** A stimulus she has passed does not come back until
the Report Card at the end of the section.

## Consequences

**The chunk cursor and its rail come out.** `chunkStates`, the `read`/`speak`
phase pair and the per-chunk advance rules lose their purpose. What replaces
them is a stimulus-grained cursor with two states: working, and recalling.

**She loses her place marker.** The rail told her how much of the stimulus was
left against the clock, and nothing replaces it. Accepted: the stimulus is four
sentences on screen in front of her, which is its own marker.

**Grading gets harder and content gets easier.** Attributing an utterance to a
Detail is now the grader's problem rather than something the cursor answered for
free. In exchange, content no longer has to cut chunks at boundaries she will
agree with, because she is never held to them.

## Alternatives considered

**One tap per chunk to move the rail, ungraded.** Offered and declined. It keeps
a control on screen whose only job is to be ignored by the grader, and a
mistimed tap still desyncs the display from the recording.

**Advance on silence after the Recall instead of a second press.** Rejected once
the press existed anyway. A second press on the same key costs nothing and
removes a timeout that would cut her off mid-sentence if she paused to think.

# 0014 — The section goal is calibrated, and accommodations scale it

Date: 2026-09-01
Status: accepted
Extends [0010](./0010-rrc-section-is-the-unit-of-work.md).

## Context

The source method states its time goals precisely, and section mode shipped
exactly them: **20 minutes** standard, **25** at a 53-minute accommodation,
**30** at a 70-minute one. They are in the drill card, restated in the foundation
routine, and transcribed into the spec's "as captured" table.

Two problems have shown up now that whole sections are actually being run.

**20 minutes is not what a real section takes.** The recorded runs land nearer
25. A goal nobody reaches is not a goal; it is a clock that reads failure every
time, and the drill's own gate ("the goal is valid only if every stimulus was
recalled, coloured and given a Read") makes hitting it by cutting work
impossible on purpose. The number has to describe the work.

**The accommodated goals do not scale.** An accommodation is extra time on the
same 35-minute section: 53 minutes is 53/35 of one and 70 minutes is two of
them. The source's numbers move by a flat +5 and +10, so a student with double
the time is given a goal only half again as long. Her accommodation is scored as
slowness, which is the one thing three numbers exist to prevent.

## Decision

**The standard goal is 25 minutes**, calibrated to what a section actually takes
rather than inherited from the source's 20.

**The accommodated goals are derived, never authored.** Each is the standard goal
times that accommodation's own multiplier, rounded half up:

| Accommodation | Multiplier | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| none (35 min) | 1 | 25 |
| 53 min | 53/35 | 38 |
| 70 min | 70/35 | 50 |

`build_section_content.py` computes them from `STANDARD_GOAL_MINUTES` and stamps
them into every section it assembles, so a future change to the standard goal
carries the accommodated ones with it and cannot leave them stale.

## Consequences

**We have departed from the source method on a stated number.** Deliberately, and
this ADR is the record of it. The spec's §2 table still transcribes the source's
20 / 25 / 30, because that section documents what was captured rather than what
we ship; it now points here.

**Every built section is re-seeded.** The goal lives in `time_goal_minutes` on
each section's content JSON, so PT102.S3, PT105.S2 and PT134.S3 were rebuilt and
a fresh seed migration emitted. Sections built later inherit it from the script.

**Where the numbers are pinned is now split, on purpose.** The content fixture
test asserts the values and the multiplier. The machine's own test reads the goal
off the content instead of writing it out, since what it checks is that the right
key is picked and turned into seconds. Moving the goal again should touch one
script and one fixture test.

**A Report Card that scores time has a fair clock to score against.** It did not
before, for anyone with an accommodation.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep 20 and treat the gap as something to train down to.** The foundation
routine has its own mechanism for this: start at your real time, move to the
midpoint, tighten. That is a per-student schedule, not the section's goal, and
encoding it as one fixed number for everyone is what the routine specifically
does not do.

**Author all three numbers, corrected by hand.** Rejected. Three authored numbers
is three chances to drift, and the relationship between them is the part that
matters and is exactly what an authored list forgets.

98	# 0016 — A drill link names a run, it does not contain one

100	Date: 2026-09-01

103	## Context
104	
105	Seventeen places start a drill. Nine build a URL; eight hand the drill to
106	`/drill` in `location.state` and put nothing in the address bar. Refresh one of
107	those eight and the drill is gone: a saved set, a Smart Drill, a Testing Centre
108	section. `ptRun` is worse than gone — it never went through the resolver at all
109	(`Drill.tsx:202`), so a refreshed practice-test leg loses the run id it needs to
110	write its close-out, and that section can never be marked finished.
111	
112	`getResolvedDrillRouteState` reads both transports into one shape but only one
113	way. Nothing turns a launch back into a URL, so the URL grammar is whatever each
114	caller happened to build, and the two can disagree: several links carry an
115	assignment id *and* a copy of that assignment's question list.
116	
117	The obvious fix is to put the questions in the URL. That is the wrong fix. There
118	is already a durable per-student Run Record holding the ordered question list,
119	every attempt, the position and the clock spent, and on resume the runner adopts
120	that stored queue whole rather than re-shuffling (`Drill.tsx:786`). Refresh
121	already returns the identical run. The only thing refresh destroys is the app's
122	ability to work out *which run the student was in*.
123	
124	Separately, `mode` is not the kind. Six materially different things all launch as
125	`mode=type-drill`, told apart only by which extra flag rides along
126	(`guidedAssignmentId`, `assignmentId`, `guidedSetId`, `practiceSlug`, `present`).
127	
128	## Decision
129	
130	A **Drill Launch** is carried in the URL and nowhere else, and it carries the
131	*name* of a run, not its contents. `location.state` stops being a transport.
132	
133	The launch is one discriminated union over nine kinds: Smart Drill, Section,
134	Saved set, Assignment, Guided drill, Practice set, Bootcamp day, Questions (an
135	ad-hoc list), and Filter. A practice-test leg is a **Section with a run
136	attached**, not its own kind: it runs, looks and closes out identically, and the
137	only difference is that something is waiting on the result.
138	
139	Anything that has an id is named by that id alone. `?set=abc123` and not the six
140	qids inside it, so editing a set cannot leave an old link running last week's
141	questions. Only the ad-hoc list and the filter put a payload in the link, because
142	they have no row to point at. Guided drills and assignments already resolve their
143	own questions (`guidedDrills.ts:82`), so nothing is lost.
144	
145	Switches are split rather than pooled: the cross-cutting ones (back link,
146	heading, which question to open on) sit alongside the kind, and the rest live
147	inside the kind that owns them (sealed and timing on a Section, duration on a
148	Guided drill). A switch that does not belong to its kind is **rejected** when it
149	would change what the student experiences, and ignored when it is decoration. A
150	sealed section that silently is not sealed is a broken measurement; a missing
151	heading is not.
152	
153	`launchDrill()` and `parseDrillLaunch()` are a matched pair and round-trip.
154	
155	## Consequences
156	
157	- A dead link (deleted assignment, deleted set, another student's set) shows an
158	  error with a way back, rather than falling back to a generic drill. A student
159	  quietly drilling something nobody assigned is worse than a student reporting a
160	  broken link.

252	# 0017 — The grader hears the run through the same ear as the standard
253	
254	Date: 2026-09-01
255	Status: accepted
256	Amended: 2026-09-04 (see "Amendment: the title was a claim the code did not honour")
257	Extends [0013](./0013-she-chunks-freely-and-the-app-does-not-track-it.md).
258	
259	## Context
260	
261	A finished section leaves two recordings of what she said, and they are not
262	equivalent.
263	
264	The first is the audio: one unbroken file per section, `getUserMedia` into a
265	`MediaRecorder` started once on mount and stopped once at the end
266	(`useSectionRecorder.ts`), uploaded to `drill-recordings` at
267	`<student_id>/<assignment_id>.webm`. Nothing in the app reads it back.
268	
269	The second is text: `useSectionTranscript.ts` runs the browser's
270	`SpeechRecognition` continuously through the section and slices it by the same
271	phase boundaries, and that slice is what lands in
272	`activity_item_responses.response_text` and `rrc.transcript_segments`, tagged
273	`transcript_source: "browser-speech"`.
274	
275	The cheap move is to grade the text that is already there. Three things rule it
276	out.
277	
278	`SpeechRecognition` is a Chrome interface. In Safari and Firefox it does not
279	exist, `said` is empty, and the writer stores `response_text: ""` with
280	`transcript_source: null` (it stored `null` until ADR 0027, and a null in a
281	NOT NULL column meant the row was never saved at all). The section still
282	records audio, still writes its row, and produces no text to grade — a silent,
283	browser-dependent hole rather than an error.
284	
285	It carries no usable timing. Dead air is a graded criterion and it is only
286	meaningful inside The Recall, where silence is unambiguously a failure rather
287	than a silent read. Measuring it needs word-level timestamps.
288	
289	And it is a different ear from the one that read the standard. The 1,294
290	Details were authored and calibrated against Joshua's own runs, and those runs
291	were re-transcribed on Deepgram Nova-3 on 2026-09-01 precisely because the local
292	Whisper model paraphrased away the hesitations and false starts that are the
293	content. Grading a student on a weaker transcript than the reference does not
294	make the grader lenient; it makes it wrong in one direction, dropping her words
295	before any judgement is made and charging the loss to her Coverage.
296	
297	## Decision
298	
299	**The graded transcript is produced server-side by Deepgram Nova-3 from the
300	uploaded recording.** The same engine that read the standard reads the run.
301	
302	**The browser transcript stays, and is never graded.** It costs nothing, it is
303	the only text that exists if the upload fails, and it is useful as a diagnostic
304	when a Coverage score is disputed. It is stored as what it is, and the grade
305	records which transcript it was computed from.
306	
307	**Segmentation is not an audio problem.** `rrc.audio_segments` already carries
308	`{slug, order, phase, startMs, endMs}` for every `working` and `recalling`
309	window, written from the two presses of the Space key. Deepgram's word
310	timestamps are cut against those offsets. Nothing has to infer stimulus
311	boundaries from the audio, which is the whole reason ADR 0013's second press
312	exists.
313	
314	## Consequences
315	
316	**One new secret.** `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY` in the edge function environment. It is
317	the first ASR dependency the app has had; every LLM call still goes through
318	`gemini-proxy`, and this does not change that.
319	
320	**Grading is asynchronous and the Report Card has a pending state.** A 25-minute
321	section is one transcription plus roughly 25 judgement calls, one per stimulus.
322	It does not complete inside the request that finishes the section, and it must
323	not be tied to the tab staying open.
324	
325	**Cost is not a constraint.** The whole 714-file corpus transcribed for $6.62.
326	A section is about $0.11.
327

329	is known to write WebM containers with unreliable duration metadata, and
330	`startMs` is measured from the recorder starting rather than from the first
331	sample landing in the file. If the two clocks disagree, every stimulus boundary
332	shifts and Coverage is computed against the wrong span. The first thing the
333	grader proves on real audio is that a known utterance lands inside the window
334	that claims it.
335

337	`student_shaped.py` to remove the verbatim reads the student never speaks, are

404	# 0018 — Full Coverage is not 100%
405	
406	Date: 2026-09-01
407	Status: accepted
408	Amended: 2026-09-04 (see "Amendment: a recital wore the top band anyway")
409	Extends [0012](./0012-the-recall-is-a-recap-from-memory.md).
410	
411	## Context
412	
413	ADR 0012 made Coverage the score: how many of a stimulus's authored Details she
414	carried, as one number per stimulus. It did not say what number is good, and the
415	obvious reading of a percentage is that 100 is the target.
416	
417	That reading is measurably false. Joshua's own recordings are the drill done
418	correctly, and they are the standard the Details were calibrated against. Put
419	through `student_shaped.py`, which removes the verbatim reads a student never
420	speaks, **all 71 stimuli of the three built sections were scored Detail by
421	Detail** — a census, not a sample, of 1,294 judgements:
422	
423	| | Coverage |
424	|---|---|
425	| Mean | 94.7% |
426	| Median | 95% |
427	| 10th percentile | 86% |
428	| Minimum | 68% |
429	| Maximum | 100% |
430	| Runs at 100% | 22 of 71 |
431	
432	He reaches a clean sweep on fewer than a third of them, and the distribution has
433	a real left tail: two runs below 83%, and one at 68%.
434	
435	The two lowest are both explained rather than mysterious. PT134.S3.Q16's
436	recording starts mid-sentence, so part of the stimulus was never spoken at all;
437	it measures the recording. PT134.S3.Q1 ends in a fill-in-the-blank conclusion
438	("it would be reasonable for organizations… to _______"), which he answers in
439	four words, so a whole chunk's Details go uncarried. Excluding those two, the
440	range is 79 to 100 with a median of 96.
441	
442	He is not sloppy and the key is not wrong. The calibration method already
443	settled this: "his own recall drops details, and that is not a defect… his run
444	is not a ceiling on the key." What is new is the shape of the distribution and
445	what sits in the misses. Across the census they are, in order of frequency:
446	**a small specific** — an adjective, a place, a time, a material ("the water is
447	seawater", "the stalagmites are numerous", "the announcement was earlier
448	today"); **a quantifier or hedge** ("the devices would go in all cars", "it is
449	generally so", "this is stated with certainty"); and **a speaker role or
450	discourse marker** ("Tom is the first speaker", "a lawyer is speaking").
451	
452	Those are the exact words the drill exists to train. Coverage is measuring the
453	right thing; the scale is what is wrong.
454	
455	A second measurement bears on how the judgement is asked. Classifying all 1,294
456	Details by what they actually assert
457	(`system/scripts/activity-centre/classify_details.py`) splits them three ways,
458	not two:
459	
460	| Kind | Count | Share | Example |
461	|---|---|---|---|
462	| content | 1,133 | 87.6% | "The commitment is long-standing." |
463	| structure | 137 | 10.6% | "This is the conclusion." · "This is a contrast." · "An archaeologist is speaking." |
464	| modality | 24 | 1.9% | "This is hedged as probable." · "This is an average." · "This is almost always so." |
465	
466	A **structure** Detail is carried by how she says the stimulus back, not by which
467	words she uses. Joshua carries "This is a contrast." by saying "Glass is
468	different from steel, though"; he carries "This is the conclusion." with a "so".
469	A judge asked *did she say this?* marks both missed. A judge asked *is this
470	relationship present in her retelling?* marks both carried.
471	
472	A **modality** Detail is neither a fact nor a relationship but the strength of a
473	claim, and it is the class his runs drop most.
474	
475	The three-way split is itself the finding. A two-way content/structure cut looks
476	sufficient until it is run: "This is about Sweden." and "This is the conclusion."
477	share an opening and are opposites, and a classifier keying on that opening puts
478	a topic fact in with the discourse roles. 12.4% of every score turns on asking
479	the right question of the right kind.
480	
481	## Decision
482	
483	**Coverage is reported against a band calibrated on the standard, never as a
484	raw percentage against an implied 100.**
485	
486	| Band | Coverage | What it means |
487	|---|---|---|
488	| At the standard | 86% and above | The run the drill is asking for |
489	| Nearly there | 72–85% | Holding the argument, losing the detail |
490	| Thin | 55–71% | The shape survived and the content did not |
491	| Lost it | below 55% | Read it again |
492	
493	**Only the top boundary is evidenced, and it is the 10th percentile of the
494	census** — nine correct runs in ten clear it. It is deliberately not the minimum:
495	the minimum of any sample is the most fragile statistic available, and here it is
496	a stimulus whose recording is broken.
497	
498	**The three lower cutoffs are provisional and are marked as such.** The corpus
499	contains only correct runs, so it can calibrate where good ends and nothing else.
500	Where "nearly there" stops being nearly and starts being thin is unknown until
501	student runs exist, and inventing precision there would be dressing a guess as a
502	measurement.
503	
504	**Each kind is judged by its own question.** Details are classified `content`,
505	`structure` or `modality` at build time, and the judge is asked: for content,
506	whether she stated the fact; for structure, whether the relationship is present
507	in how she told it; for modality, whether her qualifier survived. The
508	classification lives with the build, so a section carries it and the grader
509	never has to guess.
510	
511	**The classifier is a closed set plus a residue detector, not a heuristic.**
512	`structure` and `modality` are matched against authored phrasings that are
513	enumerated, and anything opening like a discourse role that falls through to
514	`content` is reported rather than silently accepted. On the three built sections
515	the closed set leaves 4 residue lines out of 1,294, and all four are correctly
516	content. A new section's residue is read before it ships; a growing residue
517	means the authoring vocabulary has drifted, which is worth knowing on its own.
518	
519	**Numbers above the top band are not ranked.** 100% and 88% are the same
520	outcome. The Report Card says a run is at the standard and moves on to what she
521	missed; it does not put her in a race against an unreachable maximum, and it
522	does not congratulate a difference the measurement cannot support.
523	
524	## Consequences
525	
526	**ADR 0012's estimate of the grading job is corrected.** It records "roughly 92
527	Detail judgements per section". The three built sections carry **426, 419 and
528	449** Details. 92 is approximately the *chunk* count (80, 82, 87), which is what
529	the number was when Coverage was still conceived per chunk; ADR 0013 moved
530	Coverage to the stimulus and the estimate did not follow. The job is about five
531	times the recorded size. It stays about 25 model calls, because the Details of
532	one stimulus are judged together.
533	
534	**Reciting and Listing become load-bearing, not decorative.** With the top band
535	starting at 86%, a student who recites reaches it trivially. ADR 0012 already
536	names both failures and puts them in the tips rather than the number. That
537	division only holds if the tips actually fire, so Reciting is graded in code
538	against the chunk text and cannot be skipped as a nice-to-have.
539	
540	**A student can be at the standard and still be told what she lost.** The band
541	answers "was this run good"; the missed Details answer "what did you drop". They
542	are different questions and the Report Card shows both, which is what stops the
543	top band from reading as nothing left to learn.
544	
545	**The floor rests on 71 runs and should eventually rest on more.** The corpus
546	supports 713, and the remaining 642 are stimuli from sections that are not built
547	yet. Re-running the census is the natural first use of the regression suite in
548	[0017](./0017-the-grader-hears-the-run-through-the-same-ear-as-the-standard.md),
549	and the cutoff moves by measurement rather than by taste when it does.
550	
551	**A fill-in-the-blank stimulus needs its own handling and does not have it.**
552	PT134.S3.Q1 is the census minimum because its final chunk is a blank the speaker
553	completes in four words, and its Details describe a conclusion that is not on the
554	page. Every section will have a few. Nothing in this ADR fixes that, and a
555	student will hit the same floor on the same stimuli.
556	
557	## Validated against a real model, 2026-09-02
558	
559	The bands above were drawn from judging done by hand and by agent. The grader
560	does not use either. `scripts/grade-census.ts` ran the actual judge -- the real
561	prompt, `gemini-2.5-flash` at temperature 0 -- over all 71 census stimuli:
562	
563	| | min | p10 | median | mean |
564	|---|---|---|---|---|
565	| Census (hand + agent) | 68 | **86** | 95 | 94.7 |
566	| Judge (gemini-2.5-flash) | 59 | **85** | 94 | 93.1 |
567	
568	**The floor holds.** Judge p10 is 85 against a census p10 of 86: the two agree
569	within a point, which is validation rather than discrepancy. Mean delta is
570	**-1.6 points**, so the model is very slightly stricter than a person, and 58 of
571	71 land within 5 points. The floor stays at 86; moving it one point on this
572	evidence would claim a precision the measurement does not have.
573	
574	**87% of correct runs land at the standard**, 11% at "nearly there", one at
575	"thin". At section level the mean is 93%, comfortably inside the top band, so a
576	correct run reads as correct; the spread is per stimulus.
577	
578	**Nothing failed to parse.** 71 replies, zero unreadable, zero Details left
579	unjudged.
580	
581	**The decoys caught something, which is the point.** 4 of 71 stimuli had the
582	judge credit a Detail belonging to a different stimulus -- one decoy of two,
583	each time. That is the failure this ADR's judging rule exists to prevent, it is
584	invisible in a Coverage number, and without decoys we would not know it happens
585	at a 5.6% rate. It is recorded per grade so a run carrying one can be treated as
586	suspect rather than reported as fact.
587	
588	**Cost is not a consideration.** ~1,750 tokens per stimulus; ~124,000 for the
589	whole census; roughly 41,000 to grade one student's section.
590	
591	## Alternatives considered
592	
593	**Normalise each stimulus against Joshua's own Coverage on it, so his run is
594	100.** Rejected. It makes the scale depend on how well he happened to do that
595	day, and on the 22 stimuli where he scored 100 it leaves her no headroom while
596	on the 68% one it hands her 32 points. The calibration method is explicit that his run
597	is one sample and not a ceiling; a per-stimulus normaliser treats it as both.
598	
599	**Show the raw percentage and put the target in the copy.** Offered and
600	rejected. A number and a caption disagreeing is a number winning: 88% next to
601	"this is a good run" reads as consolation.
602	
603	**Drop the structural Details from the key so Coverage is content only.**
604	Rejected. They are 10.6% of the key and they are the argument's skeleton, which
605	is the thing The Recall exists to test. The fix is to ask the judge the right
606	question, not to stop asking.
607	
608	**Weight Details by importance so a proper noun counts less than a conclusion.**
609	Rejected for now. It needs a per-Detail authored weight over 1,294 rows, and the
610	band already absorbs the variance a weighting would model.

131	
132	The rule is not ceremony. Stopping and starting a `MediaRecorder` drops audio at
133	every boundary, so twenty-six restarts means twenty-six small holes in exactly
134	the places her recall begins. One failed restart loses every stimulus after it.
135	And a student who hears the microphone cycle between stimuli starts performing
136	for it, which is the self-editing the drill exists to break.
137	
138	## Decision
139	
140	**Record once. Cut afterwards.**
141	
142	The microphone runs unbroken for the whole section, exactly as now. When she
143	finishes, the single file is cut into one **Clip** per stimulus using the
144	millisecond marks the runner already writes to `rrc.audio_segments`, and the
145	Report Card shows one player per stimulus.
146	
147	**A Clip is per stimulus, never per question.** Two questions can share a
148	stimulus: PT105.S2 has twenty-five questions and twenty-three stimuli. She read
149	that stimulus once and recorded it once, so cutting per question would hand her
150	the same audio twice under two labels and imply she did the work twice.
151	
152	**The marks are the cut, and they are already trustworthy.** They come from the
153	two presses of the Space key that [0013](./0013-she-chunks-freely-and-the-app-does-not-track-it.md)
154	introduced, not from listening to the audio. Nothing has to infer where a
155	stimulus began.
156	
157	## Consequences
158	
159	**The unbroken-take rule survives contact with a feature that appeared to need
160	breaking it.** That is the whole point of writing this down: the next person to
161	want per-stimulus audio will reach for per-stimulus recording, and it will look
162	like the simpler design right up until the drill is quietly ruined.
163	
164	**One upload, not twenty-six.** Storage, the bucket policy and the grader are
165	untouched. The grader already segments the same file by the same marks, so the
166	Report Card and the score are cut from one artefact rather than two that could
167	disagree.
168	
169	**Cutting has to happen somewhere, and it is not free.** Either the player seeks
170	within one file using the offsets, or the file is split server-side into stored
171	clips. Seeking is the cheaper of the two and needs no new storage; splitting is
172	the more robust and survives a browser that seeks badly in a WebM container.
173	This ADR does not settle which, because the answer depends on how a real
174	recording behaves, and no student has yet made one.
175	
176	**A Clip is only as good as the clock.** If the recorder's millisecond marks and
177	the audio's own timeline disagree, every Clip is offset and she hears the wrong
178	stimulus. That is the same alignment the grader depends on, so the two are
179	proved together or not at all.
180	
181	## Alternatives considered
182	
183	**Record one file per stimulus.** The direct route to the feature, and rejected
184	on the rule it breaks. Twenty-six restarts, twenty-six chances to drop audio,
185	and a microphone that visibly cycles while she is being asked not to perform.
186	
187	**Keep the single recording and give her one player for the whole section.**
188	What the design said before this. It is less work and it is worse: at
189	twenty-five minutes she will not go looking, so the playback is present and
190	unused, which is the same as absent but with storage attached.
191	
192	**No playback at all.** Offered in this session and declined. The argument for it
193	was that she is not the audience for her own hesitations. The argument against
194	is better: hearing herself drop the word "only" is the fastest way to believe
195	she dropped it.

197	# 0021 — A partial grade is written, and says so
198	
199	Date: 2026-09-03
200	Status: accepted
201	Extends [0019](./0019-a-grade-the-student-can-write-is-not-a-grade.md).
202	
203	## Context
204	
205	The first end-to-end trial of a Read, Recall, Colour section ran on 2026-09-03:
206	PT102.S3, 22 stimuli, 21m34s against a 25-minute goal, driven through the real
207	product. Everything up to the grade worked. The recording was one unbroken
208	21.5-minute file, the upload landed, the clock proof held to within 1 ms, and all
209	22 response rows were written with their phase boundaries.
210	
211	`grade-rrc-section` then returned 504, and `activity_section_grades` was empty --
212	not for this run, for its whole life. The production grader had never
213	successfully written a row.
214	
215	The cause is in the shape of the function rather than in any of its logic. It
216	judged the 22 stimuli in a `for` loop, one model call after another, and 22 round
217	trips do not fit inside an edge function's wall clock. Judged all at once against
218	the same saved transcript, the same calls take about 20 seconds.
219	
220	Two further things follow from that loop, and both are worse than the timeout.
221	A single failed judge call threw out of the loop, past the write, and lost the
222	whole section -- including the transcription already paid for. And because the
223	client `await`ed a request it documents as fire-and-forget, the student sat on
224	"Section finished" for the entire 150 seconds before the 504, with a Report Card
225	screen already built and polling on the other side of it.
226	
227	## Decision
228	
229	**The stimuli are judged concurrently, at a fixed bound.** Eight at a time,
230	through one helper in the shared grading module. Bounded rather than unbounded
231	because the judge is a rate-limited API and an unbounded fan-out trades a timeout
232	for a wall of 429s. Results come back in section order, not completion order,
233	because the Report Card is read top to bottom.
234	
235	**A stimulus that cannot be judged costs its own grade and not the section's.**
236	The failure is caught inside the per-stimulus work, and that stimulus is returned
237	carrying `ungraded` with the reason. The section is written with whatever landed.
238	A run that was recorded, uploaded and transcribed correctly is never discarded
239	because a model call failed.
240	
241	**`ungraded` is not zero, and every count knows the difference.** An unjudged
242	stimulus is excluded from Coverage's denominator and from the Reciting, Listing,
243	uncoloured and decoy tallies. Counted as a zero it would deflate her Coverage;
244	counted as uncoloured or as a decoy pass it would report our outage as her habit.
245	On the trial data, two failed judge calls would have shown 82% instead of 91%,
246	and six uncoloured stimuli instead of four.
247	
248	**`section.complete` says whether every stimulus actually got a judgement**, and
249	it is the one flag the sweeper reads. A partial grade must not pass for a
250	finished one, or the run that most needs re-driving is the one nothing will ever
251	look at again. A grade row written before this flag existed has no `complete` key
252	and is treated as complete: it is a real grade, and re-driving every one of them
253	would be a re-grade, not a sweep.
254	
255	**A stored grade keeps the section's authored shape.** Every stimulus grade
256	carries its `item_order`, and the section summary carries `stimuli_in_section`
257	from the assignment snapshot. The Report Card labels a row with that stored
258	position rather than its array index. If response rows are absent, it names the
259	real saved count and the real section count, and says the missing rows are ours.
260	Grades written before these fields existed keep their array-order labels and do
261	not receive a missing-row claim that cannot be proved from their stored data.
262	
263	**`sweep-rrc-grading` is the guarantee behind the client's ask.** It re-drives
264	sections that are ten minutes past finishing and have either no grade row or an
265	incomplete one, three per run, oldest first. It writes no booking heartbeat: an
266	ungraded section is not a booking outage.
267

317	
318	**A partial Report Card is now a state the screen can be handed.** Nothing reads
319	`ungraded` yet: a Detail she did not carry and a Detail nobody asked about look

350	# 0022 — Reciting is a share, not a streak
351	
352	Date: 2026-09-03
353	Status: accepted
354	Amended: 2026-09-04 (see "Amendment: the backstop was firing on the standard")
355	Extends [0017](./0017-the-grader-hears-the-run-through-the-same-ear-as-the-standard.md).
356	
357	## Context
358	
359	Reciting is the drill's named failure: saying a stimulus back close to
360	word-for-word rather than in her own words. Coverage looks perfect and nothing
361	was understood. The grader decides it in code rather than asking the model,
362	because it is a measurement.
363	
364	It was measured as `longestSharedRun >= 12 || bigramOverlap >= 0.50`. Neither
365	number was measured or written down. Both were wrong, and the first trial and
366	the model-run corpus say so in the same direction.
367	
368	Three populations, all real, all on PT102.S3:
369	
370	|                                       | longest run | bigram share |
371	| ------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------ |
372	| Joshua, reading aloud then recalling   | 19 – 43     | 0.180 – 0.404 |
373	| a student, done correctly              | 3 – 18      | 0.145 – 0.652 |
374	| one deliberate recite                  | 48          | 0.989        |
375	
376	At 12 and 0.50 that check flagged **all 22 of Joshua's own model runs** — the
377	recordings that define a correct performance — and **4 of the 21 correct
378	stimuli** in the trial. One deliberate recite was planted and five stimuli came
379	back flagged.
380	
381	The prior finding proposed windowing the measure to `recalling` only, described
382	as a two-line fix. Measured on the trial it changes which stimuli are flagged
383	and not how many: five before, five after. It is not the fix.
384	
385	## Decision
386	
387	**The bigram share is the test. The longest run is a backstop.**
388	
389	`RECITING_RUN = 45` and `RECITING_BIGRAM = 0.80`, in `_shared/rrcOverlap.ts`
390	beside the measures themselves, with the table above beside them.
391	
392	The two measures behave differently in a way the old thresholds ignored. Joshua
393	reads each chunk aloud before recalling it, so a single read sentence pins the
394	longest run no matter how well he retells around it — 19 to 43 while doing the
395	drill perfectly. The bigram share is a proportion of everything said, so the
396	same reading aloud dilutes it rather than inflating it, and never carries him
397	past 0.404. A share measures "in her own words"; a streak measures whether any
398	sentence was ever read out loud.
399	
400	So the run threshold sits **above the entire reading-aloud population** rather
401	than below it, where it can no longer confuse reading the text with reciting it
402	from memory. Those are different acts and only one is a failure.
403	
404	**The thresholds are set to avoid false positives, not to catch every marginal
405	case.** A Reciting flag tells a student something about herself that the Report
406	Card gives her no way to argue with. A missed flag only fails to.
407	
408	**Every stimulus now records `reciting_run` and `reciting_bigram`.** The verdict
409	was stored and the reading was not, so recalibrating meant reconstructing the
410	numbers from a scratchpad and re-measuring the model runs. The next calibration
411	reads real student runs out of the grade table.
412	
413	**The measure stays over the whole window, matching the glossary.** Reciting is
414	defined as a failure of the Reading Recall, which happens while the text is on
415	screen, so windowing to The Recall would measure a different step. Whether that
416	definition is the right one is a question for the curriculum and not for the
417	grader; the thresholds above hold either way, because they were calibrated
418	against runs that include the reading.
419	
420	## Consequences
421	
422	**Nobody is told they recited for reading the text aloud.** Which is what the
423	old check would have done to any student who did what the seeded rules
424	ambiguously invite.
425	
426	**The upper bound rests on one deliberate recite.** That is thin, and it is the
427	side where being wrong is cheap. The recorded readings are what fixes it.
428	
429	**ADR 0017's asymmetry is narrowed but not closed.** The standard was produced
430	by `student_shaped.py`, which cuts the verbatim reads out before scoring; the
431	grader still has no equivalent step. This makes the thresholds tolerant of a
432	run that contains reading rather than making the two ears identical. Porting the
433	read detection is a separate change and remains the real answer.
434	
435	## Alternatives considered
436	
437	**Window the measure to `recalling` only.** The proposed two-line fix. Rejected
438	on measurement: it moves which stimuli are flagged without reducing how many,
439	and it contradicts the glossary, where Reciting is a failure of the Reading
440	Recall rather than of The Recall.
441	
442	**Drop the longest run and keep only the share.** Clean, and very nearly right:
443	the share alone separates all three populations. Rejected because a student who
444	recites one long passage inside an otherwise original retelling is exactly the
445	case a share is weakest at, and a backstop set above the reading-aloud
446	population costs nothing.
447	
448	**Ask the judge to decide Reciting.** Rejected before this ADR and still
449	rejected. It is a measurement, it is cheap, and a model's opinion of it would
450	vary between two runs of the same recording.
451	
452	---
453	
454	## Amendment: the backstop was firing on the standard
455	
456	Date: 2026-09-04
457	
458	The decision above is unchanged in principle and was wrong in one detail: the
459	run was written as an independent trigger, `run >= RECITING_RUN || bigram >=
460	RECITING_BIGRAM`. An OR is not a backstop. It is a second, weaker test with a
461	veto.
462	
463	The table this ADR rests on was measured on PT102.S3 alone, where Joshua's runs
464	top out at 43. PT134.S3 contains a 46-word read, and the 2026-09-03 audit caught
465	the consequence: his own stimulus in the standard flagged as "said back close to
466	word for word" at run 46, share 0.258. A share of 0.258 says plainly that this
467	was not a recital. The run arm overrode it.
468	
469	So the calibration was redone across every captured section rather than one.
470	274 model runs, twelve sections, measured by the functions themselves from

496	0.70 and 0.80 is the band the "Drop the longest run" alternative was rejected
497	for: a long recited passage inside an otherwise original retelling, still caught,
498	now without the veto.
499	
500	`RECITING_RUN` stays 45 and `RECITING_BIGRAM` stays 0.80. Neither was the defect.
501	
502	### Consequences
503	
504	**Zero of the 274 model runs flag, and both deliberate recites still do.** The
505	assertion is derived from the fixture rather than written beside it, so a
506	section captured later cannot quietly fall outside the population the numbers
507	were chosen against.
508	
509	**Report Cards already graded keep the old verdict.** `reciting` is stored, not
510	recomputed on read, so a run graded before this lands still shows the false
511	positive until it is graded again.
512	
513	**A third calibration should not be needed, and the reason the first two were is
514	now fixed.** Both previous attempts generalised from too little: the first from
515	nothing measured, the second from one section. This one is every section there
516	is, and the fixture makes the next one an append rather than a re-derivation.

518	# 0023 — A section never resumes
519	
520	Date: 2026-09-03
521	Status: accepted
522	Extends [0010](./0010-rrc-section-is-the-unit-of-work.md).
523	
524	## Context
525	
526	Every Activity in the Centre resumes, and should. `activity_item_responses` is
527	a complete log of where a student got to, `ActivityRunner` replays it on open,
528	and the intro is the front door exactly once: a student working an Activity over
529	five sittings should not read the rules five times.
530	
531	Read, Recall, Colour in section mode inherited that, and it is wrong in both
532	directions.
533	
534	**There is nothing to resume into.** The unit judged is the whole section
535	(ADR 0010) against one unbroken recording. A run rejoined at stimulus 12 is a
536	different sitting with a different recording, and the grader would score the
537	second sitting's audio against a mixture of both.
538	
539	**And resuming it is dangerous rather than merely wrong.** The resume branch
540	lands on `running`, and for this player `running` means the microphone opens and
541	the clock starts with the briefing skipped. So once the link had been opened
542	once — including by accident — every later visit began a live, recording,
543	clocked run. That is how the trial run of 2026-09-03 began: by accident, from a
544	link, with no rules page and no start.
545	
546	The state is sticky, which is what turns a one-off into a permanent condition.
547	A section abandoned halfway leaves `status = 'in_progress'` for ever, because
548	nothing marks an abandoned run as anything, so the dangerous path is the only
549	path the assignment ever takes again.
550	
551	There is a third fault sitting on top of both. The rows an abandoned sitting
552	leaves behind are kept on purpose — they are what makes a crashed run
553	recoverable — and `reconstructProgress` read them as progress through this run,
554	so the briefing offered "Back to stimulus 6" over a button that correctly starts
555	a fresh Attempt at stimulus 1.
556	
557	## Decision
558	
559	**A section always opens on its briefing and a deliberate start.** Regardless of
560	`status`, regardless of how many stimulus rows are on record, on every device
561	and at any later date.
562	
563	**The rows from an abandoned sitting stay where they are, and cost nothing.**
564	The next run takes the next `attempt_no` and the grader reads only the Attempt
565	it was asked for, defaulting to the newest.
566	
567	**Between Start and the first stimulus there are three seconds.** The recorder
568	is already running through them, so the level indicator is live and a muted
569	microphone is visible before any of it counts; the section's own clock does not
570	start until the beat ends, so the three seconds are not charged to her goal.
571	
572	A finished section is unaffected: it routes to its Report Card, which is the
573	only completion screen it has.
574	
575	## Consequences
576	
577	**A section cannot be continued, only re-run.** That is the honest position and
578	it was already true; what changes is that the product now says so instead of
579	pretending otherwise and producing a corrupt grade.
580	
581	**Every open of a section costs the briefing.** For a student doing three a
582	night that is a page she skims. The alternative was a page she never saw again
583	after the first night, on the one drill whose rules she is most likely to have
584	misremembered.
585	
586	**An abandoned run still leaves `in_progress` behind.** Not fixed here, and no
587	longer harmful: `status` is advisory (ADR 0019) and nothing now reads it as
588	"resume into a live recording". A run that is never finished is simply never
589	graded. (Superseded on this point by ADR 0027: starting again from the library
590	marked the old run `completed`, which is exactly what gets graded. It is now
591	marked `abandoned`, and the grader refuses a run that does not cover its
592	section.)
593	
594	## Alternatives considered
595	
596	**Resume only when the recording could be continued.** It cannot: a
597	`MediaRecorder` is per-tab and per-sitting, and appending a second file to the
598	first would break the offsets every Coverage score is cut against.
599	
600	**Ask her whether to resume or start again.** A dialog on the way into a
601	twenty-five minute unrepeatable take, whose correct answer is always the same,
602	in front of a student who is about to concentrate. The correct answer being
603	always the same is the argument for not asking.
604	
605	**Clear `in_progress` on abandonment.** Nothing observes the abandonment. The
606	tab is gone, which is the whole problem, and a server-side sweep would be a
607	second thing deciding what a run was.

student reading aloud, plenty do, and it is the natural thing to do with a text
you are about to be tested on. The trial run measured 34 words of it across two
chunks from a synthetic reader that was not even trying.

When she does read aloud, the source text lands in the only evidence the judge
is shown and every Detail in that chunk becomes trivially present. So the
student who reads aloud is scored HIGHER for doing less — on the same recording
that also gets her flagged for Reciting. Two measures on one run, pointing
opposite ways, both wrong.

## Decision

**The verbatim reads are cut out of what she said before the judge sees it**,
by the same rule and on the same side of the same comparison as the corpus.

**Only inside the `working` window.** She reads while the text is up. The Recall
is from memory by definition, so a word-for-word passage there is a recital and
has to survive in order to be caught as one by ADR 0022's measure.

**A cut requires a long run that also covers most of a chunk.** At least eight
consecutive words, and at least 60% of the chunk's length. A chunk shorter than
eight words is never cut at all. This errs deliberately: leaving a short read in
costs a little accuracy on one Detail, and cutting a short paraphrase costs her
the Detail outright.

**Chunks are taken in order, each cut advancing a cursor**, because she reads
them in order. Without that, a phrase repeated across two chunks could be cut
twice, and the second cut would land on her recall of the first.

**How much was cut is recorded on the grade** — `read_aloud_words` and
`chunks_read_aloud` — and shown to her as an observation rather than a mark.

## Consequences

**Coverage moves, slightly, and in the right direction.** Re-grading the trial
run took it from 91% to 92%, with 34 words cut. A student who reads the whole
section aloud would move much further, which is the point.

**Reciting gets more accurate for free.** The measure now runs on the same
stripped text, so a genuine recital during The Recall still shows and reading
aloud during `working` no longer inflates the overlap. The 45 / 0.80 thresholds
from ADR 0022 were calibrated on unstripped text, so on stripped text they are
strictly more conservative.

**Reading aloud becomes visible.** It was invisible before and it is worth
knowing: a student who reads the whole section out loud is doing a different
drill from the one she thinks she is doing, and nothing else in the product
would ever have said so.

**It is a heuristic, and it is bounded in the safe direction.** It can miss a
read; it can hardly ever invent one.

## Alternatives considered

**Port `transcript_align` wholesale.** The corpus tool locates every read
precisely and is the right tool for a batch job with a human checking it. In an
edge function on a student's live grade, its failure mode is silently cutting
her own words, and it needs the whole alignment machinery to do what one
constrained longest-common-run does at this threshold.

**Tell her not to read aloud and leave the grader alone.** The briefing already
says to read silently. A grader that is only correct when the student follows
instructions is not a grader, and this particular instruction is the hardest one
in the drill to keep.

**Grade only the `recalling` window.** Clean, and it throws away the
say-it-back and the colouring, which are two of the drill's four beats and carry
Details she genuinely produced.

## Amendment: the ear was matching two different tokenisations

Date: 2026-09-04

The decision above is unchanged in principle. Both mechanisms it chose were
wrong, and the smaller of the two was the one this ADR argued about.

The 2026-09-03 audit measured the deployed stripper against the standard's own
PT134.S3 run. It cut **582 of 8,148 words, 7%, and found 38 of the 87 chunks**.
Joshua reads every chunk aloud, so the honest number is 87, and the corpus
aligner these Details were calibrated against finds 86 of them and cuts 19%.
"At the standard" was therefore a statement about the stripper.

### What was actually wrong

Two things, and the first was not the heuristic at all.

**The two sides of the comparison were tokenised differently.** The grader
passes Deepgram's `punctuated_word` into the stripper — `Thus,` and `Hot` and
`don't`, capitalised and punctuated — and the stripper compared those tokens
against `rrcOverlap.words(chunk)`, which is lowercase and strips everything
outside `[a-z0-9]`. `Thus,` can never equal `thus`. Only a word that was already
lowercase and unpunctuated could match at all, so the "eight consecutive words"
requirement was really "eight consecutive words that happen to carry no capital
and no comma between them".

The scale of it is measurable on the corpus, whose transcripts can be fed
through either tokenisation. Across the twelve captured sections, 956 chunks:

|                                        | chunks found | words cut |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------ | --------- |
| as deployed (mismatched tokenisation)   | 463 (48%)    | 7.8%      |
| both sides normalised, same heuristic   | 800 (84%)    | 15.1%     |
| the corpus aligner, for reference       | 949 (99%)    | 19.5%     |

Simulating the deployed tokenisation on PT134.S3 gives 39 of 87 chunks and 7.4%
of words, against the audit's field measurement of 38 and 7% on a different
recording of the same section. That is the defect, reproduced.

**The exact run is the smaller half.** Even with the tokenisation fixed it
leaves 16% of the reads in, because a recording that opens mid-sentence, a
hesitation inside a chunk, or one misheard word in seven all break a run.

### Decision

**The reads are located by fuzzy alignment, `transcript_align.locate` ported.**
Every chunk is scored against every window of the working text at its own length
and at a third longer, by `difflib.SequenceMatcher(autojunk=False).ratio()`
ported rather than approximated — the calibration band was set on that
function's output, and a different similarity measure with a re-tuned threshold
would be a second ear again, which is the whole defect.

**Chunks are located independently and the ordering is resolved afterwards**,
highest-confidence first, admitting a match only where it does not cross one
already accepted. A single advancing cursor is what the original decision chose
and it starves every chunk after one whose read is missing or truncated:
PT134.S3.Q16 opens mid-sentence, and its chunk 1 matched badly enough to lose
chunks 2 to 5, all of which had been read aloud.

**The alignment has its own tokeniser**, `readWords`, which keeps contractions
whole and folds the curly apostrophe the stimulus text uses in 18 of the
corpus's 990 chunks and no engine emits. `rrcOverlap.words` was not widened
instead: ADR 0022's reciting thresholds were recalibrated on its output the same
day, and moving it would move them.

**The cut is made on the original tokens.** The normalising is for the
comparison only, so the evidence the judge reads and the transcript she is shown
on the expanded row keep their capitals and full stops.

**The tolerance is 0.80, and it is not the corpus aligner's 0.55.** This is the
one place the port deliberately departs, because the two tools are answering
different questions. `student_shaped.py` asks *where in this transcript is the
read*, of a corpus where every chunk is known to have been read aloud. The
grader asks *was this chunk read aloud at all*, of a window that very often
holds no read — and the wrong answer there is not a missed read, it is her own
recall silently deleted, which is exactly the failure this ADR rejected the
wholesale port for.

So the threshold was measured rather than inherited. `model-run-reads.json`
holds the best ratio for all 956 chunks scored twice: against the transcript
holding the read, and against the same transcript with the read already removed
by the corpus aligner, which leaves his retelling and colouring and nothing else
— the shape a student's recording has. Chunks under the eight-word floor are
excluded, since the floor is part of the policy.

| tolerance | reads found | her own words cut by mistake |
| --------- | ----------- | ---------------------------- |
| 0.55 (the corpus aligner's) | 100% | 27% |
| 0.70      | 98.1%       | 6.6%                         |
| **0.80**  | **96.1%**   | **1.7%**                     |
| 0.85      | 92.2%       | 0.7%                         |

The eight-word floor stays, and matters more to a fuzzy measure than it did to
an exact one: at 0.80 the false cuts that survive it are short chunks whose
words recur in her own retelling.

### Consequences

**On PT134.S3, the section the audit measured: 39 chunks to 74, and 7.4% of
words to 17.9%**, against the corpus aligner's 86 and 19.5%. Across all twelve
sections, 463 to 880 chunks and 7.8% to 18.6%. The word fraction is the number
that decides how much source text reaches the judge, and it is now within a
point of the reference the Details were calibrated against.

**Coverage will fall, and that is the point.** The audit's section mean was
grader 97%, census 94%, model-validated census 93.1%, with all 26 stimuli at
the standard where the census had two below it. How far it actually moves is
one measurement and it cannot be made from a development machine: it needs this
deployed and one `force` re-grade of the standard's run. Until that is run and
recorded here, this ADR claims a stripper that matches the corpus, not a mean
that does.

**A chunk she only half read is still not cut.** Half a chunk scores near 0.60
however it is measured, because the half she did not read is still in the
needle, and 0.60 is also roughly what a close paraphrase scores. Half of one
chunk's source text stays in the evidence. Closing it needs a second measure,
not a lower tolerance.

**The cut is window-shaped.** The span is the chunk's length or a third longer,
whichever scores better, and neither is a sentence boundary, so a read that is
interrupted can leave its last two words behind and one that starts late can
take her first two with it. The corpus aligner cuts the same way, so both ears
are wrong by the same couple of words.

**It costs about 20 ms per stimulus**, worst case 21 ms across the 274 captured
runs, against a judge call that costs seconds.

**"It can hardly ever invent a read" is no longer free.** The original decision
could say that because an exact eight-word run is nearly impossible to hit by
accident. This one buys its recall with a measured 1.7%, and that number is a
test rather than a claim: `reads.fuzzy.test.ts` re-derives it from the fixture,
so a section captured later cannot quietly fall outside the population the
threshold was chosen against.

# 0025 — The Read is the second number

Date: 2026-09-03
Status: accepted
Extends [0018](./0018-full-coverage-is-not-a-hundred-percent.md).

## Context

Every one of the 1,294 authored stimuli carries `the_read`: the analytical
takeaway a student should arrive at, plus `the_read_type` naming which of four
moves it is (13 Loophole, 4 Inference, 4 Conflict, 1 other on PT102.S3 alone).

Three places promise it is checked.

- The briefing tells her, in her own words, "This is the part that gets scored."
- The coach rubric authored on the Activity says "Score The Read against the
  captured key for that stimulus, not against a retelling. A different name for
  the same move is fine; missing the move is not."
- The section spec makes it the drill's accuracy criterion: "Accuracy: −5 or
  better on The Read."

Nothing measured it. Coverage was the only number: the memory half. The
reasoning half — the half the method is actually built around, and the half that
predicts an LSAT score — was authored, promised twice, and looked at by nobody.

On the trial run, re-graded, 15 of 22 stimuli got a Read at all and 12 of those
found the move. Seven had none. A card reporting 92% Coverage and nothing else
told that student she had a good night.

## Decision

**The Read is judged, per stimulus, in the same model call as Coverage.** The
key is put in the prompt; a stimulus with no authored Read simply omits the
whole block rather than asking about an empty one.

**Two flags, not one.** `read_given` — did she say anything analytical about
what the argument is DOING, as opposed to retelling what it says. `read_matches`
— was it the same move as the key. They fail differently and need different
things said to her: no Read at all is a habit that stopped at the retelling, a
Read that missed the move is comprehension.

**A Read that was never given cannot match.** Enforced in `gradeStimulus`
rather than trusted to the judge, so a model answering both true cannot invent
one.

**Absent is "no Read", never a pass.** Same rule as colour. A judge that forgets
the field must not be able to award it, and a grade written before The Read was
measured reports honestly as not having one rather than as a zero.

**The Report Card shows two numbers side by side**, Held and Understood. A card
showing only Coverage says the drill is about memory.

**And the goal now depends on it.** The spec says the goal counts "only if every
stimulus was recalled, coloured, AND given a Read", so `within_goal` requires
all three and `within_time` carries the raw clock separately. "Inside the time
but skipped the colouring on four" and "over the time" are different things to
be told.

## Consequences

**No extra model call and no extra cost.** It rides the judgement already being
made per stimulus.

**`within_goal` gets harder, correctly.** The trial run was inside 25 minutes at
21:34 and now does not make the goal, because four stimuli were uncoloured and
seven had no Read. Before today it was told it had made it.

**`the_read_type` is stored per stimulus and not yet used.** Four kinds of move,
recorded on every graded stimulus, waiting for enough runs to say whether a
student misses Loophole reads specifically. That is a report, not a decision.

**Older grades are legible as older.** `read_given_count` is absent rather than
zero on anything graded before today, and every surface that reads it says "not
asked" rather than drawing a nought.

## Alternatives considered

**A second, separate judgement call per stimulus.** 22 more calls per section,
for a judgement that needs the same transcript and the same context. Concurrency
already made the judging stage cheap; doubling it to keep two prompts tidy is
paying in latency for an aesthetic.

**Score The Read numerically, as the spec's "−5 or better" suggests.** That
scale belongs to a full section score and means nothing per stimulus. Given the
move / missed the move is the judgement the rubric actually describes, and it is
the one a student can act on.

**Fold The Read into Coverage as extra Details.** It would disappear into an
average of twenty other things, and it is the one that predicts a score. Two
numbers, and the second is the important one.

# 0026 — A Report Card per Attempt

Date: 2026-09-03
Status: accepted
Extends [0019](./0019-a-grade-the-student-can-write-is-not-a-grade.md).

## Context

The section module's own glossary states the rule: Attempt 1, 2 and 3 each keep
their own date and their own Report Card, "because the point of running a
section twice is seeing what changed."

Nothing in the product could show two.

`activity_section_grades` was keyed on `assignment_id` alone, so the grader's
upsert overwrote. Every other link in the chain already carried the Attempt:
`activity_item_responses` has `attempt_no` with a unique index over
(assignment_id, student_id, item_order, attempt_no), the runner reads the
highest on record and takes the next, and the audio path carries it so a second
recording cannot overwrite the first. The grade was the only place two sittings
became one, and the one destroyed was always the earlier run she wanted to
compare against.

The reachability half was worse than the storage half. `SectionReport` was
mounted only from `onComplete`, in the tab that produced the run — and grading
finishes minutes after she stops, so the Report Card is by construction
something she comes BACK to. Coming back was the one path that did not lead to
it: a completed section fell through to the generic free-text summary and
presented a 91% run as "Not scored · 0 correct · 0 almost". The Attempt list on
the section library rendered past runs as flat grey text.

## Decision

**The grade's key is (assignment_id, attempt_no).**

**The grader takes an optional attempt and defaults to the newest on record.**
Its already-graded short-circuit asks "is THIS sitting graded", not "has this
section ever been graded" — which is what used to report a second run as
already-graded and never mark it.

**The sweeper reads the latest Attempt per assignment.** Keyed on the assignment
it called a second run graded because the first one was, so the run nothing
would ever look at again was exactly the run it skipped.

**An Attempt is reachable at its assignment's own address.** A completed section
routes to its Report Card rather than to the generic summary, from any device at
any later date, and the library's Attempt list links to it and says what she
scored.

## Consequences

**Existing grades are Attempt 1**, which is what they were: the overwrite meant
only one ever survived per assignment. The migration is additive.

**Two Attempts of one section can be compared, and nothing yet does it.** The
data is now shaped for it and `SectionRunHistory` lists them; a side-by-side
diff of two Report Cards is a further step.

**Most Attempts are their own assignment anyway.** The section library creates a
fresh assignment per Attempt, so the collision this fixes is reachable mainly
through re-running a single assignment. That is exactly the path a crashed or
abandoned run leaves behind, which is the path least likely to be noticed and
most likely to matter.

## Alternatives considered

**Keep one grade and store a history array inside it.** One row that grows for
ever, no way to read the latest without parsing the lot, and a re-grade of an
old Attempt has to rewrite a document containing every other. The key is the
right place for the distinction the domain already makes.

**Let a second Attempt overwrite, and archive the first elsewhere.** Two places
holding grades, one of which nothing reads. That is the situation being fixed.

# 0027 — A run is graded once, and an abandoned one never

Date: 2026-09-03
Status: accepted
Extends [0023](./0023-a-section-never-resumes.md) and [0026](./0026-a-report-card-per-attempt.md).

## Context

The first end-to-end run of a section as a student (PT134.S3, 26 stimuli,
46 minutes, audited 2026-09-03) produced a Report Card, and four things on the
ordinary path lost or mis-scored her work.

**A stimulus with no browser transcript was never saved.** The runner wrote
`response_text: null` into a NOT NULL column. Postgres refuses an explicit null
even when the column has a default, so any stimulus Chrome's recogniser heard
nothing in vanished from the run, the end-of-run backstop failed as a whole
statement with it, and a browser with no `SpeechRecognition` at all (Safari,
Firefox) could not save a single row. ADR 0017 described the null as the
design; it was the bug.

**The grader ran twice.** The runner asks for a grade as she finishes; the
Report Card asks again after twenty seconds "in case the first ask was lost". A
grading takes one to three minutes and wrote nothing until it finished, so the
second ask found no grade and ran too. Two transcriptions, two sets of verdicts
on the same audio, and a card that showed the first while the database kept
the second. Temperature 0 is not determinism.

**A second tab discarded the live run's crash backup.** The local backup marks
a run as live in a module-level Set, which is per tab. Any other section page
opened mid-run ran recovery, found the live run pending with no claim it could
see, uploaded its first minutes over the run's own path and threw the backup
away.

**Starting again from the library graded the abandoned run.** The RPC that
opens a fresh Attempt marked the old, unfinished one `completed`, and
`completed` with rows is exactly what the grader and the sweeper grade. A run
abandoned at stimulus 3 came back as "0%, 2 stimuli, inside the time". A
recording recovered from a crash was graded with 44% of its words inside any
window because the clock proof refused only at zero.

## Decision

**`response_text` is a string, never null.** An empty string is a stimulus
with no words; `transcript_source: null` is what says the words were not
captured. The two were conflated and only one of them is a column constraint.

**A grading is claimed before it starts.** `activity_section_grading_claims`
holds one row per Attempt in flight. The grader inserts it before it downloads
audio and deletes it in `finally`; an insert that conflicts with a fresh claim
returns false and the grader answers `202 grading`, which the Report Card
treats like any other ask: it keeps polling for the row. A claim older than ten
minutes is stale and can be taken over, so a grader that died mid-run cannot
hold its Attempt for ever. The Report Card's repeat ask moves from twenty
seconds to two minutes, so the repeat is for a lost ask rather than a slow one.

**The backup record carries its own heartbeat.** `liveUntil` is written when
the run is announced and pushed forward by every piece stored, and recovery
skips any run whose heartbeat is in the future, from any tab. The in-memory
Set stays as the fast path for the recording tab itself.

**An abandoned run is `abandoned`.** A fourth `activity_assignments.status`,
advisory like the others (ADR 0019). `start_section_attempt` marks the old run
with it instead of `completed`; the sweeper only reads `completed`; the
library labels it "not finished" as it already did for `in_progress`. Its rows
are kept, because they are what makes its recording recoverable.

**Only a whole section is graded.** The grader refuses a run whose rows do not
cover every stimulus in the assignment's snapshot, with `409` and the counts.
This is distinct from ADR 0021's partial grade, which is the judge failing on a
complete recording. The sweeper checks the same thing before driving a run, so
a run that lost rows is reported as skipped rather than re-driven for ever.

**The clock proof is a share.** A run with fewer than half its transcribed
words inside any recorded window is refused, not graded against the wrong
words.

## Consequences

- Safari and Firefox students can now finish a section. Their rows carry no
  browser transcript, which was always the design; the grader hears Deepgram.
- A Report Card cannot disagree with itself across devices. There is one
  grading per Attempt and the row it writes is the row every read returns.
- A run abandoned yesterday no longer becomes a graded 0% today. A student who
  abandons and returns sees "not finished" and a fresh Attempt.
- A run that genuinely lost rows is now refused rather than mis-numbered. That
  is a visible failure where there was an invisible one; the sweeper's
  `skipped` list is where it shows.
- Applying the migration is a schema change: a new table, two functions, a
  widened status check, and a rebuilt partial unique index.

## Alternatives considered

**A Postgres advisory lock.** The obvious lock, and it does not survive
PostgREST: a transaction-level lock releases when the RPC returns, a
session-level lock is held by a pooled connection the function never sees
again. A row with a timestamp is the lock that fits the transport.

**Writing a placeholder grade row as the in-flight marker.** It would have
rendered as a Report Card, or needed every reader taught to skip it, and a
stale placeholder would have looked like a finished grade to the sweeper.

**Leaving the abandoned run `in_progress`.** The partial unique index allows
one live run per student and activity, so an old `in_progress` row blocks the
new one. Deleting it throws away the rows that make its recording recoverable.
A status that means what happened is the honest third option.

**Grading a partial run as partial.** ADR 0021's partial grade means "the
recording is whole, the judge lost some stimuli, re-drive it". A run that was
never finished is not that, and labelling it so would have put it in the
sweeper's re-drive queue for ever.

# 0028: A failed response row is retried and named

Date: 2026-09-03
Status: accepted
Amended: 2026-09-04 (see "Amendment: the missing row has a way back")
Extends [0027](./0027-a-run-is-graded-once-and-an-abandoned-one-never.md).

## Context

The section runner sends a response row whenever a stimulus finishes, then
re-sends every row when the section ends. The first save only logged a failure.
The final save put every response into one database statement, so one rejected
row could reject every good row beside it. The student saw neither failure.

The grader refuses a run whose response rows do not cover the assignment. A
lost row is therefore not a partial score. It is a finished recording that
cannot yet produce a Report Card.

## Decision

Each response is stored in its own idempotent upsert. A failed in-run save gets
three attempts with bounded exponential backoff while the student continues
working. The retry queue is naturally capped by the section's stimulus count.

When the section ends, pending timers are cancelled and in-flight saves are
allowed to settle. The backstop then submits every response again, one row per
statement. One rejected row cannot prevent another row from reaching storage.

If any response is still absent after the backstop, the finishing screen names
the real count. It says the recording reached us only when the upload actually
succeeded. The runner does not ask the grader to mark an incomplete attempt and
does not hand the student to a Report Card that can only wait. The assignment
is still closed because she did the work; the storage failure is ours.

## Consequences

A brief outage can recover during the reading time that remains in the section.
A permanent row-specific failure leaves the other responses stored. The
student is interrupted only after the one-take run is over.

An attempt with a permanently missing response stays on the finishing screen
with a route back to the Activity Centre. It needs an operational recovery
before grading. This is preferable to displaying a spinner for a grade the
server has correctly refused to invent.

*(Superseded 2026-09-04 by the amendment below: the recovery is no longer
operational, and no longer a thing only Joshua can do.)*

## Alternatives considered

**Keep the multi-row backstop and retry the batch.** Rejected because a single
bad row would continue to poison every retry.

**Show a warning as soon as a save fails.** Rejected because a transient network
failure should not interrupt an unrepeatable spoken run, and most such failures
can recover before the student finishes.

**Ask the grader even when a row is missing.** Rejected because the grader's
coverage check returns 409 by design. Sending the request would create traffic,
not a Report Card.

## Amendment: the missing row has a way back

Date: 2026-09-04

The decision above is unchanged. What it left behind was a run that could say
what was wrong with it and could never become right.

The rows live in `records.current` — in memory, in a component about to
unmount. She leaves the finishing screen and the answers are gone, and ADR 0027
then refuses that run for ever, the sweeper included. The cruel version of it is
the common one: the recording uploaded perfectly, and there is nothing that will
ever mark it.

This was not a regression. Before this ADR the same run was equally ungradable;
it polled in silence instead of saying so. Naming the failure made it visible
and did not repair it.

### Decision

**A row that does not land is stashed on her own machine, beside the
recording,** in the same IndexedDB database `recall/recordingBackup.ts` already
keeps the audio in, and re-sent by `recoverPendingRows` on the next entry into
any section. This is the mechanism the other half of the same failure has had
since the backup existed; rows simply had no sibling.

**A separate store, not a field on the run record.** The obvious shared
lifetime is the wrong one. A run whose recording uploads is discarded the
instant it lands — that is what `discard` is for — and the missing-row case is
very often exactly that run. Hung off the run record, the rows would be deleted
by the success of the upload. They are bounded by their own age instead, on the
same seven days, by `purgeStaleRows`.

**The grade is asked for when the run's last stashed row lands, and at no other
point.** Asking on a partial recovery buys the certain 409 that this ADR's own
"Ask the grader even when a row is missing" alternative rejected.

**Silent and best-effort, like the recording pass.** It runs on the way into a
screen she asked for, and a failed recovery must never be the reason that
screen does not open.

**The finishing sentence names the repair**, in all three of its shapes — rows
lost, recording lost, both lost — rather than only for the audio.

### Consequences

**The finishing screen stops being a dead end.** It is still an interruption
after a one-take run, and it is now an interruption with an instruction that
works: open the section again from the same browser.

**Recovery no longer means Joshua.** The paragraph above that called for "an
operational recovery before grading" described the absence of this.

**It repairs the tab, not the machine.** IndexedDB is per browser profile, so a
student who finishes on a laptop and next opens the app on her phone still has
an unrecoverable run. The same limit the recording backup has always had, and
the same one the copy states plainly.

**A stash that cannot be written changes nothing.** Private windows and full
disks make IndexedDB throw, and the run is then exactly as ungradable as it was
before this existed, and no worse.

# 0029: A spoken section does not start without a microphone

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: accepted
Reverses part of the recorder's original intent; see Context.

## Context

`useSectionRecorder` was written so that a refused or missing microphone would
not stop the drill, on the reasoning that the section is still a valid sitting
if she is recording on a handheld device. The runner therefore counted its ready
beat to zero under the heading "Recording now" while the footer said "No
microphone. The drill still runs, but nothing is being recorded", and handed her
a full 26-stimulus section.

The grader refuses a run with no recording. There is no path by which audio
captured on a handheld reaches this assignment. So the reasoning no longer
holds: what the beat was counting into was twenty-five unrepeatable minutes of
spoken work that nothing in the system can ever mark, with no way to ask for the
microphone again and no way to stop before the clock started.

The beat was also unguarded in a second way. `getUserMedia` raises a permission
prompt the student has to answer, and answering it can take longer than three
seconds, so the section could begin while the microphone had said nothing at
all.

## Decision

The ready beat waits for the microphone to answer, and holds if the answer is
no.

While it holds, the screen says "No microphone", names the consequence -- this
drill is spoken, so there is nothing to mark at the end -- and offers both ways
forward: ask again, or leave. Nothing has started and no clock has run, so
leaving costs her nothing. Asking again re-requests permission, which is safe
here because it happens before any recording exists; restarting a recorder
mid-section remains forbidden.

The heading never claims to be recording when nothing is.

## Consequences

A student without a working microphone cannot run a Read, Recall, Colour
section. That is the point: she could not produce a gradable one before either,
and now she finds out in the first three seconds rather than after twenty-five
minutes.

The recorder's own refused-microphone path still exists and still reports the
fault. What changed is that the runner treats that report as a gate rather than
as a caption.

Every test of the section runner now grants a microphone, because a run without
one no longer reaches the loop.

## Alternatives considered

**Let her continue without a recording, and say so louder.** Rejected. The
loudest possible warning still ends in twenty-five minutes of work and no Report
Card, and the honest version of that warning is a door rather than a caption.

**Offer a "run it anyway" escape.** Rejected for the same reason. An escape that
leads only to a refused grade is a trap with a button on it.

**Keep counting while the permission prompt is open.** Rejected: it is the case
where the section is most likely to start into nothing, because a student
reading a browser dialog is not watching the beat.

# 0030 — The card says one thing to do next

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: accepted
Extends [0018](./0018-full-coverage-is-not-a-hundred-percent.md).

## Context

The 2026-09-03 audit asked one question of the Report Card that none of the
numbers on it answer: would she know what to do tomorrow?

She would not. Every line described what happened. The band said "Nearly
there". The habits row said "3 with no reaction of your own". Nothing said that
the reaction is the half of the drill that makes the memory stick, or which of
the five observations was the one to work on. A student who reads the card
learns that she held 83% and nothing about what to change, and the gap is not
missing data — the grader measured all of it and the card rendered it as
description.

Two smaller versions of the same silence sat beside it.

**Going over the goal got no comment at all.** "46:12 against a 25 minute goal"
rendered as plain uncoloured text. The qualifier under it fired only when she
was *inside* the time and short of the goal, so the one case most in need of a
word was the one case with none.

**A verdict resting on a mis-hearing had nothing to say for itself.** The v1
spec named transcription error as the top risk and named showing her the
transcript as the mitigation. The transcript was shown and nothing said what it
was for. In the audit's run, stimulus 5's judge reason quoted her as saying
"clearly moral characters"; the recording says **immoral**, Deepgram dropped
the prefix, and she was told she gave the wrong Read. There was no way for her
to know that was a possibility, let alone say so.

## Decision

**One sentence, derived from stored fields, or none.**
`recall/nextStep.ts` reads the grade and returns a single instruction, ranked:

1. She gave no Read at all on a third or more of the run. First because it is
   the criterion the method is built around: coverage answers "did you hold
   it", The Read answers "did you understand it", and a run with every detail
   and no Read is a memory exercise.
2. Reciting, then listing, then no colour, each on a third or more. Ordered by
   distance from the drill: saying it back word for word is not recall, a list
   is recall without understanding, no reaction is understanding with nothing
   to hold it.
3. The kind of Detail she dropped most, when at least three of one kind account
   for a third of her misses.
4. Otherwise nothing.

**A third, because below that it is one bad stimulus**, which that stimulus's
own row already says, and hoisting it to the top of the card overstates it.

**Never "plain facts".** One card cannot compute a share — it stores the
Details she missed and not the ones she carried — so the ranking at (3) is a
count, and a count always crowns the residual bucket, which outnumbers
everything else about eight to one. It is excluded rather than allowed to be on
every card ever printed.

**Never a generic encouragement.** A card that always says something is a card
whose advice she learns to skip. Silence is the correct output for a run that
said nothing clearly.

**Over the goal gets a sentence in the same register.** Not a rebuke: early
runs are slow by design and the briefing already says to go slower while it is
new, so it says what the goal is for rather than that she was late.

**Where the transcript is shown, one line says what it is for**: the marking is
done from these words, and if they are not what she said the marking is wrong.
"Hear it back" is already on that row, which is what lets her check, so the line
sits under the words rather than anywhere else on the card.

## Consequences

**The card asks for something, once.** It is the only line on it that does, and
that is what makes it readable rather than another observation in a list.

**A clean run still gets nothing.** By design, and it is the case that proves
the rule is not a fallback chain.

**A dispute is a conversation, not a record.** The transcript line ends with
"tell Joshua" because there is nowhere for a dispute to be stored. Where one
should land — a column, a table, the indicator ledger — is his call and was not
made, so the sentence shipped without the control. The sentence is most of the
value: she could not previously know a mis-hearing was possible.

**The advice re-derives, so a better ranking is one deploy away.** Nothing is
written into the grade, which means every Report Card ever produced gets the
sentence the current rules give it rather than the one that was current when it
was marked.

# 0032 — A dispute is an event, with its own table

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: accepted
Completes [0030](./0030-the-card-says-one-thing-to-do-next.md), whose W7 shipped
its sentence and left its control unbuilt.

## Context

The 2026-09-03 audit found the failure this exists for. Stimulus 5's judge
reason quoted her as saying "clearly moral characters". The recording says
**immoral**. Deepgram dropped the prefix, and the card told her she had given
the wrong Read.

The v1 spec had already named transcription error as the top risk and named
showing her the transcript as the mitigation. The transcript was shown. Nothing
said what it was *for*, and nothing let her say the marking was wrong. ADR 0030
shipped the sentence — the marking is done from these words, and if they are
not what you said the marking is wrong, not you — and deliberately stopped
there, because where a dispute is stored is a product decision and nobody had
made it.

Joshua made it on 2026-09-04: its own table.

## Decision

**`public.activity_grade_disputes`, one row per disputed stimulus per sitting.**

It is not a column on `activity_section_grades`, and the session that asked the
question had just proved why by accident. A `force` re-grade of assignment
`f8684334` attempt 2 ran that morning and **replaced the whole grade row**. A
dispute living on that row would have been destroyed by precisely the operation
it was about.

That is the same shape as the mistake [0028](./0028-a-failed-response-row-is-retried-and-named.md)'s
sibling order avoided in W17: a stash that shares a lifetime with the thing it
protects against gets deleted by the event it exists for. Here the event is a
re-grade, and a re-grade is the most likely consequence of a dispute being
right.

Two further reasons the column was wrong:

- **There can be several.** She may dispute four stimuli in one section, at
  different moments. A column holds one; a table holds what happens.
- **It has to outlive its subject.** `source_section`, the band and the judge's
  reason are denormalised onto the row on purpose, so the dispute stays legible
  after a re-grade has rewritten everything it referred to.

**It is deliberately not a workflow.** No status, no assignee, no resolution
state. The order asked for a stored flag and a place Joshua can see it, and a
triage state machine would be answering a question nobody asked. Visibility is
an admin `select` policy, so he reads them the way he reads everything else in
this project.

**She may raise and read; she may not edit or withdraw.** A dispute is an
event, and an event that can be unsaid is not evidence of anything. One row per
`(assignment, attempt, stimulus)`, so a second tap is a student checking it
registered rather than a second complaint.

**A dispute that did not save never looks like one that did.** Every read path
in this feature treats a missing table as an empty result, which is right for a
read and exactly wrong here. `raiseDispute` returns `not-configured` and
`failed` distinctly from success, and the control says something true in both
cases. Telling a student "Noted" while the row went nowhere would be a lie told
on the one screen whose entire subject is the app getting her wrong.

## Consequences

- The control is on the expanded row, not the card head: it is about one
  stimulus's verdict, and the clip and the transcript are beside it, so she can
  check before she claims.
- It is a quiet text button rather than a call to action. The card must not
  invite a student to dispute every band she dislikes.
- Nothing reads the table yet beyond the admin policy. A teacher surface is a
  follow-up if Joshua wants one, and is explicitly not part of this decision.
- The table is applied to production as of 2026-09-04, with RLS on and all
  three policies live. The migration is
  `20260904140000_a_verdict_resting_on_a_mishearing_can_be_disputed.sql`.

# 0033 — A phone is warned and let through, so it must be able to advance

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: accepted

## Context

The drill advances on Space and on nothing else. That is deliberate and it is
the whole interaction: one key, no mouse in the loop, so her hands never leave
the argument.

A phone has no Space key.

The 2026-09-03 audit found what that costs. The briefing, the door and the
library all render correctly at 390x844, so nothing before the run said a
keyboard was required. She could open the drill, read stimulus 1, and then find
there was no way to reach stimulus 2 — by which point the recording was already
running and the take was already spoiled. The only control on the screen was
"Leave".

The order (W9) put two options to Joshua and refused to pick: support the
phone, or refuse it at the door. The recommendation was to refuse.

## Decision

**Neither. Warn her, and let her through.** Joshua's call, 2026-09-04, against
the recommendation.

That answer carries a requirement the order did not state, and it is the whole
of this ADR: **letting her through is only honest if she can actually run the
drill.** A warning followed by a screen she cannot advance is not a choice she
was given, it is a trap she was told about. So the decision is two things, and
neither works alone:

1. **The door says what the run needs.** A notice on `SectionLaunch` when the
   pointer is coarse: this is built for a laptop, it records for the whole
   section in one take so a call or a locked screen ends the run, the phone
   microphone hears her less well, and she can carry on if she wants to. It
   disables nothing. Start stays exactly where it was.
2. **The run gives her a way to advance.** An on-screen control in the stage
   footer calling the same `press()` the keyboard calls.

**`(pointer: coarse)`, not a width.** The question is "is there a keyboard",
not "is the screen small". A narrow desktop window has a keyboard and must keep
the pure one-key loop; a large tablet has none and needs the control. Width
answers neither, and `useIsMobile`'s 768px breakpoint would have got both wrong.

**Space stays authoritative everywhere,** including on the device that gets the
button. The control is an addition for a device that cannot reach the key, not
a second way of doing things.

**The control is not rendered where a keyboard exists.** This is the part that
protects the drill. "No mouse in the loop" is a rule about attention, and a
laptop run is untouched by this decision.

## Consequences

- It is drawn as chrome, not as a call to action: a large filled button in the
  footer of a drill that judges nothing would read as a verdict, and nothing in
  that footer may.
- It is disabled through the ready beat, for the same reason the key hint reads
  "in a moment": nothing may advance before the section has started.
- Its label names the next act ("Recall full stimulus", "Next stimulus") rather
  than a key, because on this device there is no key to name.
- 44px minimum height. A 25-minute run is many presses and a mis-tap costs a
  take that cannot be repeated.
- The known hazards of a phone run remain real and are now hers to accept: an
  incoming call suspends the tab, the microphone is worse, and
  `SpeechRecognition` is unreliable. The notice says so in her language. If
  those turn out to spoil real runs, the evidence for revisiting this is a run
  that ended early, not an argument.

# send-section-report

Emails the teacher a finished section: the complete per-question record, then
the diagnosis built from it (`docs/section-analysis-engine.md`).

Called by the student's browser immediately after `persistSectionResults`
writes the `section_history` row — the first moment both halves exist.

## Behaviour

- The caller's JWT identifies the student; the row is read with the service
  role; the two must match. A caller can only send their own sitting.
- Nothing is decided here. `analysis_json` was written once at completion and
  this function formats it. A second place deciding what a section meant is a
  second place that can disagree with what the student was already shown.
- Sends the `section-report` template, which carries its own fixed recipient,
  so there is no code path that can be talked into another address.
- Idempotent per sitting: `report_emailed_at` short-circuits a repeat, and the
  send itself carries `section-report:<id>` as its idempotency key.
- On failure the error string is written to `report_email_error` on the row. An
  email that never arrives and never explains itself is exactly the failure this
  feature exists to avoid.

## Environment

| Name | Required | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `LOVABLE_API_KEY` | yes | Managed email API, via `_shared/sendAppEmail.ts`. |
| `APP_BASE_URL` | recommended | Base URL for the link back to the student's record. |
| `SUPABASE_URL` / `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` / `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` | yes (auto) | Provided by the runtime. |

## Deploy

```
supabase functions deploy send-section-report
```

`analysis_json`, `report_emailed_at` and `report_email_error` to
`section_history`. Without it every send fails on the update and says so.

## Read, Recall, Colour — the pipeline as designed

Sources read: `docs/specs/2026-08-31-rrc-section-mode.md`, `docs/specs/2026-09-01-rrc-section-grader.md`, `docs/adr/0009`–`0033`, `src/features/activity-centre/CONTEXT.md` (no repo-root or `supabase/functions` CONTEXT.md exists).

### Stages

**1. Assignment.** Invariant: an RRC run is one LR section, items = distinct stimuli with shared stimuli collapsed (0010). Content is snapshotted per student. Tables: `public.activities` (`mode`, `time_goal_minutes`, `source_section`), `activity_assignments` (`items`, `status`, `attempt_no` chain). Failure handling: the section library creates a fresh assignment per Attempt; `start_section_attempt` marks the prior unfinished run `abandoned` — not `completed`, which used to make an abandoned run gradable (0027). Known hole: students hold UPDATE on their own assignment row; the immutability trigger `guard_activity_assignment_immutables` covers exactly ten columns and not `score`/`status`/`completed_at`, so all of those are **advisory only** (0019). RLS filters rows not columns, so `items` — every Detail, `the_read`, `correct_answer` — reaches her browser (0015; grader spec §10).

**2. Briefing / launch.** Invariant: **a section never resumes** (0023). Every open lands on `SectionLaunch` (component, not a world — 0009 amendment) regardless of `status` or rows on record; abandoned rows are kept, not read as progress. Phone: `(pointer: coarse)` shows a notice — one unbroken take, call or lock ends the run, worse mic — and lets her through (0033). Rejected: a resume dialog, a width breakpoint (`useIsMobile` 768px).

**3. Mic gate.** Invariant: a spoken section does not start without a microphone (0029). The ready beat waits for `getUserMedia` to answer and holds on refusal, offering ask-again or leave. Rationale: the grader refuses a run with no recording, so "run it anyway" is a trap.

**4. Countdown.** 3-second beat between Start and stimulus 1; recorder already running so the level indicator is live; the section clock does not start until the beat ends (0023). The advance control is disabled through the beat (0033).

**5. Recorder start.** Invariant: one unbroken file per section, `MediaRecorder` started once on mount, stopped once at the end; restarting mid-section is forbidden outright (0020). Bucket: private `drill-recordings`, `<student_id>/<assignment_id>.{webm|m4a}`, attempt in the path so a second recording cannot overwrite the first (0026). Student+admin read/write own; **no delete policy anywhere**, deliberate.

**6. Per-stimulus windows.** Invariant: the app does not track which chunk she is on (0013). One key, pressed twice: text vanishes, then next stimulus. Two phases only — `working`, `recalling`. `mark()` writes `{slug, order, phase, startMs, endMs}` into `rrc.audio_segments`. Nothing revisitable; no coach, no verdict, no network call in the loop (spec §4). Step 2 is the **Retelling** / "Tell it back" (0031).

**7. Response rows.** `activity_item_responses`, one per stimulus, `rrc` jsonb (`audio_bucket`, `audio_path`, `audio_duration_ms`, `audio_segments`, `elapsed_seconds`, `working_seconds`, `recall_seconds`, `section_total_seconds`, `accommodation`, `goal_minutes`, `transcript_segments`). Owner-or-admin SELECT, owner INSERT, **no UPDATE/DELETE**. `response_text` is a string, never null — an explicit null into a NOT NULL column silently lost every stimulus Chrome heard nothing in, and blocked Safari/Firefox entirely (0027). Retries: each row is its own idempotent upsert; a failed in-run save gets **3 attempts** with bounded exponential backoff; the end-of-run backstop re-sends every row one statement each (0028).

**8. Upload / backup / recovery.** Failed rows are stashed in the same IndexedDB database as the audio backup, in a **separate store**, purged at **7 days**, re-sent by `recoverPendingRows` on the next entry into any section; the grade is asked for only when the last stashed row lands (0028 amendment). Backup records carry a `liveUntil` heartbeat so a second tab cannot discard a live run's backup (0027). Limits: per browser profile only — finish on a laptop, open on a phone, unrecoverable; a private window or full disk makes IndexedDB throw and the run is as ungradable as before.

**9. Grade trigger.** Client calls `grade-rrc-section` after upload resolves and does not wait; Report Card re-asks once at **120 s** (was 20 s, which caused double grading). Sweeper `sweep-rrc-grading`: sections **10 minutes** past finishing with no grade row or `section.complete !== true`, **3 per run**, oldest first, latest Attempt per assignment, `completed` only, shares `BOOKING_WORKER_SECRET`, writes no booking heartbeat (0021, 0026).

**10. Grader.** Edge function, service role. Claim/lock: `activity_section_grading_claims`, one row per Attempt, inserted before download, deleted in `finally`; conflict → `202 grading`; claim stale after **10 minutes** (0027). Service-role key accepted as trusted caller, constant-time compare, checked before `getUser` (0021). Coverage check: refuses `409` unless rows cover every stimulus in the snapshot. Transcription: Deepgram Nova-3, word-level timestamps, stored for replay (0017); ~$0.11/section, $6.62 for the whole 714-file corpus. Clock proof: assert the first `working` span contains speech and the last `recalling` span ends before the file; refuse if **fewer than half** the transcribed words land inside any window (0027). Reads cut: fuzzy alignment (`difflib.SequenceMatcher(autojunk=False)` ported), `working` window only, ≥**8** consecutive words and ≥**60%** of chunk length, window = chunk length or a third longer, tolerance **0.80**; cut made on original tokens; `read_aloud_words` / `chunks_read_aloud` recorded (0024). Judge: `google/gemini-2.5-flash`, temp 0, via `gemini-proxy`, one call per stimulus, **8 concurrent**, results in section order; Details classified `content`/`structure`/`modality` and asked a different question each; **2 decoy Details** per call must come back missed. Partial grades: a failed call yields `ungraded` for that stimulus, excluded from every denominator; `section.complete` is the only flag the sweeper reads (0021). The Read is judged in the same call: `read_given` and `read_matches`, absent = no Read, never a pass (0025).

**11. Report card.** `activity_section_grades`, key **(assignment_id, attempt_no)** (0026), student SELECT only, service-role write (0019). Absence of a row = pending; one write, at the end. Bands: **≥86 / 72–85 / 55–71 / <55**; only the top boundary is evidenced. A stimulus flagged Reciting **wears no band** — "Said back word for word", neutral (0018 amendment). Two numbers, Held and Understood. Clips: one player per stimulus, cut from the one file by the marks (0020); cut-vs-seek unsettled. One next-step sentence from `recall/nextStep.ts`, thresholds at **a third**, "plain facts" excluded, silence allowed (0030). Joshua's model run is not unlocked in v1.

**12. Disputes.** `public.activity_grade_disputes`, one row per (assignment, attempt, stimulus), denormalised so it outlives a re-grade; raise and read, no edit or withdraw; no workflow state; `raiseDispute` distinguishes `not-configured` / `failed` from success (0032).
