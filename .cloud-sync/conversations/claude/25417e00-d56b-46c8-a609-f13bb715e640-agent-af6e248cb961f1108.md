137	## Overview
138	
139	**Creative North Star: "LSAT Academy"**
140	
141	One institution with two registers, and the seam between them is deliberate.
142	
143	**The Hall** is the door. Unauthenticated, dark, and material: near-black ground, warm marble text, a bronze that carries the light, and a serif that behaves like carved lettering. Its own source calls it a museum nocturne, carved marble filmed in darkness, read by modern instruments. Its job is to make a prospective student feel they are entering somewhere, not signing up for something. It is the only place in the product where atmosphere outranks throughput.
144	
145	**The Workroom** is everything past sign-in. Light, neutral, and quiet, because a student sits inside it for an hour reading dense argument text and the interface must not compete with the passage. There is no warmth here and that is the decision, not an oversight: warm accents need a dark ground to read, and on the Workroom's white they collapse. The Hall's bronze measures 8.58:1 on its own night and 2.30:1 on the Workroom's page. The two registers do not share a palette because they cannot.
146	
147	What they do share is restraint about meaning. In both, colour is a signal. Green, red and amber say something happened. Nothing is coloured to be warm, to look premium, or to fill a space. Depth comes from hairlines and background tone rather than shadow, and hierarchy comes from size, weight and position rather than hue.
148	
149	Inside the Workroom, individual rooms may commit fully to their own world. The eight Activity Centre worlds, the scoped bootcamps and the inbox each carry a painted identity behind a wrapper class. That is sanctioned and it is what an academy is: neutral corridors, characterful rooms. The corridor never takes on a room's paint.
150	
151	**Key Characteristics:**
152	- Two registers, one institution: a dark marble Hall at the door, a light neutral Workroom inside.
153	- Colour means something or it is absent.
154	- Flat surfaces, hairline separation, no drop shadows.
155	- Precise and quiet controls that stay out of the reader's way.
156	- Rooms may be worlds; corridors never are.

244	This is a decision, not a description. The codebase currently ships five shadow tokens tuned for a dark ground, from `0 1px 3px rgba(0,0,0,0.4)` up to `0 8px 32px rgba(0,0,0,0.7)`, and applies the smallest of them to every card. Because the app ships light by default, those shadows land at 40 percent black under a white card. The light theme never redefined them, and the one glow token is white, so it is invisible on white. The tokens are retired by this record and the code has not caught up yet.
245	
246	The Hall is the exception and keeps its own depth: fixed ambience gradients, a grain overlay, and an inset highlight on its call to action. That depth is atmospheric rather than structural, and it does not transfer.
247	
248	### Named Rules
249	
250	**The Flat Rule.** Depth in the Workroom comes from a hairline or a tonal step. A `box-shadow` on a Workroom surface is a defect.
251	
252	**The Two-Step Rule.** Page, then card, then at most one raised step. A surface that needs a fourth level of layering is a surface that needs restructuring.
253	
254	## Shapes
255	
256	Corners are gently rounded and consistent: 8px for cards and containers, 6px for buttons and inputs, 4px for the smallest chips and insets, and a full pill only for genuine badges. The scale derives from one 0.5rem radius token, so a change to the base moves the whole system together.
257	
258	The Hall uses a near-square 2px radius instead. Its edges are cut, not softened, which is what separates a carved surface from a card.
259	
260	Borders are one pixel. There is no second border weight in the Workroom.
261	
262	### Named Rules
263	
264	**The Hairline Rule.** One pixel, always. A thicker border used as a coloured accent stripe on the leading edge of a surface is banned, including when faked with an inset shadow. Active state is carried by background tone.
265	
266	## Components
267	
268	### Buttons
269	
270	- **Character:** precise and quiet. A button is a plain rectangle with a label. It darkens slightly on hover and does nothing else. It does not lift, does not cast a shadow, and does not scale on press.
271	- **Shape:** gently rounded (6px).
272	- **Primary:** ink fill (`#121212`), page-coloured label, 8px by 16px padding.
273	- **Outline:** page background, one-pixel field border, ink label.
274	- **Ghost:** no fill at rest, a tonal fill on hover.
275	- **Sizes:** default 44px tall, small 40px, large 48px, icon 44 by 44. Every size clears the 44px touch minimum by being that height, never by relying on wrapped text or padding accidents.
276	- **Focus:** a solid two-pixel ink outline, offset two pixels from the edge. This is the only focus treatment in the Workroom.
277	- **Hall CTA:** 60px tall, near-square corners, transparent fill over a bronze hairline, with a bronze sheen that sweeps across on hover and a cyan outline on focus.
278	
279	### Cards / Containers
280	
281	- **Corner:** 8px.
282	- **Background:** card white on the page ground.
283	- **Border:** one-pixel hairline. This is what separates the card, not a shadow.
284	- **Shadow:** none.
285	- **Padding:** 24px, with header, content and footer sharing that inset.
286	
287	### Inputs / Fields
288	
289	- **Style:** page background, one-pixel field border, 6px corners, 44px minimum height, 8px by 12px padding.
290	- **Focus:** the same two-pixel ink outline as buttons, offset two pixels. Do not express focus as a border colour change alone; a border shift is too quiet to serve as the only indicator.
291	- **Mobile:** 16px text below 768px, so focusing a field never zooms the viewport.
292	
293	### Navigation
294	
295	Neutral chrome throughout. The sidebar runs one tonal step off the page ground with a hairline edge, and the active item is marked by background tone rather than by a coloured rule on its leading edge.
296	
297	### Signature Component: the scoped world
298	
299	The product's distinctive pattern is not a control, it is a container. A room commits fully to its own palette, typefaces and atmosphere behind a single wrapper class, and nothing inside it leaks out. Scene layers are `aria-hidden` furniture painted beneath the content, so the accessible tree is identical whether the room is painted or bare. A new world is added by writing a new wrapper and its prefixed tokens, never by adding hex to a shared component.
300	
301	## Do's and Don'ts
302	
303	### Do:
304	
305	- **Do** resolve every colour from a token, and treat a token that resolves to nothing as a defect. Verify a new token renders before documenting it.
306	- **Do** give every interactive element a visible focus indicator: a solid two-pixel ink outline offset two pixels in the Workroom, cyan in the Hall.
307	- **Do** check contrast against the ground the colour will actually land on, in the theme that actually ships, in a browser. The theme file has read as correct while the page rendered a light card on a near-black ground.
308	- **Do** separate surfaces with a one-pixel hairline and one tonal step.
309	- **Do** keep colour semantic: correct, incorrect, warning, danger. Nothing else earns it.
310	- **Do** scope a new room's identity behind its own wrapper class with prefixed tokens, the way the bootcamps and the eight Activity worlds already do.
311	- **Do** hold LSAT passage and stimulus text to a readable line length, and leave it on the Outfit question lock.
312	- **Do** end every world's stylesheet with a `prefers-reduced-motion` block that stops continuous motion rather than speeding it up.
313	
314	### Don't:
315	
316	- **Don't** add a `box-shadow` to a Workroom surface. Depth is hairline and tone.
317	- **Don't** carry a Hall colour into the Workroom. Bronze reads 8.58:1 on the Hall's night and 2.30:1 on the Workroom's page.
318	- **Don't** introduce a warm or metallic accent into the Workroom to make it feel premium. Premium here is depth, hierarchy and type.
319	- **Don't** remove the browser's focus outline without shipping a replacement in the same rule. `outline-none` with no working ring is worse than leaving the default alone.
320	- **Don't** animate the focus indicator. A `transition-all` on a control means the ring fades in, which delays the one signal a keyboard user depends on.
321	- **Don't** use a border thicker than one pixel as a coloured accent stripe, including when faked with an inset shadow.
322	- **Don't** put a second typeface into the Workroom chrome, and don't let a serif cross in from the Hall.
323	- **Don't** animate layout properties. Animate `opacity`, `transform` and `color`. No bounce, no elastic, no lift on hover.
324	- **Don't** write a hex value into a component. If a room needs its own colour, promote it to named tokens behind that room's wrapper.

    return "journal";
  }
  // Flagged lives outside the /classroom path but inside the same frame.
  if (pathname === "/flagged" || pathname.startsWith("/flagged/")) {
    return "flagged";

    pathname.startsWith("/classroom/item/")
  ) {
    return "notes";

  if (
    pathname.startsWith("/classroom/ta/") ||
    (/^\/classroom\/[^/]+$/.test(pathname) &&
      pathname !== "/classroom/drill" &&
      pathname !== "/classroom/uploads" &&

  return null;

# Classroom

The student's own room: the first surface a 1:1 student lands on. It is a reading surface, not a workspace. Its language is second person, and the coach is never named in the furniture.

## Terms

**Classroom frame** — the persistent layout every Classroom route is drawn in: the cream left rail, an optional middle list, and the white reading surface. Implemented by `ClassroomLayout`. Navigating between surfaces it draws swaps the content only and never remounts the rail. A page "sits in the Classroom frame" regardless of its URL; `/flagged` and `/journal` both do.

**StudentShell** — the older, separate frame with its own 240px rail, still drawing `/mainhub`, `/practice` and the rest. A student moving between a Classroom-frame page and a StudentShell page sees the whole page rebuild. Reducing that boundary is ongoing work, one surface at a time.

**Reading column** — the white content column. Most routes use one reading column on the right. A Wrong answers detail uses a four-column Classroom frame: cream rail, entries list, vertical question, and review panel. Below its journal-only breakpoints the entries list becomes an overlay and the review panel moves under the question.

**Drill landing workspace** — the pre-question surface at `/classroom/drill`, drawn inside the Classroom frame. It includes drill selection, launch controls, practice insights, and today's work; it excludes the official question-answering experience.
_Avoid_: Drill page (when the question experience could be confused with the landing surface), Mainhub

**Question finder** — the one control that turns something a student can say about a question into that question opened. It takes free text, matched against question text, and it takes a question address (PT 90, Section 1, Question 12) typed or picked from PrepTest / Section / Question selectors. It searches both banks and groups its results by bank, Logical Reasoning first, because a hit lands differently in each: an LR hit opens that one question, an RC hit opens its whole passage with every question on it. The finder is a finding aid, not the scope of the work it opens. It appears on Classroom Home and in the Drill landing workspace; it never builds a set, it hands that to the Drill console.
_Avoid_: search bar, command palette, spotlight, quick access, find bar

**Drill console** — the control surface inside the Drill landing workspace where a student assembles a set: the filters, the preview of the exact set, and the start action. Finding one named question is not its job; that is the Question finder.
_Avoid_: Control pane, picker card

**Drill canvas** — the route-specific visual composition to the right of the persistent Classroom rail, containing the Drill hero, Drill console, practice/insight workspace, and Today focus. The Classroom rail is not part of the canvas.
_Avoid_: Drill side pane, dashboard grid

**Section** — a middle-column list paired with the reading column: Notes and recaps, Assigned work, Flagged. A section is picked in the rail, lists its items in the middle column, and shows the chosen item in the reading column.

**Flag** — a come-back-to-this marker the student raises *during* a question, from a drill or a blind review. It records intent to return, and says nothing about whether the question was answered correctly. Stored in `flagged_questions`.

**Flagged question** — a question carrying a flag. The Flagged section is the student's unfinished business: questions they chose to set aside. Clearing a flag is the student's own act (Unflag), not something the system does when they get the question right.

**Wrong answer** — a question the student actually missed, recorded in `wrong_answer_journal` and worked through in Wrong answers at `/journal`. **Ask** — sending a question to the coach from the surface the student is looking at, with their own comment attached. Always labelled "Ask", never with the coach's name; the name belongs in messaging itself, not in the furniture that leads to it.

# Assessment A — LLM Design Review: "Notes and recaps" (`/classroom/recaps`)

**Method:** read `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md`, `src/components/classroom/CONTEXT.md`, and the five named source files; inspected the live production page in my own tab (tab-2) in the shipped light theme at 1440×900 and 375×812, with measured computed styles and contrast ratios.

**Caveat on the data I saw:** I was signed in as Joshua (`is_admin`, role label "Teacher"), so the rail carried an extra "Teaching" group a student never sees, and the three notes present are his own test content — one is literally titled *"Verification: unread notes"* with the body *"Safe to delete."* That is not a design finding, but it does mean I could not observe a real student's note mix. Everything below about the zero-note path comes from reading the code, not from mutating data.

## 1. AI slop verdict

**No. This does not read as AI-made, and it is not close.** It reads as hand-built and opinionated — a warm paper room with hairline separation and nothing else. Against the absolute bans:

| Ban | Status |

| Side-stripe borders (>1px coloured leading edge) | **Clean** on this surface. The only >1px left borders in `classroom.css` are a blockquote rule (`classroom.css:2343`) and journal callouts (`:2704`, `:2749`), none of which render here. |
| Gradient text | **Clean.** No `background-clip: text` anywhere. |

- **A second typeface in Workroom chrome — confirmed.** `classroom.css:39` sets `.classroom-root { font-family: "Outfit", … }`, and `<body>` is Lexend. The classroom rail *is* the corridor. Either DESIGN.md is stale here or the classroom is out of bounds; the record as written says out of bounds.
- **Colour used for something other than meaning — confirmed.** `--classroom-link: #ff8906` (`classroom.css:14`) fills the Messages unread badge; the panel-wash gradients (`classroom.css:130-146`) paint a decorative warm sheen on the rail and list card. Neither says correct, incorrect, warn, or danger.
- **A token that resolves to nothing — confirmed, and it is the exact failure DESIGN.md's Named Ground Rule was written for.** `.classroom-unread-dot` and `.classroom-unread-count` consume `var(--classroom-accent, …)` and `var(--classroom-surface, #fff)` at `classroom.css:4514`, `:4528-4529`. Neither variable is defined anywhere in `src/`. The unread dot therefore falls back to `currentColor` — the same ink as the note title next to it — so the "unread" marker renders as a bullet point. **The real failure mode here is the opposite of slop.** Slop is over-decorated sameness. This is under-designed emptiness.

## 2. Nielsen's 10 heuristics — 21/40

**1. Visibility of system status — 2/4.** Read/unread is modelled honestly and sorted unread-first. But the only status sentence on the page, "All 3 read.", renders at **2.10:1 contrast** and is `display: none` below 1280px (`classroom.css:4484`), so on mobile the student gets no count at all. Nothing shows how many notes exist, when the last one arrived relative to their last session, or that more are coming.

**2. Match with the real world — 2/4.** "Notes and recaps" silently merges four DB types — `note`, `session_recap`, `announcement`, `smartboard_recap` (`classroomData.ts:12-17`) — and **no row shows which one it is.** The page title promises two categories and delivers an undifferentiated list. When you open one, the kind label is the raw enum with underscores swapped (`ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:168`: `item.type.replace(/_/g, " ")`), rendering lowercase "note" / "smartboard recap". "Smartboard recap" is internal vocabulary.

**3. User control and freedom — 2/4.** The section overlay (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:1104-1119`) and the mobile menu (`:1024-1053`) have **no `role="dialog"`, no `aria-modal`, no focus trap, no Escape handler, and no focus return.** The only `Escape` listener in the file is at `:160`, for the profile menu. A keyboard user can tab straight out of an open drawer into the page underneath it. No breadcrumb or back control in the note view; back is browser-only.

**4. Consistency and standards — 2/4.** The Flagged panel and the Wrong answers panel each get a search field (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:635`, `:779`) and each get an empty-state sentence (`:722`, `:822`). Notes gets neither. Same shell, same panel component, three different levels of finish. The `.classroom-empty-copy` class — named for empty states — is also used to render a note's real `description` (`ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:171`), so authored content inherits empty-state styling.

**5. Error prevention — 3/4.** Little to get wrong. Error and loading states exist and are real (`DetailError`, `DetailLoading`). Deducting only because the "This item is no longer available" fallback (`ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:144-161`) fires for *any* id miss including a still-loading cache, and offers only "Back to your room".

**6. Recognition over recall — 2/4.** A row is a title and a date, nothing else. To know whether "Conditional Logic: the Cheat Sheet" is a recap of your 23 Aug session or a standalone handout, you must open it. Two of the three rows I saw differ only by a parenthetical. No excerpt, no type, no length, no session link.

**7. Flexibility and efficiency — 1/4.** No search, no filter, no type toggle, no month grouping, no keyboard shortcut. Fine at three notes; unusable at forty session recaps a year in, which is exactly the trajectory of a 25-sessions-a-week practice. The sibling panels already solved this and the solution was not carried over.

**8. Aesthetic and minimalist design — 2/4.** The aesthetic is good — genuinely calm, warm, un-generic. The *proportion* is broken. At 1440px: sidebar 336px + section list 336px = **672px of chrome (47% of the viewport) before content**, and the 784×900 stage holds **89 pixels of content** (measured: dateline 22px, h1 41px, hint 25px). This is not minimalism; it is a layout that forgot to fill its largest region.

**9. Error recovery — 3/4.** `DetailError` gives a plain message and a working "Try again" that calls `query.refetch()`. Good. Deducting for the 27 uncaught 404s in the console on load and for `classroom-panel-search:focus { outline: none }` (`classroom.css:2503-2505`), which removes the focus ring and replaces it with a border-colour change — the exact thing DESIGN.md:290 says is too quiet to be the only indicator.

**10. Help and documentation — 2/4.** **The page says nothing about what these items are.** There is no sentence explaining that a coach writes them, that recaps follow sessions, or what to do with one. The only prose on the entire index is a count. Not zero, because the note bodies themselves teach well.

## 3. Cognitive load

**Decision point 1 — landing at desktop.** Visible options: 14 sidebar nav buttons for a student (17 for an admin), plus brand, profile trigger, Log Out, the panel heading, and 3 note rows. **~20 competing targets. Flag: >4 by 5×.** Zero of them are in the stage, which is where the eye goes first because it is the biggest, brightest, emptiest region on screen.

**Decision point 2 — which note.** 3 today, unbounded and unfiltered tomorrow. Rows are visually identical; the only discriminators are a title and a date at 2.10:1 contrast.

**Decision point 3 — inside a note.** **Zero options.** The note ends and the page stops. No Ask, no next, no related work, no mark-done, no back.

**Progressive disclosure:** structurally present (rail → list → item is a sound three-pane), but **inverted at desktop**. The 784px pane discloses 89px; the 336px pane carries the entire payload. The disclosure gradient runs the wrong way.

## 4. Emotional journey

**Arrival:** calm and slightly warm. The cream rail and the paper wash read as a study, not a SaaS dashboard. This is a real asset and it is rare.

**Two seconds in:** the eye lands on 700px of white with a 36px heading and one sentence too faint to read, and the feeling flips to *"did this fail to load?"* Blank space in the largest, brightest region does not read as restraint. It reads as absence.

**Opening a note:** the peak, and it is genuinely good. Real prose, a real argument, authored typography carried by the note's own `<style>` (`classroomData.ts:34-68`). This is the moment the coach exists.

**The end:** the note stops mid-air. No signature, no "ask me about this", no next thing. **Peak-end lands badly:** a strong peak followed by a dead stop, wrapped by an index that evokes emptiness.

**Against "a real coach is in it":** the index is the weakest link in the whole claim. Three rows of title-plus-date are indistinguishable from an RSS feed. Nothing signals a human wrote them, met this student, or looked at their work. `CONTEXT.md` deliberately says "the coach is never named in the furniture" and that is a defensible rule — but a list *of his writing* is not furniture, and the rule as applied has stripped the surface of the one thing PRODUCT.md says a competitor cannot copy.

## 5. What's working

1. **The read/unread model is Principle 1 done properly, including the hard part.** `classroomData.ts:81-89` documents that read state deliberately does *not* live in `status`, because the DB's `classroom_items_status_check` rejects the `'started'` value the TypeScript type declares. Rather than write a value the database would refuse, it stamps `metadata_json.read_at`. The marker is also never colour-only: `StageIndexList.tsx:52-57` pairs the dot with an `sr-only " (unread)"`, and `classroom.css:4501-4506` records the reasoning. This is the standard the rest of the surface should be held to.

2. **The panel and the page cannot disagree about ordering.** Both call the same `groupNotes` (`classroomData.ts:106-114`), and `ClassroomLayout.tsx:583-585` says why in one sentence. A whole class of "the list says one thing and the page says another" bug is designed out rather than tested for.

3. **The room is genuinely flat.** Measured 0 shadowed elements under `.classroom-root` on the live page. Depth comes from a 1px `--classroom-line` and one tonal step, exactly as DESIGN.md's Flat Rule asks — and this surface honours it while the shared `ui/card.tsx` still does not.

## 6. Priority issues

### P0-1 — The main content column is empty at desktop width
**What.** At ≥1280px, `.classroom-stage-index` is `display: none` (`classroom.css:4473-4477`), so `ClassroomRecapsView` renders only a dateline, an `<h1>`, and a one-line count into a 784×900 stage. Measured live at 1440×900: **89px of content in a 900px column.** The list the page is *for* lives entirely in the 336px side panel. The `StageIndexList` doc comment (`StageIndexList.tsx:12-25`) shows this was a deliberate fix for *narrow* windows — the wide case was never revisited.

**Why it matters.** The largest and brightest region of the screen is the one carrying the least information, so the page reads as broken on first sight. It also violates Principle 2 directly: a student arriving here is shown a menu in the periphery and told nothing in the centre.

**Fix.** Stop treating the stage as a placeholder that the panel happens to duplicate. Give the index route a stage worth landing on: the newest unread note **opened inline** (or its first ~150 words with a "read it" control), a one-line statement of what this surface is, and the "Ask" affordance `CONTEXT.md` already defines. If the intent is genuinely master-detail, then redirect `/classroom/recaps` to the newest note the way a mail client does, and let the panel be the index. Either is defensible; rendering an empty column is not. **P0.**

### P0-2 — Every piece of secondary text on the surface fails WCAG AA, measured in the shipped theme
**What.** `--classroom-meta-soft: rgba(67, 64, 60, 0.4)` (`classroom.css:25`) is used as a *text* colour. Measured in the browser on the live white ground:

| Element | Colour | Size | Contrast | AA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dateline "September 8, 2026 \| Classroom" | rgba(67,64,60,.4) | 14px | **2.10:1** | fail |
| "All 3 read." (the page's only sentence) | rgba(67,64,60,.4) | 15px | **2.10:1** | fail |
| Every row date, "Aug 24, 2026" | rgba(67,64,60,.4) | 12px | **2.10:1** | fail |
| Rail group labels (CLASSROOM/STUDY/REVIEW) | rgb(166,162,158) | 12px | **2.54:1** | fail |
| Role label "Student"/"Teacher" | rgb(166,162,158) | 12px | **2.54:1** | fail |
| Messages unread badge, white on `#ff8906` | — | 11px | **2.38:1** | fail |
| `.classroom-panel-empty` (flagged/journal empties) | rgba(67,64,60,.4) | 13px | **2.10:1** | fail |

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md:99 makes AA a binding defect gate, not an aspiration. And `classroom.css:15-22` **already documents this exact problem and already ships the fix**: `--classroom-meta-text: #6d6a67` (5.38:1) and `--classroom-link-text: #a25704` (5.39:1), with the comment "Use these two instead whenever the colour carries words or notation." The badge carries a *number*. The tokens exist and are used 2 times against 15 uses of `--classroom-meta-soft` and 53 of `rgb(var(--classroom-meta))`.

**Fix.** Swap `--classroom-meta-soft` → `--classroom-meta-text` at every site where the value lands on text (`.classroom-dateline` `:2063`, `.classroom-empty-copy` `:2480`, `.classroom-panel-item time` `:2707`, `.classroom-panel-empty` `:2509`, `.classroom-nav-group > b`, `.classroom-profile-copy small`), and `--classroom-link` → `--classroom-link-text` on `.classroom-nav-badge` `:2299`. Keep `-soft` for rules and fills only. This is a token swap, not a redesign, and the file already argues for it. **P0.**

### P1-3 — A student with zero notes sees a completely blank page
**What.** Read the path. In `ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:215`, everything below the heading is gated on `total > 0`, so at zero notes the stage renders *only* the dateline and the `<h1>` — no hint, no list, no copy. In parallel, `ClassroomLayout.tsx:717-727` renders `.classroom-panel-empty` **only when `section === "flagged"`**; the `notes` and `work` branches have no empty state at all. So a new student who clicks "Notes and recaps" gets a panel with a heading and nothing under it, beside a stage with a heading and nothing under it. Roughly 90% of the viewport is blank white.

**Why it matters.** This is a direct hit on Principle 1: *"an honest empty state beats invented content. If nothing has been assigned, the product says so and stops."* It does not say so. It stops without saying. And it is the surface a brand-new 1:1 student is most likely to click first, looking for evidence that a coach is on the other side.

**Fix.** The correct copy is already written elsewhere in the repo — `RecapsSection.tsx:20`: *"Recaps from your sessions with Joshua will show up here."* Render an empty state in **both** columns: the panel gets a `.classroom-panel-empty` line for `notes` and `work` (matching flagged and journal), and the stage gets a short paragraph saying what notes and recaps are, that Joshua writes them after sessions, and — if the student has a session booked — when the first one is expected. Nothing invented; all of it traces to real data. **P1.**

### P1-4 — Below 1280px the same list is offered twice, once inline and once in a drawer
**What.** Two mechanisms fire at the *same* breakpoint. `.classroom-stage-index` becomes `display: grid` at `max-width: 1279px` (`classroom.css:4479-4482`), and `.classroom-section-trigger` becomes `display: flex` at `max-width: 1279px` (`classroom.css:3044-3052`). Verified live at 375px: the stage shows the three notes inline *and*, above the `<h1>`, a full-width 48px button labelled "Open the list" whose drawer contains the same three notes. The trigger is also the **first** thing in the reading column, above the page title.

**Why it matters.** The student is offered a control to reach content already on screen. It is the only prominent button on the page, so it attracts the tap that should have gone to a note. It also puts a chrome control ahead of the page's own `<h1>` in both visual and DOM order.

**Fix.** The trigger exists for routes with no inline index (a note detail, an assignment). Suppress it on routes that render a `StageIndexList` — pass a flag through `ClassroomLayoutContext` from the index views, or scope the trigger's `display: flex` to `:not(:has(~ * .classroom-stage-index))`. Whichever, the invariant is: **never show both.** **P1.**

### P2-5 — A note is a dead end, and the row tells you nothing before you open it
**What.** `ClassroomItemView` (`ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:163-185`) renders heading, description, body, and stops. No author line, no "Ask", no link to the session it recaps, no next note, no back. And on the way in, a row (`StageIndexList.tsx:51-59`, `ClassroomLayout.tsx:665-682`) shows only title + date — no type, so a `session_recap`, an `announcement` and a `smartboard_recap` are indistinguishable on a page titled "Notes **and** recaps".

**Why it matters.** Principle 2 says compute the next step. The end of a note is the highest-intent moment on the surface — the student has just read something their coach wrote about them — and the product does nothing with it. `CONTEXT.md` already defines the affordance ("**Ask** — sending a question to the coach from the surface the student is looking at, with their own comment attached. Always labelled 'Ask'"), and this surface does not use it.

**Fix.** (a) Add the type as a quiet meta line on each row, using student-facing words, not the DB enum — "Session recap · 23 Aug" vs "Note · 24 Aug". Map `smartboard_recap` to something a student would say. (b) Put an "Ask" control at the foot of a note, prefilled with the note's title, routing to the existing 1:1 inbox thread. (c) Add "Next note" / "Back to notes and recaps" so the reading column is navigable on its own at every width. **P2.**

## 7. Minor observations

- **Overlay semantics.** `.classroom-section-overlay` and `.classroom-mobile-menu` use a `<button class="classroom-overlay-backdrop">` as scrim with no `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, focus trap, Escape handler, or focus restore (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:1024-1053`, `:1104-1119`). Roll them onto the Radix Dialog already in the dependency tree.
- **34px rail targets.** `.classroom-nav` computes to **34px** tall (`classroom.css:244-261`: 14px × 1.45 + 7px padding × 2). PRODUCT.md:104 requires 44×44 "by being that size rather than by a side effect". The same class draws the **mobile drawer**, where it is a genuine touch violation. The mobile menu button itself is a correct 44×44 (`classroom.css:2417-2422`).
- **The dateline on an index prints today's date.** `ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:206` passes `new Date().toISOString()`, so the page reads "September 8, 2026 | Classroom" above a list whose newest item is 24 Aug. It looks like a publication date and dates nothing. Same pattern in `ClassroomUploadsView:251` and three not-found states. Drop the dateline on index routes.
- **Three identical strings on one screen.** "Notes and recaps" appears as the active rail item, the panel `<h2>`, and the stage `<h1>` simultaneously. DOM heading order is h2 → h3 → h3 → h3 → h1.
- **`<h3>` inside `<button>`** on every row, in both list copies. Works for the accessible name, but a heading that is also a control is unusual; `aria-label` on the button with a plain `<span>` inside would be cleaner.
- **27 uncaught 404s** in the console on page load, plus `Found 4 questions with unknown types`.
- **Notes can restyle themselves.** `sanitizeClassroomHtml` (`classroomData.ts:34-68`) deliberately allows `<style>`, and it is well-reasoned and well-commented. But it means each note can carry arbitrary CSS into the reading column — the one I opened rendered its own small-caps eyebrow. There is no containment (no `@scope`, no shadow root), so a note's `h2 { }` styles the room, not just the note.
- **`.classroom-panel-search:focus { outline: none }`** (`classroom.css:2503-2505`) removes the ring in favour of a border-colour change on the Flagged and Journal panels. Same shell, and DESIGN.md:290 names this specifically.
- **Hex literals in the stylesheet outside the token block:** `#ffffff` at `:290` and `:302`, `#fff` at `:4529`.

## 8. Provocative questions

1. **Is this page a page, or is it a redirect that hasn't admitted it?** Mail clients don't render an "Inbox" landing screen — they open the newest message. If the panel is the index at every width where it exists, what is `/classroom/recaps` actually *for*, and would the surface be better if it simply opened the newest unread note?

2. **The rule is "the coach is never named in the furniture." Is a list of his writing furniture?** Applied literally, the rule has produced an index of the coach's work in which the coach is invisible — the exact opposite of "a real coach is in it." Where is the seam between chrome and his voice, and does this surface fall on the wrong side of it?

3. **Why do notes arrive without a next step when everything else in the product computes one?** A note is the highest-intent artefact the platform holds — a human wrote it about this student. It ends in silence. What would the surface look like if every note ended with the one thing Joshua would say next?

4. **What breaks at forty notes?** The Flagged and Journal panels both got search. Notes did not. With ~25 sessions a week, this list is measured in hundreds within a year. Does it need search, or does it need something the other two don't — a session timeline, where a recap sits beside the work it produced?

5. **If "Notes and recaps" merges four record types, is the page named after the student's mental model or after a database filter?** `NOTE_TYPES` is a `Set` of four enums. A student does not think "note vs. smartboard recap." What are the two or three things they *do* think, and does the page's name and grouping match them?

6. **What is the classroom, architecturally?** DESIGN.md says corridors are never worlds, and the classroom rail is the corridor — yet the classroom ships its own typeface, its own warm palette, and its own orange accent. Either DESIGN.md needs a sanctioned entry for `.classroom-root` with its own brief under `.impeccable/surfaces/` (there is none — only `drill-hub.md` and `src-features-activity-centre.md`), or the classroom needs to come back to the Workroom's Lexend and neutrals. It is currently neither, and the gap is invisible until someone measures it.
