191	## Session recaps: write a reusable reference, not a play-by-play (2026-09-12)
192	
193	**Cut:** an opener saying the student got two questions right, followed by a summary of
194	which answer they chose and how they explained it.
195

197	short diagnostic check, and the exact practice they need to complete.

201	a compact study note, not a record of the conversation. Name a question only when the
202	details make the concept easier to apply. Praise belongs only when it identifies a
203	durable skill or meaningful change, not merely that an answer was correct.

206	concept, show the logic clearly, and finish with tangible practice.

211	without checking whether the stimulus supplied a fact at all.

214	relationship. It tells you what follows if its trigger is present, but it does not say
215	that the trigger occurred. Some stimuli contain rules only, so the work is to chain
216	them. When a fact is present, apply a matching rule or a valid contrapositive.

On Inference questions (Most Supported / Must Be True / Agree-Disagree), we're always reading for Overlapping Information.
The common denominator in the first and second sentence here is the concept of "large bodies of water whose temp exceeds 26º at a depth of 60m".
And whenever we see conditional or causal wording, we ask ourselves whether there's any chain of conditional/causal ideas.
When we see conditional/causal + an Overlapping Concept, it suggests that we should be connecting a conditional/causal chain.
For example,GIVEN:If X, then Y. X causes Y. If Y, then Z. Y causes Z.INFER:If X, then Z X causes Z.
In both examples, the overlapping idea of Y is allowing us to connect X and Z.
