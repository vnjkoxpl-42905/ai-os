# 2. How the Kling plate is composited

## 2.1 The rule that removes the hard problem

**The Kling plate contains no monitor.** The prompt asks for an empty desk, a window camera-left, a wall, soft daylight, dust in the light. Remotion draws the entire monitor: bezel, chin, stand, screen, glow spill, contact shadow.

One line of reasoning: generative video cannot be trusted to hold a rectangle still across 15 seconds, and there is no tracker on this machine, so we never ask it to draw the one rectangle everything must lock to.

Consequences, all good:
- No perspective solve at composite time. The plate is authored square-on to the desk, so the monitor is square-on to camera, and at rest the screen transform is `translate(448px, 236px) scale(1.3)`. No `matrix3d`, no keystone, no rotation.
- No plate drift can show a seam, because the bezel is opaque Remotion geometry, not a matte over generated pixels.
- No hallucinated second screen, no hallucinated text, no glowing fake UI. The Round 3 constraint at brainstorm line 285 is satisfied structurally.

## 2.2 The layer stack, bottom to top

| z | Layer | Component | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Kling plate | `<Plate>` / `OffthreadVideo` | 3840x2160 source, drawn into a 2560x1440 frame at `scale = cam.scale * (2560/3840) * OVERSCAN`, `OVERSCAN = 1.18` so pans have headroom. Downsampling 4K to 1440p is what kills generative shimmer. |
| 1 | Grade | CSS filter on the plate | `saturate(0.94) contrast(1.04) brightness(1.02)` plus a `linear-gradient` warm-cool split matching `KEY_LIGHT_DEG`. |
| 2 | Screen glow spill | radial gradient, `mix-blend-mode: screen`, 8% | Sells the monitor as a light source in the room. Sits on the desk, not on the bezel. |
| 3 | Monitor | `<Monitor>` | Bezel `#1B1B1F` with a left-edge specular from `KEY_LIGHT_DEG`, chin, stand, and a soft contact shadow ellipse on the desk. |
| 4 | Screen | `<Screen>` inside `<Monitor>` | Sized `SCREEN.w x SCREEN.h`, containing a 1280x720 div at `transform: scale(1.3); transform-origin: 0 0`. |
| 5 | Room layer, far | `<RoomLayer depth={0.85}>` | The `ChunkMarker`. Sits beside the monitor, in front of the bezel plane. |
| 6 | Room layer, near | `<RoomLayer depth={1.0}>` | Popups and `SpaceKey`. Largest parallax. |
| 7 | Captions | `<RoomCaptions>` | Locked to frame, not to room. Subtitles are an accessibility surface, not a prop. |
| 8 | Grain and vignette | static | Grain at 3%, `mix-blend-mode: overlay`. Vignette 12%. Both above everything so the composite reads as one photograph. Neither touches contrast inside `SCREEN` by more than 2%, which check 20 enforces. |

## 2.3 Perspective matching

Matched at prompt time, not at composite time. Three things must agree and all three are one constant:

1. `KEY_LIGHT_DEG = 285` (window camera-left, slightly above). The plate prompt states it. The bezel specular gradient, the popup drop shadows, and the marker's desk shadow all derive their angle from it.
2. Camera height. The plate prompt fixes the horizon so the desk surface runs across the lower third. `SCREEN.y = 236` and the stand geometry are set against that horizon once, from a still, and never move.
3. Focal feel. The plate is prompted "50mm equivalent, no wide angle distortion". A 50mm view of a monitor at 70cm is effectively rectilinear over the monitor's angular size, which is what makes the zero-perspective screen honest.

During a camera move, a real pan of a flat plane keystones. So:

```ts
const cam = cameraAt(frame, track);
const m = cam.yaw === 0 ? null : matrix3dFromQuad(SCREEN.w, SCREEN.h, quadForYaw(cam.yaw, cam.x, cam.scale));
```

`matrix3d` is applied **only while `cam.yaw !== 0`**, which is only during look-aways, when the monitor is leaving frame and its text is not being read. At rest, yaw is exactly 0 and the transform is a plain 2D scale and translate.

## 2.4 How the screen stays pixel-legible

Four separate mechanisms, each one checkable:

1. **Output resolution carries it.** The stimulus is 19 logical px Fraunces. At `SCALE = 1.3` that is **24.7 device px** in a 2560x1440 frame. At 1920x1080 it would be 18.5 px, which is the size that made the v1 render marginal.
2. **The scale factor is exact.** 1664/1280 and 936/720 are both exactly 1.3. No fractional-pixel resampling of the app's own hairlines and borders.
3. **No perspective at rest**, so Chrome's text rasteriser stays on the 2D path.
4. **The `ScreenOnly` composition is the ground truth.** It renders `<Screen>` natively at 1664x936, no scale, no plate, no room. Check 20 crops the composited frame to `SCREEN` and computes SSIM against the `ScreenOnly` still at 12 timestamps. Gate is **SSIM >= 0.985**.

If Chrome under-rasterises the `scale(1.3)` layer, check 20 fails and the fix is known: give `Screen` a `u` unit prop and express every length as `u * n` with `u = 1.3`, removing the transform entirely. That is the fallback, gated, not assumed.

# 3. The amber teaching band and the amber chunk highlight

Both die. Explicitly, and for different reasons.

**The band (`TeachBand`, `Stage.tsx`) is deleted.** It is a flat rail across the top of a flat frame carrying the pill "Teaching overlay, not part of the drill". R3-Q13 forbids disclosure of any kind, so the pill is banned outright. And the band's whole geometry, a 96px strip that pushes the app down to 984px, contradicts the POV premise: nothing in a first-person shot of a room has a chrome rail bolted to the top of it. `BAND_H`, `APP_SCALE`, `APP_X`, `AppFrame` are all void. Its teaching content moves into `film/popups.ts` and is spoken by floating glass cards.

**The amber chunk highlight in `Stimulus.tsx` is also deleted**, and this is the less obvious call, so here is the reasoning in full.

Round 1 set the hard constraint: the overlay must never read as app UI, because if it does she expects a highlight in the real drill and there is not one. Round 3 met that constraint by geometry: annotation floats in the room. The chunk highlight cannot float in the room. It is painted inside the paper card. Any per-chunk emphasis rendered into the monitor image reads as app behaviour, and this is true whether the emphasis is amber fill, an underline, a dim on the other chunks, or a shallow depth-of-field falloff. Changing the styling does not change the geometry, which is exactly the failure mode Round 3's decision was designed to be immune to.

So the highlight is **relocated, not removed**. New component `src/pov/ChunkMarker.tsx`:

- A slim vertical bar, 6px wide, 110px tall, `ROOM.accent` at 0.85, rounded ends, with a soft `ROOM.shadow` falling right and down per `KEY_LIGHT_DEG`.
- It hangs in room space at `x = BEZEL.x - 54`, that is **54px outside the left bezel edge**, at a different depth from the screen. It is never inside `SCREEN`. Check 21 proves this for every sampled frame.
- Its `y` is the vertical centre of the Chunk currently being read, computed from a one-time measurement of chunk line boxes exported by `ScreenOnly` into `public/content/chunk-boxes.json`.
- It slides down between Chunks on `power3.inOut` over 420ms, and it fades to 0 the moment `reading` goes false, because after The Read the marker has nothing to point at.
- Governed by `SCAFFOLD[slug].chunkMarker`, so it is on for Q1, on for Q2's first segment only, and off for Q3.

A student cannot mistake this for the app: nothing in her drill floats 54 pixels to the left of her monitor and casts a shadow on her desk. That is Round 1's constraint met by geometry, the same way the popups meet it.

`TEACH.amber` survives only as `ROOM.accent`, shifted from `#f59e0b` to `#C2761B` because the original was picked to sit on a `#1a1635` indigo field and reads acid against warm daylight oak.

# 5. Camera as data

`src/pov/camera.ts` exports one pure function. This is what makes the whole composite lockable without a tracker.

```ts
export type CamKey = { t: number; x: number; y: number; scale: number;
                       yaw: number; focus: number; ease: EaseName };
export function cameraAt(frame: number, fps: number, track: CamKey[]): Cam;
```

- `x, y` are plate-space pixels. `scale` is plate zoom. `yaw` is degrees, non-zero only mid-look-away. `focus` is 0 when the eye is on the screen and 1 when it is on the window.
- Every layer consumes the same `Cam`. The plate translates by `-x`, the monitor translates by `-x * 0.94` (very slight parallax, it is nearly at the plate's depth), the far room layer by `-x * 0.85`, the near room layer by `-x * 1.0`.
- `focus` drives two blurs: plate `blur(focus * 0 -> 0)` inverted, screen `blur(focus * 9px)`, window region sharpens as `focus` rises. **The screen is never blurred while `focus < 0.08`**, and `focus` is exactly 0 for every frame where any read or said utterance is active on screen. That is check 20's precondition and it is asserted directly in check 21b.
- Idle life: a 0.35px amplitude, 0.11 Hz Perlin drift on `x` and `y` at all times, scaled by `plateEnergy`. This is the "breathing" that keeps it from reading as a still, and it is far below the threshold where text resampling is visible.

The look-away in full, `hero` variant, 4.2 seconds:

| t offset | x | scale | yaw | focus | ease |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0 | 1.00 | 0 | 0 | |
| 0.35 | -60 | 1.005 | -0.4 | 0.05 | power2.in |
| 1.60 | -980 | 1.02 | -7.0 | 0.85 | power2.inOut |
| 2.90 | -1040 | 1.02 | -7.4 | 1.00 | power1.inOut |
| 4.20 | 0 | 1.00 | 0 | 0 | power3.inOut |

The `drift` variant is the same shape at 1.4s with `x` peaking at -320 and `yaw` at -2.1, which never fully leaves the monitor. That is the compression R3-Q11 asked for.

Plate handoff: at `t = 0.35` the `desk` plate crossfades over 180ms into the `lookaway` plate at matched velocity, and back at `t = 2.90` into `return`. Because the monitor is Remotion geometry and the camera is data, the monitor keeps moving correctly across the plate cut, which is what sells it as one continuous head turn rather than two shots.

`public/plate/plates.json` therefore needs exactly four clips, all 4K, 16:9, `kling-video-v3_0`, all prompted "locked-off camera, no camera movement, no people, no hands, no monitor, no screen, no text":

| id | contents | length |
|---|---|---|
| desk | empty oak desk, window camera-left out of frame, wall, dust in a shaft of light | 10s |
| lookaway | starts on the desk edge and lamp, pans left to the window | 10s |
| window | window held, outside thrown out of focus, leaves moving | 8s |
| return | window back to the desk edge | 6s |

`desk` is ping-pong looped by `<Plate>`, so 10s covers the whole film.

# 8. Output spec, and the 4K decision

**Master and deliver 2560x1440, 30 fps, H.264 High 4.1, yuv420p, AAC 160 kbps at 48 kHz, faststart.**

The reasoning, since 4K was asked for and is available:

- The video is placed in-app on the RRC briefing page, inside a content column that will be roughly 900 to 1100 CSS px wide. On a 2x display that is about 2200 device pixels. 1440p matches that. 4K is entirely thrown away by the player and costs about 4x the bytes and roughly 4x the render time on a CPU-only box.
- 1080p is the actual failure case, not 4K. The hero object is 19px Fraunces. At 1080p it renders at 18.5 device px and the v1 cut is already marginal. At 1440p it renders at **24.7 device px**, which is comfortable.
- For this material, resolution is nearly free at a fixed bitrate. The stimulus card is static high-detail text: it costs bits once in an I-frame and is almost free in every P-frame after. So 1440p at 3.5 Mbps is genuinely sharper than 1080p at 3.5 Mbps here, which is not true of high-motion footage.
- The Kling plates stay 4K natively and are downsampled into the 1440p frame. That is a gain, not a loss: downsampling is the cheapest available cleanup for generative shimmer and mosquito noise.

Expected delivery size at CRF 21 with `maxrate 6M`: 90 to 150 MB for a 7 to 8 minute film of this kind. Check 29 gates it at **160 MB**. If it exceeds that, the ordered response is: CRF 21 to 23, then re-run check 20; only if SSIM drops below 0.985 do we drop the resolution.

A 1280x720 CRF 26 fallback is also emitted for slow connections. It is not the primary.

export type Payload = {
  stimuli: Record<string, { slug: string; chunks: Chunk[] }>;
  timeline: Record<string, { duration: number; segments: Seg[] }>;
  cues: Record<string, Cue[]>;
};

export const ORDER = ["PT105_S2_Q1", "PT105_S2_Q2", "PT105_S2_Q3"];
export const TITLE_S = 9, READY_S = 4, RECALL_S = 9, OUTRO_S = 9;

/** Frame-exact plan, so duration is derived once and never guessed twice. */
export function plan(timeline: Payload["timeline"], fps: number) {
  const out: { name: string; from: number; frames: number; slug?: string }[] = [];
  let f = 0;
  const push = (name: string, secs: number, slug?: string) => {
    const frames = Math.round(secs * fps);
    out.push({ name, from: f, frames, slug }); f += frames;
  };
  push("title", TITLE_S);
  push("ready", READY_S);
  for (const slug of ORDER) {
    push(`work:${slug}`, timeline[slug].duration, slug);
    push(`recall:${slug}`, RECALL_S, slug);
  }
  push("outro", OUTRO_S);
  return { scenes: out, total: f };
}

export const RRC: React.FC<Payload> = ({ stimuli, timeline, cues }) => {
  const { fps } = useVideoConfig();
  const { scenes } = plan(timeline, fps);
  return (
    <AbsoluteFill style={{ background: SEC.field }}>
      {scenes.map((s) => {
        const i = s.slug ? ORDER.indexOf(s.slug) : -1;
        let inner: React.ReactNode = null;
        if (s.name === "title") inner = <TitleCard />;
        else if (s.name === "ready") inner = <ReadyBeat />;
        else if (s.name.startsWith("work:") && s.slug)
          inner = (
            <Working
              slug={s.slug}
              chunks={stimuli[s.slug].chunks}
              segments={timeline[s.slug].segments}
              cues={cues[s.slug]}
              where="PT105 Section 2"
              qIndex={i + 1}
              qTotal={23}
            />
          );
        else if (s.name.startsWith("recall:") && s.slug)
          inner = <RecallCard detailCount={stimuli[s.slug].chunks.reduce((a, c) => a + c.details.length, 0)} />;
        else if (s.name === "outro") inner = <Outro />;
        return (
          <Sequence key={s.name} from={s.from} durationInFrames={s.frames} name={s.name}>
            {inner}
          </Sequence>
        );
      })}
    </AbsoluteFill>
  );
};

## Corrections

### "Recall Rephrase Colour" → "Read, Recall, Colour"
The door's heading was hardcoded to a name the product does not have, one line
under a chip reading "Read, Recall, Colour". "Rephrase" belongs to a different
Activity, Conditional Rephrase. A test asserted the wrong name, which is why
2,600 passing tests never caught it.
**Lesson:** when a test pins a string, check the string is right, not just that
it matches.

### "Intake only. Nothing comes back out yet…" → plain instruction
Clinical and abstract, and the sentence then told her to read "with full
intonation" while step one is silent reading. The clause that made intonation
internal, "hear it naturally in your head", had been dropped in an earlier edit.
**Lesson:** a word like "intake" reads as jargon. And when copy is trimmed,
check what the trimmed clause was load-bearing for.

### "Three beats through the stimulus" → "Three steps"
"Beat" is the internal code name for one of the four method steps. The glossary
says students see **step**. The interface ten pixels below labelled the same four
things Step 1 to Step 4, so the page used two names for one thing.
**Lesson:** never let an implementation name reach the page.

### "22 pieces to read" → line removed
A **piece** is one chunk, roughly a sentence, and the instructions on that same
page use the word that way. The count was of stimuli, so "22 pieces" read as 22
sentences.
**Lesson:** a unit word must mean one thing per surface. Check what a word
already means on the page before reusing it.

### "say it back" → "tell it back", "The Read" → "The Recall"
The library door still carried both retired names. "The Read" was step four's
former name and collided with step one, which is why it was renamed.
**Lesson:** a rename is not finished until every row carries it. The door was
stale for months because it is a different database row from the sections.

### "then a fourth" → "then a fourth step"
The noun sat three words back and the reader had to carry it.

### "once you have got all of it" → "once you have read all of it"
Ambiguous between having read it and having remembered it, in the one clause
that triggers step four.
**Lesson:** precision matters most where the sentence is an instruction.

### The three-fact notice → one fact
"Nothing is recording yet, the microphone opens on Start, and the section
records in one take that you cannot pause." Two of those are said again in step
four and in the long version, so she skipped the paragraph.
**Lesson:** say it once, in the place it matters.

### The method, told twice → the long version collapsed
The page rendered four numbered steps and then the whole written briefing saying
the same thing at length, both open. She read the method twice and could not
tell which one to follow.
**Lesson:** two explanations of one thing is worse than either alone.

### "PT105.S1 · 24 stimuli · about 25 min" → removed
The section was already the second line of the title. The clock is at the foot
and in the long version. The stimulus count measured the work in a unit she has
no feel for.
**Lesson:** delete a line that repeats what is above it. Do not reword it.
