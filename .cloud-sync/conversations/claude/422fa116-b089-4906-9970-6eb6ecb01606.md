# Aspiring Attorneys tutoring operation

The language of Joshua's tutoring business as the brain models it: who a student
is, how they pay, and how they get on the calendar. This is a glossary, not a
spec. Implementation lives in the skills and scripts; decisions live in
`decisions/log.md`.

## Language

### Paying and scheduling

**Package**:
Five or ten hours of tutoring bought upfront through Stripe, with
between-session coaching included.
_Avoid_: Bundle, plan, subscription, credits (credits are the pass's units, not the product)

**Booking pass**:
The prepaid entitlement a student holds when sessions are **owed** to them,
carrying the count owed and the count used. Reachable at `/book/<slug>`.
_Avoid_: Booking link (that is the pass's URL, not the thing), booking access, pass token

**Owed sessions**:
Sessions a student has paid for and not yet taken. The booking pass is the
**only** record of these; if no pass exists, nothing is owed.
_Avoid_: Balance, remaining sessions, package balance (there is no separate balance model)

**Arrangement**:
What a student actually bought, and when. A ten-hour package on a given date,
or pay-per-session at a given rate. Authored on the student's page, durable,
and never rewritten: it is history, not state.
_Avoid_: Plan, deal, terms

**Live status**:
The regenerated mirror of a student's current backend state, including how many
owed sessions remain. Lives in `live-status.generated.md`, is rebuilt from the
backend, and is never hand-edited or hand-corrected.
_Avoid_: Balance sheet, status page

**Single session**:
One tutoring session paid for individually through a Stripe-linked Cal.com
link, rather than out of a package. Needs no booking pass.
_Avoid_: One-off, ad hoc session, hourly

**Three-tier offer**:
The standard offer presented after a consultation: a single session at $45 per
hour, a five-hour package at $40 per hour, and a ten-hour package at $35 per
hour. Each offer carries its own verified action link. A prospect-specific
arrangement agreed during the consultation replaces the standard offer
entirely.
_Avoid_: Pricing menu, universal rate, package discount table

**Consultation**:
A free introductory call, booked through the Cal.com complimentary link. Does
not make the person a student.
_Avoid_: Intro call, discovery call, free session

**Consultation follow-up**:
A message to a prospect who completed a consultation and has not paid. Payment
ends the consultation follow-up stage and moves the person into onboarding. The
message presents the three tutoring offers so the prospect can choose how to
begin.
_Avoid_: Onboarding email, post-payment email, student follow-up, application form email

**Onboarding**:
The stage triggered by confirmed payment when a new student receives LSAT U
access and completes intake inside the platform. It does not require the person
to reply to the consultation follow-up.
_Avoid_: Consultation follow-up, application form, sales follow-up

# Classroom

The student's own room: the first surface a 1:1 student lands on. It is a reading surface, not a workspace. Its language is second person, and the coach is never named in the furniture.

## Terms

**Classroom frame** — the persistent layout every Classroom route is drawn in: the cream left rail, an optional middle list, and the white reading surface. Implemented by `ClassroomLayout`. Navigating between surfaces it draws swaps the content only and never remounts the rail. A page "sits in the Classroom frame" regardless of its URL; `/flagged` and `/journal` both do.

**StudentShell** — the older, separate frame with its own 240px rail, still drawing `/mainhub`, `/practice` and the rest. A student moving between a Classroom-frame page and a StudentShell page sees the whole page rebuild. Reducing that boundary is ongoing work, one surface at a time.

**Reading column** — the white content column. Most routes use one reading column on the right. A Wrong answers detail uses a four-column Classroom frame: cream rail, entries list, vertical question, and review panel. Below its journal-only breakpoints the entries list becomes an overlay and the review panel moves under the question.

**Drill landing workspace** — the pre-question surface at `/classroom/drill`, drawn inside the Classroom frame. It includes drill selection, launch controls, practice insights, and today's work; it excludes the official question-answering experience.
_Avoid_: Drill page (when the question experience could be confused with the landing surface), Mainhub

**Question finder** — the one control that turns something a student can say about a question into that question opened. It takes free text, matched against question text, and it takes a question address (PT 90, Section 1, Question 12) typed or picked from PrepTest / Section / Question selectors. It searches both banks and groups its results by bank, Logical Reasoning first, because a hit lands differently in each: an LR hit opens that one question, an RC hit opens its whole passage with every question on it. The finder is a finding aid, not the scope of the work it opens. It appears on Classroom Home and in the Drill landing workspace; it never builds a set, it hands that to the Drill console.
_Avoid_: search bar, command palette, spotlight, quick access, find bar

**Drill console** — the control surface inside the Drill landing workspace where a student assembles a set: the filters, the preview of the exact set, and the start action. Finding one named question is not its job; that is the Question finder.
_Avoid_: Control pane, picker card

**Drill canvas** — the route-specific visual composition to the right of the persistent Classroom rail, containing the Drill hero, Drill console, practice/insight workspace, and Today focus. The Classroom rail is not part of the canvas.
_Avoid_: Drill side pane, dashboard grid

**Section** — a middle-column list paired with the reading column: Notes and recaps, Assigned work, Flagged. A section is picked in the rail, lists its items in the middle column, and shows the chosen item in the reading column.

**Flag** — a come-back-to-this marker the student raises *during* a question, from a drill or a blind review. It records intent to return, and says nothing about whether the question was answered correctly. Stored in `flagged_questions`.

**Flagged question** — a question carrying a flag. The Flagged section is the student's unfinished business: questions they chose to set aside. Clearing a flag is the student's own act (Unflag), not something the system does when they get the question right.

**Wrong answer** — a question the student actually missed, recorded in `wrong_answer_journal` and worked through in Wrong answers at `/journal`. Distinct from a flag in both meaning and storage: a flag is chosen, a wrong answer is earned. A question can be both, one, or neither. Do not treat the two lists as views of the same thing.

**Journal entry** — the review of one wrong answer at `/journal/:qid`. The URL is the selection: it resolves only by `qid`, never by the journal row id, and `/journal` does not silently select the first entry. Its review fields autosave without a visible save state.

**Ask** — sending a question to the coach from the surface the student is looking at, with their own comment attached. Always labelled "Ask", never with the coach's name; the name belongs in messaging itself, not in the furniture that leads to it.

## Boundaries

The Classroom owns presentation and the student's own annotations of their work. It does not own the work: assignments, drills, attempts and the question bank all belong to their own contexts and are read here, not authored here.

  error: string | null;

## Hard rules

1. **Chat output only.** Never write the handoff to a file. Never update memory from this skill. (That is `wrapup`'s job, not this one.)
2. **Never invent state.** If a section has nothing to report, write "none" — do not omit the section. Structure stability is the whole point.
3. **Absolute paths always.** The next agent may have a different working directory, or be Codex.
4. **If a plan file drove the session, name it first** in "Key files" so the next agent reads it before anything else.
5. **No emojis, no hype, no "great job" summaries.** Terse and concrete — paths, commands, shell IDs, decisions. Tone of a seasoned engineer handing off at end-of-shift.
6. **Background process IDs are critical.** If you started any `run_in_background` shells, their IDs must appear in "Running state" with the kill command — the next agent cannot find them otherwise.
7. **Stamp verified state, and separate it from assumed state.** Run `date "+%H:%M"` and use the real time. Every claim about live systems (deploy status, DB contents, what was sent, what is published) is either something you actually checked this session — say how — or explicitly marked NOT re-verified. A handoff that presents stale belief as current state makes the next session re-derive everything, which is the exact cost this skill exists to remove.
8. **Open questions are obligations, not notes.** Anything under OPEN must be resolved by the next session or re-asked to Joshua before it starts new work. Requirements have been silently re-typed across sessions, and a promised artifact once never arrived because nobody re-asked. If a question was open in the handoff you are picking up from, it is the first thing you raise.

- Summarizing the last 3 turns and calling it a handoff.
- Listing files by relative path.
- Skipping "Running state" because "nothing is running" — write "none" instead.
- Writing the summary to any file. This is chat-only by design.
- Adding a "what went well / what went poorly" retrospective. This isn't a retro.
- Recommending next steps beyond the single "Pick up here" line. The next agent decides; you just hand off.
