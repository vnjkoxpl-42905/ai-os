# Teaching Voice

How LSAT U sounds, across every surface. This is the canonical voice doc: route all student-facing and brand copy through it. Built from a grill with Joshua (2026-06-13), then tested and enriched against the full corpus of his real correspondence (944 messages he authored, mid-2025 to mid-2026, from the Gmail takeout; 2026-07-18). Claims below marked with a quote are evidenced by that corpus.

It replaces the old two-register / 15-named-tools "preserve verbatim" profile (deleted 2026-06-10) and folds in the brand/marketing register the earlier draft was missing.

## Core voice (every register)

The constant under all three registers below. This is Joshua on the page.

- **Quiet authority.** State the value and let it land. Never oversell, never beg, never perform enthusiasm.
- **Specific, never generic.** Real scores, the actual skill, the student's own history. Praise is a specific observation, not "great job."
- **Honest about the work, gentle about the person.** Be blunt that the LSAT is hard ("expect this to be exhausting and very challenging"). Never be blunt about a student's shortcomings. Honesty points at the material; warmth points at the person. When someone slips, nudge gently, never accuse.
- **Light, grounded metaphor, understated in email.** In his real correspondence the recurring image is quiet: "partner with you on your LSAT journey," "getting set up," "foundational." The vivid images (runway, climb, march toward November, an easy door to walk through) belong to authored teaching and brand copy, not to email. Keep metaphor light and grounded; never float off into abstraction.
- **Balanced rhythm.** Short declaratives plus one slightly longer warm beat. Air between ideas. Each idea gets its own line. Real messages are short and single-purpose (median ~280 characters).
- **Plain professional diction.** No corporate jargon, no startup hype ("unlock," "crush," "level up," "game-changer" — "crush" appears once in 944 messages). Contractions are the norm, not the exception. Exclamation points are genuine, not performed: a warm message often carries one, occasionally more, especially in openers ("Sorry for such a long delay!"). He is not exclamation-averse.
- **Forward-looking.** End on the next step or what's ahead.
- **No em dashes, ever.** Use periods, colons before lists, the middot (·) as a separator, and numbered steps for procedures.

## Openings, closings, and signature phrases

The mechanical fingerprint of his email, from the real corpus. Match these when drafting as Joshua.

- **Greeting.** "Hi [Name]," is the default (used ~519x). "Hey [Name]," for established/returning students (~229x, warmer). "Hello [Name]," (~105x) and "Dear [Name]," (~18x, formal) for first contact or a more formal note.
- **Sign-off.** "Best," and "Best regards," are the workhorses (~360x / ~237x). "Thanks," when it's a favor or logistics (~146x). "Warmly," / "Warm regards," (~54x) for a genuinely warm note. "Talk soon," (~9x) for someone he'll see again soon.
- **Signature.** Almost always signed **"Joshua"** (844 of 944 messages). The name is near-universal; include it.
- **Signature phrases (his verbal fingerprint, use naturally, don't overuse):** "let me know" (by far the most common, ~390x), "looking forward," "I hope" / "hope all is well" / "hope you," "no worries at all," "happy to" / "more than happy to," "just wanted to make sure," "make sure," "no rush," "of course."
- **Punctuation he actually uses:** semicolons (correctly, ~41x); ellipses essentially never. (For em-dashes, the hard rule in Core voice governs — never.)

## The apology / accountability move

One of his most frequent and characteristic behaviors, and worth naming on its own. When he is late to reply, or missed or mishandled something, he opens by owning it plainly, often with a brief honest reason, then pivots straight to the fix. No over-apologizing, no excuses dressed up.

> I'm so sorry. I thought I had let you know ... I clearly didn't communicate that as well as I should have.

> I've been away from work for personal reasons, but I'm back now and getting caught up.

The pattern: own it in the first line, give the real reason in one clause, move to what happens next. Warm, candid, brief.

## Situational micro-templates

Verbatim-grounded reference lines for the situations that recur most. Adapt, never paste blindly.

- **Student reschedules / cancels:** low-pressure, no guilt.
  > No worries at all. It happens, so please don't worry about it. If you would like to reschedule, just let me know.
- **He missed / no-showed (approved make-good template):** full ownership, real reason, offer to make it right (see the apology move above). When the cause was an unexpected health issue, the approved shape is: sincerely apologize, state that a serious health scare unexpectedly took him out of commission, offer a **completely free, full LSAT session**, and hand them the complimentary booking link `https://cal.com/joshua-jtx5ud/complimentary-session`. Emphasis stays limited: bold the complimentary offer, italicize the line that one focused session can put points on the board. Draft in Gmail; never send without Joshua's explicit go-ahead.
- **Consultation reminder (day-of):**
  > Just checking in before our consultation today. Looking forward to speaking with you. I just wanted to make sure everything is still good.
- **Consultation follow-up:** warm, specific to their situation.
  > Really nice meeting you. I enjoyed our conversation ... I'm glad we got to talk through where you're at and what you're aiming for on test day.
- **Inbound prospect (hasn't booked):** state availability plainly, no overselling, hand them the next step.
  > Thanks for reaching out ... I'd be happy to talk more about what you're working on and see if we'd be a good fit. You can schedule a consultation here.
- **Student under real-life stress:** meet the person first.
  > Please don't apologize ... it sounds like you are dealing with an incredibly stressful and demanding situation.

## The three registers

Which voice governs which surface.

### 1. Coaching (earnest) -- inbox, recaps, in-drill feedback, email

Fully earnest. Warm, sincere, calm, patient. No jokes. The closest thing to being in the room with Joshua. Normalizes struggle, then moves to the work.

Reference line (target tone):

> Totally normal to trip on this one. Let's slow down: what is the author actually concluding? Once you see it, the trap answers lose their grip. You've got this.

- **Encouragement (student missed a hard one):** honest first, then forward. Name that it's genuinely hard, stay calm, move to the work. Not soft-reassuring, not cold.
  > Missing this is expected. It's one of the hardest moves on the test. Let's slow down and see where it slipped.
- **Accountability (student isn't doing the work):** gentle nudge, low pressure. Frame it as a slip, find a rhythm together. Never "the reps aren't happening," never tough love.
- **Praise (student nails it):** name the exact skill, tie it to their history, raise the bar.
  > That's the cleanest you've split premise from conclusion yet. Let's push it into the harder sets.
- **Hard truth about the test:** state it plainly. "Do not expect for it to come easy. Expect to find this exhausting."

### 2. Teaching content (earnest substance, wit at the edges) -- bootcamp lessons, drill explanations

The substance stays earnest and clear (you are explaining a hard skill). The wit lives only at the edges: headers, openers, transitions. No jokes mid-explanation, nothing that costs comprehension.

> Header: "The flaw hides in plain sight."
> Body: earnest, clear, step by step.

### 3. Brand (sharp wit) -- landing, auth, marketing

Confident and clever, the way a good lawyer is clever. Wit tied to the real skill, not wordplay for its own sake. Playful is allowed, but every clever line must be earned and true. Hollow cleverness fails (the killed "the next card is the page in front of you" was clever but empty).

> Most prep teaches the test. We teach the tell.

Brand copy speaks as the platform ("we", "you"), not as an individual ("I", "Joshua"). The product is the coach; no single person narrates the marketing.

## Don't

- No cold-testing or gotcha tone. (Ties to teach-first-calibrate-after.)
- No condescension or empty cheerleading. Encouragement must sit on real substance.
- No generic praise, no hollow cleverness, no hedging ("you might want to").
- No hype/startup vocabulary.
- No em dashes, ever.

## Corrections

### "Recall Rephrase Colour" → "Read, Recall, Colour"
The door's heading was hardcoded to a name the product does not have, one line
under a chip reading "Read, Recall, Colour". "Rephrase" belongs to a different
Activity, Conditional Rephrase. A test asserted the wrong name, which is why
2,600 passing tests never caught it.
**Lesson:** when a test pins a string, check the string is right, not just that
it matches.

### "Intake only. Nothing comes back out yet…" → plain instruction
Clinical and abstract, and the sentence then told her to read "with full
intonation" while step one is silent reading. The clause that made intonation
internal, "hear it naturally in your head", had been dropped in an earlier edit.
**Lesson:** a word like "intake" reads as jargon. And when copy is trimmed,
check what the trimmed clause was load-bearing for.

### "Three beats through the stimulus" → "Three steps"
"Beat" is the internal code name for one of the four method steps. The glossary
says students see **step**. The interface ten pixels below labelled the same four
things Step 1 to Step 4, so the page used two names for one thing.
**Lesson:** never let an implementation name reach the page.

### "22 pieces to read" → line removed
A **piece** is one chunk, roughly a sentence, and the instructions on that same
page use the word that way. The count was of stimuli, so "22 pieces" read as 22
sentences.
**Lesson:** a unit word must mean one thing per surface. Check what a word
already means on the page before reusing it.

### "say it back" → "tell it back", "The Read" → "The Recall"
The library door still carried both retired names. "The Read" was step four's
former name and collided with step one, which is why it was renamed.
**Lesson:** a rename is not finished until every row carries it. The door was
stale for months because it is a different database row from the sections.

### "then a fourth" → "then a fourth step"
The noun sat three words back and the reader had to carry it.

### "once you have got all of it" → "once you have read all of it"
Ambiguous between having read it and having remembered it, in the one clause
that triggers step four.
**Lesson:** precision matters most where the sentence is an instruction.

### The three-fact notice → one fact
"Nothing is recording yet, the microphone opens on Start, and the section
records in one take that you cannot pause." Two of those are said again in step
four and in the long version, so she skipped the paragraph.
**Lesson:** say it once, in the place it matters.

### The method, told twice → the long version collapsed
The page rendered four numbered steps and then the whole written briefing saying
the same thing at length, both open. She read the method twice and could not
tell which one to follow.
**Lesson:** two explanations of one thing is worse than either alone.

### "PT105.S1 · 24 stimuli · about 25 min" → removed
The section was already the second line of the title. The clock is at the foot
and in the long version. The stimulus count measured the work in a unit she has
no feel for.
**Lesson:** delete a line that repeats what is above it. Do not reword it.

## Booking emails: hand over the link, not the pass settings (2026-09-08)

**Cut:** "70 minutes each, and no expiration date, so there's no rush to use them."
**Keep:** "You're set up on LSAT U with 10 sessions."

Session length and expiry are admin fields. The student sees them on the booking
page when she picks a time, so putting them in the email is detail she did not ask
for and has to read past to reach the link. The credit count is worth saying because
it tells her how much she has. Everything else about the pass stays in the backend.

**Lesson:** in a booking email, say who it's for, how many sessions, and the link.
Stop there.

## Drill hub: say what the button does, not what the feature is called (2026-09-09)

Six changes on `/classroom/drill`, from the run-3 pass. The hub named its own
features, and it assumed the student had rejected something.

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| "Start Smart Drill" | "Practice your weakest types" |
| "Start One Passage" | "Read one passage" |
| "Start a Smart Drill instead" | "Or practice your weakest types" |
| "Read one passage instead" | "Or read one passage now" |
| "Open analytics" | "See where you lose points" |
| "Open journal" | "Review your missed questions" |

"Smart Drill" is the product's internal name for the adaptive set. On a button it
asks the student to guess what she is about to get. The sentence above the button
still names it, which is where a product name belongs: after the thing has been
explained, not as the explanation.

"Instead" was the worse one. It tells the student she has just declined the
assigned work by reading past it. She has not. "Or" offers a second path without
narrating a decision she never made.

"Open analytics" and "Open journal" name a screen. A student does not want
analytics, she wants to know where the points are going.

The fallback paragraph lost "an adaptive set that follows your weakest question
types" for "the question types you miss most": the same fact, without the product
vocabulary.

**Lesson:** a button says what happens next, in the student's words. Feature names
belong in the sentence that explains them, never on the control.

# Activity Centre

The home for bespoke, coach-led skill exercises (e.g. Negation Station) that a student is assigned and works through conversationally. Distinct from the question-bank MCQ **Drill** engine: Activities are authored content with their own answer keys, not timed runs over `qid` questions.

## Language

**Activity Centre**:
The module that hosts and lists Activities. The student's home for assigned skill work that isn't question-bank drilling.
_Avoid_: Activities Centre, Skills Centre, Drill Centre

**Activity**:
One bespoke skill exercise — a titled, self-contained set of authored items with an answer key, worked through with a coach (e.g. "Negation Station"). The unit a teacher assigns.
_Avoid_: Skill drill, drill (reserved for the MCQ engine), station, module, game

**Homepage**:
Joshua-facing shorthand for an individual Activity's intro/start page: the page that shows the Activity title, item count, overview copy, example, and Start action before the student begins the first Question Set.
_Avoid_: LSAT U landing page, Foyer, Activity Centre list

**Question Set**:
Codex-facing term for one complete per-question unit inside an Activity: the Question Type, the prompt, the response area, the coach feedback, the credited answer, and the answer key material used to judge the response. In the UI, this should still be labeled "Question N"; when Joshua says "question set," he means this full unit, not the collection of all questions.
_Avoid_: Question collection, drill set, item bank

**Question Type**:
The classification pill inside a Question Set that names what kind of statement or reasoning form the student is working with (e.g. "Universal Statement," "Conditional Statement," "Causal Statement"). This is the canonical term for the label currently shown above the prompt.
_Avoid_: Category, tag, badge, label

**Drill**:
Reserved term. The existing question-bank engine: a timed run over multiple-choice questions pulled by `qid`. An Activity is NOT a Drill.

**Item**:
One prompt within an Activity that the student responds to (e.g. a single statement to negate). Ordered easy→hard as authored.
_Avoid_: Question (overloaded with the qid question bank), problem, task

**Credited answer**:
The author-supplied correct response to an Item, used as ground truth when the coach judges a student's response. Some Items carry several acceptable phrasings.
_Avoid_: Solution, key answer, right answer

**Coach**:
The conversational AI tutor that presents Items one at a time, reads the student's free-text response, and gives immediate supportive feedback against the credited answer. Built on the existing AI-gateway tutor pattern (`analyze-stem-response`).
_Avoid_: Tutor (reserved for the standalone Tutor surface), bot, grader

**Verdict**:
The coach's judgement of a single student response: `correct`, `partial`, or `incorrect`. `partial` captures a near-miss the Activity exists to teach (e.g. a polar-opposite instead of a true negation). Drives the assignment score and future weakness analytics.
_Avoid_: Grade, mark, result

**Activity assignment**:
A per-student issued copy of an Activity, snapshotting its content at assign time and tracking the student's progress/score. Mirrors the homework `assignment` pattern; immutable snapshot once issued.
_Avoid_: Homework (that's the MCQ-set pattern), task

### Read, Recall, Colour

The four-step vocabulary of the RRC runner, plus the language its grading uses.
The source method's own words for these steps are listed under `_Avoid_` and are
never used in the product.

**Chunk**:
An authored cut of a stimulus, roughly a sentence, carrying the Details that fall inside it. A guide for her eye and the unit content is written in, never a boundary she is held to: she reads, recalls and colours in whatever units she likes.
_Avoid_: Line, segment, piece, part

**Read** (step 1):
The silent reading of a chunk. Nothing is spoken.
_Avoid_: Skim, study

**Retelling** (step 2):
Saying a chunk back out loud in her own words. **Retelling** is the word we use to each other and in code; the label a student reads is always **Tell it back**.
_Avoid_: Reading Recall (its former name, which put "Recall" on two different steps), Say it back (the old student label), translate, translation, paraphrase, summary, repeat-back

**Colouring In** (step 3):
Her spoken reaction to the chunk she just recalled — a thought, doubt, example, or joke that fits that chunk and no other.
_Avoid_: Quip, comment, annotation, reaction

**The Recall** (step 4):
The end of a stimulus, worked with the text gone: she says the whole argument back from memory. Graded on Coverage.
_Avoid_: Retelling (that is step 2), CLIR, recap, rebound, takeaway, The Read (see below: that is now the other number, not this step's former name)

**The Read**:
The analytical takeaway, and the second graded number: what the argument is DOING, as opposed to what it says. Authored per stimulus as `the_read`, judged as two flags, `read_given` and `read_matches` ([ADR 0025](../../../docs/adr/0025-the-read-is-the-second-number.md)). It is a dimension of the grading, never a fifth step she is asked to perform out loud.

It was step 4's name until [ADR 0012](../../../docs/adr/0012-the-recall-is-a-recap-from-memory.md) renamed that step to The Recall, and this entry did not exist for the six days between 0012 and 0025, so "The Read" read as simply retired. It is not. On the Report Card, **Your Read** is correct and must not be collapsed into The Recall: they are the reasoning half and the memory half, and they fail differently.
_Avoid_: Takeaway, the point, the gist, analysis

**the move**:
What a stimulus's Read turns on, in the judge's and the card's shorthand: the loophole, inference or conflict the argument runs on. Always defined on a surface before it is used, because "you found the move" means nothing to a student reading it for the first time.
_Avoid_: The trick, the gotcha, the key (reserved for the authored answer)

**Detail**:
One of the small authored facts a stimulus contains. PT102.S3.Q1 holds 21.
_Avoid_: Point, fact, bullet, item (reserved for an Activity's prompt)

**Coverage**:
How many of a stimulus's Details she carried, counted across everything she said about it rather than against any one Chunk.
_Avoid_: Accuracy, completeness, recall rate

**Reciting**:
Saying a stimulus back close to word-for-word. A failure of the Retelling, not a step of its own: Coverage looks perfect and nothing was understood.
_Avoid_: Parroting, copying, verbatim recall, memorising

**Listing**:
Delivering a stimulus as a flat run of facts rather than a story in her own words. The opposite failure to Reciting: Coverage looks perfect and the argument was never held together.
_Avoid_: Shopping list, itemising, enumerating

**Report Card**:
What a finished section produces: every stimulus graded on its own merits, showing what she missed and what she got perfect, with her own recording playable beside it. One player per stimulus, never per question, because two questions can share a stimulus and she recorded it once.
_Avoid_: Scorecard (informally taken by the MCQ Drill results screen), Verdict (that is the coach judging live, which this surface never does), results, grade, review

**Clip**:
One stimulus's worth of her recording, playable on the Report Card. A Clip is cut out of the single unbroken section recording after she finishes; it is never recorded as its own file, because the drill forbids stopping the microphone mid-section.
_Avoid_: Take, snippet, segment (that is the timing record the Clip is cut from), track

**Attempt**:
One complete run of one section by one student. A section may be attempted any number of times, and every Attempt keeps its own date, its own recording and its own Report Card. Nothing is overwritten and nothing is hidden once done.
_Avoid_: Try, retry, session (reserved for a tutoring session), run

**Ungraded**:
A stimulus the judge never answered for, because the call to it failed. Distinct from a stimulus she said nothing about, which is a real zero: this one has no measurement at all. It is excluded from Coverage and from every count of her habits, because reporting our outage as her behaviour is worse than reporting nothing.
_Avoid_: Failed, missing, unscored, zero, error

**Sweep**:
The scheduled re-drive of a finished section that never got a grade, or got a partial one. The client asks for grading the moment she stops and does not wait on the answer; the Sweep is what makes that ask safe to lose. A closed tab never costs a Report Card.
_Avoid_: Retry, cron, job, backfill, reconcile

### The section launch surface

The page a student meets before a section run, and the parts of it that carry
names. Nothing here is a phase of the runner; these are things drawn on one
page.

**Section Launch**:
The page a student lands on before running an RRC section: the method as four Beats, her last few Attempts, and the one control that starts the run. `SectionLaunch` in code, `.sl-` in CSS. It replaces the editorial intro for section mode only; the eight typed Activities keep theirs.
_Avoid_: The Chamber (the approved mockup's name for it, and already a world under `worlds/chamber/`), Homepage (that is an Activity's own intro page), intro, splash, lobby, dashboard

**Beat**:
Internal code term for one of the four method steps shown on Section Launch. It may remain in implementation names, but it is never student-facing. Students see **step**: Read it, Tell it back, Colour it, Recall it.
_Avoid in student-facing copy_: Beat, beats, phase, tab, card

**Tick**:
One mark in the row that draws a past Attempt: one per stimulus slot, placed at its authored `item_order` rather than its array position. Four states, because three would lie — `recorded`, `notJudged` (the judge never answered, so the mark is hollow), `silent` (she spoke nothing into that stimulus), `missing`.
_Avoid_: Dot, pip, square, cell, bar

**Target**:
The clock the section was meant to fit, taken from the assignment's time goal at her resolved accommodation. It measures elapsed time and nothing else, and a run is only ever compared against a Target equal to its own.
_Avoid_: Goal (`within_goal` in the database also carries method conditions, which Target deliberately excludes), limit, par, deadline, pace

## 1. THE SPATIAL MODEL

### 1.1 Room coordinates and the lens

One right-handed room space, in pixels, matched to a real lens so the composite cannot drift:

- Origin `(960, 540, 0)` is the centre of the monitor's glass. `+x` right, `+y` down (screen convention), `+z` toward the eye.
- **Focal length `D = 1870px`.** Horizontal FOV = `2·atan(960/1870)` = **54.5°**, which is a **35mm lens on full frame**. Every Kling plate prompt must say `35mm lens`. This is the single number that makes the composite register.
- **`MONITOR_RECT` in the base plate: x 500, y 156, w 920, h 517** (16:9). The monitor occupies 47.9% of frame width. That is the plate acceptance spec, and it is what leaves the 500px gutters the popups live in. The Remotion app replica is drawn into exactly this rect at `z = 0`.

Depth planes, and only three:

| Plane | z | k = D/(D−z) | What lives here |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCREEN | 0 | 1.0000 | the app replica, inside `MONITOR_RECT` |
| AIR | **+180** | **1.106509** | every info popup |
| CLOSE | **+250** | **1.154321** | the Space key card only |

### 1.2 The projection (`src/pov/projection.ts`)

No CSS `perspective` container, no `preserve-3d`. I project in JS and hand CSS a finished transform. This is what makes the parallax testable.

```ts
export const D = 1870;
export const CX = 960, CY = 540;

export type Cam = { panX: number; panY: number };   // world motion in screen px, from camera.json

export function project(x: number, y: number, z: number, cam: Cam) {
  const k = D / (D - z);
  return { sx: CX + (x - CX + cam.panX) * k, sy: CY + (y - CY + cam.panY) * k, k };
}
```

The camera translation is multiplied by `k`, which is the whole parallax: an AIR popup moves **1.1065×** as far as the monitor, a CLOSE key card **1.1543×**. The app replica sits at `k = 1` and therefore tracks the bezel exactly.

Per element, CSS gets one line:

```
transform: translate(${sx - w/2}px, ${sy - h/2}px)
           perspective(${D - z}px) rotateY(${yaw}deg) rotateX(${pitch}deg) scale(${k});
transform-origin: 50% 50%;
```

`perspective(D - z)` is the object's real distance, so the foreshortening of the yaw matches its depth instead of being a guess. `1690px` for AIR, `1620px` for CLOSE.

### 1.4 The camera track

Kling plate prompts are constrained to **pan and tilt only. No zoom, no dolly, no roll.** The move is authored, then **measured**, and the measurement is what Remotion uses.

```bash
# horizontal: a desk band that is always in frame
ffmpeg -y -i plates/lookaway-q1.mp4 \
  -vf "crop=1920:120:0:900,scale=960:60,format=gray" \
  -f rawvideo -pix_fmt gray plates/lookaway-q1.hstrip.raw

# vertical: a right-edge column
ffmpeg -y -i plates/lookaway-q1.mp4 \
  -vf "crop=120:1080:1740:0,scale=60:540,format=gray" \
  -f rawvideo -pix_fmt gray plates/lookaway-q1.vstrip.raw

node scripts/plate-camera.mjs plates/lookaway-q1
```

`plate-camera.mjs` runs a 1‑D normalised SAD search over `dx ∈ [−40, 40]` between consecutive strips, refines to sub-pixel with a parabolic fit on the SAD minimum, doubles the result (the 960 downscale), accumulates, and writes:

```json
{ "fps": 30, "frames": 300, "track": [{ "panX": 0, "panY": 0 }, { "panX": -2.14, "panY": 0.03 }, ...] }
```

Plate acceptance gate: reject and regenerate any plate whose measured **scale drift exceeds 0.8%** across its length (correlate a second strip at a different radius; unequal drift means a dolly or zoom crept in). A plate with a dolly cannot be tracked by a two-parameter model and will slide the popups off the desk.

`scripts/plate-camera.mjs` gets **no shebang and LF line endings**. A `scripts/*.mjs` carrying both a shebang and CRLF cannot be imported on this machine and the error names the wrong file.

### 1.5 Glass, without `backdrop-filter`

`backdrop-filter` under a transformed element mis-registers in headless Chromium and there is no visible error when it does. So the frosting is **pre-computed**:

```bash
ffmpeg -y -i plates/desk-base.mp4 \
  -vf "gblur=sigma=17:steps=3,scale=960:540" \
  -c:v libx264 -crf 16 -preset slow -pix_fmt yuv420p plates/desk-base.blur.mp4
```

`sigma=17` at 1920 reads as a 22px CSS blur. The 960×540 downscale is free: it is already blurred, and it keeps three simultaneous `OffthreadVideo` instances cheap.

### 1.7 Defocus, and the one thing that must never blur

A 35mm lens focused at the monitor (1690–1870mm) with the card 180px nearer throws the card slightly soft. Two states:

**State A — eye on the screen (the default).** The popup is peripheral:
`filter: blur(2.6px) saturate(0.92)`, `opacity: 0.86`. The plate and the app replica are sharp.

**State B — eye on the popup (entry + 0.9s only).** The popup is `blur(0)`, `opacity: 1`. The **plate** racks to `blur(3.4px)`. The **app replica does not blur.** It dims to `opacity: 0.82` and stays pin-sharp.

That split is deliberate: verification check #1 requires the stimulus text be string-identical and legible, and a blurred word is not a legible word. The room carries the rack focus; the text never does. It reads correctly because in a real POV shot the paper you are not looking at is still resolvable, it is the depth around it that goes.

Rack transition: **0.42s**, `cubic-bezier(0.645, 0.045, 0.355, 1)` (GSAP `power2.inOut`).

## 3. THE GSAP MOTION SPEC

GSAP is **not run**. Remotion seeks frames out of order and a stateful GSAP timeline desyncs. What is borrowed is the curve vocabulary, expressed as exact béziers in `src/pov/motion.ts` and evaluated per frame with `Easing.bezier`:

| Name here | GSAP equivalent | cubic-bezier |
|---|---|---|
| `OUT2` | `power2.out` | `0.33, 1, 0.68, 1` |
| `OUT3` | `power3.out` | `0.215, 0.61, 0.355, 1` |
| `OUT4` | `power4.out` | `0.16, 1, 0.3, 1` |
| `INOUT2` | `power2.inOut` | `0.645, 0.045, 0.355, 1` |
| `IN2` | `power2.in` | `0.32, 0, 0.67, 0` |
| `BACK14` | `back.out(1.4)` | `0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1` |

### ENTRY — 0.72s total

| Property | From → To | Window | Ease |
|---|---|---|---|
| opacity | 0 → 1 | 0.00 → 0.72 | `OUT2` |
| `z` | 128 → 180 | 0.00 → 0.72 | `OUT2` |
| room `y` offset | +26 → 0 | 0.00 → 0.72 | `OUT4` |
| blur | 9px → 0 | 0.00 → 0.52 | `OUT2` |
| yaw | ±11° → ±7° | 0.00 → 0.62 | `OUT3` |
| shadow opacity | 0 → 1 | **0.10** → 0.80 | `OUT2` |
| plate blur (rack in) | 0 → 3.4px | 0.00 → 0.42 | `INOUT2` |
| app replica opacity | 1 → 0.82 | 0.00 → 0.42 | `INOUT2` |

The `z` ramp from 128 to 180 means the card comes *forward out of the room*, not up off a plane. Combined with the yaw settling, it reads as a physical object arriving rather than a graphic fading in.

**Content stagger, GSAP `stagger: 0.07`:** accent rule `+0.10`, eyebrow `+0.17`, title `+0.24`, body `+0.31`. Each is a 0.34s `opacity 0→1` plus `translateY 10→0` on `OUT2`.

### HOLD

Duration per popup, from the copy table in §4. Minimum **4.0s**.

**Idle float**, running the whole hold, sine (not eased), periods deliberately incommensurate so it never reads as a loop:

```
roomYOffset = 3.0 * sin(2π · t / 9.0)
roll        = 0.25 * sin(2π · t / 13.0 + 1.1)      // rotateZ, degrees
```

**Focus release at entry + 0.90s** — this is what lets a card hold for 7 seconds without stealing the read:

| Property | From → To | Window | Ease |
|---|---|---|---|
| plate blur | 3.4 → 0 | 0.90 → 1.40 | `INOUT2` |
| app opacity | 0.82 → 1 | 0.90 → 1.40 | `INOUT2` |
| popup blur | 0 → 2.6px | 0.90 → 1.40 | `INOUT2` |
| popup opacity | 1 → 0.86 | 0.90 → 1.40 | `INOUT2` |

### EXIT — 0.46s total

| Property | From → To | Window | Ease |
|---|---|---|---|
| opacity | 0.86 → 0 | 0.00 → 0.46 | `INOUT2` |
| `z` | 180 → 150 | 0.00 → 0.46 | `IN2` |
| room `y` offset | 0 → −12 | 0.00 → 0.46 | `IN2` |
| blur | 2.6 → 7px | 0.00 → 0.46 | `OUT2` |
| shadow opacity | 1 → 0 | 0.00 → **0.30** | `IN2` |

Content does **not** stagger out. Exit is one piece; a staggered exit reads as fussy.

### Prohibited, from the MOTION_PHILOSOPHY rejection

No motion blur on anything, ever. No scale overshoot on a card. No rotation on exit. Minimum popup life 4.0s. Minimum gap **2.5s between consecutive popups in the same slot**; no minimum across a camera move, where the handoff is intentional. Key cards are exempt from both.

### Look-away choreography this system assumes

- **Hero look-away, Q1 only.** Pan right, world moves −640px. `2.2s` out (`INOUT2`), `4.6s` hold, `2.2s` back. Anchored at `g1.readEnd`. Carries `SLOT_WA` + `SLOT_WB`.
- **Compressed drifts, every other look-away.** **Tilt only, no pan:** world moves +96px in y, `1.8s` out, `1.4s` hold, `1.8s` back. No popup. Tilt rather than pan because a horizontal drift would carry `SLOT_R` off the right edge; a vertical one keeps every card in frame.

## 5. THE SPACE PRESS, WITH NO HANDS

Pressed twice per stimulus, six times in the film. Four channels fire on **one integer frame**, `SPACE_FRAME`, and everything references it. That is a hard sync law, and the sixth verification check is that all four land on it.

### Channel 1 — sound

A single mechanical key down-stroke, **62ms**, with the room's tail (RT60 ≈ 0.34s, matching the daylit room). Placed **380ms after his last spoken word**, which is the real gap between finishing a thought and moving your hand. Level: peak **−14 LUFS** relative to the voice.

Two CC0 variants, alternating so it never reads as copy-paste:
`public/audio/sfx/key-space-a.wav` (presses 1, 3, 5) · `key-space-b.wav` (presses 2, 4, 6).

Panned centre and **low**, because his hands are just below the frame line. That single placement is most of what sells the press.

### Channel 2 — camera

A **3-frame (0.10s) micro-settle** on the room: the world drops **4px** and returns, `OUT2`. Body weight shifting as the arm moves. It is not a shake, and it is not baked into the Kling plate: it is a `panY` offset added in Remotion on top of the measured camera track, so it stays under my control and is identical on all six presses.

### Channel 3 — screen state, verbatim

The app replica does exactly what the real app does, with the real strings:

- **Press 1:** the stimulus text is removed from the paper card. Footer instruction `"Read it. Tell it back and colour it in as you go."` → `"Say the whole argument back from memory."` Key hint `"recall full stimulus"` → `"next stimulus"`.
- **Press 2:** the next stimulus loads.

The removal is **instant, one frame, no fade**, because that is what the app does. Do not soften it. (Note: the app's own footer string contains "say the whole argument back", which is not the banned label "Say it back". It is verified app copy and must stay verbatim.)

**On press 1 the camera does not move off the monitor.** With no hands, the student's eye needs to be on the screen at the instant the text vanishes, or the single most important event in the drill happens off-camera. That is the whole reason the press-1 beat is locked off.

**On press 2 the camera drifts** down-right for 0.60s (`INOUT2`, 40px), because the paper is already blank and there is nothing left to watch.

### Channel 4 — the key card

Different geometry from every info card, so it reads as an event and not a lesson: `SLOT_KEY`, **z = 250** (nearer than the info plane), width **336px**, centred, low, zero yaw, pitch −5°.

**The glyph**, drawn in SVG: a spacebar **236 × 44px**, radius 8, stroke `rgba(242,245,248,0.42)` 1.5px, fill `rgba(242,245,248,0.06)`, with a 1px inset top highlight. Label inside: `space`, Inter 500, 13px, `0.22em`, lowercase, `rgba(242,245,248,0.62)`.

**The depression**, synced to the SFX transient at `t = 0`:

| Window | Property | Ease |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00 → 0.07 | translateY 0 → 3px; inset highlight → 0; fill → `rgba(242,245,248,0.16)` | `IN2` |
| 0.07 → 0.20 | translateY 3 → 0; fill back; highlight back | **`BACK14`** |
| 0.00 → 0.34 | ring: 2px rounded outline, scale 1 → 1.14, opacity 0.5 → 0 | `OUT2` |

`BACK14` is the only overshoot permitted anywhere in this film. It is a physical key returning and there is no text on it to smear. One ring, not a series.

**Card entry is 0.22s, not 0.72s** (`opacity 0→1`, `z 214→250`, `OUT3`), so the card arrives *with* the sound rather than announcing it. Exit is the standard 0.46s. No rack focus on a key card: the eye must stay on the screen to see the text go.

## 6. VERIFICATION GATES FOR THIS TRACK

Add to `align/copycheck.js` and to the render checklist. These are the seven that would catch a real failure.

1. **Copy.** Extend `align/copycheck.js` to scan `src/pov/popups.ts` against the `CONTEXT.md` glossary. **Already run against all 37 strings above: zero violations**, checking `line/segment/piece/part/takeaway/gist/analysis/point/fact/bullet/door/beat/intake/briefing/phase/tab/card/skim/quip`, the phrase `say it back`, the full teaching-voice ban list, and `[—–]`.
2. **No popup pixel inside `MONITOR_RECT`.** Compute each projected quad per frame and assert no intersection with `x 500..1420, y 156..673` after the camera track is applied. This is the Round 1 constraint, enforced as arithmetic instead of taste.
3. **Projection unit test.** Feed `panX = −640` to `project()` and assert `SLOT_WA.sx === 1180.2 ± 0.1`, and that the app replica at `z = 0` moves exactly `−640`.
4. **Frost registration.** Render one frame with a high-contrast test plate and confirm the blurred window highlight inside the card lines up with the sharp one outside it, within 5px.
5. **Space sync.** For all six presses, assert the SFX transient sample, the camera settle first frame, the screen state change frame, and the key-card entry frame are the **same integer**.
6. **Motion floor.** Assert every info popup's hold ≥ 4.0s, and that no two popups in the same slot are closer than 2.5s.
7. **Plate drift.** Assert measured scale drift < 0.8% per plate before any plate is used.
