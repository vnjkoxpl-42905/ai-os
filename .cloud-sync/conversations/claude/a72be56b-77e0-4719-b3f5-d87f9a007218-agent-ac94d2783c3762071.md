## Named rules

**The text/fill split is the load-bearing rule here.** `--classroom-meta` and
`--classroom-meta-soft` are tuned as fills and measure 1.85:1 and 2.10:1 as text on the
page. Every muted *word* resolves from `--classroom-meta-text`. The same split governs
`--classroom-link` and `--classroom-link-text`. Getting this wrong put every date, every
rail group label and the unread badge below the AA floor for months.

**Opacity multiplies against the colour.** Swapping a token is not enough if an
`opacity` sits on the same rule. `.classroom-nav-group > b` was `#6d6a67` at `0.7` and
still measured 2.92:1. Muted text carries its colour in the token, not in an alpha.

**A themed pair, not a shared colour, for anything on an accent fill.** The amber that
works on the light ground is `#a25704`; on the dark ground it is `#ff8906` with near-black

renders its own `StageIndexList`; the "Open the list" trigger is suppressed by

where nothing else reaches the list.

**Overlays are dialogs.** The section drawer and the mobile menu go through
`useDialogOverlay`: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, an accessible name, focus moved in on
open, a focus trap that wraps both ways, Escape, and focus restored to the trigger. Focus
is moved on a timer rather than `requestAnimationFrame`, because rAF does not fire while
the document is not being rendered.

# Design System: LSAT Academy

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "LSAT Academy"**

One institution with two registers, and the seam between them is deliberate.

**The Hall** is the door. Unauthenticated, dark, and material: near-black ground, warm marble text, a bronze that carries the light, and a serif that behaves like carved lettering. Its own source calls it a museum nocturne, carved marble filmed in darkness, read by modern instruments. Its job is to make a prospective student feel they are entering somewhere, not signing up for something. It is the only place in the product where atmosphere outranks throughput.

**The Workroom** is everything past sign-in. Light, neutral, and quiet, because a student sits inside it for an hour reading dense argument text and the interface must not compete with the passage. There is no warmth here and that is the decision, not an oversight: warm accents need a dark ground to read, and on the Workroom's white they collapse. The Hall's bronze measures 8.58:1 on its own night and 2.30:1 on the Workroom's page. The two registers do not share a palette because they cannot.

What they do share is restraint about meaning. In both, colour is a signal. Green, red and amber say something happened. Nothing is coloured to be warm, to look premium, or to fill a space. Depth comes from hairlines and background tone rather than shadow, and hierarchy comes from size, weight and position rather than hue.

Inside the Workroom, individual rooms may commit fully to their own world. The eight Activity Centre worlds, the scoped bootcamps and the inbox each carry a painted identity behind a wrapper class. That is sanctioned and it is what an academy is: neutral corridors, characterful rooms. The corridor never takes on a room's paint.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Two registers, one institution: a dark marble Hall at the door, a light neutral Workroom inside.
- Colour means something or it is absent.
- Flat surfaces, hairline separation, no drop shadows.
- Precise and quiet controls that stay out of the reader's way.
- Rooms may be worlds; corridors never are.

## Colors

Two grounds, one shared vocabulary of meaning.

### Primary

- **Workroom Ink** (`#121212`): the Workroom's near-black. It is the text colour, the fill of every primary button, and the colour of the focus ring. The Workroom has no accent, so ink carries emphasis by itself.
- **Hall Bronze** (`#c6a467`): the Hall's warm light. Hairline borders on the call to action, a sheen across it on hover, and the selection highlight. It exists only on the Hall's night ground and never crosses into the Workroom.

### Secondary

- **Hall Cyan** (`#8ad8e2`): the Hall's cool instrument note against the bronze, and the colour of its focus outlines. Rare by design; it marks precision where bronze marks warmth.
- **Teacher Amber** (`#c7841f`): the single accent of the teacher workspace, with a hue-locked ramp for hover (`#af7318`), text on tinted fills (`#895910`) and a light step (`#e0a648`). Deliberately theme-independent: the workspace is always the light format. It is the one accent in the product, and it belongs to the operator, not the student.

### Tertiary

Individual rooms carry their own palettes behind a wrapper class and are documented in their own surface briefs, not here. The wrappers in use are `.classroom-root`, `.mc-bootcamp`, `.ff-bootcamp`, `.rc-bootcamp`, `.topic-abstract`, `.cs-punk`, and the eight Activity Centre worlds.

`.classroom-root` was added to this list on 2026-09-08, after an audit found the classroom running on Outfit and a warm palette while this record still described it as neutral Workroom chrome. It is a sanctioned world, not drift: the warmth and the typeface are deliberate and they are what makes the classroom feel like a study rather than a dashboard. The record was wrong, not the code. Its brief is `.impeccable/surfaces/classroom.md`. Note that this is the one sanctioned world that is also a corridor, so the rule below about corridors never being worlds now has exactly one documented exception.

### Neutral

- **Workroom Page** (`#fcfcfc`) and **Card** (`#ffffff`): the page ground and the surface that sits on it. One step of separation, no more.
- **Workroom Raised** (`#f7f7f7`) and **Fill** (`#f2f2f2`): the two tonal steps available for layering and for resting fills.
- **Workroom Ink Muted** (`#737373`): secondary text. Measures 4.62:1 on the page, which clears the floor with almost nothing to spare. Do not lighten it.
- **Workroom Hairline** (`#e0e0e0`) and **Field** (`#e6e6e6`): the borders that do the work shadows used to.
- **Hall Marble** (`#eae5da`), with secondary (`#a6a093`) and muted (`#6e695e`) steps: warm off-white text on the night ground.

### State (semantic only, never decorative)

- **Correct** (`#10b77f`) and **Incorrect** (`#f43e5c`): a student's attempt was right or wrong. Held under their own names, separate from system status, so a future shift to one cannot silently reframe the other.
- **Warn** (`#f59f0a`): a bonus, a stretch, a non-blocking notice.
- **Success** (`#21c45d`), **Warning** (`#f59f0a`), **Destructive** (`#dc2828`): system status, not student performance.

### Named Rules

**The Ground Decides Rule.** A warm colour is legible on the Hall's night and illegible on the Workroom's page. Never carry a colour across the seam on the strength of liking it; measure it against the ground it will land on first.

**The Meaning-Only Rule.** A colour appears in the Workroom because something is right, wrong, cautionary, or dangerous. There is no fifth reason. If a surface feels flat and the impulse is to add a colour, the answer is type and space.

**The Named Ground Rule.** No colour is written as a literal hex in a component. It resolves from a token, and a token that resolves to nothing is a defect, not a subtle effect. Three colour names in this codebase pointed at variables that were never defined, and the product silently lost its focus ring for five months.

## Typography

**Workroom Body:** Lexend (with Outfit, then the system stack)
**Hall Display:** Fraunces (with Iowan Old Style, Times New Roman, serif)
**Hall Inscription:** Cinzel (with Trajan Pro, serif)
**Mono:** IBM Plex Mono
**Question Lock:** Outfit, held deliberately for LSAT question surfaces

**Character:** The Workroom is one humanist sans doing everything, sized and weighted rather than swapped. The Hall is a serif institution: an optical serif for its headings and an inscriptional face for its few carved lines. The split is the whole typographic idea. Inside, type gets out of the way; at the door, type is the architecture.

### Hierarchy

- **Title** (600, 24px, line-height 1, tracking -0.025em): card and section headings in the Workroom.
- **Body** (400, 14px rising to 16px at 640px and above, line-height 1.6): everything a student reads in the Workroom chrome.
- **Question** (400, 16px, line-height 1.6, Outfit): LSAT stimulus and answer choices. Font-locked to Outfit even though the rest of the app moved to Lexend, so question text does not shift when the shell's face changes.
- **Label** (500, 14px): button and control labels.
- **Hall Display** (380, fluid, tracking -0.015em, Fraunces): Hall headings. The unusually light 380 weight is the point; it reads as carved rather than printed.
- **Hall Inscription** (500, tracking 0.05em, Cinzel): the Hall's few short carved lines. Never body copy.
- **Hall Mono** (400, 13px, tracking 0.04em, IBM Plex Mono): the Hall's instrument labels.

### Named Rules

**The One Face Rule.** The Workroom uses a single family. Hierarchy is built from size, weight and space. A second family inside the Workroom is a defect unless it is the Outfit question lock.

**The Question Never Moves Rule.** LSAT question text is font-locked. A change to the shell's typeface must not reflow a passage a student is mid-way through reading.

**The Serif Stays At The Door Rule.** Fraunces and Cinzel belong to the Hall. A serif appearing in the Workroom chrome means the seam has leaked.

## Layout

The Workroom is a centred single column with generous side gutters. The container centres by default, carries 32px of horizontal padding, and stops widening at 1400px.

Breakpoints are the framework defaults up to 1280px, with the largest step pulled in to 1400px. Body type steps from 14px to 16px at 640px, and form controls are forced to 16px below 768px so that iOS does not zoom the page when a field takes focus. Named breakpoints `phone` (up to 600px), `tablet` (601 to 900px) and `desktop` (901px and up) exist for bootcamp surfaces.

Spacing runs on a 4px base. The steps in real use are 4, 8, 16, 24 and 32. Card padding is 24px on all sides; button padding is 8px vertical and 16px horizontal.

Reading measure matters more here than in most products. Passage and stimulus text is held to a comfortable line length rather than allowed to run the full container width.

### Named Rules

**The Reading Measure Rule.** Any surface carrying an LSAT passage, stimulus, or explanation constrains its own line length. The container's maximum is a ceiling for layout, never a licence for prose.

## Elevation & Depth

**Flat, with hairline separation.** Surfaces sit on the page rather than above it. A card is distinguished from its ground by a one-pixel border and, where more is needed, by one step of background tone. There are no drop shadows in the Workroom.

This is a decision, not a description. The codebase currently ships five shadow tokens tuned for a dark ground, from `0 1px 3px rgba(0,0,0,0.4)` up to `0 8px 32px rgba(0,0,0,0.7)`, and applies the smallest of them to every card. Because the app ships light by default, those shadows land at 40 percent black under a white card. The light theme never redefined them, and the one glow token is white, so it is invisible on white. The tokens are retired by this record and the code has not caught up yet.

The Hall is the exception and keeps its own depth: fixed ambience gradients, a grain overlay, and an inset highlight on its call to action. That depth is atmospheric rather than structural, and it does not transfer.

### Named Rules

**The Flat Rule.** Depth in the Workroom comes from a hairline or a tonal step. A `box-shadow` on a Workroom surface is a defect.

**The Two-Step Rule.** Page, then card, then at most one raised step. A surface that needs a fourth level of layering is a surface that needs restructuring.

## Shapes

Corners are gently rounded and consistent: 8px for cards and containers, 6px for buttons and inputs, 4px for the smallest chips and insets, and a full pill only for genuine badges. The scale derives from one 0.5rem radius token, so a change to the base moves the whole system together.

The Hall uses a near-square 2px radius instead. Its edges are cut, not softened, which is what separates a carved surface from a card.

Borders are one pixel. There is no second border weight in the Workroom.

### Named Rules

**The Hairline Rule.** One pixel, always. A thicker border used as a coloured accent stripe on the leading edge of a surface is banned, including when faked with an inset shadow. Active state is carried by background tone.

## Components

### Buttons

- **Character:** precise and quiet. A button is a plain rectangle with a label. It darkens slightly on hover and does nothing else. It does not lift, does not cast a shadow, and does not scale on press.
- **Shape:** gently rounded (6px).
- **Primary:** ink fill (`#121212`), page-coloured label, 8px by 16px padding.
- **Outline:** page background, one-pixel field border, ink label.
- **Ghost:** no fill at rest, a tonal fill on hover.
- **Sizes:** default 44px tall, small 40px, large 48px, icon 44 by 44. Every size clears the 44px touch minimum by being that height, never by relying on wrapped text or padding accidents.
- **Focus:** a solid two-pixel ink outline, offset two pixels from the edge. This is the only focus treatment in the Workroom.
- **Hall CTA:** 60px tall, near-square corners, transparent fill over a bronze hairline, with a bronze sheen that sweeps across on hover and a cyan outline on focus.

### Cards / Containers

- **Corner:** 8px.
- **Background:** card white on the page ground.
- **Border:** one-pixel hairline. This is what separates the card, not a shadow.
- **Shadow:** none.
- **Padding:** 24px, with header, content and footer sharing that inset.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Style:** page background, one-pixel field border, 6px corners, 44px minimum height, 8px by 12px padding.
- **Focus:** the same two-pixel ink outline as buttons, offset two pixels. Do not express focus as a border colour change alone; a border shift is too quiet to serve as the only indicator.
- **Mobile:** 16px text below 768px, so focusing a field never zooms the viewport.

### Navigation

Neutral chrome throughout. The sidebar runs one tonal step off the page ground with a hairline edge, and the active item is marked by background tone rather than by a coloured rule on its leading edge.

### Signature Component: the scoped world

The product's distinctive pattern is not a control, it is a container. A room commits fully to its own palette, typefaces and atmosphere behind a single wrapper class, and nothing inside it leaks out. Scene layers are `aria-hidden` furniture painted beneath the content, so the accessible tree is identical whether the room is painted or bare. A new world is added by writing a new wrapper and its prefixed tokens, never by adding hex to a shared component.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** resolve every colour from a token, and treat a token that resolves to nothing as a defect. Verify a new token renders before documenting it.
- **Do** give every interactive element a visible focus indicator: a solid two-pixel ink outline offset two pixels in the Workroom, cyan in the Hall.
- **Do** check contrast against the ground the colour will actually land on, in the theme that actually ships, in a browser. The theme file has read as correct while the page rendered a light card on a near-black ground.
- **Do** separate surfaces with a one-pixel hairline and one tonal step.
- **Do** keep colour semantic: correct, incorrect, warning, danger. Nothing else earns it.
- **Do** scope a new room's identity behind its own wrapper class with prefixed tokens, the way the bootcamps and the eight Activity worlds already do.
- **Do** hold LSAT passage and stimulus text to a readable line length, and leave it on the Outfit question lock.
- **Do** end every world's stylesheet with a `prefers-reduced-motion` block that stops continuous motion rather than speeding it up.

### Don't:

- **Don't** add a `box-shadow` to a Workroom surface. Depth is hairline and tone.
- **Don't** carry a Hall colour into the Workroom. Bronze reads 8.58:1 on the Hall's night and 2.30:1 on the Workroom's page.
- **Don't** introduce a warm or metallic accent into the Workroom to make it feel premium. Premium here is depth, hierarchy and type.
- **Don't** remove the browser's focus outline without shipping a replacement in the same rule. `outline-none` with no working ring is worse than leaving the default alone.
- **Don't** animate the focus indicator. A `transition-all` on a control means the ring fades in, which delays the one signal a keyboard user depends on.
- **Don't** use a border thicker than one pixel as a coloured accent stripe, including when faked with an inset shadow.
- **Don't** put a second typeface into the Workroom chrome, and don't let a serif cross in from the Hall.
- **Don't** animate layout properties. Animate `opacity`, `transform` and `color`. No bounce, no elastic, no lift on hover.
- **Don't** write a hex value into a component. If a room needs its own colour, promote it to named tokens behind that room's wrapper.

/**
 * First ~55 words of a note, for the landing preview.
 *
 * Notes are authored HTML and may carry their own <style>, so the tags are
 * stripped through a detached element rather than a regex, and any <style> or
 * <script> subtree is dropped first so CSS text never leaks into the excerpt.
 */
function noteExcerpt(item: {
  description: string | null;
  content_markdown?: string | null;
  content_html: string | null;
}) {
  const source = item.description || item.content_markdown || item.content_html || "";
  if (!source) return "";
  let text = source;
  if (/[<>]/.test(source)) {
    const holder = document.createElement("div");
    holder.innerHTML = source;
    holder.querySelectorAll("style, script").forEach((node) => node.remove());
    text = holder.textContent ?? "";
  }
  const words = text.replace(/\s+/g, " ").trim().split(" ").filter(Boolean);
  if (words.length === 0) return "";
  return words.length <= 55 ? words.join(" ") : `${words.slice(0, 55).join(" ")}…`;
}

/**
 * Read notes bucketed by the month they were written, newest month first.
 * Input is already sorted newest-first by groupNotes, so insertion order is
 * the display order and no second sort is needed.
 */
function groupNotesByMonth<T extends { created_at: string }>(notes: T[]) {
  const buckets = new Map<string, { key: string; label: string; notes: T[] }>();
  for (const note of notes) {
    const date = new Date(note.created_at);
    const key = `${date.getFullYear()}-${date.getMonth()}`;
    let bucket = buckets.get(key);
    if (!bucket) {
      bucket = {
        key,
        label: new Intl.DateTimeFormat("en-CA", {
          month: "long",
          year: "numeric",
        }).format(date),
        notes: [],
      };
      buckets.set(key, bucket);
    }
    bucket.notes.push(note);
  }
  return [...buckets.values()];
}

export function ClassroomRecapsView() {
  const query = useClassroomItems();
  const { unread, read } = groupNotes(query.data ?? []);
  const total = unread.length + read.length;
  // Unread first, because the reason to open this page is almost always
  // something that just arrived. Falls back to the newest read note so the
  // stage still says something once the student is caught up.
  const featured = unread[0] ?? read[0] ?? null;

  // The page promises two categories and the feed carries four types, none of
  // which the row showed. A session recap, an announcement and a board from a
  // session were indistinguishable until opened.
  const row = (note: (typeof unread)[number], isUnread: boolean) => ({
    key: note.id,
    title: note.title,
    meta: `${noteKind(note.type)} · ${shortDate(note.created_at)}`,
    route: `/classroom/item/${encodeURIComponent(note.id)}`,
    unread: isUnread,
  });

  return (
    <>
      {/* No dateline on an index. It printed `new Date()`, so it stamped the
          page with the day the student happened to visit while sitting in the
          slot that, on a note, holds the day Joshua wrote it. It was the one
          number on the page that did not trace to stored data. */}
      <h1 className="classroom-title">Notes and recaps</h1>
      {query.isLoading && !query.data ? <DetailLoading /> : null}
      {query.error ? (
        <DetailError
          message="Could not load your notes and recaps."
          retry={() => void query.refetch()}
        />
      ) : null}
      {/* The zero case is a state, not an absence. Without a branch here the
          page rendered a heading and stopped, which is the surface a brand new
          student is most likely to open first. The wording is the line already
          shipped by RecapsSection so the two places cannot drift, and it claims
          nothing that is not true: it says where recaps come from and stops. */}
      {!query.isLoading && !query.error && total === 0 ? (
        <p className="classroom-empty-copy">
          Notes and recaps from your sessions with Joshua will show up here.
        </p>
      ) : null}
      {!query.isLoading && !query.error && total > 0 ? (
        <>
          <p className="classroom-surface-lede">
            What Joshua writes for you after a session, plus anything he sends
            between them.
          </p>
          {featured ? (
            <FeaturedNote note={featured} unread={unread.length > 0} />
          ) : null}
          <StageIndexHint>
            {unread.length > 0
              ? `${unread.length} unread of ${total}.`
              : `All ${total} read.`}
          </StageIndexHint>
          {unread.length > 0 ? (
            <StageIndexList
              label="Unread notes and recaps"
              heading="Unread"
              items={unread.map((note) => row(note, true))}
            />
          ) : null}
          {/* Read notes group by month once the list is long enough that a flat
              run stops being scannable. At ~25 sessions a week this is measured
              in hundreds within a year, and Flagged and Wrong answers both got a
              filter while this list got nothing. Grouping beats a search box for
              a chronological archive: a student looks for "the one after the
              February session", not for a word. Below the threshold a flat list
              is clearer, so the headings stay out of the way. */}
          {read.length > MONTH_GROUP_THRESHOLD
            ? groupNotesByMonth(read).map((group) => (
                <StageIndexList
                  key={group.key}
                  label={`Notes and recaps from ${group.label}`}
                  heading={group.label}
                  items={group.notes.map((note) => row(note, false))}
                />
              ))
            : read.length > 0 ? (
              <StageIndexList
                label="Notes and recaps you have read"
                heading={unread.length > 0 ? "Read" : undefined}
                items={read.map((note) => row(note, false))}
              />
            ) : null}
        </>
      ) : null}
    </>
  );
}

export function ClassroomTAAssignmentView() {
  const { assignmentId } = useParams<{ assignmentId: string }>();
  const query = useStudentTAAssignment(assignmentId);
  const assignment = query.assignment;
  const structured = assignment ? hasStructuredWorkspace(assignment) : false;
  const [completing, setCompleting] = React.useState(false);
  const [downloading, setDownloading] = React.useState(false);
  const viewedRef = React.useRef<string | null>(null);

  React.useEffect(() => {
    if (!assignment || assignment.status !== "assigned") return;
    if (viewedRef.current === assignment.id) return;
    viewedRef.current = assignment.id;
    void markTAAssignmentViewed(assignment.id).then((result) => {
      if (result.ok) void query.refresh();
    });
  }, [assignment, query]);

  const safeHtml = React.useMemo(
    () => sanitizeClassroomHtml(assignment?.content_html ?? ""),
    [assignment?.content_html],
  );

  const download = async () => {
    if (!assignment) return;
    setDownloading(true);
    try {
      const { downloadAssignmentPdf } = await import("@/lib/taPdf");
      await downloadAssignmentPdf({
        title: assignment.title,
        contentHtml: safeHtml,
      });
    } catch (error) {
      toast.error(error instanceof Error ? error.message : "PDF download failed");
    } finally {
      setDownloading(false);
    }
  };

  const complete = async () => {
    if (!assignment) return;
    setCompleting(true);
    const result = await markTAAssignmentCompleted(assignment.id);
    if (result.ok) {
      toast.success("Marked complete");
      await query.refresh();
    } else {
      toast.error(result.error ?? "Could not mark this complete");
    }
    setCompleting(false);
  };

  if (query.loading && !assignment) return <DetailLoading />;
  if (query.error) {
    return (
      <DetailError
        message="Could not load this assignment."
        retry={() => void query.refresh()}
      />
    );
  }
  if (!assignment) {
    return (
      <DetailHeading
        date={new Date().toISOString()}
        kind="Assignment"
        title="Assignment not found"
      />
    );
  }

  return (
    <>
      <DetailHeading
        date={assignment.assigned_at}
        kind="Assignment"
        title={assignment.title}
      />
      <div className="classroom-note-meta">
        {assignment.status.replace(/_/g, " ")}
      </div>
      {safeHtml ? (
        <article
          className="classroom-prose"
          dangerouslySetInnerHTML={{ __html: safeHtml }}
        />
      ) : null}
      {structured ? (
        <StructuredTAAssignmentWorkspace
          assignment={assignment}
          refresh={query.refresh}
        />
      ) : null}
      <div className="classroom-detail-actions">
        <button
          type="button"
          className="classroom-button"
          disabled={downloading}
          onClick={() => void download()}
        >
          {downloading ? "Preparing PDF" : "Download PDF"}
        </button>
        {!structured && assignment.status !== "completed" ? (
          <button
            type="button"
            className="classroom-button classroom-button-primary"
            disabled={completing}
            onClick={() => void complete()}
          >
            {completing ? "Saving" : "Mark complete"}
          </button>
        ) : null}
      </div>
    </>
  );
}

import * as React from "react";

/**
 * Modal semantics for the classroom's two hand-built overlays: the mobile menu
 * and the section list drawer.
 *
 * Both shipped as a backdrop <button> beside an <aside> carrying no attributes
 * at all. Measured before this hook existed: opening one left focus on the
 * element behind it, Escape did nothing, and Tab walked straight out of the
 * drawer into the page underneath, which is still rendered and still scrollable.
 *
 * Radix Dialog is in the tree and would supply all of this. It is deliberately
 * not used here: nine CSS rules key off `.classroom-section-overlay` and
 * `.classroom-mobile-menu`'s exact structure, and this file already hand-rolls
 * the same pattern for the profile menu. Matching the local idiom keeps the
 * change to behaviour rather than to markup and styling.
 *
 * Returns props to spread on the panel element. The caller keeps ownership of
 * open/close so the existing backdrop and close-button handlers still work.
 */
export function useDialogOverlay({
  open,
  onClose,
  label,
}: {
  open: boolean;
  onClose: () => void;
  label: string;
}) {
  const panelRef = React.useRef<HTMLElement | null>(null);
  // Captured at open, restored at close. Without this, dismissing the drawer
  // drops focus to <body> and a keyboard user restarts from the top of the page.
  const returnTo = React.useRef<HTMLElement | null>(null);

  React.useEffect(() => {
    if (!open) return;
    returnTo.current = document.activeElement as HTMLElement | null;

    const focusables = () =>
      Array.from(
        panelRef.current?.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>(
          'a[href], button:not([disabled]), input:not([disabled]), select:not([disabled]), textarea:not([disabled]), [tabindex]:not([tabindex="-1"])',
        ) ?? [],
      ).filter((el) => el.offsetParent !== null);

    // Deferred by a timer rather than requestAnimationFrame on purpose. rAF does
    // not fire while the document is not being rendered (a background tab, or a
    // hidden preview pane), which would leave the drawer open with focus still
    // outside it. A timer runs either way.
    const focusIn = window.setTimeout(() => {
      const first = focusables()[0];
      (first ?? panelRef.current)?.focus();
    }, 0);

    const onKeyDown = (event: KeyboardEvent) => {
      if (event.key === "Escape") {
        event.stopPropagation();
        onClose();
        return;
      }
      if (event.key !== "Tab") return;
      const items = focusables();
      if (items.length === 0) {
        event.preventDefault();
        return;
      }
      const first = items[0];
      const last = items[items.length - 1];
      const active = document.activeElement;
      // Wrap at both ends, and pull focus back in if it has already escaped.
      if (event.shiftKey && (active === first || !panelRef.current?.contains(active))) {
        event.preventDefault();
        last.focus();
      } else if (!event.shiftKey && active === last) {
        event.preventDefault();
        first.focus();
      }
    };

    document.addEventListener("keydown", onKeyDown);
    return () => {
      window.clearTimeout(focusIn);
      document.removeEventListener("keydown", onKeyDown);
      // Only steal focus back if it is still inside the panel being unmounted.
      // Opening a note from the drawer moves focus deliberately; restoring here
      // would yank it back to the trigger the student just used.
      const active = document.activeElement;
      if (!active || active === document.body || panelRef.current?.contains(active)) {
        returnTo.current?.focus();
      }
    };
  }, [open, onClose]);

  return {
    ref: panelRef as React.RefObject<any>,
    role: "dialog" as const,
    "aria-modal": true,
    "aria-label": label,
    tabIndex: -1,
  };
}

Opens `.classroom-mobile-menu`, a **left** drawer holding the entire `ClassroomSidebar`: brand, four rail groups (Home, Drill, Testing Centre, Assigned work, Notes and recaps, Bootcamps, Activities, Wrong answers, Flagged, Analytics, Admissions, History, Messages, Schedule), profile menu, Log Out.
2. **Bar label** reading "Notes and recaps" (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:1035-1049, 1078`). Not interactive, but it sits inside a bordered sticky bar immediately beside a button, so it presents as one.
3. **"Open the list"**, 48px tall, full width, `PanelLeftClose` icon (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:1152-1161`). Opens `.classroom-section-overlay`, a **right** drawer holding `SectionList` for the notes section only.

So: two controls and one control-shaped label, before a single word the coach wrote.

**The pair a student cannot tell apart from labels alone: #1 and #3.** The hamburger's only visible context is the words "Notes and recaps" printed next to it. The button 16px below says "Open the list". From the labels, both read as "the notes and recaps thing". Neither names its own contents: one opens fourteen rail destinations, the other opens one list of notes. The only genuine differentiator is which screen edge the drawer enters from, which the student learns after the tap, not before it.

Worse, the `PanelLeftClose` glyph actively mispredicts: it is lucide's *collapse the left panel* icon, and the drawer it opens is anchored `right: 0` (`classroom.css:2540-2547`). A close icon on an open control, pointing at the wrong edge.

## Priority issues

**P1. The control's boundary fails WCAG 1.4.11 in both themes, and the fix token already exists.**
*What:* `.classroom-section-trigger` fills with `--classroom-trigger` (`#fefcf7`) on `--classroom-page` (`#ffffff`) — about 1.03:1, effectively invisible. Its only boundary is `border: 1px solid var(--classroom-line)` (`classroom.css:2085`), and `classroom.css:3-8`'s own comment measures `--classroom-line` at 1.22:1 against the page. Dark is worse: `#0a0913` on `#0f0e17` is about 1.03:1 with a `#17161c` border at about 1.04:1.
*Why it matters:* `PRODUCT.md:101` makes 3:1 on "the visual boundaries of interactive controls" a binding AA floor and a defect gate. `--classroom-line-strong` was introduced for precisely this case — the comment at `classroom.css:3-8` says so verbatim, "a border that is itself the affordance (WCAG 1.4.11)" — and it is used exactly once in 5,101 lines, at `classroom.css:2584`.
*Fix:* `border-color: var(--classroom-line-strong)` on `.classroom-section-trigger` (`classroom.css:2085`) and on `.classroom-mobile-menu-button` (`classroom.css:2480`), which shares the same failing pair.

**P2. 80px of navigation chrome renders above the article's dateline and h1.**
*What:* 48px height plus 32px bottom margin. Below it, `.classroom-dateline` (14px text, 16px margin, `classroom.css:2097-2105`) then `.classroom-title` at 30px (`classroom.css:2107-2113`). At 375px, adding the 56px sticky bar and the column's 16px top pad (`classroom.css:3226-3228`), the note's title starts roughly 190px down — about 28% of an iPhone SE viewport before the student sees what they opened.
*Why it matters:* the trigger is the only bordered box on the page, so it outranks the h1 by shape even though the h1 wins on type size. The surface brief's own anti-reference is "the classroom is a room with a desk in it, and the thing on the desk is what the coach wrote". Right now the first thing on the desk is a door handle.
*Fix:* see the direct answer.

**P3. The two call sites of this class disagree with each other.**
*What:* `ClassroomLayout.tsx:1154-1160` renders "Open the list" with `aria-label="Open the wrong answers list"`. `JournalViews.tsx:336-343` renders the same class labelled "Wrong answers" with no `aria-label` at all.
*Why it matters:* on `/journal/:qid` a screen reader hears "Wrong answers, button" with no verb, next to an h1 that also says "Wrong answers" (`JournalViews.tsx:100`). That is the defect `CLASSROOM-ROUTING-AUDIT.md:562` documents as already fixed.
*Fix:* extract one component. Both sites take the section, produce the same visible text and the same `aria-label`.

**P4. The trigger has no hover, no focus rule of its own, and no dialog semantics.**
*What:* all five occurrences of `classroom-section-trigger` in `classroom.css` are structural (`:2082` base, `:3112` and `:3119` journal show/suppress, `:3142` and `:3159` shared show/suppress). There is no `:hover`, no `:focus-visible`, no `transition`. Focus falls through to the app-wide zero-specificity floor at `src/index.css:196`, which paints `hsl(var(--foreground))` rather than the classroom's own `var(--classroom-link)` idiom used at `classroom.css:414-418` and `450-453`.
*Why it matters:* at 1024-1279px this is a pointer device, and the button does not acknowledge the cursor while every neighbouring control in the same stylesheet does (`classroom.css:407-412`, `444-449`, `707-709`). Combined with the near-zero border contrast from P1, at rest it does not look clickable and on hover it does not confirm that it is.
*Fix:* add `:hover { background: var(--classroom-hover); border-color: var(--classroom-line-strong); }` and `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--classroom-link); outline-offset: 2px; }`, plus `aria-haspopup="dialog"` and `aria-expanded={sectionOverlayOpen}` on the button.

**P5. The icon predicts the wrong edge and the wrong verb.**
*What:* `PanelLeftClose` at `ClassroomLayout.tsx:1159` and `JournalViews.tsx:342`.
*Why it matters:* it is a close icon on an open control, and it points left at a drawer that enters from the right.
*Fix:* `PanelRightOpen`, or drop the icon and let the words carry it, which is the reasoning already applied to the sign-out control at `classroom.css:420-423` ("Leaving is a labelled control, not a glyph").

## The direct answer

**No. It does not earn 48px plus 32px above the article's own dateline and h1, and the CSS comment's claim is not true as written.**

The comment at `classroom.css:3145-3156` says the trigger "still earns its place on a detail route (a note, an assignment), where nothing else reaches the list." Three other things reach the list from a note detail:

1. **The rail's "Notes and recaps" item.** At 1024-1279px the sidebar is fully on screen (`classroom.css:3199-3201` hides it only below 1024px), that item carries a `›` chevron (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:336`, `railCatalog.ts:63`), and clicking it runs the identical `openSection("notes")` (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:252`), which at ≤1279px opens the identical drawer (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:1005-1012`). One click, same result. At ≤1023px the same item is two taps behind the hamburger, and `onSection` already closes the menu on the way (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:1113-1116`).
2. **The note's own "Back to notes and recaps" button** (`ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:285-289`), which lands on `/classroom/recaps` where, below 1280px, `StageIndexList` renders every row inline (`classroom.css:4607-4610`).
3. **Browser back**, for anyone who arrived from the list in the first place, which is the overwhelmingly common path.

So the strict reading of the principle the comment states — never offer a control that opens a copy of what is already on screen — is satisfied. But the principle immediately adjacent to it, never offer a second control for something already one click away on the same screen, is violated outright at 1024-1279px. The trigger is a duplicate of a rail item that is permanently visible three inches to its left.

**The position:**

- **At 1024-1279px: remove it.** Add `@media (min-width: 1024px) and (max-width: 1279px) { .classroom-root .classroom-section-trigger { display: none } }`. The rail is on screen and already does this job with a better affordance (a named item with a chevron). Reflect the drawer's state on that rail item with `aria-expanded` instead.
- **At ≤1023px: move it, do not delete it.** It genuinely saves a tap there, and the rail is behind a hamburger. But it does not belong in the reading column. Make `.classroom-mobile-bar-label` (`ClassroomLayout.tsx:1078`) the control: the bar already prints the section's real name, it is already sticky so it stays reachable during a long note instead of scrolling away, and turning a label that names the list into the button that opens the list dissolves the hamburger/trigger confusion in one move. The hamburger becomes unambiguously "the room"; the section name becomes unambiguously "the things in this section". The reading column then opens on the dateline, which is what a note should open on.

That is a removal at one breakpoint and a relocation at the other. Restyling alone will not fix it: no amount of quieting a 48px full-width box changes the fact that it is the first thing between a student and something their coach wrote about them.

## Provocative questions

1. The comment at `classroom.css:3145-3156` asserts that on a detail route "nothing else reaches the list." A note detail *ends* with "Back to notes and recaps" (`ClassroomRouteViews.tsx:288`) and *begins* with "Open the list." Both reach the same rows by different routes. Which of the two do you want a student to learn, and once you have answered that, what is the other one for?

2. The visible label was stripped of the section name so it would not repeat the `h1` (`CLASSROOM-ROUTING-AUDIT.md:562`). But at ≤1023px the trigger renders 32px of margin plus a full dateline *above* that `h1`. If the heading the label was leaning on for context is below the control and not above it, what is "Open the list" actually referring to at the moment a student reads it?

3. The whole drawer exists because a 336px list column is hidden at 1280px while the reading column is capped at 720px (`classroom.css:783`). At 1100px that leaves roughly 380px of stage padding doing nothing. What is the 336px column actually costing at that width that a modal, a trigger, a `:has()` suppression rule, a focus trap, and a duplicated `StageIndexList` component are cheaper than?
