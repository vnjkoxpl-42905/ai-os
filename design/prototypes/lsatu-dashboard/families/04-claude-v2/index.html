LSAT U home redesign, 2026-10-23. Prototypes with sample data.

Three ideas about what a home page is.

Each direction answers the same jobs: for a student, know what to do next, resume it, and hear from your teacher; for the teacher, see who is waiting, what is scheduled, and assign work. They differ in the idea underneath: light, objects, and language.

LamplightCarrelMarginalia

Direction one

Lamplight

An evening study. One thing sits under the lamp; everything else waits in the shadow until you bring it forward.

Hierarchy
The only light surface on the page is the task that matters now. For the teacher, the lamp falls on a person, not a channel: Celia's message and her session are one entry.
Motion
The light animates, never the content: the lamp comes on with a filament flicker, sways toward your pointer, and flickers again when something new is set under it. The week's windows light up as you reach them.
Type
Cormorant Garamond and Hanken Grotesk on blue-black, with warm and cool light standing for due work and sessions.

Direction two

Carrel

A library study desk. Real objects whose physical behaviour matches the job they do.

Hierarchy
Assignments are index cards with divider tabs; prompt progress is a punch strip; Joshua's note is a letter with a postage stamp that turns to a postmark when read. The teacher gets a rolodex of students, a letter tray, a desk diary drawn to the hour and a work-order pad.
Motion
Physical and brief: a card lifts out of the pile and is set down on top, the holes punch in, the counter flips to 38, the rolodex flicks through cards to the student you typed, the work order tears off when you assign.
Type
Schibsted Grotesk on green linoleum, paper, and one signal orange.

Direction three

Marginalia

The home page as a short letter. Every phrase that carries data is underlined in blue pencil, and its details and actions sit in a numbered sidenote beside it.

Hierarchy
Reading order is priority order. Margin labels name each paragraph; the week is set as a table of contents; the student index reads like the back of a book; assigning work is a sentence with blanks.
Motion
The pencil: underlines ink in as the page is read, tally marks write themselves, hovering a phrase draws a pencil line to its note, and marking a note read strikes through "Unread".
Type
Literata for the letter, IBM Plex Sans in the margin, one editor's blue pencil.

Try: hover or tab to an underlined phrase; on a phone, tap a note to open it; on the teacher page choose a student in the assign sentence, and type in "Find a student".

Every page has four states

Use the small "Sample data" control at the bottom right to switch between full, empty, loading and error. Unknown counts show as unknown, never as zero, and calendars say that a blank evening is not proof of a free one.

What was checked, 2026-10-23

  • Headless Microsoft Edge at 1440 and 390 wide, in all four states: no horizontal overflow, no script errors.
  • A keyboard walkthrough of every page: skip link first, visible focus, and each signature interaction done with keys alone.
  • Reduced motion: the first frame is the final frame, with no running animations.
  • Marking a message read lowers the inbox count and keeps the history.
  • Natural-speed frame captures of each entrance and signature interaction, including a real drag and a phone swipe.

What this is not

These are standalone interactive prototypes with sample data. Actions are simulated with a confirmation message; nothing reaches a backend, and none of this is in the LSAT U app yet. The Design canvas holds static screenshots of these pages; motion and interaction exist only here.
