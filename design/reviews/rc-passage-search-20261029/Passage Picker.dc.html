Critique and rationale

One address field, three ways in

The three dependent selects become one combobox that accepts a typed address (140 3 1, PT140 S3 P1, 140.3.1), a partial PrepTest, or a title. Picking instead of typing walks the same address one step at a time inside an anchored panel. Chosen parts become removable tokens, so the dependency rules stay visible. Customize keeps its drawer and shares the same parser and row anatomy.

What was wrong with the current draft

  • The PrepTest menu is a 59-row list with five rows visible and nothing to orient by. It opens upward over "Choose your next passage".
  • Three boxes about 130px wide put disabled placeholders ("Test first", "Section first") where the controls are. They truncate on phones ("Tes...").
  • The drawer already keeps selected order, has reorder controls, and applies changes only on Apply or Cancel. In the screenshot those features have little prominence. Nothing in the visible area shows what is selected or in what order, apart from "0 selected" in a status line the sort menu covers. The search field looks like any form field.
  • Exact lookup and browsing look like unrelated features, even though both put passages into the same drill.

Why one field beats three selects

A student who knows the address types six characters and presses Enter. Three selects need six clicks or tab-and-arrow sequences. A student who doesn't know it gets a PrepTest grid grouped by decade, then sections, then passages with titles, all in the same panel. Tradeoff: the dependency between fields is no longer shown by three fixed boxes side by side. Tokens, the breadcrumb, and stepping back with Backspace have to carry it. Typed shorthand also has to be learned. The helper line and placeholder teach it, and picking still works without it.

Decisions and the local source behind each

Typed exact addresses and shorthand, results scoped by PTreferences.md, Linear Search: exact identifiers, shorthand, scoped filters. Linear's "recent items before a query" is deliberately left out, because showing recents would mean inventing student history.
Choosing adds to the drill. It never opens or starts anything.principles.md, "How should question controls behave?": distinguish opening one question from selecting questions into a set.
Four layers: paper ground, lighter field and panel, charcoal conditions panel, darker drawerprinciples.md, "Give each surface a physical job" and "Compose layers rather than piling on cards". The panel uses a fine contact edge plus a low ambient shadow, not a haze.
Selected is sage with a check and label. Keyboard highlight is a warm tint with a ring.principles.md: reserve accents for meaningful states and pair colour with text or a symbol. The two states never use the same signal.
Richer rows: title, address, subject, question countmethods.md, 2026-10-24: "precise reference controls, useful result detail" inside the same visual family, not a palette change.
Motion only on open (150ms panel, 220ms drawer). Filtering never replays it.principles.md, "Which transition should run?" Reduced motion turns both off.
Warm paper and charcoal kept. The LSATy navy and gold tokens are not used.principles.md, "Which colours are authoritative?": current user direction outranks historical palettes. The brief's screenshots win over the design system here.

Contracts kept in this prototype

  • Changing the PT clears section and passage. Changing the section clears the passage. Each token's × and Backspace step back one level.
  • Only a full address can choose a passage. Choosing it updates the drill panel and nothing launches. Start stays behind Review.
  • The drawer works on a draft copy. Cancel, ×, the backdrop and Escape discard it. Apply commits it. Focus returns to Customize drill. Picks and their order survive search, filter and sort.
  • Tutor's choice is one tap in the drawer and one button ("Use tutor's choice") on the page.
  • Rows show metadata only. Demo titles are fictional, except the three lifted from the screenshot (Marine ecosystems, The history of public libraries, Judicial interpretation). Section numbers, counts and difficulty are generated.

Self-critique and refinements

Pass 1, phone drawer: search, four wrapping subject chips, the status line and sort stacked up, so the first result started around 60% of the way down an 844px screen. On phones, filters and sort now sit behind a "Filters" toggle that shows a count when any are active.

Pass 2, phone selected rows: long titles wrapped beside three icon-only 44px controls. Below 560px, each row now puts the title and address on their own line, then a separate action row with labelled Earlier, Later and Remove buttons. Each button is still 44px tall and they share the width. A disabled Earlier or Later shows where the passage sits. On wider screens the controls stay icon-only, with full accessible names.

Inspected vs. only intended

Rendered and looked at: the true 1440px layout, shown scaled to fit the preview pane: two columns, the title search for "libraries", and the 140 3 1 match. That pass surfaced a clipped Timing select, now fixed. The 768px frame: single column, a full-width panel, the drawer with two passages added. 390 and 320: the field, tokens, the drawer in both modes, folded filters, and the relabelled selected rows. Also the PT grid, section step, wrong-section hint and no-results state.

Checked with scripted events at 768: opening Customize moves focus into the drawer (to "Tutor's choice"). Shift+Tab on the first control wraps to the last. After adding a passage (draft: 1 selected), Escape closed the drawer, returned focus to Customize drill, and left the panel on "Tutor's choice · 1", so the draft was discarded. These were synthetic key events, not a person at a keyboard.

Unverified: real keyboard and touch input, screen-reader output, the reduced-motion setting, motion timing during playback, browser zoom, and native select rendering across browsers. No automated review result is available yet. None of the reference links were opened, and Mobbin imagery was never seen.

Implementation risks

  • The parser assumes three-digit PTs and single-digit section and passage numbers. Inputs like "1403" are ambiguous and need a decision about real IDs.
  • The section step assumes one RC section per PT, as in the demo. Real data may differ, and the row label has to come from the backend.
  • The PT grid uses listbox semantics with two-dimensional arrow keys. Screen readers announce it as a list, so it needs testing with VoiceOver and NVDA.
  • The panel always opens below the field and scrolls the page into view. Short landscape phones may need a sheet instead.
  • The drawer renders results 24 at a time. A full index needs virtualization or server paging, and that must keep selected IDs that fall outside the loaded page.
  • Native selects stay in place for Timing and Show answers. Nothing here proves backend search, exposure recording or drill creation behave correctly.

Implementation handoff

  • Address field: an ARIA 1.2 combobox with aria-activedescendant. State is {pt, sec, psg, query}. Choosing a PT sets sec and psg to null. Choosing a section sets psg to null. The × on a token and Backspace on an empty field remove the last level. Only a full address writes a selection.
  • Parser: accepts 140 3 1, PT140 S3 P1, 140.3.1 and 140/3/1. One or two digits match as a PT prefix. Anything else is a title or subject search. The drawer search uses the same parser to scope results.
  • One selection model: appliedIds[] in order. The address field replaces it with one ID. The drawer edits a draft copy, and only Apply commits it. Cancel, ×, the backdrop and Escape discard the draft. Focus goes back to Customize drill.
  • Rows: title, PT · S · P, subject, question count. Difficulty appears in the drawer only. No passage text, questions or answers.
  • States: selected is sage plus a check and a label. Keyboard highlight is a warm tint plus a 1.5px ring. Phone rows, tiles and controls are at least 44px.
  • Side effects: searching, opening the panel and choosing a passage write no exposure and start nothing. Start stays behind Review.
