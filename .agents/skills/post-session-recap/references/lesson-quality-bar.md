# Post-session note quality bar

The standard since 2026-10-25 is the **Session Manual**: `session-manual/model-reading-flaw-answer-choices.html`, with historical builds in `session-manual/examples/` that contain superseded wording; they are not current copy authorities. Earlier references (the Some, Most, and All lesson, the 2026-09-30 facts-and-rules drafts) are historical. Some, Most, and All remains a good example of teaching order, but its code-box arrows, letter variables and "establish" were later rejected.

## What the reader gets

- A serif title naming the reasoning, a role kicker (`Session recap · date`, `Before you start · drill`, or `Reference`), and a lede that says why students stall.
- Sections in Session Manual form, taking what fits the teaching: the first thing to do, kind cards and comparisons where useful, one section per idea, numbered steps, callouts, practice checks, a practical checklist, and the week's work. Do not invent categories to fill a component.
- Fresh everyday examples. The original test stimulus is never reproduced.
- Credit lines that name what the student actually did. Mistakes are taught as traps, never pinned on the student.
- Two or three tap-to-check drills that test the reasoning just taught.
- Every assignment and promise from the session, exact.

## Each note earns its place

- Recap: concept first, Joshua's reasoning, useful distinctions and traps, specific credit, two or three practice checks and the student's next work. About a five-minute read.
- Primer: the actual drill task, its rule and trap, and one habit. Short enough to read immediately before starting.
- Method reference: the complete reusable procedure, reasoning, limits and examples for that question type. Lightly revise existing content before creating another reference.

The transcript governs session content. Corpus sources ground the method reference. The inventory governs reusable presentation. A source map distinguishes Joshua's teaching, assistant-authored examples and uncertain evidence. Do not force every topic into an artificial taxonomy or turn all three notes into duplicate recaps.

Record every offer and promise privately with owner, timestamp and status. Preserve tentative wording; do not turn an offer into assigned homework. Teacher delivery and account tasks stay private unless relevant to the student's next action. Complete independent note preparation while homework choices remain pending, then present one concrete proposal for approval.

## Reasoning review

For each claim, ask whether it is guaranteed, merely possible, or impossible, and try the smallest counterexample. Keep conditional direction, negation, "and"/"or" under contraposition, and quantifier strength explicit. Drill answers get the same check.

## Before handoff

- The builder lint is ok, the recap has been opened beside the model, and every delivered primer and reference has also been opened at desktop and phone widths. Any unrendered page is explicitly unverified.
- No component appears that isn't in the model, the stylesheet or `INVENTORY.md`, unless there is a stated reason.
- Words follow the `wiki/COPY-CORRECTIONS.md` table.
- Chips toggle, the console is clean, and there's no horizontal scroll at phone width.
- Chips also work with Enter and Space, show keyboard focus and expose expanded state. Validate hidden answers. Name any browser or mobile check that was not completed; a source check or dry-run is not visual verification.
- The assignment list matches the transcript, and items Joshua must pick are flagged privately.
- Current primary Markdown matches current HTML wording and answers. Retain earlier text privately; private continuation never enters the posted document.
- The recap holds the private continuation below its divider. Report what was made and that nothing was posted.
- Show all three notes and the homework proposal together. Teaching prepared, homework verified, user approval and publication are separate states; do not re-ask settled design questions while waiting for permission to post.
