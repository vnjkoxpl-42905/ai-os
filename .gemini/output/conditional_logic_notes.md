## Part 1: Conditional Logic: Cheat Sheet

> **Card description:** Every conditional is one sentence with a trigger and a requirement. Find the word, and you know where both halves go.

### The Four Fundamental Moves

1. **Words that put it on the left (Sufficient / Trigger): 19 words**
   - Words: `if`, `when`, `whenever`, `wherever`, `anytime`, `provided that`, `in the event that`, `all`, `any`, `every`, `each`, `everyone`, `everything`, `leads to`, `produces`, `results in`, `guarantees`, `ensures`, `always`.
   - Rule: Whatever follows the indicator is the trigger. It goes on the left exactly as written. Nothing gets negated.
   - Example: *"If it rains, practice is cancelled"* &rarr; `rain` &rarr; `practice cancelled`.

2. **Words that put it on the right (Necessary / Requirement): 16 words**
   - Words: `only`, `only if`, `only when`, `requires`, `required`, `prerequisite`, `must`, `needs`, `essential`, `necessary`, `depends on`, `then`, `implies`, `signals`, `means`, `will`.
   - Rule: The word marks what is required. That idea goes on the right side of the arrow.
   - Example: *"You pass only if you attend"* &rarr; `pass` &rarr; `attend`.

3. **Words that negate the left (If Not): 4 words**
   - Words: `unless`, `until`, `without`, `except`.
   - Rule: All four are "if not" in disguise. Read as "if not X, then Y", or put X on the right and negate the left.
   - Example: *"You cannot enter unless you have a pass"* &rarr; `enter` &rarr; `pass` (or `NOT pass` &rarr; `NOT enter`).

4. **Words that negate the right: 5 words**
   - Words: `no`, `none`, `never`, `cannot`, `not`.
   - Rule: Trigger stays on the left; the negative attaches to the outcome on the right.
   - Example: *"No freshman may apply"* &rarr; `freshman` &rarr; `NOT apply`.

### Words on No List
- Ask: What is guaranteed, and what is required?
- The requirement goes on the right, even when it happens first chronologically.
- Example: *"Learning to walk is a prerequisite to running"* &rarr; `running` &rarr; `learned to walk`.

### Flipping a Rule (The Contrapositive)
Three mechanical steps:
1. **Swap:** Left and right switch sides.
2. **Negate:** Positive becomes negative; negative becomes positive.
3. **Switch connectors:** `AND` becomes `OR`; `OR` becomes `AND`.
- Example: `A and B` &rarr; `C` flips to `NOT C` &rarr; `NOT A or NOT B`.

### The Two Things You Do With a Rule
1. **Chain:** When an idea repeats between two rules (`A` &rarr; `B` and `B` &rarr; `C` yields `A` &rarr; `C`).
2. **Trigger:** Apply a real-world situational fact to a rule or to its contrapositive to reach a guaranteed deduction.

## Part 2: Conditional Logic: The Breakdown

> **Card description:** Eleven parts, one at a time. The cheat sheet tells you what to write. This tells you why it is right.

### Section Breakdown

1. **01: Words that put it on the left**
   - Detailed walkthrough of all 19 sufficient indicators with individual sentence examples and practice checks.
2. **02: Words that put it on the right**
   - Walkthrough of all 16 necessary indicators, contrasting "guaranteed path" vs "required hurdle".
3. **03: Words that negate the left**
   - Canceling double negatives with `unless`, `until`, `without`, and `except`.
4. **04: Words that negate the right**
   - Standardizing `No A is B` into `A` &rarr; `NOT B`.
5. **05: Words that are on no list**
   - Analytical extraction of requirement vs guarantee when facing unfamiliar phrasing.
6. **06: And, Or, and the Flip**
   - Visual diagrams of compound conditions and De Morgan's laws applied to the LSAT.
7. **07: Both directions at once (Biconditionals)**
   - `if and only if`, `vice versa`, `then and only then`. Creates a two-way arrow (`A` &harr; `B`).
8. **08: Not conditionals at all (Quantifiers)**
   - Warning against diagramming `some`, `most`, and `few` as strict conditional arrows.
9. **09: The Two Moves That Are Never Allowed**
   - **Mistaken Reversal:** Confusing the necessary condition for the sufficient condition (assuming the arrow runs backward).
   - **Mistaken Negation:** Denying the sufficient condition and assuming the necessary condition cannot occur.
10. **10: The Two Things You Do With a Rule**
    - Chaining multi-step conditionals.
    - Triggering rules with situational facts.
11. **11: When to draw at all**
    - Process rule: Diagram during stimulus setup when formulas or chains repeat; do not diagram answers blindly.
12. **Test Yourself (Interactive Quiz)**
    - 10 full self-check scenarios with expandable explanations testing indicator conversion, contrapositives, and chaining.
