# Batch 12, part A: Parallel Flaw

## PT101-S3-Q16 | Parallel Flaw | A

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** The accusation that K falsified data should be dismissed.

**Evidence:** The data in question are gone. K's more recent data were checked and none were falsified.

**Evaluate:** The accusation is about the missing data. The evidence is about different, later data. Clean later work doesn't show the earlier work was clean. The flaw: dismiss a charge about one set of records using other records.

**Goal:** Find an argument that dismisses a charge about lost records using evidence from different records.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** The records that matter were destroyed, so L's current clients' clean records are used to dismiss the charge. Same move.

**(B) Bad Conclusion Match.** This concludes the charge should be discussed, not dismissed, and the evidence actually supports the charge.

**(C) Bad Premise Match.** The other work came after N's, so N couldn't have copied it. That is a real defense based on the evidence at issue.

**(D) Bad Conclusion Match.** This supports the accusation instead of dismissing it.

**(E) Bad Premise Match.** This relies on disagreement about the public interest, not on evidence from unrelated records.

## PT101-S3-Q21 | Parallel Flaw | A

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Millville used more electricity this August than last August.

**Evidence:** Each day's electricity use is directly proportional to that day's peak humidity. Average peak humidity was higher this August.

**Evaluate:** Recipe: a quantity in each unit is proportional to a factor in that unit. The factor averaged higher this period than last. So the total quantity was higher this period. Look for the same pieces: per-unit proportion, comparison of the factor across two periods, conclusion about the total.

**Goal:** Per-unit proportion, factor higher overall this period, so the total is higher this period.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Supplies in each class are proportional to that class's students. More students overall last year, so more supplies last year. Every piece lines up.

**(B) Bad Conclusion Match.** This compares painting courses with sculpture courses, not one quantity across two periods.

**(C) Bad Conclusion Match.** This ends in a recommendation about what the institute must do, not a comparison of totals.

**(D) Bad Premise Match.** Fees are proportional to the number of classes per student, but the evidence is about the number of students. The factor in the evidence isn't the one in the proportion.

**(E) Bad Conclusion Match.** This links two proportions to a third. It never compares two periods.

## PT102-S4-Q12 | Parallel Flaw | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** The teaching has improved.

**Evidence:** The principal believes bad teaching causes failures. Failures disappeared.

**Evaluate:** Nothing was observed about the teaching. The principal assumes the theory is right, sees the effect go away, and concludes the cause went away. Failures could have stopped for many other reasons.

**Goal:** Find someone who holds a theory about a cause, sees the effect stop, and concludes the cause stopped.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Bad Premise Match.** Here the cause (overeating) is observed to stop and the effect is predicted. The original observes the effect and infers the cause.

**(B) Bad Premise Match.** The manager actually changed the jobs. In the original, nobody did anything to the teaching.

**(C) Bad Premise Match.** The nutritionist hands out charts and concludes the complaints stopped. No cause is inferred from a vanished effect.

**(D) Correct.** The manager's theory: complaints come from too little work. Complaints stopped, so the manager concludes the workers are busy. Same move.

**(E) Bad Premise Match.** This relies on a report that the cause stopped and then concludes the effect stopped. That runs the other way.

## PT106-S3-Q21 | Parallel Flaw | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** The city has no obligation to provide trash cans.

**Evidence:** If the law punishes littering, the city must provide trash cans. The law doesn't punish littering.

**Evaluate:** Punishes littering → obligation. The argument denies the trigger and concludes the result is absent. That's denying the antecedent: the obligation could come from somewhere else.

**Goal:** If A then B. Not A. So not B.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Bad Premise Match.** Holiday → bakery closed. Closed, so holiday. That affirms the result to conclude the trigger, a different flaw.

**(B) Bad Premise Match.** This reads as a contrapositive: no balloons yet, so not the party. Different shape.

**(C) Bad Premise Match.** Success requires adherence. Adherence, so success. That treats a requirement as a guarantee, a different flaw.

**(D) Correct.** Late flight → miss meeting. Not late, so won't miss. Same denial of the trigger.

**(E) Bad Premise Match.** Enforced → some jailed. No one jailed, so not enforced. That is a valid contrapositive.

## PT107-S1-Q23 | Parallel Flaw | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Some people who appreciate poetry are illogical.

**Evidence:** No scientist appreciates poetry. Most scientists are logical.

**Evaluate:** Recipe: No A are B. Most A are C. So some B are not C. The facts about scientists say nothing about the people who appreciate poetry, since none of them are scientists. The conclusion jumps to a new group.

**Goal:** No A are B, most A are C, so some B are not C.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Bad Conclusion Match.** No marsupials lay eggs, most are Australian, so some Australians don't lay eggs. That's valid: the marsupials themselves are those Australians. The original's conclusion is about the excluded group.

**(B) Correct.** No fathers want candy at bedtime. Most fathers are adults. So some candy-wanters are not adults. Same recipe, same flaw.

**(C) Bad Conclusion Match.** This concludes California wine is inferior. That's a claim about the "most" group, not about the excluded group.

**(D) Bad Conclusion Match.** Valid: most instant film is color, and no color film is that sharp, so some instant film isn't.

**(E) Bad Conclusion Match.** This says some tax-likers are honest. The original concludes the excluded group lacks the "most" trait, so a match would say some are dishonest.

## PT108-S3-Q14 | Parallel Flaw | A

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Miller: Jurgens should be elected.

**Evidence:** Wu says Jurgens is dishonest. Miller replies that honest people usually aren't tough enough to run a city.

**Evaluate:** Miller doesn't defend Jurgens's dishonesty. Miller attacks the opposite trait, honesty, by naming a downside. Showing honesty has a cost doesn't show Jurgens is a good choice, or even tough.

**Goal:** Wu objects to a trait; the reply names a downside of the opposite trait.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Wu objects to slow service. Miller says fast service often means poor food. Same move: attack the opposite trait.

**(B) Bad Premise Match.** This is the popular trap. Wu objects to aggression, and Miller praises aggression itself ("aggressive people don't get pushed around"). That defends the criticized trait directly. The original attacks the opposite trait instead.

**(C) Bad Premise Match.** Miller praises brightness itself, the trait Wu criticized.

**(D) Bad Premise Match.** Miller defends the high price itself as a sign of quality.

**(E) Bad Conclusion Match.** Miller recommends a different applicant. The original argues for the same candidate Wu rejected.
