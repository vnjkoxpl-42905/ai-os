## PT101-S3-Q15 | Must Be True | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Many of Dr. Q's characterizations of Dr. Z's work are imprecise. Such imprecise characterizations do not give an adequate basis for sound criticism.

**Evaluate:** "Such characterizations" points back to the imprecise ones. So: many characterizations are imprecise, and every imprecise one fails as a basis for criticism. "Many" guarantees at least one. So at least one characterization fails as a basis for criticism. We learn nothing about the precise ones.

**Goal:** At least one of Dr. Q's characterizations fails to give an adequate basis for criticism.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported.** We know nothing about the precise characterizations. Every one of Dr. Q's characterizations could be imprecise, and then none would work.

**(B) Unsupported.** This assumes precision is enough for a sound basis. Dr. Z only says imprecision is enough to fail.

**(C) Reversed.** Dr. Z says imprecise → inadequate. This says inadequate → imprecise. A characterization could be precise and still inadequate for some other reason.

**(D) Unsupported.** Same mistake as (B), now stated as a general rule about anyone's work.

**(E) Correct.** Many characterizations are imprecise, and imprecise ones are inadequate. So at least one is inadequate.
