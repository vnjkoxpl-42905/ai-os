## PT113-S4-Q1 | Most Supported | C

> Criminals often have an unusual self-image. Embezzlers often think of their actions as “only borrowing money.” Many people convicted of violent crimes rationalize their actions by some sort of denial; either the victim “deserved it” and so the action was justified, or “it simply wasn’t my fault. ” Thus, in many cases, by criminals’ characterization of their situations, _______.
>
> Which one of the following most logically completes the passage?
>
> (A) they ought to be rewarded for their actions
> (B) they are perceived to be the victim of some other criminal
> (C) their actions are not truly criminal
> (D) the criminal justice system is inherently unfair
> (E) they deserve only a light sentence for their crimes

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Embezzlers call it "only borrowing". Violent criminals say the victim "deserved it", so it was justified, or that it "simply wasn't my fault".

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. Every example is a criminal describing the act so that it stops being a crime: borrowing isn't theft, a justified act isn't wrongdoing, and if it wasn't your fault, you didn't do anything criminal. The blank asks what follows "by criminals' characterization".

**Goal:** In their own telling, what they did wasn't really a crime.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** Saying an act wasn't wrong is not saying it deserves a reward. None of the examples claim credit.

**(B) Unsupported.** This is the most popular wrong answer. "The victim deserved it" puts blame on the victim; it doesn't make the criminal a victim of some other criminal. No example says that.

**(C) Correct.** Borrowing, justified, not my fault: each description denies that the act was criminal. That is what all three examples share.

**(D) Out of Scope.** The criminals talk about their own acts, not the justice system.

**(E) Unsupported.** These criminals aren't asking for leniency. They deny there was a crime at all.

## PT113-S4-Q3 | Most Supported | D

> An instructor presented two paintings to a class. She said that the first had hung in prestigious museums but the second was produced by an unknown amateur. Each student was asked which painting was better. Everyone selected the first. The instructor later presented the same two paintings in the same order to a different class. This time she said that the first was produced by an unknown amateur but the second had hung in prestigious museums. In this class, everyone said that the second painting was better.
>
> The statements above, if true, most strongly support which one of the following?
>
> (A) Most of the students would not like any work of art that they believed to have been produced by an unknown amateur.
> (B) None of the claims that the instructor made about the paintings was true.
> (C) Each of the students would like most of the paintings hanging in any prestigious museum.
> (D) In judging the paintings, some of the students were affected by what they had been told about the history of the paintings.
> (E) Had the instructor presented the paintings without telling the students anything about them, almost all of the students would have judged them to be roughly equal in artistic worth.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Same two paintings, same order, two classes. Class one was told painting 1 hung in museums and painting 2 was by an amateur: everyone picked painting 1. Class two was told the reverse: everyone picked painting 2.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. The paintings never changed. Only the story changed, and the verdict flipped with it. So what the students were told made a difference to at least some of them.

**Goal:** Some students' judgments were swayed by what they were told about the paintings.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** This is the most popular wrong answer. One comparison against one museum painting tells us nothing about whether students would dislike *any* amateur work.

**(B) Unsupported.** The two classes heard conflicting stories, so some claims were false. But that doesn't mean *none* were true.

**(C) Too Strong.** Preferring one museum painting over one amateur painting says nothing about liking most paintings in any museum.

**(D) Correct.** Same paintings, opposite verdicts, and the only change was the story. "Some" is all we need, and the flip gives us that.

**(E) Unsupported.** We have no idea what students would say with no information. Maybe they'd all pick the same one.

## PT114-S2-Q6 | Most Supported | D

> One thousand people in Denmark were questioned about their views on banning cigarette advertising. The sample comprised adults who are representative of the general population, and who, ten years previously, had been questioned on the same issue. Interestingly, their opinions changed little. Results show that 31 percent are in favor of such a ban, 24 percent are against it, 38 percent are in favor, but only for certain media, and 7 percent have no opinion.
>
> The survey results in the passage best support which one of the following conclusions?
>
> (A) People's opinions never change very much.
> (B) A minority of Denmark's population feels that banning cigarette advertising would set a bad precedent.
> (C) Most of Denmark's population is not seriously concerned about cigarette advertising.
> (D) Most of Denmark's population favors some sort of ban on cigarette advertising.
> (E) Most of Denmark's population does not smoke cigarettes.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** A representative sample of 1,000 Danish adults: 31% favor a ban on cigarette advertising, 24% oppose it, 38% favor a ban for certain media, 7% have no opinion. Their views barely changed in ten years.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer with the numbers. 31% want a full ban and 38% want a partial one. That's 69% who want some kind of ban. The sample is representative, so that carries over to the population.

**Goal:** Most Danes favor some sort of ban.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** These people's views changed little on one issue. That says nothing about whether opinions "never" change.

**(B) Out of Scope.** The survey doesn't ask about setting a precedent.

**(C) Unsupported.** 93% have an opinion and 69% want a ban. Nothing suggests they aren't concerned.

**(D) Correct.** 31% + 38% = 69% favor a ban of some kind, and the sample represents the population.

**(E) Out of Scope.** The survey is about advertising, not who smokes.

## PT114-S4-Q24 | Most Strongly Supported | E

> Most land-dwelling vertebrates have rotating limbs terminating in digits, a characteristic useful for land movement. Biologists who assume that this characteristic evolved only after animals abandoned aquatic environments must consider the Acanthostega, a newly discovered ancestor of all land vertebrates. It possessed rotating limbs terminating in digits, but its skeleton was too feeble for land movement. It also breathed using only internal gills, indicating that it and its predecessors were exclusively aquatic.
>
> The statements above, if true, most strongly support which one of the following?
>
> (A) Many anatomical characteristics common to most land animals represent a disadvantage for survival underwater.
> (B) None of the anatomical characteristics common to most aquatic animals represent an advantage for survival on land.
> (C) Acanthostega originated as a land-dwelling species, but evolved gills only after moving to an underwater environment.
> (D) All anatomical characteristics not useful for land movement but common to most land animals represent an advantage for survival underwater.
> (E) Certain anatomical characteristics common to some aquatic animals represent an advantage for survival on land.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Most land vertebrates have rotating limbs ending in digits, which help on land. Some biologists assume this trait evolved only after animals left the water. But Acanthostega, an ancestor of all land vertebrates, had those limbs and digits while being exclusively aquatic: feeble skeleton, internal gills only.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. Acanthostega was an aquatic animal with a trait that is useful for moving on land. So a characteristic found in an aquatic animal can be an advantage on land. That is exactly the fact the biologists need to "consider".

**Goal:** Some traits of water-dwelling animals are useful on land.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported.** Nothing says land traits are a disadvantage underwater. Acanthostega had them and lived in water.

**(B) Opposite.** Acanthostega is a counterexample: an aquatic animal with a trait useful on land.

**(C) Opposite.** This is the most popular wrong answer. The gills indicate that Acanthostega *and its predecessors* were exclusively aquatic. It didn't start on land.

**(D) Too Strong.** "All" characteristics is far beyond one fossil.

**(E) Correct.** Rotating limbs with digits help on land, and Acanthostega, an aquatic animal, had them. "Certain" and "some" keep it to what the fossil shows.

> Cardiologist: Coronary bypass surgery is commonly performed on patients suffering from coronary artery disease when certain other therapies would be as effective. Besides being relatively inexpensive, these other therapies pose less risk to the patient since they are less intrusive. Bypass surgery is especially debatable for single-vessel disease.
>
> The cardiologist's statements, if true, most strongly support which one of the following?
>
> (A) Bypass surgery is riskier than all alternative therapies.
> (B) Needless bypass surgery is more common today than previously.
> (C) Bypass surgery should be performed when more than one vessel is diseased.
> (D) Bypass surgery is an especially expensive therapy when used to treat single-vessel disease.
> (E) Sometimes there are equally effective alternatives to bypass surgery that involve less risk.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Bypass surgery is commonly done when other therapies would be as effective. Those therapies are cheaper and less risky, because they are less intrusive. Bypass is especially debatable for single-vessel disease.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. The first two sentences together: in some cases there are other therapies that work as well and carry less risk. That is a direct combination, nothing added.

**Goal:** Sometimes an equally effective, less risky alternative to bypass exists.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** This is the most popular wrong answer. The cardiologist compares bypass with "these other therapies", not with every alternative there is.

**(B) Unsupported.** Nothing compares today with the past.

**(C) Unsupported.** "Especially debatable" for one vessel doesn't mean surgery *should* be done for more than one.

**(D) Unsupported.** The passage says the alternatives are cheaper, not that bypass costs more for single-vessel cases specifically.

**(E) Correct.** "As effective" and "less risk" come straight from the first two sentences. "Sometimes" matches "commonly performed when".

## PT117-S3-Q9 | Most Supported | D

> Rossi: It is undemocratic for people to live under a government in which their interests are not represented. So children should have the right to vote, since sometimes the interests of children are different from those of their parents. Smith: Granted, children's interests are not always the same as their parents'; governmental deficits incurred by their parents' generation will later affect their own generation's standard of living. But even if children are told about the issues affecting them, which is not generally the case, their conceptions of what can or should be done are too simple, and their time horizons are radically different from those of adults, so we cannot give them the responsibility of voting.
>
> Smith's statements can most directly be used as part of an argument for which one of the following views?
>
> (A) A democratic government does not infringe on the rights of any of its citizens.
> (B) Children have rights that must be respected by any political authority that rules over them.
> (C) News programs for children would give them enough information to enable them to vote in an informed way.
> (D) If there are any limitations on full democracy that result from denying the vote to children, such limitations must be accepted.
> (E) If parents do not adequately represent their children's interests in the political sphere, those interests will be adequately represented by someone else.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Rossi: it's undemocratic to live under a government that doesn't represent your interests; children's interests sometimes differ from their parents', so children should vote. Smith: granted, their interests differ, but children's ideas are too simple and their time horizons too different, so they can't be given the vote.

**Evaluate:** Smith concedes Rossi's key fact: children's interests aren't always represented by their parents. Smith still refuses them the vote. So Smith is accepting some shortfall in democracy as the price of keeping children from voting.

**Goal:** Smith accepts that denying children the vote limits democracy, and accepts the limit.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Out of Scope.** Smith says nothing about whether a democracy can infringe on rights.

**(B) Out of Scope.** Smith never talks about children's rights.

**(C) Opposite.** Smith says that even if children are informed, their ideas are too simple. Information wouldn't be enough.

**(D) Correct.** Smith grants that children's interests can go unrepresented, which Rossi calls undemocratic, and still says they can't vote. That is accepting the limitation.

**(E) Unsupported.** This is the most popular wrong answer. Smith never says someone else will represent children's interests. Smith's deficit example suggests no one is.

## PT119-S2-Q12 | Most Supported | E

> Researcher: We studied two groups of subjects over a period of six months. Over this period, one of the groups had a daily routine of afternoon exercise. The other group, the control group, engaged in little or no exercise during the study. It was found that those in the exercise group got 33 percent more deep-sleep at night than did the control group. Exercising in the afternoon tends to raise body temperature slightly until after bedtime, and this extra heat induces deeper sleep.
>
> The researcher's statements, if true, most strongly support which one of the following?
>
> (A) Regular afternoon exercise is one of the things required for adequate deep-sleep.
> (B) Exercise in the morning is almost as likely to have as many beneficial effects on sleep as is exercise in the afternoon.
> (C) The best way to get increased deep-sleep is to induce a slight increase in body temperature just before bedtime.
> (D) No one in the control group experienced a rise in body temperature just before bedtime.
> (E) Raising body temperature slightly by taking a warm bath just before bedtime will likely result in increased deep-sleep.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** "Required" means no one gets enough deep sleep without afternoon exercise. The study only shows exercisers got *more* deep sleep.

**(B) Unsupported.** The mechanism depends on body heat lasting until bedtime. Morning exercise is never discussed, and its heat could easily wear off by night.

**(C) Too Strong.** "The best way" needs a comparison with every other method. The passage describes one method that works.

**(D) Too Strong.** This is the most popular wrong answer. "No one" covers every person in the control group. They exercised little, but someone could have been warmed by a hot meal or a fever. The study reports group results, not every individual.

**(E) Correct.** The researcher says the deep sleep comes from a slight rise in body temperature lasting past bedtime. A warm bath right before bed creates that same condition, so it should help too. "Will likely" is exactly as strong as the mechanism allows.

**(E) Opposite.** Anissa meets Rule 2 and Jehan is a second-year, so Anissa goes first, not Jehan.
