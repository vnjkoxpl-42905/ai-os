# Batch 07, part B: Most Supported

The first sixteen Most Supported gaps (filed as "Most Supported" or "Most Strongly Supported"; same task). Fifteen are in the database with no explanation. Modelled on `models/MBT-MBF.md`:
- Pause and infer after the statements. Say what the statements add up to before the choices.
- The Goal names the answer's content and how strong it can be.
- Weak wording is argued for ("sometimes", "may", "some"): say how little evidence it needs.

## PT102-S2-Q8 | Most Supported | A

> Toddlers are not being malicious when they bite people. For example, a child may want a toy, and feel that the person he or she bites is preventing him or her from having it.
>
> The situation as described above most closely conforms to which one of the following generalizations?
>
> (A) Biting people is sometimes a way for toddlers to try to solve problems.
> (B) Toddlers sometimes engage in biting people in order to get attention from adults.
> (C) Toddlers mistakenly believe that biting people is viewed as acceptable behavior by adults.
> (D) Toddlers do not recognize that by biting people they often thwart their own ends.
> (E) Resorting to biting people is in some cases an effective way for toddlers to get what they want.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Toddlers who bite aren't being malicious. Example: a child wants a toy and feels the person they bite is keeping it from them.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. The example gives the biting a purpose: there is an obstacle (someone in the way of the toy), and the bite is the toddler's way of dealing with it. That is problem-solving, crude as it is. Notice what the passage does *not* say: whether biting gets the toddler the toy.

**Goal:** Something weak like "toddlers sometimes bite to deal with an obstacle." Watch for answers that say biting *works*.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** The toddler wants the toy, sees a person blocking it, and bites. That is an attempt to solve a problem. "Sometimes" and "try to" keep the claim as small as the example.

**(B) Out of Scope.** The example is about a toy, not getting adults' attention.

**(C) Out of Scope.** Nothing in the passage tells us what toddlers believe adults think about biting.

**(D) Unsupported.** We have no information on whether biting thwarts toddlers' goals, or whether toddlers realize it.

**(E) Unsupported.** This is the most popular wrong answer, and it shows the gap between *trying* and *succeeding*. The passage tells us why the toddler bites. It never says the toddler gets the toy. Biting could fail every single time and the passage would still be true. A only claims an attempt; E claims a result.

## PT103-S2-Q2 | Most Supported | B

> Once a child's imagination becomes developed, a host of imaginary creatures may torment the child. But this newly developed cognitive capacity may also be used to render these creatures harmless. For instance, a child's new toy may be imagined as an ally, powerful enough to ward off any imaginary threats.
>
> The type of situation described above most closely conforms to which one of the following propositions?
>
> (A) Some newly developed capacities only give rise to problems.
> (B) Sometimes the cause of a problem may also provide its solution.
> (C) Children are not able to distinguish between real and imaginary threats.
> (D) The most effective way for children to address their fears is to acknowledge them.
> (E) Most problems associated with child-rearing can be solved with a little imagination.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Once a child's imagination develops, imaginary creatures may torment the child. But the same new capacity can make those creatures harmless, for instance by imagining a toy as a powerful ally.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. One thing, a developed imagination, causes the problem (the scary creatures) and also solves it (the imaginary ally). The "But" and "this newly developed cognitive capacity" tie both halves to the same source.

**Goal:** A general principle: the thing that causes a problem can also fix it.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Opposite.** The passage shows a new capacity that does more than cause problems. It also solves them.

**(B) Correct.** Imagination causes the torment and also supplies the cure. "Sometimes" and "may" keep it as modest as the one example.

**(C) Out of Scope.** The passage never says children can't tell real threats from imaginary ones.

**(D) Out of Scope.** Acknowledging fears never comes up, and "most effective" is a comparison the passage can't support.

**(E) Too Strong.** One example about imaginary creatures can't support a claim about *most* child-rearing problems.

## PT105-S4-Q1 | Most Supported | A

> Shortly after Isaac Newton circulated some of his theories of light in 1672, his colleague Robert Hooke claimed that most of those theories were based on Hooke’s own work. A modern reader might interpret Newton’s famous comment, “if I have seen further it is by standing on the shoulders of giants,” as a conciliatory gesture acknowledging indebtedness to Hooke and other contemporary scientists for some of his theories. Conciliatory gestures acknowledging indebtedness were uncharacteristic of Newton, however, and in his day such allusions to “giants” typically referred to the ancient Greeks, not to contemporary scientists.
>
> The statements in the passage, if true, most strongly support which one of the following?
>
> (A) Newton did not intend the quoted comment to be an acknowledgment that his theories of light were largely derived from Hooke’s.
> (B) Newton did not take credit for any advances that Hooke made in the theory of light.
> (C) Newton did not believe that any of Hooke’s theories of light were based on those of the ancient Greeks.
> (D) Newton intended to credit some contemporary scientists other than Hooke for some of the advances that Newton made in the theory of light.
> (E) Newton was not familiar with Hooke’s work on the theory of light.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Hooke claimed most of Newton's light theories came from Hooke's work. A modern reader might read Newton's "shoulders of giants" line as a polite nod to Hooke and other contemporaries. However, Newton didn't make conciliatory gestures like that, and in his day "giants" usually meant the ancient Greeks.

**Evaluate:** The "however" is the author rebutting the modern reading. Both facts after it point the same way: the line probably wasn't a nod to Hooke. It wasn't Newton's style, and "giants" pointed to the Greeks, not to contemporaries.

**Goal:** Newton probably didn't mean the comment as credit to Hooke.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** This is the modern reading, denied. The author gives two reasons to doubt that Newton meant the line as an acknowledgment of Hooke.

**(B) Out of Scope.** The passage is about what Newton's comment meant, not about whether Newton took credit for Hooke's work.

**(C) Out of Scope.** Nothing says what Newton thought about where Hooke's ideas came from.

**(D) Opposite.** This is the most popular wrong answer. The passage points away from contemporaries altogether: "giants" usually meant the ancient Greeks, "not ... contemporary scientists". So if anything, Newton was not crediting other contemporaries either.

**(E) Out of Scope.** Whether Newton knew Hooke's work is never discussed.

## PT105-S4-Q3 | Most Supported | E

> Nutritionist: A study revealed that although most adults estimated their diets to correspond closely with the recommendations of standard nutritional guidelines, most of their diets did not come close to those recommendations. Both women and men underestimated the amount of fat in their diets and overestimated their intake of most other foods. In most food categories, especially fruits and vegetables, women’s diets did not meet the recommendations. Men underestimated their fat intake by half, and though they met the recommendations for breads, they fell short in all other categories.
>
> Which one of the following is most strongly supported by the information offered by the nutritionist?
>
> (A) Both men and women in the study misjudged their compliance with the nutritional guidelines in every food category.
> (B) In the study, more men than women were aware that in some food categories their diet failed to reflect the recommendations closely.
> (C) Women in the study were more aware than men were of the recommended intake of breads.
> (D) Men in the study estimated their daily intake of fruits and vegetables to be significantly lower than it in fact was.
> (E) Most men in the study did not consume the amounts of fruits and vegetables that the nutritional guidelines recommend.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Most adults in the study thought their diets matched the guidelines, but most didn't. Everyone underestimated fat and overestimated most other foods. Women missed the targets in most categories, especially fruits and vegetables. Men underestimated fat by half, met the target for breads, and fell short in every other category.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer on the men. "Fell short in all other categories" means every category except breads. Fruits and vegetables are a category, so men in the study fell short on fruits and vegetables. That is a direct consequence of the last sentence.

**Goal:** Men in the study didn't eat the recommended fruits and vegetables.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** This is the most popular wrong answer. "Every food category" is the problem. We know people overestimated "most other foods", not all of them, and men actually met the bread target. We have no evidence that anyone misjudged breads. One category is enough to sink "every".

**(B) Unsupported Comparison.** The passage says nothing about how many men or women were *aware* of falling short.

**(C) Unsupported Comparison.** Men met the bread target, but that doesn't mean they were more aware of it, and the passage never says what women knew about breads.

**(D) Opposite.** People overestimated their intake of most foods other than fat. If anything, men thought they ate *more* fruits and vegetables than they did, not less.

**(E) Correct.** Men "fell short in all other categories" besides breads, which includes fruits and vegetables. The passage describes the men as a group, so "most men" is a safe reading.

## PT107-S1-Q8 | Most Supported | D

> Nutritionist: Many people claim that simple carbohydrates are a reasonable caloric replacement for the fatty foods forbidden to those on low-fat diets. This is now in doubt. New studies show that, for many people, a high intake of simple carbohydrates stimulates an overproduction of insulin, a hormone that is involved in processing sugars and starches to create energy when the body requires energy, or, when energy is not required, to store the resulting by-products as fat.
>
> Which one of the following is most strongly supported by the nutritionist’s statements?
>
> (A) People on low-fat diets should avoid consumption of simple carbohydrates if they wish to maintain the energy that their bodies require.
> (B) People who produce enough insulin to process their intake of simple carbohydrates should not feel compelled to adopt low-fat diets.
> (C) People who consume simple carbohydrates should limit their intake of foods high in fat.
> (D) People who wish to avoid gaining body fat should limit their intake of foods high in simple carbohydrates.
> (E) People who do not produce an excessive amount of insulin when they consume foods high in simple carbohydrates will not lose weight if they restrict only their intake of these foods.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** People say simple carbs are a good substitute for the fatty foods a low-fat diet forbids. That is now in doubt. For many people, a high intake of simple carbs causes too much insulin. Insulin turns sugars into energy when the body needs it, and stores the by-products as fat when it doesn't.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. Follow the chain: lots of simple carbs → too much insulin → when energy isn't needed, the by-products get stored as fat. So eating lots of simple carbs can make you store fat. That is why swapping fat for simple carbs is "in doubt".

**Goal:** If you want to avoid gaining fat, go easy on simple carbs.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported.** This is the most popular wrong answer. The passage never says simple carbs reduce your energy. Insulin *creates* energy when the body needs it. The problem is the extra fat stored when it doesn't.

**(B) Out of Scope.** The passage says nothing about who should or shouldn't adopt a low-fat diet.

**(C) Out of Scope.** The passage is about cutting simple carbs, not cutting fat.

**(D) Correct.** High simple carbs → too much insulin → fat storage. So limiting simple carbs is a reasonable way to avoid gaining body fat.

**(E) Too Strong.** "Will not lose weight" is far stronger than anything here. The passage doesn't talk about losing weight at all.

## PT107-S4-Q12 | Most Supported | A

> Editorialist: Drivers with a large number of demerit points who additionally have been convicted of a serious driving-related offense should either be sentenced to jail or be forced to receive driver re-education, since to do otherwise would be to allow a crime to go unpunished. Only if such drivers are likely to be made more responsible drivers should driver re-education be recommended for them. Unfortunately, it is always almost impossible to make drivers with a large number of demerit points more responsible drivers.
>
> If the editorialist’s statements are true, they provide the most support for which one of the following?
>
> (A) Drivers with a large number of demerit points who have been convicted of a serious drivingrelated offense should be sent to jail.
> (B) Driver re-education offers the best chance of making drivers with a large number of demerit points responsible drivers.
> (C) Driver re-education is not a harsh enough punishment for anyone convicted of a serious driving-related offense who has also accumulated a large number of demerit points.
> (D) Driver re-education should not be recommended for those who have committed no serious driving-related offenses.
> (E) Drivers with a large number of demerit points but no conviction for a serious driving-related offense should receive driver re-education rather than jail.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Drivers with many demerit points and a serious driving conviction should get jail or driver re-education. Re-education should be recommended only if it is likely to make them more responsible. But it is almost always impossible to make such drivers more responsible.

**Evaluate:** Diagram the middle rule: re-education recommended → likely to become more responsible. The last sentence denies the right side: these drivers are almost never going to become more responsible. Contrapositive: so re-education shouldn't be recommended. The first sentence gives only two options, jail or re-education. Knock one out and the other is left.

**Goal:** These drivers should go to jail.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Two options, and the rules eliminate re-education. Jail is what remains.

**(B) Opposite.** The editorialist says it's almost impossible to make these drivers more responsible, re-education included.

**(C) Unsupported.** This is the most popular wrong answer. It reaches the right result for the wrong reason. Re-education is ruled out because it won't *work*, not because it isn't *harsh enough*. Harshness never comes up.

**(D) Out of Scope.** The rules are about drivers *with* a serious conviction. We can't say anything about drivers without one.

**(E) Out of Scope.** Same problem. Drivers with no serious conviction are outside every rule in the passage.

## PT109-S1-Q7 | Most Supported | B

> Consultant: Most workers do not have every item they produce judged for quality, but each piece a freelance writer authors is evaluated. That is why freelance writers produce such high-quality work.
>
> The consultant’s statements, if true, most strongly support which one of the following?
>
> (A) A piece authored by a freelance writer is generally evaluated more strictly than the majority of items most workers produce.
> (B) By having every piece of their work evaluated, some workers are caused to produce highquality work.
> (C) No other workers produce higher quality work than do freelance writers.
> (D) Only freelance writers have every item they produce evaluated for quality.
> (E) Some workers produce high-quality work in spite of the fact that not every item they produce is judged for quality.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Most workers don't have every item they produce checked for quality. Every piece a freelance writer writes is evaluated. That is why freelance writers produce such high-quality work.

**Evaluate:** "That is why" is a causal claim: being evaluated on every piece causes freelance writers' high quality. Freelance writers are workers. So for at least some workers, having all their work evaluated causes high-quality work.

**Goal:** Evaluating every piece causes high-quality work, at least for some workers.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported Comparison.** This is the most popular wrong answer. The passage is about *how many* pieces get checked, not *how strictly*. Freelance writers have every piece evaluated; most workers don't. Nothing says a freelance piece gets a tougher review than any one item another worker makes.

**(B) Correct.** Freelance writers are "some workers", and the consultant says evaluating every piece is why their work is high quality.

**(C) Too Strong.** The passage says freelance writers do high-quality work, not that nobody does better.

**(D) Too Strong.** "Most workers" don't have every item evaluated. That leaves room for some other workers who do.

**(E) Unsupported.** The passage never says anyone does high-quality work *without* full evaluation.

## PT111-S4-Q26 | Most Strongly Supported | B

> Zachary: The term “fresco” refers to paint that has been applied to wet plaster. Once dried, a fresco indelibly preserves the paint that a painter has applied in this way. Unfortunately, additions known to have been made by later painters have obscured the original fresco work done by Michelangelo in the Sistine Chapel. Therefore, in order to restore Michaelangelo’s Sistine Chapel paintings to the appearance that Michelangelo intended them to have, everything except the original fresco work must be stripped away. Stephen: But it was extremely common for painters of Michelangelo’s era to add painted details to their own fresco work after the frescos had dried.
>
> Stephen’s response to Zachary, if true, most strongly supports which one of the following?
>
> (A) It is impossible to distinguish the later painted additions made to Michelangelo’s Sistine Chapel paintings from the original fresco work.
> (B) Stripping away everything except Michelangelo’s original fresco work from the Sistine Chapel paintings would be unlikely to restore them to the appearance Michelangelo intended them to have.
> (C) The painted details that painters of Michelangelo’s era added to their own fresco work were not an integral part of the completed paintings’ overall design.
> (D) None of the painters of Michelangelo’s era who made additions to the Sistine Chapel paintings was an important artist in his or her own right.
> (E) Michelangelo was rarely satisfied with the appearance of his finished works.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Zachary: fresco is paint applied to wet plaster, and it lasts. Later painters' additions have obscured Michelangelo's fresco work. So to restore the paintings to how Michelangelo intended them, strip away everything except the original fresco. Stephen: painters of that era commonly added painted details to their *own* frescoes after they dried.

**Evaluate:** Stephen's point undercuts the plan. If Michelangelo did what his peers commonly did, some of the paint on top of the dry fresco is Michelangelo's own. Strip away everything but the fresco, and you strip those details too. The result wouldn't be what Michelangelo intended.

**Goal:** Stripping everything but the original fresco probably won't restore Michelangelo's intended look.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported.** This is the most popular wrong answer. Stephen says nothing about whether the additions can be told apart. The problem is different: even if restorers could identify every non-fresco layer perfectly, the plan says to remove *all* of them, including Michelangelo's own.

**(B) Correct.** Michelangelo probably added his own details after the fresco dried. Strip everything but the fresco and those details go too. "Unlikely" fits the "extremely common" in Stephen's evidence.

**(C) Opposite.** Stephen's point only matters if those added details were part of the finished work. Nothing suggests they weren't.

**(D) Out of Scope.** Whether the other painters were important artists is irrelevant.

**(E) Out of Scope.** Nothing is said about Michelangelo's satisfaction with his work.

## PT112-S1-Q4 | Most Supported | C

> Most antidepressant drugs cause weight gain. While dieting can help reduce the amount of weight gained while taking such antidepressants, some weight gain is unlikely to be preventable.
>
> The information above most strongly supports which one of the following?
>
> (A) A physician should not prescribe any antidepressant drug for a patient if that patient is overweight.
> (B) People who are trying to lose weight should not ask their doctors for an antidepressant drug.
> (C) At least some patients taking antidepressant drugs gain weight as a result of taking them.
> (D) The weight gain experienced by patients taking antidepressant drugs should be attributed to lack of dieting.
> (E) All patients taking antidepressant drugs should diet to maintain their weight.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Most antidepressants cause weight gain. Dieting can reduce the weight gained, but some of the gain probably can't be prevented.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. If the drugs cause weight gain and some of it can't be prevented, then some patients will gain weight because of the drugs. The claim is small, and that is what makes it safe.

**Goal:** At least some patients gain weight from the drugs.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Out of Scope.** The passage gives no rule about what doctors should prescribe.

**(B) Out of Scope.** Again, no advice about who should ask for antidepressants.

**(C) Correct.** The drugs cause weight gain, and some of it "is unlikely to be preventable". "At least some" asks for very little, and the passage gives more than enough.

**(D) Opposite.** The passage says some gain probably can't be prevented even with dieting. So the gain isn't simply due to not dieting.

**(E) Too Strong.** "All patients should diet" is a recommendation the passage never makes.

## PT112-S4-Q10 | Most Supported | E

> Twelve healthy volunteers with the Apo-A-IV-1 gene and twelve healthy volunteers who instead have the Apo-A-IV-2 gene each consumed a standard diet supplemented daily by a high-cholesterol food. A high level of cholesterol in the blood is associated with an increased risk of heart disease. After three weeks, the blood cholesterol levels of the subjects in the second group were unchanged, whereas the blood cholesterol levels of those with the Apo-A-IV-1 gene rose 20 percent.
>
> Which one of the following is most strongly supported by the information above?
>
> (A) Approximately half the population carries a gene that lowers cholesterol levels.
> (B) Most of those at risk of heart disease may be able to reduce their risk by adopting a low-cholesterol diet.
> (C) The bodies of those who have the Apo-A-IV-2 gene excrete cholesterol when blood cholesterol reaches a certain level.
> (D) The presence of the Apo-A-IV-1 gene seems to indicate that a person has a lower risk of heart disease.
> (E) The presence of the Apo-A-IV-2 gene may inhibit the elevation of blood cholesterol.

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Twelve people with the Apo-A-IV-1 gene and twelve with Apo-A-IV-2 ate the same diet plus a high-cholesterol food every day. After three weeks, the IV-2 group's blood cholesterol hadn't changed. The IV-1 group's rose 20%. High blood cholesterol is linked to higher heart disease risk.

**Evaluate:** Pause and infer. Same diet, different genes, different results. The IV-2 group ate the extra cholesterol and their levels didn't rise. So the IV-2 gene may be keeping blood cholesterol from going up. With only twelve people per group, "may" is as far as we should go.

**Goal:** The IV-2 gene may keep blood cholesterol from rising.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported.** The study picked twelve of each. That tells us nothing about how common either gene is.

**(B) Out of Scope.** The study didn't test a low-cholesterol diet, and says nothing about most people at risk.

**(C) Unsupported.** This is tied for the most popular wrong answer. It names a specific mechanism, excretion at a certain level. The study shows the IV-2 group's cholesterol didn't rise, not *how* their bodies managed it.

**(D) Opposite.** This is also tied for most popular. The IV-1 group's cholesterol rose 20%, and high cholesterol goes with higher heart risk. So IV-1, if anything, points to a *higher* risk.

**(E) Correct.** The only difference between the groups was the gene, and only the IV-2 group's cholesterol held steady. "May inhibit" is cautious enough for a small study.
