## PT107-S4-Q6 | Main Conclusion | E

> Some judges complain about statutes that specify mandatory minimum sentences for criminal offenses. These legal restrictions, they complain, are too mechanical and prevent judges from deciding when a given individual can or cannot be rehabilitated. But that is precisely why mandatory minimum sentences are necessary. History amply demonstrates that when people are free to use their own judgment they invariably believe themselves to act wisely when in fact they are often arbitrary and irrational. There is no reason to think that judges are an exception to this rule.
>
> Which one of the following sentences most accurately expresses the main point of the passage?
>
> (A) People believe that they have good judgment but never do.
> (B) Mandatory minimum sentences are too mechanical and reduce judicial discretion.
> (C) Judges should be free to exercise their own judgment.
> (D) Judges are often arbitrary and irrational.
> (E) Mandatory minimum sentences are needed to help prevent judicial arbitrariness.

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** Some judges complain that mandatory minimum sentences are too mechanical and stop judges from deciding who can be rehabilitated.

**Conclusion:** That is exactly why mandatory minimum sentences are necessary.

**Evidence:** People free to use their own judgment think they act wisely but are often arbitrary and irrational, and judges are no exception.

**Evaluate:** "But that is precisely why" marks the pivot. Swap "that" for what it refers to: minimums are necessary because they limit judges' discretion. The last two sentences explain why limiting discretion is good: judges' judgment is often arbitrary.

**Goal:** Mandatory minimum sentences are needed because they keep judges from acting arbitrarily.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong Premise.** This is a distorted version of the evidence. The passage says people are often arbitrary, not that they never have good judgment.

**(B) Opposing Point.** This is the judges' complaint, which the author is answering.

**(C) Opposite.** The author argues that judges' discretion should be limited.

**(D) Premise.** This is the support for the conclusion. Judges being often arbitrary is why minimums are needed.

**(E) Correct.** Minimums are necessary, and the reason is to limit arbitrary judicial discretion. This combines the pivot sentence with what "that" refers to.

## PT110-S2-Q18 | Main Conclusion | B

> Editorialist: The positions advanced by radical environmentalists often contain hypotheses that are false and proposals that are economically infeasible. But there is a positive role to be played even by these extremists, for the social and political inertia that attends environmental issues is so stubborn that even small areas of progress can be made only if the populace fears environmental disaster, however untenable the reasons for those fears may be.
>
> Which one of the following most accurately expresses the main conclusion of the editorialist’s argument?
>
> (A) The little progress that has been made in improving the environment is mainly due to the fear created by radical environmentalists.
> (B) Radical environmentalists, by promoting their views, stimulate progress on environmental issues.
> (C) Social and political inertia is most effectively overcome by an extremely fearful populace, regardless of whether its fears are well-founded.
> (D) Radical environmentalists often put forth untenable positions in order to produce the fear that is required to bring about moderate reforms.
> (E) Radical environmentalists advocate positions without regard for factual support or economic feasibility.

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** Radical environmentalists' positions often contain false hypotheses and economically infeasible proposals.

**Conclusion:** Even these extremists play a positive role.

**Evidence:** Inertia on environmental issues is so stubborn that progress happens only if people fear environmental disaster, however ill-founded the fear.

**Evaluate:** "But there is a positive role" is the pivot, and "for" introduces the support. What is the positive role? Radicals create the fear that makes progress possible. So the conclusion is that radicals, by pushing their views, help bring about progress.

**Goal:** Radical environmentalists play a positive role by stimulating progress.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** The editorialist doesn't say how much progress has been made, or that it is mainly due to radicals. The claim is only that radicals play a positive role.

**(B) Correct.** This states the positive role: by promoting their views, radicals help produce progress. That is the claim the "for" clause supports.

**(C) Premise, Distorted.** This restates the evidence about fear and inertia, and adds "most effectively," which the passage doesn't say.

**(D) Wrong Motive.** This is the most-picked trap. It says radicals put forth untenable positions in order to produce fear. The editorialist never says the radicals intend this. They have a positive effect, whatever their motives.

**(E) Opposing Point, Exaggerated.** This overstates the concession at the start. It's the part the author grants before the "but."

## PT114-S4-Q3 | Main Conclusion | E

> Journalist: Obviously, though some animals are purely carnivorous, none would survive without plants. But the dependence is mutual. Many plant species would never have come to be had there been no animals to pollinate, fertilize, and broadcast their seeds. Also, plants’ photosynthetic activity would deplete the carbon dioxide in Earth’s atmosphere were it not constantly being replenished by the exhalation of animals, engine fumes, and smoke from fires, many set by human beings.
>
> Which one of the following most accurately expresses the main conclusion of the journalist’s argument?
>
> (A) The photosynthetic activity of plants is necessary for animal life, but animal life is also necessary for the occurrence of photosynthesis in plants.
> (B) Some purely carnivorous animals would not survive without plants.
> (C) The chemical composition of Earth and its atmosphere depends, at least to some extent, on the existence and activities of the animals that populate Earth.
> (D) Human activity is part of what prevents plants from depleting the oxygen in Earth’s atmosphere on which plants and animals alike depend.
> (E) Just as animals are dependent on plants for their survival, plants are dependent on animals for theirs.

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** Obviously no animals would survive without plants.

**Conclusion:** The dependence is mutual.

**Evidence:** Many plant species exist only because animals pollinate, fertilize, and spread their seeds. Without animals, plants' photosynthesis would deplete the atmosphere's carbon dioxide.

**Evaluate:** "But the dependence is mutual" is the pivot. Spell out "mutual": animals depend on plants, and plants also depend on animals. The two "Also" points are evidence for the plant-on-animal side.

**Goal:** Animals and plants depend on each other.

**(A) Too Narrow.** This makes photosynthesis the whole story. The journalist's point covers pollination and seeds too, and this choice also says animals are needed for photosynthesis to occur, which the passage doesn't say.

**(B) Premise.** This is part of the opening concession, not the conclusion.

**(C) Too Narrow.** This picks up only the carbon dioxide point and turns it into a claim about Earth's chemistry. It leaves out the mutual dependence.

**(D) Wrong Detail.** The passage says plants would deplete carbon dioxide, not oxygen. It also isn't the conclusion.

**(E) Correct.** Animals depend on plants, and plants depend on animals. That is what "the dependence is mutual" means.

**Opposing Point:** Recent studies say the operas of Bizet and Verdi reinforce stereotypes about women in our society.

**Evidence:** Only a small minority of North Americans, opera lovers, have had any significant exposure to these operas.

**(D) Too Broad.** The critic talks only about these operas, not opera as a whole as a force on social attitudes.

**(E) Too Strong.** The critic needs only that limited exposure means limited influence. "Cannot be influenced" by anything not directly seen goes much further. It's closer to an assumption than to the conclusion.
