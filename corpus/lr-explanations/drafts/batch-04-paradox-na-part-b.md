## PT102-S2-Q6 | Necessary Assumption | C

> One test to determine whether a person has been infected with tuberculosis consists of injecting the person with proteins extracted from the tuberculosis bacterium. Once a person has been infected by a bacterium, the person's immune system subsequently recognizes certain proteins present in that bacterium and attacks the bacterium. This recognition also takes place in the test and results in a skin irritation at the injection site. Hence the physicians who designed the test reasoned that anyone who reacts in this manner to an injection with the tuberculosis proteins has been infected with tuberculosis.
>
> Which one of the following is an assumption on which the physicians' reasoning depends?
>
> (A) All of the proteins present in disease-causing bacteria can be recognized by the body's immune system.
> (B) Localized skin irritations are a characteristic symptom of tuberculosis in most people.
> (C) The ability of the proteins present in the tuberculosis bacterium to trigger the skin irritation is exclusive to that bacterium.
> (D) Some people who have been injected with proteins extracted from the tuberculosis bacterium will contract tuberculosis as a result of the injection.
> (E) The body's immune system cannot recognize infectious bacteria unless there are sufficient quantities of the bacteria to cause overt symptoms of disease.

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Anyone who gets the skin irritation from the injection has been infected with tuberculosis.

**Evidence:** Once infected, the immune system recognizes certain proteins from the bacterium and attacks. In the test, this recognition causes the skin irritation.

**Evaluate:** The evidence shows that infection leads to the reaction. The conclusion runs the other way: the reaction proves infection. That only works if nothing else can trigger the reaction. What if someone was infected with a *different* bacterium whose proteins also set off the irritation?

**Goal:** A defender: nothing other than tuberculosis infection can make these proteins cause the irritation.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** The argument is about tuberculosis proteins. It does not need every protein in every disease-causing bacterium to be recognizable.

**(B) Out of Scope.** Whether skin irritation is a symptom of the disease itself is irrelevant. The test is about a reaction to an injection.

**(C) Correct.** Negate it: the reaction is *not* exclusive to tuberculosis, so other bacteria can trigger it too. Then a reaction does not show tuberculosis infection, and the conclusion falls apart.

**(D) Weakens.** If the injection itself can cause tuberculosis, that is a problem for the test, not something the physicians assume.

**(E) Out of Scope.** This is about whether infected people always react. The conclusion only claims that everyone who reacts is infected.

## PT102-S2-Q12 | Necessary Assumption | A

> It has long been thought that lizards evolved from a group of amphibians called anthracosaurs, no fossils of which have been found in any rocks older than 300 million years. However, a fossil of a lizard was recently found that is estimated to be 340 million years old. Lizards could not have evolved from creatures that did not exist until after the first lizards. Therefore, lizards could not have evolved from anthracosaurs.
>
> An assumption made in the argument is that there are no
>
> (A) unknown anthracosaur fossils older than 340 million years
> (B) unknown lizard fossils older than 340 million years
> (C) known lizard fossils that predate some anthracosaur fossils
> (D) known anthracosaur fossils that predate some lizard fossils
> (E) known lizard fossils whose age is uncertain

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Lizards could not have evolved from anthracosaurs.

**Evidence:** No anthracosaur fossils have been found in rocks older than 300 million years. A lizard fossil has been found that is about 340 million years old. Lizards cannot have evolved from creatures that came after them.

**Evaluate:** The argument moves from "no anthracosaur fossils *found* older than 300 million years" to "anthracosaurs did not exist before then." Not finding a fossil is not the same as the animal not existing. Older anthracosaur fossils might be out there undiscovered.

**Goal:** A defender: there are no undiscovered anthracosaur fossils old enough to come before the first lizards.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Negate it: there *are* unknown anthracosaur fossils older than 340 million years. Then anthracosaurs existed before this lizard, and lizards could have evolved from them. The conclusion falls apart.

**(B) Not Necessary.** Older lizard fossils would only push lizards back further, which helps the argument. The author does not need to rule them out.

**(C) Opposite.** The stimulus already gives us a known lizard fossil older than every known anthracosaur fossil. The author is not assuming there are none.

**(D) Not Necessary.** Known anthracosaur fossils can be older than some younger lizard fossils. What matters is the first lizard, and nothing known is older than it.

**(E) Out of Scope.** Lizard fossils of uncertain age do not affect the argument, which rests on the dated 340-million-year-old fossil.

## PT103-S3-Q11 | Necessary Assumption | A

> Lindsey has been judged to be a bad songwriter simply because her lyrics typically are disjointed and subjective. This judgment is ill founded, however, since the writings of many modern novelists typically are disjointed and subjective and yet these novelists are widely held to be good writers.
>
> Which one of the following is an assumption on which the argument depends?
>
> (A) Disjointed and subjective writing has a comparable effect in modern novels and in songs.
> (B) Some readers do not appreciate the subtleties of the disjointed and subjective style adopted by modern novelists.
> (C) Song lyrics that are disjointed and subjective have at least as much narrative structure as any other song lyrics do.
> (D) A disjointed and subjective style of writing is usually more suitable for novels and song lyrics than it is for any other written works.
> (E) The quality of Lindsey’s songs is better judged by the quality of their lyrics than by the quality of their musical form.

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Judging Lindsey a bad songwriter because her lyrics are disjointed and subjective is ill-founded.

**Evidence:** Many modern novelists write in a disjointed, subjective way and are still considered good writers.

**Evaluate:** This is an argument by analogy. It only works if novels and song lyrics are alike in the way that matters. Maybe that style works in a long novel but not in a short song.

**Goal:** A defender: the disjointed, subjective style works in songs the same way it does in novels.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Negate it: this style does *not* have a comparable effect in novels and songs. Then the novelists tell us nothing about Lindsey's lyrics, and the analogy collapses.

**(B) Out of Scope.** Whether some readers miss the subtleties of modern novels does not affect the comparison with Lindsey.

**(C) Out of Scope.** Narrative structure is never discussed. The argument does not need Lindsey's lyrics to match other lyrics in structure.

**(D) Irrelevant Comparison.** The argument does not compare novels and songs with other kinds of writing.

**(E) Not Necessary.** The judgment being criticized is already about her lyrics. The author does not need lyrics to matter more than the music.

## PT104-S1-Q13 | Necessary Assumption | C

> Problems caused by the leaching of pollutants from dumps and landfills are worst in countries with an annual per capita economic output of $4,000 to $5,000, and less severe for considerably poorer and considerably richer countries. This is so because pollution problems increase during the early stages of a country’s industrial development but then diminish as increasing industrial development generates adequate resources to tackle such problems. Therefore, problems caused by such leaching in Country X, where the annual per capita economic output is now $5,000, should begin to diminish in the next few years.
>
> Which one of the following is an assumption on which the argument depends?
>
> (A) Within the next few years, Country X will impose a system of fines for illegal waste disposal by its industrial companies.
> (B) Countries surrounding Country X will reduce the amount of pollution that their factories release into the air and water.
> (C) Industrial development in Country X will increase in the next few years.
> (D) Country X will begin the process of industrialization in the next few years.
> (E) No other country with a similar amount of industrial development has pollution problems that are as severe as those in Country X.

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Leaching problems in Country X should begin to diminish in the next few years.

**Evidence:** Leaching problems are worst at $4,000 to $5,000 per capita output, because pollution rises early in industrial development and falls once further development brings the resources to deal with it. Country X is now at $5,000.

**Evaluate:** The problems fall as *increasing* industrial development generates resources. The argument predicts a decline, so it is counting on Country X to keep developing. If development stalls, nothing produces the resources.

**Goal:** Country X's industrial development will keep increasing.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Not Necessary.** Fines might help, but the argument relies on development bringing resources, not on any specific policy.

**(B) Out of Scope.** Pollution from neighboring countries does not affect leaching from Country X's dumps.

**(C) Correct.** Negate it: industrial development in Country X will *not* increase. Then the resources to tackle pollution never arrive, and there is no reason to expect the problems to diminish.

**(D) Opposite.** Country X is already at the income level where the problems peak, so it has already begun industrializing. The author does not assume it has yet to start.

**(E) Out of Scope.** How Country X compares with other countries does not affect what will happen to it.

## PT104-S4-Q19 | Necessary Assumption | A

> Most people feel that they are being confused by the information from broadcast news. This could be the effect of the information’s being delivered too quickly or of its being poorly organized. Analysis of the information content of a typical broadcast news story shows that news stories are far lower in information density than the maximum information density with which most people can cope at any one time. So the information in typical broadcast news stories is poorly organized.
>
> Which one of the following is an assumption that the argument requires in order for its conclusion to be properly drawn?
>
> (A) It is not the number of broadcast news stories to which a person is exposed that is the source of the feeling of confusion.
> (B) Poor organization of information in a news story makes it impossible to understand the information.
> (C) Being exposed to more broadcast news stories within a given day would help a person to better understand the news.
> (D) Most people can cope with a very high information density.
> (E) Some people are being overwhelmed by too much information.

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** The information in typical broadcast news stories is poorly organized.

**Evidence:** People feel confused by broadcast news. This could be because the information comes too fast or because it is poorly organized. News stories are far below the information density most people can handle.

**Evaluate:** The author names two possible causes, rules out one, and concludes the other. That only works if those are the only two. What if something else causes the confusion?

**Goal:** A defender: rule out some other cause of the confusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Negate it: the *number* of news stories a person sees *is* the source of the confusion. That is a third cause, and poor organization is no longer the only explanation left.

**(B) Too Strong.** The argument does not need poor organization to make understanding *impossible*, only to be the cause of confusion.

**(C) Out of Scope.** Whether seeing more stories would help is not about what causes the confusion now.

**(D) Too Strong.** The evidence already says stories are well below what people can handle. The argument does not need people to cope with a *very* high density.

**(E) Weakens.** Some people being overwhelmed by too much information points toward the "too fast" explanation the author ruled out.

## PT106-S1-Q12 | Necessary Assumption | B

> People who say that Dooney County is flat are clearly wrong. On flat land, soil erosion by water is not a problem. Consequently, farmers whose land is flat do not build terraces to prevent erosion. Yet I hear that the farms in Dooney County are dotted with terraces.
>
> The author’s conclusion in the passage depends on the assumption that
>
> (A) the only cause of soil erosion is water
> (B) there are terraces on farmland in Dooney County which were built to prevent soil erosion
> (C) terraces of the kind found on farmland in Dooney County have been shown to prevent soil erosion
> (D) on flat land there is no soil erosion
> (E) the only terraces in Dooney County are on farmland

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** People who say Dooney County is flat are wrong.

**Evidence:** On flat land, water erosion is not a problem, so farmers on flat land do not build terraces to prevent erosion. Dooney County's farms are dotted with terraces.

**Evaluate:** The premise only rules out terraces built *to prevent erosion* on flat land. Farmers might build terraces for some other reason. If Dooney's terraces were built for something else, they tell us nothing about whether the land is flat.

**Goal:** A link: the terraces in Dooney County were built to prevent erosion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Too Strong.** The argument does not need water to be the *only* cause of erosion.

**(B) Correct.** Negate it: *none* of the terraces in Dooney County were built to prevent erosion. Then the evidence about flat land and erosion terraces does not apply, and the terraces say nothing about the land.

**(C) Not Necessary.** What matters is why the farmers built the terraces, not whether the terraces actually work.

**(D) Too Strong.** The stimulus says erosion is not a *problem* on flat land. The argument does not need there to be no erosion at all.

**(E) Out of Scope.** Terraces elsewhere in the county do not affect the argument, which rests on the terraces on farms.

## PT106-S3-Q19 | Necessary Assumption | A

> On a certain day, nine scheduled flights on Swift Airlines were canceled. Ordinarily, a cancellation is due to mechanical problems with the airplane scheduled for a certain flight. However, since it is unlikely that Swift would have mechanical problems with more than one or two airplanes on a single day, some of the nine cancellations were probably due to something else.
>
> The argument depends on which one of the following assumptions?
>
> (A) More than one or two airplanes were scheduled for the nine canceled flights.
> (B) Swift Airlines has fewer mechanical problems than do other airlines of the same size.
> (C) Each of the canceled flights would have been longer than the average flight on Swift Airlines.
> (D) Swift Airlines had never before canceled more than one or two scheduled flights on a single day.
> (E) All of the airplanes scheduled for the canceled flights are based at the same airport.

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Some of the nine cancellations were probably due to something other than mechanical problems.

**Evidence:** Nine Swift flights were canceled in one day. Cancellations are usually caused by mechanical problems with the plane scheduled for the flight. Swift is unlikely to have mechanical problems with more than one or two planes in a day.

**Evaluate:** The argument switches from nine *flights* to one or two *planes*. A single plane often flies several routes in a day. If one or two broken planes were scheduled for all nine flights, mechanical problems explain every cancellation.

**Goal:** The nine flights were scheduled on more than one or two planes.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Negate it: only one or two planes were scheduled for all nine flights. Then one or two mechanical problems could account for every cancellation, and there is no reason to suspect another cause.

**(B) Irrelevant Comparison.** How Swift compares with other airlines does not matter. The argument is about Swift on this day.

**(C) Out of Scope.** Flight length plays no role in the argument.

**(D) Not Necessary.** Past cancellation records do not affect what caused these nine.

**(E) Out of Scope.** Where the planes are based does not affect how many there were.

## PT113-S3-Q2 | Necessary Assumption | D

> For the last three years, entomologists have been searching for a parasite to help control a whitefly that has recently become a serious crop pest. Believing this new pest to be a variety of sweet-potato whitefly, the entomologists confined their search to parasites of the sweet-potato whitefly. Genetic research now shows the new pest to be a distinct species, the silverleaf whitefly. Therefore, the search for a parasite has so far been wasted effort.
>
> Which one of the following is an assumption on which the argument relies?
>
> (A) All varieties of the sweet-potato whitefly are serious crop pests.
> (B) If a crop pest has a parasite, that parasite can always be used to control that pest.
> (C) The chances of successfully identifying a useful parasite of the new pest have increased since the proper identification of the pest.
> (D) No parasite of the sweet-potato whitefly is also a parasite of the silverleaf whitefly.
> (E) In the last three years, the entomologists found no parasites of the sweet-potato whitefly.

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** The search for a parasite has so far been wasted effort.
