## PT124-S2-Q1 | Paradox | A

> The water of Lake Laberge, in Canada, currently contains high levels of the pesticide toxaphene. Authorities are puzzled because toxaphene was banned in North America in the early 1980s and now is used only in a few other parts of the world.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, does most to explain why the water of Lake Laberge currently contains high levels of toxaphene?
>
> (A) Levels of pesticides in the environment often continue to be high for decades after their use ends.
> (B) Lake Laberge's water contains high levels of other pesticides besides toxaphene.
> (C) Toxic chemicals usually do not travel large distances in the atmosphere.
> (D) North American manufacturers opposed banning toxaphene.
> (E) Toxic chemicals become more readily detectable once they enter organisms the size of fish.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** Toxaphene was banned in North America in the early 1980s and is now used only in a few other parts of the world.

**How can it be that:** Lake Laberge, in Canada, has high levels of toxaphene today?

**Evaluate:** Two ways the pesticide could be there. It could have arrived recently from somewhere it is still used, or it could be left over from before the ban. Either story resolves the puzzle.

**Goal:** Find a source: old toxaphene that never went away, or new toxaphene arriving from elsewhere.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** Pesticide levels often stay high for decades after use ends. The ban was only a few decades ago, so the toxaphene in the lake can simply be left over from before it.

**(B) No Impact.** Other pesticides in the water do not explain where the toxaphene came from.

**(C) Deepens Paradox.** If toxic chemicals rarely travel far through the air, toxaphene from distant countries is less likely to reach the lake. This closes off one explanation.

**(D) No Impact.** Manufacturers opposing the ban does not mean anyone kept using toxaphene after it.

**(E) Out of Scope.** The puzzle is toxaphene in the lake's water. How easily it is detected in fish says nothing about why it is there.

## PT127-S1-Q11 | Paradox | B

> Studies have shown that the more high-stress points a bridge has, the more likely it is to fracture eventually. This might lead one to expect fractures to develop at high-stress points. Surprisingly, however, fractures develop not at high-stress points but elsewhere on the bridge.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, contributes most to an explanation of why bridges fracture elsewhere than at high-stress points?
>
> (A) In many structures other than bridges, such as ship hulls and airplane bodies, fractures do not develop at high-stress points.
> (B) Fractures do not develop at high-stress points, because bridges are reinforced at those points; however, stress is transferred to other points on the bridge where it causes fractures.
> (C) In many structures, the process of fracturing often causes high-stress points to develop.
> (D) Structures with no high-stress points can nonetheless have a high probability of fracturing.
> (E) Improper bridge construction, e.g., low-quality welding or the use of inferior steel, often leads both to the development of high-stress points and to an increased probability of fracturing.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** The more high-stress points a bridge has, the more likely it is to fracture.

**How can it be that:** The fractures develop somewhere other than the high-stress points?

**Evaluate:** Both facts have to fit. High-stress points must raise the risk of fracture, yet the break happens elsewhere. So the stress has to cause trouble somewhere else on the bridge.

**Goal:** Find a way high-stress points lead to fractures at other points.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Irrelevant Comparison.** Other structures having the same pattern does not explain why it happens in bridges.

**(B) Correct.** Builders reinforce the high-stress points, so those points hold. The stress moves to weaker parts of the bridge and causes the fracture there. More high-stress points means more stress moved around, and so a higher chance of fracture.

**(C) Deepens Paradox.** If fracturing creates high-stress points, fractures and high-stress points should show up in the same place. That is the opposite of what we see.

**(D) Half Scope.** This explains how fractures can happen without high-stress points, but not why more high-stress points still make a fracture more likely.

**(E) Half Scope.** Bad construction explains why high-stress points and fractures go together. It does not explain why the fractures land somewhere else.

## PT127-S1-Q24 | Paradox | C

> Provinces and states with stringent car safety requirements, including required use of seat belts and annual safety inspections, have on average higher rates of accidents per kilometer driven than do provinces and states with less stringent requirements. Nevertheless, most highway safety experts agree that more stringent requirements do reduce accident rates.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to reconcile the safety experts’ belief with the apparently contrary evidence described above?
>
> (A) Annual safety inspections ensure that car tires are replaced before they grow old.
> (B) Drivers often become overconfident after their cars have passed a thorough safety inspection.
> (C) The roads in provinces and states with stringent car safety requirements are far more congested and therefore dangerous than in other provinces and states.
> (D) Psychological studies show that drivers who regularly wear seat belts often come to think of themselves as serious drivers, which for a few people discourages reckless driving.
> (E) Provinces and states with stringent car safety requirements have, on average, many more kilometers of roads than do other provinces and states.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** Experts agree that stricter car safety requirements reduce accident rates.

**How can it be that:** Places with stricter requirements have higher accident rates per kilometer driven?

**Evaluate:** The comparison is between different places, and places differ in more than their rules. If the strict places are more dangerous to begin with, they can have higher accident rates even though the rules are helping.

**Goal:** Find a reason the strict places would have more accidents anyway.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Half Scope.** Newer tires support the experts, but this does not explain why strict places still have more accidents.

**(B) Opposite.** Overconfidence after inspection is a way the rules could *cause* accidents. That supports the evidence against the experts.

**(C) Correct.** The strict places have far more congested, dangerous roads. They would have more accidents without the rules, so the rules can still be lowering a rate that starts higher.

**(D) Too Weak.** Seat belts discouraging reckless driving "for a few people" supports the experts a little, but it does not explain the higher accident rate.

**(E) No Impact.** The accident rate is per kilometer *driven*. Having more kilometers of road does not change that rate.

## PT128-S2-Q4 | Paradox | B

> In the spring and fall, eastern pipistrelle bats roost deep inside caves. They feed at night on flying insects and must leave the cave to catch their prey. Flying insects are much more abundant on warm nights than on cool ones. Researchers found that many more bats leave the caves on warm nights than on cool nights, even though the temperature within the caves where the bats roost remains virtually the same from one night to the next.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to explain the researchers' findings?
>
> (A) The researchers studied only female bats, which tended to catch more insects on warm nights than did the male bats.
> (B) Eastern pipistrelle bats can detect changes in barometric pressure within the caves that correlate closely with changes in temperature outside the caves.
> (C) Eastern pipistrelle bats are incapable of long periods of sustained activity outside the roosting caves on very cool spring and fall evenings.
> (D) Because of the long period of winter inactivity, eastern pipistrelle bats tend to consume more insects per day in the spring and fall than in the summer.
> (E) During the periods in which the researchers studied the bats, on most evenings over half of the bats left the caves in search of food.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** The bats roost deep inside caves, where the temperature barely changes from night to night.

**How can it be that:** Many more bats leave the caves on warm nights than on cool ones?

**Evaluate:** It makes sense for bats to hunt on warm nights, since that is when insects are out. The puzzle is how they know it is warm outside when the cave feels the same. They need some other signal that tracks the outside temperature.

**Goal:** Find a way bats inside the cave can tell what the weather is outside.

### Choice Notes

**(A) No Impact.** Female bats catching more insects on warm nights does not explain how the bats know it is warm.

**(B) Correct.** The bats can sense barometric pressure inside the cave, and that pressure moves with the temperature outside. That is their signal.

**(C) Half Scope.** This is a reason to stay in on cool nights, but it does not explain how a bat in the cave can tell the night is cool.

**(D) No Impact.** Eating more in spring and fall than in summer does not explain the difference between warm and cool nights.

**(E) No Impact.** Knowing that more than half the bats left on most evenings says nothing about how they choose which nights.

## PT134-S1-Q6 | Paradox | E

> The number of tornadoes recorded annually in North America has more than tripled since 1953. Yet meteorologists insist that the climatic factors affecting the creation of tornadoes are unchanged.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to resolve the apparent discrepancy described above?
>
> (A) The factors affecting the creation of tornadoes were not well known to meteorologists before 1953.
> (B) The intensity of the average tornado is greater now than it was in 1953.
> (C) The number of tornadoes recorded annually has increased only slightly in the last five years.
> (D) The amount of property damage done by tornadoes has grown substantially since 1953.
> (E) Many more citizens are helping authorities detect tornadoes now than in 1953.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** The climate factors that create tornadoes have not changed since 1953.

**How can it be that:** The number of tornadoes *recorded* each year has more than tripled?

**Evaluate:** Look at the word "recorded." The number of tornadoes could stay the same while the number we notice and write down goes up. Better detection would resolve it.

**Goal:** Find a reason more tornadoes are recorded without more tornadoes happening.

### Choice Notes

**(A) No Impact.** Meteorologists understanding the factors better now does not change how many tornadoes are recorded.

**(B) Deepens Paradox.** Stronger tornadoes suggest something about the climate has changed, which makes the meteorologists' claim harder to believe.

**(C) No Impact.** A slow rise in the last five years does not explain the tripling since 1953.

**(D) Out of Scope.** Property damage depends on how much property is in the way. It does not explain more recorded tornadoes.

**(E) Correct.** Many more citizens now help detect tornadoes. More tornadoes get noticed and recorded, while the number actually forming stays the same.

## PT134-S3-Q8 | Paradox | D

> A recent study shows that those highways that carry the most traffic, and thus tend to be the most congested, have the lowest rate of fatal traffic accidents.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to explain the phenomenon described above?
>
> (A) Drivers have more accidents when they become distracted.
> (B) The highways that have the highest rate of fatal accidents have moderate volumes of traffic.
> (C) Most of the motorists on very heavily traveled highways tend to be commuting to or from work.
> (D) Most serious accidents occur when vehicles are moving at a high rate of speed.
> (E) Heavily traveled highways do not always carry a higher proportion of large trucks.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** The highways with the most traffic tend to be the most congested.

**How can it be that:** Those highways have the lowest rate of fatal accidents?

**Evaluate:** You might expect more cars to mean more deadly crashes. But congestion also changes how people drive. If congestion keeps speeds low, and speed is what makes crashes deadly, the busiest roads could be the safest.

**Goal:** Find something about congestion that makes fatal crashes less likely.

### Choice Notes

**(A) No Impact.** Distraction causing accidents does not separate busy highways from quiet ones.

**(B) Restates Paradox.** This says where the high fatal rates are instead of explaining why the busiest roads have low ones.

**(C) No Impact.** Knowing that the drivers are commuters does not tell us why they have fewer fatal accidents.

**(D) Correct.** Most serious accidents happen at high speed. Congested highways keep traffic slow, so they produce fewer of these deadly crashes.

**(E) No Impact.** Busy highways "do not always" carry more trucks. This does not connect traffic to fatal accidents either way.

## PT138-S2-Q3 | Paradox | B

> Scientist: Venus contains a hot molten core, like that of Earth. Also like Earth, Venus must expel the excess heat the core generates. On Earth, this occurs entirely through active volcanos and fissures created when tectonic plates separate. Yet Venus has neither active volcanos nor fissures caused by the movement of tectonic plates.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, does the most to resolve the apparent discrepancy described by the scientist?
>
> (A) Rock on the surface of Venus remains solid at much higher temperatures than does rock on Earth.
> (B) The surface of Venus is relatively thin, allowing internally produced heat to radiate into space.
> (C) The interior of Venus undergoes greater fluctuations in temperature than does that of Earth.
> (D) Though Venus lacks active volcanoes and heat-diffusing fissures, it has surface movement somewhat like that of Earth.
> (E) The atmosphere of Venus is significantly hotter than that of Earth.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** Venus has a hot core and must expel its excess heat, as Earth does. Earth does it entirely through volcanos and fissures.

**How can it be that:** Venus has no active volcanos and no fissures from moving plates?

**Evaluate:** Earth's method is not the only possible one. If Venus has another way to release heat, the puzzle goes away.

**Goal:** Find another way for Venus to get rid of its heat.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Half Scope.** Rock staying solid at high temperatures might explain why Venus has no volcanos. It does not explain where the heat goes.

**(B) Correct.** Venus's surface is thin, so heat from inside radiates straight into space. That is a way out for the heat without volcanos or fissures.

**(C) No Impact.** Bigger temperature swings inside Venus do not explain how it releases heat.

**(D) Unclear Impact.** Surface movement "somewhat like" Earth's is not a way of releasing heat, and the stimulus already says Venus has no fissures from that movement.

**(E) Deepens Paradox.** A hotter atmosphere would make it harder, not easier, for heat to escape.

## PT138-S4-Q5 | Paradox | C

> Recent epidemiological studies report that Country X has the lowest incidence of disease P of any country. Nevertheless, residents of Country X who are reported to have contracted disease P are much more likely to die from it than are residents of any other country.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to resolve the apparent discrepancy described above?
>
> (A) There are several forms of disease P, some of which are more contagious than others.
> (B) Most of the fatal cases of disease P found in Country X involve people who do not reside in Country X.
> (C) In Country X, diagnosis of disease P seldom occurs except in the most severe cases of the disease.
> (D) The number of cases of disease P that occur in any country fluctuates widely from year to year.
> (E) Because of its climate, more potentially fatal illnesses occur in Country X than in many other countries.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** Country X has the lowest reported rate of disease P of any country.

**How can it be that:** Residents of Country X reported to have it are much more likely to die from it than residents of any other country?

**Evaluate:** Both numbers depend on which cases get *reported*. If Country X only diagnoses the worst cases, it would record few cases, and the ones it records would be the deadliest.

**Goal:** Find a reason Country X records fewer and more severe cases.

### Choice Notes

**(A) No Impact.** Some forms being more contagious does not explain the gap between low incidence and high deaths.

**(B) Out of Scope.** The stimulus is about *residents* of Country X. Deaths among nonresidents do not explain it.

**(C) Correct.** Country X seldom diagnoses the disease except in the most severe cases. Mild cases go uncounted, which keeps the reported rate low. The cases that are counted are the deadly ones, which drives up the death rate.

**(D) No Impact.** Year-to-year swings in the number of cases affect every country and do not explain this gap.

**(E) Out of Scope.** More potentially fatal illnesses in general says nothing about disease P.

## PT147-S4-Q1 | Paradox | D

> Cool weather typically weakens muscle power in cold-blooded creatures. In the veiled chameleon, a cold-blooded animal, the speed at which the animal can retract its tongue declines dramatically as the temperature falls. However, the speed at which this chameleon can extend its tongue does not decline much as the temperature falls.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to resolve the apparent discrepancy in the information above?
>
> (A) Most cold-blooded animals are much more active in warmer weather than in cooler weather.
> (B) Many cold-blooded animals, including the veiled chameleon, have tongues that can extend quite a distance.
> (C) Veiled chameleons are found in a wide range of habitats, including ones with wide variations in temperature and ones with moderate climates.
> (D) In the veiled chameleon, tongue retraction is powered by muscles, whereas tongue extension is driven by energy stored in a rubber band-like sheath.
> (E) Compared with the muscles in the tongues of most cold-blooded animals, the retraction muscles in the veiled chameleon’s tongue are considerably stronger.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** Cool weather weakens muscle power in cold-blooded animals, and the chameleon's tongue retraction slows sharply in the cold.

**How can it be that:** The chameleon's tongue extension barely slows in the cold?

**Evaluate:** Cold weakens *muscles*. If extension does not depend on muscle power the way retraction does, the cold would not affect it the same way.

**Goal:** Find a difference between how extension and retraction are powered.

### Choice Notes

**(A) No Impact.** Animals being more active in warm weather does not explain why one tongue movement slows and the other does not.

**(B) No Impact.** How far the tongue extends says nothing about how temperature affects it.

**(C) No Impact.** Where the chameleons live does not explain the difference between extension and retraction.

**(D) Correct.** Retraction runs on muscle, which the cold weakens. Extension runs on energy stored in an elastic sheath, so the cold does not slow it much.

**(E) Irrelevant Comparison.** Comparing the chameleon's retraction muscles with other animals' says nothing about why extension holds up in the cold.

## PT159-S1-Q4 | Paradox | E

> A process of extracting caffeine from coffee without seriously compromising the coffee’s taste was introduced a number of years ago. When this decaffeinated coffee first came on the market, however, many coffee drinkers reported that it tasted rather bad.

## PT159-S1-Q9 | Paradox | B

> Market studies confirm that consumers shopping for automobiles primarily seek those that are most reliable. Strangely enough, however, some of the most reliable models do not sell well with those who say they care most about reliability.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to resolve the apparent discrepancy in the information above?
>
> (A) Consumers who are shopping for an automobile claim to care more about reliability than about fuel efficiency.
> (B) Consumers often choose automobile models based on a reputation for reliability, and reputations can be out of date.
> (C) Some owners of reliable automobiles are reluctant to trade them in for newer vehicles and tend to hold on to them for the life of the automobile.
> (D) Some automobile models that have been shown to require a lot of maintenance very rarely break down.

**Given that:** Car shoppers mainly look for the most reliable models.

**How can it be that:** Some of the most reliable models sell poorly to the people who say they care most about reliability?

**Evaluate:** Those shoppers want reliability, but they have to judge it somehow. If they judge it in a way that can be wrong, they may miss the cars that are actually reliable.

**Goal:** Find a reason shoppers who want reliability fail to identify the reliable models.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Deepens Paradox.** If shoppers care more about reliability than fuel efficiency, it is even stranger that they skip the reliable cars.

**(B) Correct.** Shoppers go by a model's reputation, and reputations can be out of date. A model that has become reliable may still carry an old reputation for breaking down, so reliability-minded shoppers pass it by.

**(C) No Impact.** Owners keeping their reliable cars does not explain why new shoppers do not buy those models.

**(D) No Impact.** A car that needs a lot of maintenance is not reliable by the stimulus's definition, so these models are not the ones in question.

## PT159-S3-Q7 | Paradox | B

> Last year a local advertising agency produced many of its television commercials in a studio with improperly installed equipment. As a result, the sound in these commercials was not perfectly synchronized with the picture; this was particularly noticeable when people were shown talking, and it even prompted complaints from some viewers. However, local automobile dealerships that relied on television commercials from this agency tended to have greater sales increases during this period than did local dealerships using television commercials from other agencies instead.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to explain the sales discrepancy described above?
>
> (A) Most viewers turned off the sound on their televisions when local automobile-dealership commercials aired during this period.
> (B) People are more likely to recall a television commercial they have seen if the sound and pictures were not properly synchronized.
> (C) Most of the advertisers that relied on commercials produced at the studio with improperly installed equipment were automobile dealerships.
> (D) Not all of the commercials produced for automobile dealerships at this studio contained video of people talking.
> (E) Most of the local automobile dealerships during this time did not rely heavily on television advertising.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** The agency's commercials had badly synced sound, and some viewers complained.

**How can it be that:** Car dealers using these commercials saw bigger sales increases than dealers using other agencies?

**Evaluate:** The flaw seems like it should hurt. To resolve this, the flaw has to help somehow. Viewers noticed it, so it made the commercials stand out.

**Goal:** Find a way the bad sync made the commercials more effective.

**(C) No Impact.** Who the studio's clients were does not explain why its commercials worked better.

**(D) Too Weak.** If some commercials had no people talking, the flaw mattered less in them. That still does not explain better sales.

**(E) Applies to Both.** Most dealers relying little on TV applies to dealers using either agency. It does not separate the two groups.

## PT159-S3-Q11 | Paradox | E

> The crocodiles of Lake Victoria, in East Africa, sometimes attack humans that venture into their habitat. The number of attacks on people by these crocodiles has increased significantly in the last 20 years. This is despite the fact that the number of crocodiles in the lake has not increased in that time and the crocodiles have no less food available.
>
> Which one of the following, if true, most helps to explain the increase in the incidence of crocodile attacks on humans?
>
> (A) Wildlife authorities on Lake Victoria sometimes destroy crocodiles that they believe to be responsible for attacks on humans.
> (B) About 100 crocodiles inhabit the part of Lake Victoria where most of the attacks on humans have occurred.
> (C) More attacks on humans by crocodiles occur in Lake Victoria than in any other part of Africa.
> (D) Adult crocodiles in Lake Victoria are generally unafraid of humans or their boats.
> (E) People now fish in more parts of Lake Victoria than they did in previous decades.

### Argument Breakdown

**Given that:** In the last 20 years, the number of crocodiles in the lake has not risen, and they have no less food.

**How can it be that:** Crocodile attacks on humans have risen significantly?

**Evaluate:** The crocodiles have not changed, so look at the humans. If people are going into crocodile territory more often, there would be more chances for an attack.

**Goal:** Find a change in how much people come into contact with the crocodiles.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Deepens Paradox.** Killing crocodiles that attack should reduce attacks, not increase them.

**(B) No Impact.** A count of crocodiles in one area does not show any change over 20 years.

**(C) Irrelevant Comparison.** Comparing Lake Victoria with the rest of Africa does not explain the increase over time.

**(D) No Impact.** Crocodiles being unafraid of humans is not shown to be new, so it does not explain why attacks went up.

**(E) Correct.** People now fish in more parts of the lake, so they meet crocodiles more often. More contact means more attacks, with the same number of crocodiles.
