## PT146-S3-Q3 | Most Strongly Supported | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** Natural superstar salespeople are rare. Many salespeople can perform like superstars if they have a good manager.

**Evaluate:** The blank follows "Therefore, companies should". Superstars are hard to find, but good managers can produce superstar results. So the effort belongs in finding good managers.

**Goal:** Companies should focus on finding good managers rather than natural superstars.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported.** The argument is about managers, not training versus evaluation.

**(B) Correct.** Superstars are rare and good managers make ordinary salespeople perform like them. So looking for good managers pays off more.

**(C) Unsupported.** How many salespeople a manager handles never comes up.

**(D) Unsupported.** Nothing says superstar salespeople make good managers.

**(E) Unsupported.** Rewards aren't discussed. The argument is about managers.

## PT117-S4-Q23 | Most Strongly Supported | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** People should be free, within broad limits, to spend their money. Candidates who vastly outspend rivals have an unfair advantage. Governments must give every voice an equal chance to be heard. Governments should not subsidize expensive campaigns for each candidate.

**Evaluate:** The answer has to fit every constraint. It must curb outspending but leave people broadly free to spend, and it can't use subsidies. A cap on each candidate's spending does all three.

**Goal:** Limit how much any candidate can spend.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Opposite.** Letting only well-funded candidates run makes the unfair advantage worse.

**(B) Correct.** A cap stops anyone from vastly outspending rivals, leaves room to spend within it, and needs no subsidy.

**(C) Contradicts.** The scientist says governments shouldn't subsidize campaigns.

**(D) Too Strong.** Banning wealthy people from spending their own money goes beyond "broad limits" on freedom.

**(E) Opposite.** This is the most popular trap. Letting each candidate match any rival's spending caps nothing. Candidates who can afford to outspend still win the advantage.

No one should hold a position involving duties they shouldn't perform. A cabinet undersecretary is expected to serve as secretary when the secretary is unavailable.

## PT118-S3-Q15 | Flaw | A

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Doctors should never prescribe sedatives for insomnia. Insomniacs need psychotherapy to relieve their stress, not drugs.

**Evidence:** Most cases of insomnia that psychologists treat are caused by stress.

**Evaluate:** This is an EXCEPT question. The argument has many holes: "most" isn't all, psychologists' patients may not be typical, therapy may not work for everyone, and sedatives might help too. Four choices name a real flaw. The answer describes something the argument never assumes.

**Goal:** Find the choice that isn't a flaw in this reasoning.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** The argument assumes stress causes insomnia, not that insomnia makes people worse at coping with stress. This reverses the direction, so it isn't something the argument presumes.

**(B) Flaw.** "Most" leaves cases not caused by stress. Sedatives might be the only treatment for those, yet the conclusion says "never".

**(C) Flaw.** The argument assumes psychotherapy works. For some people it may not.

**(D) Flaw.** Sedatives could help people manage stress, which would serve the same goal.

**(E) Flaw.** This is the most popular trap. Psychologists' patients may not be typical of insomniacs, so the sample may be unrepresentative. It's a real flaw.

## PT144-S2-Q1 | Flaw | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Industrial by-products have probably entered the swamp's ecosystem.

**Evidence:** Some industrial by-products raise hormone activity in reptiles. In reptiles, these abnormalities occur only with elevated hormone activity. Alligators with the abnormalities were found in the swamp.

**Evaluate:** The abnormalities prove elevated hormones, since they occur only with them. But the argument then jumps from elevated hormones to by-products. By-products are one cause of elevated hormones. Nothing says they're the only cause.

**Goal:** The argument ignores other causes of elevated hormone activity.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Irrelevant.** The premise says these abnormalities occur only with elevated hormones. No other explanation is needed.

**(B) Correct.** Something else could have raised the hormones, and then by-products wouldn't be needed.

**(C) Irrelevant.** By-products in the alligators' food would still be by-products in the ecosystem. That supports the conclusion.

**(D) Irrelevant.** Other reptiles don't matter. The alligators are enough to show elevated hormones.

**(E) Irrelevant.** The conclusion is about this swamp, not alligators in general.

## PT144-S2-Q4 | Flaw | C

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** The critics are wrong to doubt the study's results.

**Evidence:** The study's author is well regarded and has done excellent work for years.

**Evaluate:** The critics doubt the study because its sample was tiny, and the biologist admits they're right about that. The defense is about the author's reputation. A good researcher can still run a study too small to support its conclusion.

**Goal:** The argument defends the study by praising its author.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Target.** That generalization is the study's conclusion. The biologist's own reasoning is about whether the critics should doubt it.

**(B) Irrelevant.** The argument doesn't need to explain the mechanism.

**(C) Correct.** The author's reputation doesn't answer the sample-size problem. It sidesteps it.

**(D) Irrelevant.** The biologist admits the critics are right about the sample size, so their expertise isn't the issue.

**(E) Irrelevant.** The argument applies no standard to the critics at all.

**Conclusion:** Any modernization of religious texts and rituals will increase attendance.

**Evidence:** Some religions modernized their texts and rituals, and attendance at their places of worship then rose.

**Evaluate:** The attendance increase came after the changes. That doesn't show the changes caused it. Something else could explain the rise. The conclusion also leaps from "some religions" to "any such modernization".

## PT144-S4-Q6 | Parallel Flaw | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Lily doesn't practice hard.

**Evidence:** To be in the regional band, one must practice very hard or be very talented. Lily is in the band and is very talented.

**Evaluate:** "Or" means at least one, not only one. Lily's talent satisfies the requirement, but that doesn't rule out practicing hard too. The flaw: treating "or" as "one but not both".

**Goal:** Find an argument that sees one half of an "or" condition met and concludes the other half isn't.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Different Flaw.** This treats a requirement (good weather) as a guarantee of success. There's no "or".

**(B) Different Flaw.** This assumes that because Lois isn't on vacation, the outcome tied to vacation can't happen. It's a different error.

**(C) Different Flaw.** This treats a requirement for winning as enough to win.

**(D) Correct.** Julie meets one half of the "or" by reading a newspaper, and the argument concludes Julie doesn't watch TV news. Same error.

**(E) Different Flaw.** This assumes Wayne gets a ride, which nothing says. Its "or" reasoning is valid: if one option is gone and at least one is needed, the other must hold.

## PT144-S4-Q2 | Principle | B

**Wanda:** Visual art needs visual stimuli, just as writers need written stimuli. That's why Wanda's studio is full of things.

**Vernon:** Writers need good writing, not tabloids. Are piles of laundry and empty soda bottles inspiring?

**Evaluate:** Vernon doesn't deny that artists need stimuli. Vernon questions the kind. Tabloids and laundry are stimuli, but poor ones. The principle is that quality matters, not just having something around.

**Goal:** The quality of an artist's stimuli matters.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Unsupported.** Vernon says nothing about health.

**(B) Correct.** Good writing versus tabloids and art versus laundry both make the same point: what you surround yourself with matters.

**(C) Too Narrow.** Tabloids are one example. The principle covers all stimuli.

**(D) Too Strong.** Vernon doesn't say mess hurts creativity, only that junk may not inspire.

**(E) Opposite.** Vernon never defends an empty work area.

## PT144-S4-Q3 | Paradox | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Fact:** Listing a species as endangered brings stronger protections, such as trade limits and antipoaching laws.

**Fact:** Many species declined faster after being listed than before.

**Evaluate:** Protections should slow the decline. Something about being listed must make things worse, enough to outweigh the new protections.

**Goal:** Find a way that being listed increases pressure on the species.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Irrelevant.** A slow listing process doesn't explain a faster decline after listing.

**(B) Irrelevant.** Which species campaigns favor doesn't explain why decline speeds up after listing.

**(C) Irrelevant.** More species being listed doesn't explain why any one species declines faster.

**(D) Correct.** Listing tells collectors the animals are rare, which makes them more desirable. More demand drives more poaching.

**(E) Opposite.** If poachers find rare animals harder to locate, decline should slow.

## PT113-S3-Q8 | Must be False | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Statements:** In every species of higher animal, reproduction requires producing eggs, though not necessarily sperm. Some species are all female. One rare all-female salamander species reproduces without fertilization, so its offspring are near copies of the parent, which makes the species less adaptive than species with both sexes.

**Evaluate:** This is a "could be true EXCEPT" question. Four answers are possible; one contradicts the passage. The firmest rule is the first: every higher-animal species produces eggs.

**Goal:** Find the claim that conflicts with the passage. Look for a higher-animal species without eggs.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Could Be True.** One all-female salamander species is rare. Other salamander species can have males and females.

**(B) Correct.** Every species of higher animal requires egg production to reproduce, so some members must produce eggs. A species with no egg producers contradicts that.

**(C) Could Be True.** "Rare" describes the salamander species, not all-female species generally. There could be many.

**(D) Could Be True.** Two-sex species are more adaptive than the all-female salamander. That doesn't make every one of them very adaptive.

**(E) Could Be True.** The passage says nothing about how offspring in two-sex species resemble each parent.

## PT145-S4-Q3 | Paradox | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Fact:** The share of people starting businesses is higher in high-income countries than in middle-income ones, because high-income countries offer more business opportunities.

**Fact:** The share is even higher in low-income countries.

**Evaluate:** Low-income countries should have the fewest opportunities, so the opportunity explanation can't cover them. People there must be starting businesses for a different reason.

**Goal:** Give low-income entrepreneurs a different reason to start businesses.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Irrelevant.** Business size is the same in both and doesn't explain the start rates.

**(B) Opposite.** Government help in high-income countries would raise their rate, which deepens the puzzle.

**(C) Irrelevant.** Failure rates don't explain why more people start businesses.

**(D) Irrelevant.** Illusory opportunities in high-income countries don't explain the low-income rate.

**(E) Correct.** In low-income countries people start businesses out of necessity, because other work is missing or poor. That reason doesn't depend on opportunities.
