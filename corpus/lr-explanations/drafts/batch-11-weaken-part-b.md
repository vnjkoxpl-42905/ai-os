# Batch 11, part B: Weaken

Every gap with the Weaken label. Seven are in the database with no explanation, three already have some rows and need the rest, and six are not in the database.

PT119-S3-Q5 is in the database as Weaken but asks for the main conclusion. It is explained as Main Conclusion; its database type label is a separate fix, filed in `fixes/index.md`.

Modelled on `models/Weaken-Evaluate.md`: find the gap between evidence and conclusion, then pick the fact that widens it. Causal arguments are weakened by another cause, a reversed cause, or a problem with the data.

## PT105-S1-Q4 | Weaken | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Low activity in the premotor cortex is one cause of hyperactivity.

**Evidence:** Hyperactive adults had much less premotor activity than adults who weren't hyperactive.

**Evaluate:** This is a correlation turned into a cause. Something else could explain the low brain activity. If the hyperactive group differs in some other way that lowers brain activity, the hyperactivity may not come from the brain difference at all.

**Goal:** Find another explanation for the hyperactive group's lower brain activity.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Irrelevant.** The study measured the adults themselves, not their children.

**(B) Irrelevant.** Varied severity doesn't explain why the whole group had lower activity.

**(C) Irrelevant.** The designers being absent doesn't show the tests were done wrong.

**(D) Correct.** Every hyperactive adult took a drug that depresses brain activity, and no one in the other group did. The drug, not hyperactivity's cause, could explain the low activity.

**(E) Irrelevant.** Why the test excluded children doesn't affect the result for adults.

## PT105-S4-Q9 | Weaken | C

**Conclusion:** The representative implies that the public need not be concerned about the chemicals leaking from the dump.

**Evidence:** Federal law requires every new chemical to be tested for safety before it goes to market, just as pharmaceuticals are tested.

**Evaluate:** The law covers new chemicals. The dump is long established, so its chemicals may be older than the law. If dangerous chemicals were in use before testing began, the law does nothing to show the leak is safe.

**(A) Irrelevant.** A drawback of drug testing doesn't touch the safety of the leaking chemicals.

**(B) Irrelevant.** When the leak became noticeable doesn't say whether the chemicals are safe.

**(D) Strengthener.** Dilution makes the leak less worrying, if anything.

**(E) Irrelevant.** Silt making the river murky has nothing to do with chemical safety.

**Conclusion:** The question asks us to show that current enforcement might fail to control overall pollution.

**Evidence:** Cars are tested only while idling, by measuring exhaust at the tailpipe.

**Evaluate:** The breadcrumb is "while idling." Cars pollute mostly while driving. If passing an idle test says little about pollution on the road, or even makes it worse, enforcement could miss what matters.

## PT107-S4-Q24 | Weaken | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Adequate prenatal care significantly lowers the risk of low birth weight.

**Evidence:** Premature babies were more likely to have low birth weight. Mothers with adequate prenatal care were less likely to have low-birth-weight babies.

**Evaluate:** This is a correlation between care records and birth weight. If the records are skewed, the correlation could be built in. Premature babies tend to have low birth weight. If mothers of premature babies get labeled as having inadequate care by default, the link would appear even if care made no difference.

**Goal:** Find a flaw in how the records link care to birth weight.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Irrelevant.** Many babies of inadequate-care mothers having normal weight is consistent with care lowering the risk.

**(B) Correct.** Mothers of premature babies get marked as inadequate care whenever their records are missing. Since premature babies tend to be underweight, the records tie inadequate care to low weight automatically.

**(C) Irrelevant.** How low-weight babies are classified as premature doesn't affect the link between care and weight.

**(D) Irrelevant.** Some exceptions don't undercut a claim about lowered risk.

**(E) Strengthener.** If care reduces premature births, and premature babies tend to be underweight, care plausibly does lower the risk.

## PT119-S3-Q5 | Main Conclusion | C

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** Because of stigma and age discrimination, some elderly people feel discouraged about trying psychotherapy.

**Conclusion:** They should not be discouraged.

**Evidence:** Many younger people have benefited greatly, and older people have advantages that make a good outcome more likely.

**Evaluate:** "They should not be, however" is the pivot, and "for" introduces the support. Swap "they" for the elderly and "be" for discouraged: elderly people should not be discouraged from trying psychotherapy.

**Goal:** Elderly people should not be reluctant to try psychotherapy.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Premise.** Age discrimination by some professionals is background for why the elderly feel discouraged.

**(B) Too Strong.** The elderly have advantages that make success likely. Nothing says they benefit more than the young.

**(C) Correct.** This is the pivot sentence with "they" and "be" filled in.

**(D) Premise.** Maturity helping is support for the conclusion, not the conclusion.

**(E) Unsupported.** The passage never compares how willing the elderly and the young are to try it.
