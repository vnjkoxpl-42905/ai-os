# Batch 13, part A: Role and Main Conclusion

Every gap labelled Role, Role in Argument, Role of Statement or Argument part, plus PT145-S4-Q8 (filed as Miscellaneous; the stem asks for the main conclusion). Eight are in the database with no explanation and nine are not in the database.

Modelled on `models/MC-Role-Agree.md`: find the conclusion first, then place the named claim in the chain with a support test ("why believe X? because Y"). Name the role each wrong answer gives the claim.

Labels: Conclusion / Evidence / Evaluate / Goal, plus Opposing Point, Intermediate Conclusion or Concession where the argument has one.

## PT145-S4-Q7 | Role | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** It is widely believed that the most environmentally sensible thing is to buy food from local farmers whenever it is available.

**Conclusion:** Sometimes it is environmentally better to buy food that is not produced locally.

**Evidence:** The distance food travels is only a small part of its environmental impact. Some foods can be produced with far less impact in some places than in others.

**Evaluate:** "It is widely believed" sets up a view, and "But" turns against it. The conclusion says non-local food is sometimes better, which contradicts "whenever it is available". So the claim in the stem is the view the argument rejects.

**Goal:** Find the answer that calls the claim a view the argument rejects.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The reasoning isn't based on this principle. It argues against it.

**(B) Wrong Role.** The claim isn't used to support anything. The argument rejects it.

**(C) Wrong Role.** The claim isn't used to reject an activity. The argument rejects the claim itself.

**(D) Correct.** "Widely believed" introduces it and "But" attacks it. The conclusion says buying non-local food is sometimes the better choice, which is the opposite.

**(E) Wrong Role.** The conclusion is the last sentence, and it contradicts this claim.

## PT145-S4-Q8 | Main Conclusion | A

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Technology is radically improving life in some communities, and not only through the direct application of innovations.

**Intermediate Conclusion:** Designing, producing, testing and marketing new technology has become a growing industry that is turning around once-ailing communities.

**Evidence:** The companies involved create jobs, add to the tax base and lift the spirit of renewal.

**Evaluate:** "After all" introduces support, so the claim before it is the conclusion. Run the chain. Why believe technology helps in more than one way? Because the tech industry itself is reviving communities. Why believe that? Because those companies create jobs, add taxes and lift spirits. The first sentence sits at the top.

**Goal:** Match the first sentence: direct application is not the only way technology is improving life in some communities.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Correct.** "Not only by direct application" means direct application is not the only way. Same claim, same scope ("some communities").

**(B) Intermediate Conclusion.** This is the most tempting answer. It comes right after "After all", which marks it as a reason for the first sentence. It names the second way technology helps; the conclusion is that there is a second way.

**(C) Premise.** Jobs, taxes and renewal explain why the industry is reviving communities. This is the bottom of the chain.

**(D) Too Strong.** The argument says "some communities", not "most".

**(E) Too Strong.** "The only ways" overreaches. The argument never limits the benefits to these three, and it says direct application helps too.

## PT111-S1-Q9 | Role | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** Children's psychological problems are caused by the difficulty they have adjusting to divorce.

**Conclusion:** That inference is a mistake. It is just as reasonable to think the children learned hostility, distrust and lack of empathy from their parents, and that these learned behaviors cause the problems.

**Evaluate:** The first sentence is a fact both sides accept: children of divorced parents have more psychological problems. The dispute is about why. One explanation blames adjusting to divorce. The researcher offers another that is just as reasonable. So the first sentence is the finding both explanations try to account for.

**Goal:** Find the answer that treats the higher rate as an accepted finding the argument explains.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The conclusion is that blaming the divorce adjustment is a mistake. The rate is only the starting fact.

**(B) Wrong Role.** This is the most popular trap. The argument does refute something, but it's the causal claim about adjusting to divorce. The researcher never doubts the rate itself.

**(C) Wrong Role.** The argument never says divorce harms children. It suggests the learned behaviors do.

**(D) Wrong Role.** The stimulus says these behaviors increase the likelihood of divorce, but the higher rate of problems isn't offered as evidence for that.

**(E) Correct.** The higher rate is treated as true, and the argument proposes the learned-behavior explanation for it.

## PT112-S3-Q6 | Role | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** There is no easy way to end the country's chronic food shortages.

**Evidence:** Direct food aid would undermine long-term self-sufficiency, because effective food deliveries force local producers and suppliers out of business. Foreign capital for long-term development would cause inflation that prices food out of reach for most citizens.

**Evaluate:** The official walks through two options and shows a problem with each. Food aid hurts local farming. Foreign capital causes inflation. If both options backfire, no solution is easy. So the inflation claim is one of the two reasons for the first sentence.

**Goal:** The inflation claim supports the conclusion that there's no easy solution.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The official never argues the country must someday be self-sufficient, and the inflation claim is about foreign capital, not self-sufficiency.

**(B) Correct.** The inflation problem knocks out the second option, which supports the claim that no easy solution exists.

**(C) Wrong Role.** Nothing supports the inflation claim, and the argument never makes the self-sufficiency claim.

**(D) Wrong Role.** This is the most tempting wrong answer. The inflation claim is about foreign capital. The problem with food donations is a separate premise.

**(E) Wrong Role.** The two problems sit side by side, one for each option. The claim about producers going out of business doesn't support the inflation claim.

## PT112-S4-Q13 | Role | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Fermat was likely lying or mistaken in claiming to have proved the theorem.

**Intermediate Conclusion:** The alleged theorem probably cannot be proved.

**Evidence:** No one else has been able to prove it.

**Evaluate:** Joseph's claim that the theorem "cannot be proved" is supported ("since no one else has been able to prove it"), and it supports the final claim ("Therefore ... lying or else mistaken"). A claim that is both supported and supporting is a subsidiary conclusion. Laura's reply attacks it, but the question asks about its role in Joseph's argument.

**Goal:** A subsidiary conclusion that supports the main conclusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** It does get support: no one else has proved it.

**(B) Correct.** Supported by the lack of any other proof, and used to conclude that Fermat was lying or mistaken.

**(C) Wrong Role.** Joseph doesn't anticipate an objection. Laura raises one afterward.

**(D) Wrong Role.** Joseph relies on this claim. Laura is the one who refutes it.

**(E) Wrong Role.** It directly supports Joseph's conclusion.

## PT114-S1-Q18 | Role | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Background:** Insurers set premiums from risk factors, and premiums rise with risk. Age and accident history matter.

**Conclusion:** Premiums should also rise with how often a person drives.

**Evidence:** A person's chance of a mishap rises in proportion to how often that person drives.

**Evaluate:** "Yet" turns from background to the author's own claim. "After all" then gives the reason for it. Why should premiums rise with driving frequency? Because risk rises with frequency, and premiums track risk. Nothing is built on the frequency claim, so it's the conclusion.

**Goal:** The claim is the conclusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** This is the most popular trap. A premise supports something else. This claim is what the "After all" sentence supports.

**(B) Correct.** It's the author's claim, and "After all" introduces the reason for it.

**(C) Wrong Role.** It isn't evidence for anything. It's what the evidence supports.

**(D) Wrong Role.** No objection is anticipated or blocked.

**(E) Wrong Role.** It doesn't clarify any term. It makes a new claim about premiums.

## PT115-S2-Q16 | Role | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Shared Ground:** Both sides agree humans evolved from a fish and that frogs are related to that fish.

**Stevens-Hoyt:** The ancestor was the lungfish, because lungfish and frog mitochondrial DNA match closely.

**Grover:** The ancestor was the coelacanth, because coelacanth and tadpole hemoglobin match closely. Mitochondrial DNA changes too fast to be reliable over long periods.

**Evaluate:** Each scientist matches a fish to frogs. Lungfish to frog DNA; coelacanth to tadpole hemoglobin. Those matches only point to the human ancestor if frogs are related to that ancestor. So the frog claim is a starting point both sides share.

**Goal:** An assumption both sides use as a starting point.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The frog claim doesn't rule out lungfish. Stevens-Hoyt uses it to argue for lungfish.

**(B) Wrong Role.** It doesn't rule out coelacanths either. Grover's tadpole match depends on it.

**(C) Wrong Role.** This is the most popular trap. Neither scientist argues the answer must be one of the two fish. Each argues for one, and the frog claim alone doesn't narrow the field to these two.

**(D) Correct.** Both arguments match a fish to frogs, which matters only if frogs are related to the ancestor. Both start there.

**(E) Wrong Role.** Nobody compares lungfish to coelacanths. Each fish is compared to frogs.

## PT118-S4-Q23 | Role | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** A severe worldwide grain shortage is likely.

**Intermediate Conclusion:** Further increases in worldwide grain production will be extremely difficult.

**Evidence:** Most usable farmland is already farmed at near-maximal efficiency. Production increases have virtually stopped. Demand keeps rising, largely because of population growth.

**Evaluate:** The semicolon links the named claim to its reason: the farmland is already near its limit. Then "Hence" draws the shortage conclusion. Supply can't grow and demand keeps growing. The claim is supported and supports the conclusion, so it's an intermediate conclusion.

**Goal:** An intermediate conclusion offered as support for the main conclusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** It isn't a separate conclusion. It supports the shortage prediction.

**(B) Wrong Role.** The conclusion predicts a shortage. It doesn't explain what causes this claim.

**(C) Wrong Role.** It isn't the only premise. Rising demand is just as necessary for a shortage.

**(D) Wrong Role.** This is the most popular trap. The claim is a prediction, but its support is the farmland point after the semicolon, not the first claim about production stalling.

**(E) Correct.** The farmland point supports it, and it supports the shortage conclusion.

## PT120-S4-Q6 | Role | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Pain perception depends only partly on physiology.

**Intermediate Conclusion:** The meaning a person attaches to a wound can affect how much pain that person perceives.

**Evidence:** In World War II, a much lower share of injured soldiers asked for morphine than civilians recovering from surgery. The soldiers felt relief and joy at being alive; the civilians found surgery depressing.

**Evaluate:** The last sentence starts with "So", which makes it look like the conclusion. Run the support test. Why believe pain depends only partly on physiology? Because the meaning of a wound affects pain, and meaning is not physiology. Why believe meaning affects pain? Because of the morphine data. The "So" sentence supports the first sentence, which sits at the top.

**Goal:** The claim is the main conclusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** This is the most popular trap. An assumption is unstated. This claim is stated, and the rest of the passage supports it.

**(B) Wrong Role.** It doesn't undermine the conclusion. It is the conclusion.

**(C) Wrong Role.** The argument defends this position. It doesn't try to discredit it.

**(D) Wrong Role.** The argument gives evidence for it, so it isn't taken for granted.

**(E) Correct.** The morphine data support the meaning claim, which supports this one. Nothing sits above it.

## PT126-S4-Q2 | Role | D

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** People's responses to words depend not only on what words mean but also on how they sound.

**Evidence:** People respond positively or negatively to some meaningful words. Experiments show people also respond positively or negatively to many nonsense words.

**Evaluate:** "This shows" points back to the evidence and introduces the conclusion. Nonsense words have no meaning, so a response to them must come from something else, like sound. The nonsense-word finding is the key premise.

**Goal:** A premise supporting the conclusion about meaning and sound.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The conclusion isn't that people respond to any word. It's that responses depend on sound as well as meaning.

**(B) Wrong Role.** The nonsense-word claim is a premise. Nothing supports it but the experiments.

**(C) Wrong Role.** The meaningful-word claim doesn't support it. The two findings sit side by side as evidence.

**(D) Correct.** It's a premise, and the conclusion matches: meaning and sound both shape responses.

**(E) Wrong Role.** It isn't a conclusion, and nothing contrasts experimental and ordinary situations.

## PT128-S2-Q8 | Role | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** The opponent says the outdated school system is the major obstacle to better education.

**Concession:** The school system does need reform.

**Conclusion:** The opponent's position puts far too much blame on the schools.

**Evidence:** The position equates education with schooling, yet other parts of society are at least as responsible for educating young people.

**Evaluate:** "In fact our school system does need reform" agrees with part of the opponent's view. "Nonetheless" then turns to the candidate's real challenge. So the claim grants the opponent a point before attacking the larger position.

**Goal:** A limited concession within a broader challenge.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The main conclusion is that the opponent puts too much blame on schools, not that schools need reform.

**(B) Wrong Role.** The candidate proposes no solution.

**(C) Wrong Role.** Schools needing reform doesn't support "too much blame". It cuts the other way, which is why "Nonetheless" follows it.

**(D) Wrong Role.** The candidate never says school failings cause society's problems.

**(E) Correct.** The candidate grants that schools need reform, then argues the opponent overstates their role.

## PT128-S3-Q10 | Role | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** Biologists' announcements that an animal can use tools are completely unsurprising.

**Intermediate Conclusion:** All animals use tools.

**Evidence:** Birds build nests, fish hide in mud and squirrels use buildings as shortcuts. If an animal achieves its purpose with an external physical object, that object counts as a tool.

**Evaluate:** "Since" marks "all animals use tools" as the reason the announcements are unsurprising. The examples and the definition of a tool support that reason. Why believe the announcements are unsurprising? Because all animals use tools. Nothing is built on the "unsurprising" claim.

**Goal:** The claim is the conclusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The "unsurprising" claim says nothing about whether the animals' activities are purposeful.

**(B) Correct.** The rest of the passage supports it, and it supports nothing else.

**(C) Wrong Role.** It isn't assumed; it's argued for. The broader view of tools is the support, not the thing supported.

**(D) Wrong Role.** The definition in the last sentence is what challenges the biologists' idea of a tool. The "unsurprising" claim is what that challenge supports.

**(E) Wrong Role.** The biologists never use "external". The argument uses it in its own definition.

## PT133-S3-Q1 | Role | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Opposing Point:** Acme says it isn't liable because it never realized the knee-level switches were a hazard. It says it moved them in newer locomotives because engineers found them inconvenient.

**Conclusion:** Acme should be held liable for the wreck.

**Evidence:** Acme spent $500,000 relocating the switches. It's unlikely a company would spend that much only to fix an inconvenience.

**Evaluate:** The money is used to doubt Acme's story. If Acme wouldn't spend $500,000 for convenience, it probably moved the switches because it knew they were dangerous. That knowledge defeats Acme's defense, so Acme is liable.

**Goal:** The $500,000 is a sign that Acme knew about the hazard before the wreck.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The argument never discusses the engineer's responsibility.

**(B) Wrong Role.** Nothing suggests the wreck would have happened anyway.

**(C) Wrong Role.** The wreck is explained by the knee hitting the switch. The money doesn't explain it.

**(D) Wrong Role.** It's the reverse. The money suggests the switches were hazardous and that Acme knew it.

**(E) Correct.** A company doesn't spend $500,000 on an inconvenience, so Acme likely knew the switches were dangerous.

## PT159-S3-Q8 | Role | E

**Opposing Point:** Proponents say sinking old equipment provides needed habitat for marine life, so the practice is ecologically sound.

**Conclusion:** The practice may be ecologically unsound even if it does provide needed habitat.

**Evidence:** The practice may upset an area's ecological balance in unknown ways.

**Evaluate:** "However" turns against the proponents. The conclusion is the claim right after it. "For" then introduces the reason: the practice may upset the ecological balance. So the named claim is the premise.

**(A) Wrong Role.** It doesn't summarize anything. It's one reason.

**(B) Wrong Role.** The argument uses this claim. It refutes the proponents' inference.

**(C) Wrong Role.** This describes the habitat claim: the argument says habitat isn't enough to show the practice is sound.

## PT146-S3-Q19 | Role | B

### Argument Breakdown

**Evidence:** To keep a legal system just, it is important to guarantee that lawbreaking gives lawbreakers no unfair advantage over law abiders.

**Conclusion:** Whatever other goals criminal punishment serves, it should try to keep criminal wrongdoing profitless.

**Evaluate:** "Thus" marks the second sentence as the conclusion, so the first sentence is support. It's a general principle about justice. The conclusion applies it to punishment: if crime pays, lawbreakers gain an unfair advantage.

**Goal:** A principle offered in support of the conclusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The principle says the guarantee is important to justice, not that meeting it ensures justice.

**(B) Correct.** A general principle, followed by "Thus" and the conclusion it supports.

**(C) Wrong Role.** It's a premise. Nothing in the passage supports it.

**(D) Wrong Role.** The conclusion doesn't call profitlessness the most important goal. "Notwithstanding any other goals" leaves the ranking open.

**(E) Wrong Role.** The jurist grants that punishment may have other goals. Nothing is refuted.

## PT156-S2-Q4 | Role | E

### Argument Breakdown

**Conclusion:** It would be a mistake for the manufacturer to increase supply to meet demand.

**Evidence:** Demand comes from the perception that the watches are scarce, and they're scarce only because few are made. Excess demand makes the watches seem desirable, which fuels the desire for them. If supply met demand, the excess would vanish and the watches would no longer be desired.

**Evaluate:** "Although demand far outstrips supply" is a concession. The author's claim follows it. Every later sentence explains why raising supply would backfire, so they all support the first claim.

**Goal:** The claim is the conclusion.

### Choice Notes

**(A) Wrong Role.** The argument defends this claim. The "although" clause is the point it pushes against.

**(B) Wrong Role.** It doesn't justify the relevance of any evidence. It's what the evidence supports.

**(C) Wrong Role.** It's supported by every later sentence, so it isn't a premise.

**(D) Wrong Role.** Nothing else rests on it. It's the top of the argument.

**(E) Correct.** The rest of the passage explains why increasing supply would be a mistake.
