## What the best Strengthen explanations do

- **Build the objection first, then flip it.** "Given that ... how could we still argue ..." produces the answer before the choices (PT139-S1-Q13 coins slipping down; PT113-S2-Q6 who wrote the certificate).
- **Predict the plausibility booster by name.** No Cause, No Effect / control group, with a concrete example sentence (PT128-S3-Q1 "once people leave Dept F"; PT122-S1-Q12 inmates not on the diet).
- **Name the alternative the key rules out, in the stimulus's own terms** (PT147-S4-Q22 separate insertions; PT132-S4-Q3 music training).
- **Treat plans and recommendations as world vs world.** List feasibility, incentives and backfire, then predict the answer that removes one (PT109-S4-Q8; PT148-S3-Q25).
- **Break close ties on stated criteria**, not "B is better" (PT140-S1-Q12 B vs C; PT143-S3-Q21 absolute vs relative).
- **Rewrite a wrong answer into the version that would work.** This shows exactly where it falls short (PT142-S4-Q26 blue jays; PT122-S1-Q12 A).
- **State rules that transfer:** "If [conclusion]" is wrong on Strengthen (PT140-S3-Q4); "already true cannot explain a future change" (PT150-S2-Q7); necessary-assumption keys feel weak, so negate them (PT112-S3-Q26).
- **On EXCEPT, label the four working moves** so the odd one out is visible (PT117-S4-Q17; PT101-S2-Q4).
- **Do the arithmetic when numbers appear** (PT113-S3-Q5 12% vs 10-15%; PT122-S1-Q12 20 of 200).

## What the best Sufficient Assumption explanations do

- **Ask two questions of the conclusion first:** is there a new term, and is there a rule that outputs the conclusion's wording? (PT153-S3-Q3; PT113-S4-Q20)
- **Diagram the conclusion as a path, then subtract.** Show what the premises supply and predict the missing link in both plain and contrapositive form (PT111-S4-Q14; PT156-S4-Q13).
- **Run the trigger test on every rule-shaped wrong answer.** Does the evidence establish the left side? PT113-S4-Q20 "solely vs primarily"; PT154-S4-Q20 "cares about" vs "exhibits"; PT128-S2-Q20 "never" vs "not always."
- **Reduce reversal and negation traps to letters.** Given A and B → C, the conclusion ~C → ~A and the trap ~A → ~C (PT140-S3-Q11; PT118-S4-Q22).
- **State translation rules as rules:** unless / until = if not; "the only" = left; "requires" = arrow; "It is A to be B" = B → A; "even if" adds nothing (PT128-S2-Q20; PT122-S2-Q7; PT118-S1-Q23).
- **Prove quantifier inferences with small numbers** (PT115-S2-Q21 9 friends; PT118-S4-Q22 braiding percentages; PT118-S1-Q21 "not many" = almost none).
- **Keep the SA vs Strengthen line explicit.** Answers that make the conclusion likely are still wrong (PT112-S1-Q26 B and D; PT109-S3-Q23 A).
- **Teach flexibility where it matters:** the key may carry a smaller trigger or only imply the idea (PT139-S4-Q21 karaoke/Jenga; PT153-S3-Q3 D).
- **Collapse a conditional conclusion before solving it** (PT118-S1-Q23 Tommy; PT129-S2-Q7 play along with the "if").
