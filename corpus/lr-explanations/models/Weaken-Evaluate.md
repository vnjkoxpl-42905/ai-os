### What the best ones do (Weaken)
- **Build the objection before the choices, in content words.** PT111-S1-Q13 (the Mars example) and PT128-S2-Q17 (nomads carrying teeth) nearly write the correct answer in the Evaluate step. PT152-S4-Q22 does it with prices.
- **Name the two pressure points, then say which is more likely.** The curious-fact frame (alternate explanation first, plausibility second), plus the reflex list "reverse cause / third factor" and a judgment about which one fits this stimulus (PT141-S2-Q9, PT155-S4-Q8 not picked but same move, PT112-S4-Q17).
- **Spend the most words on the popular trap and say why it tempts.** PT141-S2-Q9 A (19%, relative vs absolute), PT152-S4-Q22 C (34%), PT139-S4-Q19 D (38%), PT142-S4-Q24 C (31%).
- **Rewrite a wrong answer into the version that would work.** PT152-S4-Q22 C, PT139-S4-Q19 D, PT101-S3-Q9 A and B. This shows the student the exact missing ingredient.
- **Work numbers when a quantity is in play.** $3 cost vs $9.99 handling (PT142-S4-Q24), $200 vs $800 tickets (PT135-S4-Q3), the price paid to buy vs to sell (PT152-S4-Q22).
- **State a rule that transfers.** Conditional arguments are weakened by making the trigger fail (PT109-S4-Q19). A "best method" claim invites false positives and false negatives (PT105-S4-Q5). A symmetric result means "make an excuse" (PT126-S4-Q25). A base rate explains a majority without causation (PT139-S1-Q22).
- **For analogies, use a sentence the answer must complete.** "Even though X, Y shouldn't follow, because..." (PT122-S2-Q8, and golden PT153-S2-Q13).
- **For EXCEPT or chains, map each working answer to a named gap** so the odd one out is visible (PT111-S4-Q17).
- **Say when attacking a premise is fair**, and why it usually isn't (PT142-S4-Q24 opinion premise; contrast PT106-S2-Q25 C).

### What the best ones do (Evaluate)
- **Say the method in one line, then use it on every choice.** Evaluate means Weaken dressed as a question: find the question with a weakening answer (PT147-S1-Q17, PT146-S3-Q8).
- **Answer each question both ways, at the extremes.** "990 chose other books" (PT112-S1-Q24). A geriatric dinosaur vs a dino-toddler (PT148-S4-Q22). 90% vs 5% of monarchs fall (PT104-S4-Q6). Zero efficacy (golden PT141-S4-Q6).
- **Name the rule: one answer strengthens and the opposite answer weakens.** PT104-S4-Q6 and PT138-S2-Q2 state it outright. PT142-S1-Q21 adds that the NO answer is a "no cause, no effect" strengthener.
- **Turn each wrong question into a Weaken answer and let the author reply.** "Experts were consulted" gets "then you wasted money on consultants too" (PT147-S1-Q17). Yang's "cool, fire them" (PT114-S4-Q19).
- **Use qualifier words in the stimulus to predict.** "Primarily" points to a plausibility answer (PT146-S3-Q8). "Any ... that fall off" is an untriggered conditional (PT104-S4-Q6). "Roasted longer" is a breadcrumb (PT146-S2-Q16).
- **Rule out the tempting frame before finding the real gap.** PT112-S1-Q24 checks sampling, rejects it, and then finds "ranked #2 is not a great number".
- **Give the fixed version of a near-miss question.** "How jurors judge the credibility of the scientific evidence" (PT146-S3-Q8 C). "Prohibitively expensive?" (PT141-S4-Q6 C). "More resin when bacteria peak" (PT152-S4-Q19 B, not picked).
- **Use a short analogy for a comparison gap.** Sharon's bench press (PT153-S2-Q3), Pop Tarts vs the grocery store (PT101-S2-Q16).
