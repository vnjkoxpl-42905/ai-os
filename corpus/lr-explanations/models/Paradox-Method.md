### What the best Paradox explanations do

- **They name the expectation and the kind of fix before the choices.** PT148-S3-Q11 says the known difference (surveys) cannot be the cause, so the answer must be a second difference between the groups. PT152-S4-Q23 says the answer must show how a few devices have a large effect.
- **They give two or three shapes the answer can take, then sort every choice into them.** PT139-S1-Q15 (trees, birds, or a coincident change) and PT148-S1-Q12 (could not eat, chose not to, ate and the herd grew anyway).
- **They use a single test that works on the next question.** "Something that changed" (PT154-S1-Q6); "is this a timeless rule?" (PT107-S4-Q19, PT113-S2-Q17); "does it separate the two groups?" (PT148-S4-Q3).
- **On math paradoxes they predict and do the numbers.** PT143-S1-Q19 (averages), PT140-S2-Q7 (cost shares), PT120-S4-Q1 (count vs per-item). Each says why math is the one place to predict.
- **On EXCEPT they show the three-and-one split.** PT113-S2-Q17 states it in the D note; PT120-S3-Q15 lists the routes up front; PT101-S3-Q14 uses a fill-in sentence frame for every choice.
- **They use the background fact as a weapon against traps.** PT120-S4-Q1 (A and D contradict "big films gross 2-3x cost"), PT140-S1-Q20 ("any answer about wool sales is doomed"), PT148-S1-Q12 (it is an island).
- **They separate "didn't decrease" from "increased".** PT139-S1-Q15 on A and B; PT152-S4-Q23 on apprehension rate vs theft rate. This precise distinction is what beats the top traps.
- **When the key does something unusual, they say so and teach it.** PT120-S1-Q6 (the answer can undercut the first fact); PT121-S4-Q19 ("explain all the data" means both facts).

### What the best Method explanations do

- **They name the move before the choices, from a fixed menu.** Most strong ones list the eight moves (analogy, counterexample, implications, ruling out, alternate explanation, overlooked consideration, dubious assumption, definition/distinction) and pick one. PT103-S1-Q18, PT107-S1-Q12, PT139-S1-Q23.
- **They split every answer into its conclusion half and evidence half.** "Argues X by Y: X must match the conclusion, Y the evidence", then check both. PT118-S4-Q12, PT137-S4-Q12, PT140-S1-Q9.
- **They state the second-speaker rule and use it.** The respondent almost never denies the first speaker's evidence. PT102-S3-Q11, PT111-S4-Q25, PT118-S1-Q4, PT108-S3-Q13.
- **They teach strength words in abstract answers.** "Unwarranted" is not "false" (PT139-S1-Q23); "inconsistent" and "self-contradictory" are almost never right (PT151-S4-Q21, PT120-S3-Q20); "while effective" sinks an otherwise perfect answer (PT102-S4-Q19).
- **They draw the line between neighbouring moves with a worked contrast.** Analogy vs counterexample with the same story (PT151-S4-Q21); common cause vs another cause (PT119-S2-Q17); distinction vs analogy (PT102-S3-Q11).
- **They rewrite a wrong answer into the version that would have been right.** PT137-S4-Q12 (B fixed), PT133-S3-Q19 (A "flip its frown"). This shows exactly which half failed.
- **They convert between Method and the Assumption family.** An objection is an assumption the author made that the objection is false (PT111-S4-Q25); "implying" in an answer is safe because any objection implies a failure to consider (PT119-S2-Q17).
- **They make abstract or dense stimuli concrete first.** PT120-S3-Q20 (Mother's Day flowers), PT137-S2-Q12 (a 2x2 grid of trials).

- **Goal predicts the wrong move and the notes never reconcile it.** PT136-S2-Q25 predicts "ruling out alternatives" and has C and D pasted.
