  [step 0 id=b4ebde2a-e0eb-475e-a5cc-5a57682cd4cd] Conclusion (Hence)
There are celestial objects in this galaxy that generate light but are not stars.
  [step 1 id=848846a8-f0b8-4aed-ab0d-6840a6d1ff35] Evidence
Stars generate light. Planets do not generate light. There are many other celestial objects in this galaxy that are not planets.
  [step 2 id=492f384f-6a7a-4799-a551-2ca7ac5c336a] Evaluate
This argument is so bad that it's hard to understand what the author is going for. She establishes a difference between stars and planets (the former generates light and the latter doesn't). Then she affirms that there are other celestial objects besides stars and planets. Then she concludes these other objects (or at least some of them) are like stars, rather than like planets, when it comes to generating light. Why? Why wouldn't we just as easily assume that the other objects are like planets and don't generate light? She seems to be assuming that planets are uniquely the celestial objects that don't generate light and that all the other celestial objects do. Her argument could be thought of as an illegal negation:
```
Planet → does not generate light
Some X's are not Planets.
Hence, Some X's do generate light.
```
She's arguing, "if there are other celestial objects that aren't planets, then there are other celestial objects that do generate light", which means she's assuming, "If it's not a planet, then it does generate light".
  [step 3 id=c7d28d53-3548-4654-945d-960ddbd4e443] Goal
We could potentially call out this Illegal Negation with an answer saying the author is confusing a condition that's sufficient to show you don't generate light (being a planet) with one that's necessary. However, since this question stem prefaced with "fails to consider" they might weaken the Nec vs. Suff error the "conversational" way, where we object to the conditional relationship the author is imagining by saying that the Left side could be true while the Right side is false. This author is imagining that "If it's not a planet, then it generates light". So we can say she fails to consider that something could be the Left side but not the Right side (something could be "not a planet" but not be "something that generates light"). Or we could just complain that the author presents two celestial objects (stars and planets), one that does generate light and one that doesn't. Then she assumes that a 3rd category of celestial objects ("other" celestial objects) belongs in the generating light category, for no apparent reason. The author fails to consider that these other celestial objects could be more like planets than they are like stars, when it comes to light-generating properties.
