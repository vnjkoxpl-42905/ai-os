# Before teaching: Must Be True

This is unspoken preparation for the shared lecture library. Open the [teleprompter](teleprompter.html) when ready to teach. Source: Patrick Tyrrell's LSAT Lab lecture, 2026-08-27, video 33013. Runtime: 97 minutes. The script follows the whole lecture, with five worked questions and an introductory scan of additional examples.

## What the lesson teaches

Accept the supplied facts. Make only the connections those facts support. The three central moves are contraposing a rule, linking compatible rules, and using a fact to trigger a rule or its contrapositive. Preserve the subject, quantifier, and strength of the conclusion. The lesson also introduces inference pivots, numerical boundaries, requirement language, passive voice, and temporal reasoning. Q4 is Most Strongly Supported, although the lecture is filed under Must Be True.

## What to check before starting

Check that the student can distinguish sufficient from necessary conditions, translate only, reverse and negate for contraposition, and negate AND as OR. If those operations take effort, pause on the opening board. Do not make familiarity with every advanced example a prerequisite; use the examples to expose gaps.

## Correct these source slips while teaching

The opening board has A leading to not B AND C, but its written contrapositive omits a negative before C. Teach B OR not C leading to not A. The preserved image shows the source typo; its caption and script correct it.

Q2's instructor verbally limits the relevant fields to the experience route. The displayed stimulus says these requirements. The adapted script follows the displayed question rather than that spoken restriction.

For Q1, negating fulfillment of both moral and intellectual responsibilities does not establish failure of both. At least one responsibility fails. Do not turn that into every university failing both responsibilities.

## Use the script without losing the reasoning

Keep autoscroll paused while showing a question. Read the question with the student, ask for a prediction, and continue the worked explanation. Pause at each conditional transformation long enough for the student to explain why it is legal. Use the adjacent timestamp buttons to select the board relevant to that part of the explanation.
