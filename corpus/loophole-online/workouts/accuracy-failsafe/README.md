### Instructions

**NOTE**: You can do an Accuracy Failsafe with any workout. Failsafes are time-training protocols that you can apply to any workout.

1. Set your accuracy goal as a target number of wrong answers per section.

   1. An accuracy goal could be -5, for example.
2. Complete an LR or RC section*  at your natural pace.

3. Take a five-minute break and Camo the section you just completed.

   1. If you have met your accuracy goal, proceed to a second section of the same type.

      1. This means if you just completed an RC section and hit your accuracy goal of -5, you would proceed to do another RC section with an accuracy goal of -5.
   2. If you have not met your accuracy goal on the first section, you have not beaten today's Failsafe.

      1. Do not decrease your target number of wrong answers tomorrow. Keep the same accuracy goal.

      2. Do not proceed to a second section of the same type today.

      3. Instead, Wrong Answer Journal and do one Recovery Workout (defined below) now.
4. If you have proceeded to a second section, take a five-minute break and Camo it.

5. If you have met your accuracy goal for the second time in a row, you have beaten today's Failsafe!

   1. Wrong Answer Journal both of today's sections.

   2. Decrease your target number of wrong answers tomorrow.

      1. This means that if you beat today's Failsafe of -5 accuracy, try for -4 accuracy on tomorrow's Failsafe.
6. If you do not meet the accuracy goal on the second section, you have not beaten today's Failsafe.

   1. Wrong Answer Journal both of today's sections.

   2. Do not decrease your target number of wrong answers tomorrow. Keep the same accuracy goal.
7. Return to Step 1 the following day.

### Ellen's Advice

#### If You Want to Win

- Failsafes are a great Workout for the student who loves data and structured goals.

- Don't be intimidated by all the steps you see in the Failsafe.

  - Once you get into it, the flow of the Workout is relatively intuitive.

  - Essentially, if you meet your goals twice in a row, you move your goal. If you don't meet your goals twice in a row, you don't move your goal.
- Which Recovery Workout should you choose?

  - It depends on where you tend to feel difficulty throughout the section.

  - If you notice you miss questions from not translating the answer choices, going toward an Answer Choice Primer or MemAC could be a great move.

  - If you notice that you are making strategic mistakes, making a Highlight Reel is a safe bet.

  - You can be the judge of which Recovery Workout is best for you, but the Recovery Workouts are designed to not allow you to simply repeat the exact same mistakes that you made in the first section of the FailSafe.

  - Even if you're tempted to go outside the list of Recovery Workout, don't do one that involves timed question answering.

## Stop Mental and Physical Self-Sabotage

### What problem are we solving?

Have you ever done a section when:

- you're falling asleep in the middle?

- someone else is taking a call a little too close to you?

- you're looking at the clock over and over again and saying to yourself "I have to speed up"?

- you're saying to yourself, "I can't get this wrong. Oh my god, I don't understand. I have to get it. I'll never get into Berkeley. My dad is right about me."?

- you just saw a previous score and you're going to cry?

- you have a stye on your left eye and also maybe COVID?

Doing the LSAT like this is mental and physical self-sabotage.

You are willfully engaging in behaviors that decrease your score.

I care about you and I care about your score, so I'm going to give you some real talk here.

Telling yourself you can't do something drastically increases the chances you won't be able to do that thing.

So stop it.

Doing LSAT in an environment or situation that pulls on your attention sets you up to fail.

So stop it.

Thinking about the past, the future, and what other people think while you're doing the LSAT ensures that you do not do your best.

So stop it.

This is your Olympics.

We need you in peak mental and physical condition while you're training to ensure that you're in peak condition on test day. This means that you have to advocate for yourself.

Create space in your life for LSAT is to be the #1 priority, not something you do after every high priority is done.

This Accuracy Fix teaches you how to be that your advocate.

## Learn From Your Mistakes

### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There's nothing interesting left anymore.

Now you "know where you are," but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights.

If you like the number, you'll walk away excited and think, "Okay, now I'm good."

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don't need to work *that* hard when you're good, right?

If you don't like the number, tears follow.

Now there's no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what's the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better.

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you're doing wrong.

What Problem Are We Solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon. 

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to &quot;see how you&#x27;re doing,&quot; &quot;understand where you are,&quot; &quot;stay motivated,&quot; and &quot;know what to work on.&quot;

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you&#x27;re done with the section.  But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There&#x27;s nothing interesting left anymore. 

Now you &quot;know where you are,&quot; but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights. 

If you like the number, you&#x27;ll walk away excited and think, &quot;Okay, now I&#x27;m good.&quot;

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don&#x27;t need to work that hard when you&#x27;re good, right? 

If you don&#x27;t like the number, tears follow.

Now there&#x27;s no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what&#x27;s the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better. 

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you&#x27;re doing wrong.

Instructions

NOTE: You can do an Accuracy Failsafe with any workout. Failsafes are time-training protocols that you can apply to any workout.

Set your accuracy goal as a target number of wrong answers per section.

An accuracy goal could be -5, for example.

Complete an LR or RC section* at your natural pace.

Take a five-minute break and Camo the section you just completed.

If you have met your accuracy goal, proceed to a second section of the same type.

This means if you just completed an RC section and hit your accuracy goal of -5, you would proceed to do another RC section with an accuracy goal of -5.

If you have not met your accuracy goal on the first section, you have not beaten today&#x27;s Failsafe.

Do not decrease your target number of wrong answers tomorrow. Keep the same accuracy goal.

Do not proceed to a second section of the same type today.

Instead, Wrong Answer Journal and do one Recovery Workout (defined below) now.

If you have proceeded to a second section, take a five-minute break and Camo it.

If you have met your accuracy goal for the second time in a row, you have beaten today&#x27;s Failsafe!

Wrong Answer Journal both of today&#x27;s sections.

Decrease your target number of wrong answers tomorrow.

This means that if you beat today&#x27;s Failsafe of -5 accuracy, try for -4 accuracy on tomorrow&#x27;s Failsafe.

If you do not meet the accuracy goal on the second section, you have not beaten today&#x27;s Failsafe.

Wrong Answer Journal both of today&#x27;s sections.

Do not decrease your target number of wrong answers tomorrow. Keep the same accuracy goal.

Return to Step 1 the following day.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Failsafes are a great Workout for the student who loves data and structured goals. 

Don&#x27;t be intimidated by all the steps you see in the Failsafe.

Once you get into it, the flow of the Workout is relatively intuitive.

Essentially, if you meet your goals twice in a row, you move your goal. If you don&#x27;t meet your goals twice in a row, you don&#x27;t move your goal. 

Which Recovery Workout should you choose?

It depends on where you tend to feel difficulty throughout the section.

If you notice you miss questions from not translating the answer choices, going toward an Answer Choice Primer or MemAC could be a great move.

If you notice that you are making strategic mistakes, making a Highlight Reel is a safe bet. 

You can be the judge of which Recovery Workout is best for you, but the Recovery Workouts are designed to not allow you to simply repeat the exact same mistakes that you made in the first section of the FailSafe.

Even if you&#x27;re tempted to go outside the list of Recovery Workout, don&#x27;t do one that involves timed question answering.
