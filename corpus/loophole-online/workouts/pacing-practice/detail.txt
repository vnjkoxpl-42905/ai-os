Instructions

Choose an interval time goal.

Your interval time goal is your target time for each question.

For example, an interval time goal may be 90 seconds per question.

Follow the procedures of How to Set a Realistic Interval Time Goal below.

Set an interval timer to repeatedly countdown from that time goal 30 times in a row.

Do not incorporate rest intervals.

We choose 30 times so we can have some extra intervals in case our timer messes up.

Start your interval timer when you begin an LR or RC section*.

For RC, only use the interval timer on the questions, not during the passage.

You must select an answer on each question before the interval timer goes off.

You cannot stay on a question longer than the interval timer permits, no matter how badly you want to.

Ellen's Advice
If You Want to Win

Pacing Practice trains you to pace yourself.

It is an ideal workout if you find yourself over-investing in a few questions throughout the section and, in doing so, causing yourself to run out of time.

You can use Pacing Practice within a test-day timed section just to ensure that you're moving on when you should, or you can use Pacing Practice on stopwatch-timed sections to gradually introduce timing in a controlled environment.

It's a versatile workout with many applications, so enjoy adapting it to your needs.
Timing Tips

To Set a Realistic Interval Time Goal

Start with your natural pace and divide that natural pace by the number of questions in a section. This result is your interval time per question.

Your natural pace is how long it takes you to complete a section at goal accuracy without worrying about the time.

For instance, if your natural pace is 50 minutes per section and you divide that by the number of questions in a given LR section (~25 questions), you would have an interval time goal of 2 minutes per question.

If your natural pace time is above your test-day time, gradually lower your interval time by 3-10 seconds after every Pacing Practice that meets your accuracy goal

Keep lowering the interval time until your interval time goes below your test-day timing.
