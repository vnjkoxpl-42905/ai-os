Test-Day Timing Marathon Routine
Why Are We Doing This?

Test-Day Timing Marathon Routines are the best way to push yourself to improve stamina and test-day readiness at the same time.

The first few sections of a Test-Day Timing Marathon may be rocky, but you should gradually acclimate to how a test-day timed section feels.

Don't attempt Test-Day Timing Marathon Routines if your Marathon-timed sections are 10+ minutes from test-day timing. Test-Day Timing Marathons are more appropriate when you're within 5 minutes of test-day timing.

Test-Day Timing Marathons don't allow for natural time progression in the same way Marathon Routines do, so they risk compromising your accuracy and process far more than the other Speed Routines.

How To Pull This Off

To maximize the value of Test-Day Timing Marathons, treat this Routine just like test day (except for no breaks after section 2 😅).

This Routine is basically test day on steroids. If you can get your goal section-level accuracy in this Routine, you are already getting ahead on our Perform Prep Stage goals.

Marathon Routine Protocols

Warm up before any Marathon-style Routine so you can hit the first section with a running start.

If you don't warm up first, the Marathon will not speed you up as much because it will take you longer to build momentum.

Always Camo + Wrong Answer Journal at the end of a Marathon Routine day to avoid building up too much Camo debt.

The normal "wait at least 24 hours before you Camo" rule does not apply to Marathons.

Never interrupt a Marathon to Camo in between sections.

Camo the Marathon sections in the order of completion.

This means you should Camo the first section of your Marathon first, the second section next, and so on.

Complete the entire Camo + Wrong Answer Journal process for each Marathon section before checking any Marathon scores and before starting the Camo process on another Marathon section. Camo and Wrong Answer Journal each section one at a time.

How To Move On

If your Test-Day Timing Marathons crash and burn even though you've seen goal time and accuracy on Marathon-timed sections before, you may just be anxious about the countdown timer and need experience to acclimate.

Mixed Timing Marathon Routines are the best remedy for that situation because they ground you in your accurate, controlled process between test-day timed sections.

If you're hitting your goal time and accuracy in your Test-Day Timing Marathon Routine, take a Practice Test (PT).
