Instructions

NOTE: Do not attempt HIIT if you have never done a Thunderdome. Ideally, you've done several Thunderdomes and begun to feel confident while Thunderdoming before ever attempting HIIT.

After you finish your Translation + CLIR on a given stimulus at 20-minute pace, complete the stimulus' Thunderdome without looking at the stimulus again*.

Cap each Thunderdome output at 30 seconds outloud.

Use AI voice dictation to record your Thunderdome outputs while keeping the workout speedy. My favorite dictation software is Flow; it's free up to 2000 words per week.

If you go over 30 seconds per Thunderdome output, don't worry about it. Just aiming for 30 seconds will make you faster.

Ellen's Advice
If You Want to Win

It is not important to look at the question stem and answer choices during a HIIT.

The point of this workout is not to answer questions, but if you want to combine it with answer-choosing, I'm not fully against it.

Don't attempt to HIIT as a beginner.

If you haven't met the Translation time goal or don't have significant experience with full LR sections, wait to HIIT until you need it.

For the right kind of plateaued, advanced test taker, HIIT offer a singular opportunity for a perspective shift.

HIITs show you where you're weak.

If you find that you always struggle with one of the HIIT outputs, review the videos and written material on that topic.

HIITs are great for when you start to run out of unseen PTs near the end of your prep.

HIITs can still be done on previously seen material.

If you are an advanced student PTing at 172+, HIITing can really take your skills to the next level.

Like Translation + CLIR Drills, HIITs are a great spot to use AI voice dictation to speed up the workout. My favorite dictation software is Flow; it's free up to 2000 words per week.
