Ellen's Advice
If You Want to Win

We want high information density in what we learn from the answer choices and question stems

We don't want "There is research about platypus"

We want "There is scientific research on the function of a platypus' bill, potentially to locate prey"

Make sure the things you learn from each question and answer choice is new and distinct

Make sure you're not simplifying the nouns (don't change neocortex to cortex)

When you hit a question with a ton of scientific jargon, treat it as a preview of what will happen in the passage.

You now know that you are going to be responsible for these nouns and verbs, so learn as much as you possibly can able these words

Give yourself a shot to quip in a ton of depth on each of the answer choices to interrelate the content of the answers on previous questions

This will allow you to put together the pieces to understand the story of the question.
