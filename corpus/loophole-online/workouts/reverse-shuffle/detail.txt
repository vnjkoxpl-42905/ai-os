Ellen's Advice
If You Want to Win

The Reverse Shuffle's bark is a lot worse than its bite. Don't be so intimidated by this workout that you don't even try it. 

The Reverse Shuffle is a great way to prove to yourself that you don't actually have to look back to the passage as much as you think you do.

For the most part, you look back at the passage to ease anxiety, not because it actually increases the number of correct answers per section. 

The Reverse Shuffle combines the best of Party Tricking with the best of Translation.

You know your Translation has to be really good because of the gap between translating the passage and actually answering its questions, so it automatically holds you to a higher standard. 

The steps between translating the passage and actually answering its questions produce a beneficial blur over the content of the passage.

This beneficial blur mimics the feeling of Party Tricking – it forces you to answer the questions conceptually, rather than relying on keywording to choose your answer.

This is why the Reverse Shuffle often yields an increase in accuracy. 

Just like the E Blackout, I'm sure you won't believe that the Reverse Shuffle was actually used on a real LSAT to produce a 180. But it did, and I have the receipts ✅.
