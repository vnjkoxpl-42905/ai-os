### Instructions

**NOTE**: You should not break up the individual Answer Choice Translation sections over more than one day, but completing the Answer Choice Primer in its entirety will take 6 prep days if you do one workout per day.

1. For the first three days, complete new one section*  per day of the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill (defined below), for a total of three Written sections.

2. For the next three days, complete one new section per day of the Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drill (defined below), for a total of three Out Loud sections.

***Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drills***

1. Make a spreadsheet with two columns, or use this template :

   1. **Column 1**: Answer Choice Text

   2. **Column 2**: Translation
2. Read only the answer choices in the section.

   1. Do not read the stimulus.

   2. Do not read the question stem.
3. Write down the text of each individual answer choice in the Answer Choice Text column.

   1. Do not copy/paste this text. Type the text yourself.
4. Write down a Translation of each answer choice in the Translation column.

   1. This means read A, write the literal text of A, and write the Translation of A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

   2. Your Translation of the answer choice cannot be a copy/paste of the answer choice text nor can it be exactly the same as the answer choice text. Translation means putting the same meaning in your own words.

***Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drills***

1. Read only the answer choices in the section.

   1. Do not read the stimulus.

   2. Do not read the question stem.
2. Translate the answer choice out loud.

3. Quip the answer choice out loud.

   1. This means read A, translate A, and quip A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

### Ellen's Advice

#### If You Want to Win

- The Answer Choice Translation Primer is responsible for bringing all the Translation skills you've built through your Translation + CLIR Drills into the answer choices.

  - This is such an important step to ensure consistent accuracy once we start answering questions.

  - Our standard of comprehension and retention must remain high throughout the entire question.
- The Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet may be slow and difficult in the beginning.

  - Do not give up on this workout.

  - It gets much easier by the third section.
- Keep your focus and commitment strong throughout the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet portion of the Answer Choice Translation Primer.

  - Do not complete this Workout at a relaxed pace with frequent breaks.

  - Every interruption in the Workout decreases your pace of improvement.
- If you're frequently taking longer than five minutes to complete one set of answer choices on the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet, use a countdown timer to force yourself to finish each set of answer choices in five minutes or less.

- Do not reuse the same sections for the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet and Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drills.

- Do not combine the Answer Choice Translation Primer with other Workouts.

## Stop Oversimplifying

### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

## Strengthen Your Working Memory

### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

Instructions

NOTE: You should not break up the individual Answer Choice Translation sections over more than one day, but completing the Answer Choice Primer in its entirety will take 6 prep days if you do one workout per day.

For the first three days, complete new one section* per day of the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill (defined below), for a total of three Written sections.

For the next three days, complete one new section per day of the Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drill (defined below), for a total of three Out Loud sections.

Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drills

Make a spreadsheet with two columns, or use this template:

Column 1: Answer Choice Text

Column 2: Translation

Read only the answer choices in the section.

Do not read the stimulus.

Do not read the question stem.

Write down the text of each individual answer choice in the Answer Choice Text column.

Do not copy/paste this text. Type the text yourself.

Write down a Translation of each answer choice in the Translation column.

This means read A, write the literal text of A, and write the Translation of A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

Your Translation of the answer choice cannot be a copy/paste of the answer choice text nor can it be exactly the same as the answer choice text. Translation means putting the same meaning in your own words.

Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drills

Read only the answer choices in the section.

Do not read the stimulus.

Do not read the question stem.

Translate the answer choice out loud.

Quip the answer choice out loud.

This means read A, translate A, and quip A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

The Answer Choice Translation Primer is responsible for bringing all the Translation skills you&#x27;ve built through your Translation + CLIR Drills into the answer choices.

This is such an important step to ensure consistent accuracy once we start answering questions.

Our standard of comprehension and retention must remain high throughout the entire question.

The Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet may be slow and difficult in the beginning.

Do not give up on this workout.

It gets much easier by the third section.

Keep your focus and commitment strong throughout the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet portion of the Answer Choice Translation Primer.

Do not complete this Workout at a relaxed pace with frequent breaks.

Every interruption in the Workout decreases your pace of improvement.

If you&#x27;re frequently taking longer than five minutes to complete one set of answer choices on the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet, use a countdown timer to force yourself to finish each set of answer choices in five minutes or less.

Do not reuse the same sections for the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet and Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drills.

Do not combine the Answer Choice Translation Primer with other Workouts.
