Ellen's Advice
If You Want to Win

It is very important that you do not take breaks between sections or check your sections in the middle of the Marathon.

Marathons only work because your brain doesn’t get the chance to re-adapt to non-LSAT thinking.

Taking breaks guarantees that the Marathon will not work.

Do not check your phone.

Do not have conversations.

Do not take a lunch break.

Put snacks and drinks on the desk to eat while you Marathon, if needed.

The only breaks allowed during Marathons are medically required or biologically required breaks.

Running to the bathroom is allowed between sections.

Accommodated breaks for medically required tasks (like insulin injections, etc) are obviously allowed.

Open all the sections you plan to complete in separate tabs before starting the Marathon.

It is exceptionally important that you do not see the score of each section in the middle of the Marathon.

If you see the scores, it will take out of the focused activity and all the intuition-building work will be undone. The Marathon will be wasted.

Marathons should be performed on only one type of section at a time unless otherwise specified (as in, Mixed Section Marathons).

This means that all the sections of the Marathon are LR, or all the sections of the Marathon are RC.

Keep track of the order of the sections in your Marathon, so you can analyze the results.

If you feel yourself getting worse as the Marathon progresses, you are likely becoming fatigued.

This is ok, but it indicates that you’ll need to do another Marathon. Fatigue is part of the process; it indicates that you need to build stamina.

At the end of the Marathon, take a break and then Camo all the sections in a big batch.

Timing Tips

How Marathon Timing Works

Use a stopwatch and a 35-minute countdown timer on LawHub to time your sections.

In LawHub, use Self-Paced Mode. Do not use Exam Mode.

If you have extended time, change the 35-minute countdown timer to the amount of time you have to complete the section according to your accommodations.

If the countdown timer runs out and you are not done, write down what question you were on when time ran out. Continue the section until you finish, and write down the time it took you to finish as soon as you finish it.

Just look down at the stopwatch when you finish it and write down what you see.

Keep track of the timed and untimed score after you review.

Do not pretend that Marathon timing is the same as test-day timing.

Do not do all the Marathon sections with bad form in order to meet test-day timing before you're ready. It is expected that you first few Marathons will contain sections that go far beyond the test-day time limit.

Pretending that you're ready for test-day timed sections before you're actually ready for them (finishing Marathons within the test-day time limit even though this deeply hurts your accuracy) will only ingrain bad form and make you significantly worse. I don't want to hurt you, so PLEASE do not do this. Let the Marathon sections take longer than test-day timed sections, if they need to in order to maintain the accuracy.

If you are consistently finishing your Marathon sections within 35 minutes (or 36/37 minutes) and your accuracy is suffering, do your Marathons with a stopwatch only and no countdown timer. Set LawHub to 400 minutes at the start of each section so the timer doesn't bother you.
