### Instructions

1. For each passage* , write out an answer to each of the following:

   1. What's the Main Point?

   2. What's the tone of the passage?

   3. Who is the author?

   4. Who is the audience?

   5. Who are the players in the passage and what do they believe?

   6. Strengthen the author's opinion

      1. If the passage feels neutral, write a confirming piece of evidence for the information the author is presenting.
   7. Weaken the author's opinion

      1. If the passage feels neutral, write a disconfirming piece of evidence for the information the author is presenting.
   8. Three Inferences from the passage

   9. Three things the author is likely to believe

   10. Parallel one piece of the passage

       1. Pick an example, one line of reasoning, or a given person's point of view.
   11. Outline the structure of the passage

       1. You can use paragraphs as an organizing structure, but you can also delineate on a more fine-grained level, if desired.
   12. Sub out three words in the passage and replace them with a new word/phrase that means the same thing.

   13. Pick three sentences out of the passage and identify their function

   14. Identify a principle underlying the passage as a whole or an argument within the passage

### Ellen's Advice

#### If You Want to Win

- **It is not important to look at the questions and answer choices during an RC Thunderdome.**

  - The point of this workout is not to answer questions, but if you want to combine it with answer-choosing, I'm not fully against it.
- Don't attempt to RC Thunderdome as a beginner.

  - If you haven't met the RC Translation time goals or don't have significant experience with full RC sections, wait to RC Thunderdome until you need it.
- For the right kind of plateaued, advanced test taker, RC Thunderdomes offer a singular opportunity for a perspective shift.

- RC Thunderdomes show you where you're weak.

  - If you find that you always struggle with one of the RC Thunderdome outputs, review the videos and written material on that topic.
- RC Thunderdomes are great for when you start to run out of unseen PTs near the end of your prep.

  - RC Thunderdomes can still be done on previously seen material.
- If you are an advanced student PTing at 172+, RC Thunderdoming can really take your skills to the next level.

- Like Translation + CLIR Drills, RC Thunderdomes are a great spot to use AI voice dictation to speed up the workout. My favorite dictation software is Flow ; it's free up to 2000 words per week.

## Stop Oversimplifying

### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

## Don't Be Desperate in the Answers

### What problem are we solving?

Sometimes you just want to get the question right so badly that every answer choice looks like an escape hatch.

"Is it right? Is it right!??

If it's right, then I can move on.

Then I won't waste any more time.

Then the stimulus must have meant this.

Then this must have been what they were looking for."

Notice the desperation dripping off this monologue.

You're looking for an answer choice to rescue you, but actually the roles need to be reversed. You have to be the strong one.

Be for real. You are better than 80% wrong.

Answer choices are designed to exploit your desire for them to be right and to prey on moments of inattention.

But you can channel your desire to get the question right into building an invulnerable fortress of analysis throughout every stage of the question. This fortress (Translation, CLIR, Mission, Back Up Plan) will allow you to see exactly why these answers are wrong without. any. drama.

The answer choices only gain manipulative power over you when you look to them to save you. But you are not the freaking damsel that answer C is here to rescue. You're the hero.

You're here to judge answer C, and you're going to judge it according to your vetted, bulletproof standards.

The answers need to impress *you*, not the other way around.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to build the fortress.

Because you are smart enough. You have the tools, the time, and the resources to never need any answer choice to save you again.

Quick Facts
Will This Help Me?

THIS WILL HELP YOU IF

You are an advanced student who wants to get more out of every RC passage and expand your scope of work so that you can find your weaknesses.

SKIP FOR NOW IF

You&#x27;re an LSAT beginner who is unfamiliar with the basics of RC.

CHALLENGE INDEX
Ellen Whyyy
LEVEL
Advanced
MINIMUM TIME
60+ minutes
TIMING COMPATIBLE WITH
Stopwatch
Instructions

For each passage*, write out an answer to each of the following:

What&#x27;s the Main Point?

What&#x27;s the tone of the passage?

Who is the author?

Who is the audience?

Who are the players in the passage and what do they believe?

Strengthen the author&#x27;s opinion

If the passage feels neutral, write a confirming piece of evidence for the information the author is presenting.

Weaken the author&#x27;s opinion

If the passage feels neutral, write a disconfirming piece of evidence for the information the author is presenting.

Three Inferences from the passage

Three things the author is likely to believe

Parallel one piece of the passage

Pick an example, one line of reasoning, or a given person&#x27;s point of view.

Outline the structure of the passage

You can use paragraphs as an organizing structure, but you can also delineate on a more fine-grained level, if desired.

Sub out three words in the passage and replace them with a new word/phrase that means the same thing.

Pick three sentences out of the passage and identify their function

Identify a principle underlying the passage as a whole or an argument within the passage

SECTIONS TO USE

Ideal for Previously Seen Sections

HOW TO TIME

Count-Up Stopwatch

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

It is not important to look at the questions and answer choices during an RC Thunderdome.

The point of this workout is not to answer questions, but if you want to combine it with answer-choosing, I&#x27;m not fully against it.

Don&#x27;t attempt to RC Thunderdome as a beginner.

If you haven&#x27;t met the RC Translation time goals or don&#x27;t have significant experience with full RC sections, wait to RC Thunderdome until you need it.

For the right kind of plateaued, advanced test taker, RC Thunderdomes offer a singular opportunity for a perspective shift.

RC Thunderdomes show you where you&#x27;re weak.

If you find that you always struggle with one of the RC Thunderdome outputs, review the videos and written material on that topic.

RC Thunderdomes are great for when you start to run out of unseen PTs near the end of your prep.

RC Thunderdomes can still be done on previously seen material.

If you are an advanced student PTing at 172+, RC Thunderdoming can really take your skills to the next level.

Like Translation + CLIR Drills, RC Thunderdomes are a great spot to use AI voice dictation to speed up the workout. My favorite dictation software is Flow; it&#x27;s free up to 2000 words per week.
