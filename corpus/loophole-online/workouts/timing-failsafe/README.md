### Instructions

**NOTE**: You can do a Timing Failsafe with any Workout. Failsafes are time-training protocols that you can apply to any Workout.

1. Set your accuracy goal as a target number of wrong answers per section.

   1. An accuracy goal could be -5, for example.
2. **Timing Rules**

   1. **First Day**: Do a stopwatch-timed section*  to establish your baseline natural pace.

   2. **All Other Days**: Adopt the timing pattern that you earned by beating or not beating the Failsafe the previous study day. Use a countdown timer for section timing once you have begun to lower your time goal.
3. Complete an LR or RC section.

4. Take a five-minute break and Camo the section you just completed.

   1. If you have met your accuracy goal, proceed to a second section of the same type with the same timing pattern.

      1. This means if you just completed an RC section in 42 minutes and you hit your accuracy goal of -5, you would proceed to do another RC section in 42 minutes with an accuracy goal of -5.
   2. If you have not met your accuracy goal on the first section, you have not beaten today's Failsafe.

      1. Don't lower your time goal tomorrow.

      2. Do not proceed to a second section of the same type today.

      3. Instead, Wrong Answer Journal and do one Recovery Workouts (listed below) now.
5. If you have proceeded to a second section, take a five-minute break and Camo it.

6. If have met your accuracy goal for the second time in a row, you have beaten today's Failsafe!

   1. Lower your time goal tomorrow.

   2. Wrong Answer Journal both of today's sections.
7. If you do not meet the accuracy goal on the second section, you have not beaten today's Failsafe.

   1. Do not move the time the next day.

   2. Wrong Answer Journal both of today's sections.
8. Return to Step 1 the following day.

### Ellen's Advice

#### If You Want to Win

- How much you should lower the time goal after a successful Failsafe?

  - It depends on how far you currently are from test-day timing and how difficult it is to beat the Failsafe at its current level.

  - If you're still 30+ minutes away from your test-day timing, it's safe to decrease time by 3-4 minutes per successful FailSafe.

  - If you're within 10 minutes of your test-day time, it's more difficult to maintain accuracy with every minute you cut. Only cut 1-2 minutes at a time when you're in this timing range to maintain accuracy more realistically.

  - Never end a section before 35 minutes is up (ever, but especially in a Failsafe).
- Failsafes are a great Workout for the student who loves data and structured goals.

- Don't be intimidated by all the steps you see in the Failsafe.

  - Once you get into it, the flow of the Workout is relatively intuitive.

  - Essentially, if you meet your goals twice in a row, you move your goal. If you don't meet your goals twice in a row, you don't move your goal.
- Which Recovery Workout should you choose?

  - Here's a list to choose from: Translation + CLIR Drill , MemAC , Answer Choice Primer, Party Tricking Jail , Vocab Training , Highlight Reel , and The Island . Which one you choose depends on where you tend to feel difficulty throughout the section.

  - If you notice you miss questions from not translating the answer choices, going toward an Answer Choice Primer or MemAC could be a great move.

  - If you notice that you are making strategic mistakes, making a Highlight Reel is a safe bet.

  - The Recovery Workouts are designed to interrupt and repair the mistakes that you made in the first section of the Failsafe.

  - Even if you're tempted to go outside the list of Recovery Workout, don't do one that involves timed question answering.

## Timing Failsafe Routine

### Why are we doing this?

Timing Failsafe Routines are one of the only ways to effectively reduce time without Marathoning.

The upsides of Timing Failsafes are:

- They can be done in shorter periods of time than a Marathon

- They allow for more systematic gradual time reduction

- Review can be more targeted and frequent

This Routine works great for students who thrive on structure.

### How to Pull This Off

Failsafe Routines work best when combined with other Routines to bring time down while maintaining accuracy.

Essentially, you have to address the reasons why the Failsafe breaks in order to get it to progress, so you'll want to consider alternating Failsafe Routines with the Accuracy Fix Routines that pertain to your mistakes.

### How to move on

Timing Failsafe Routines can get you to test-day goal time and accuracy without progressing to any other Speed Routine.

However, if you have lost every Timing Failsafe you've attempted for three consecutive prep days, here are a few options for what you can try next:

1. Set a higher baseline natural pace to start your Timing Failsafe

2. Replace the Timing Failsafe in this Routine with a Light Failsafe

3. Replace the Timing Failsafe in this Routine with Pacing Practice

4. Return to the Accuracy Fix Routines

If you sometimes hit goal time and accuracy in your Timing Failsafes, progress to any of the following Speed Routines: Failsafe Marathon Routine, Mini-Marathon Routine, or Marathon Routine.

If you're hitting your goal time and accuracy in the majority of your sections, take a Practice Test (PT).

You can move to any of the other Speed Routines, if your Timing Failsafe Routine went well. You do not have to go in order.
