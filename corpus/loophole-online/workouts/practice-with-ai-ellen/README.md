### Instructions

Any question you'd pose to me (real Ellen), you can pose to AI Ellen. Since she's my AI clone, she can be wrong, but she does a pretty great job most of the time. I read the questions you pose to AI Ellen and will often fine-tune AI Ellen's response when it differs from I would say.

Here are a few things she can do that you might want to try out:

- Use AI Ellen as your daily LSAT journaling partner. Keep her updated on your progress and she can help you keep track of everything you've done.

- She can answer your questions about the Loophole Online videos and about the Loophole book.

- She can make you a quiz on question stem identification, explain why your answer was wrong, and then adapt the quiz to concentrate on your weaknesses.

- She can quiz you on premise indicators, conclusion indicators, conditional indicators, and causal indicators. She can then requiz you only on the indicators you don't reliably know.

- She can listen to all your anxiety thoughts about the LSAT and give you strategies that can help you on test day and in your prep.

### Real Examples

***To Practice Conditional Reasoning***

- PROMPT: I want you to help me practice my conditional diagramming skills. Make me fun examples that look like real LSAT stimuli. Present these examples to me one at a time. I will attempt to diagram each example you give me or tell you the example can't be diagrammed. Make the examples difficult. Mix up the order of the conditional phrases, so they don't always start off the same way. Never draw Must Arrows pointed to the left. Do not include any examples that chain conditionals off of and/or conditions. Use a wide variety of conditional indicators in the examples, include Some, Most, Not All, or Few in 20% of the examples. 5% of your examples should not include any conditionals. Include Include or Exclusion indicators in 10% of the examples. Include "only if" in 10% of the examples. Include conditional chaining in 50% of the examples. Allow for inferencing with Some/Most chains on 10% of the examples. Check to make sure I identify the Three Valid Some/Most Inferences when they appear. Always give me feedback on my responses to help me improve my conditional diagramming skills and show me how to diagram the example correctly. Be encouraging since conditional diagramming is difficult.

## Create a Realistic Prep Plan and Study Schedule

### What problem are we solving?

The single biggest problem in LSAT prep is unrealistic planning and timelines. You absolutely can train yourself to massively improve your logic and Translation skills. But ya'll randomly pick test dates that make it impossible to train your skills in the amount of time you have before the test. This leads to panic, LSAT scores that shock and demotivate you, and an altogether toxic relationship with the test.

### How you know you need to do this

#### Is this worth my time?

4 of 4

verdict:

This is Me!

You have no plan on how the level of score improvement you need is remotely feasible by your test date

You don't prioritize LSAT prep in your calendar and will consistently skip studying whenever anything else comes up

You don't block your study time on Google Calendar

You just picked your test date because it was the next one and you want to get this over with

The Omitted Options are free points on the table for all the causal reasoning questions across every question type.
