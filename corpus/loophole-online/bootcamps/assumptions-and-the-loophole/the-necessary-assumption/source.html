As a pair, they have already gnawed through half a cigar, gotten trapped under an area rug, and claimed the counter as their own. Therefore, Hoodoo and Keith knocked over a vase of daffodils.

Why is the following not a necessary assumption in the argument above?

Any kitten who has ever engaged with a vase of daffodils may present a risk for mischievous conduct.

Correct! You got it right on the first try!

“Any kitten who has ever engaged with a vase of daffodils” places the concept behind the conclusion in the sufficient condition. Since we can only activate sufficient conditions with a premise, this is a Dormant Conditional, meaning the sufficient condition is not activated. In addition, it is a reversal of the If Our Premises, Then Our Conclusion construction.

Observed attempt 1: A Sufficient Assumption --> Conclusion True --> Necessary Assumption

Question 9

Which one of the following is the Assumption Chain?

Correct! You got it right on the first try!

The sufficient assumption proves the conclusion and, in turn, the conclusion proves the necessary assumption. Behold, the assumption chain:

SA --> Conclusion True --> NA

The sufficient assumption goes in the sufficient condition of the chain. If it's true, it activates the chain and forces the conclusion to be true. Since the conclusion is now true, the necessary assumption must also be true.

The necessary assumption goes in the necessary condition of the chain. If the conclusion is true, it must be true. But! Since you can't read the conditional chain backwards, the necessary assumption doesn't prove anything on its own.
