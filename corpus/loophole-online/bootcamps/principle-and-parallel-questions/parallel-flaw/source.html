Question 4

Many candidates for council will represent their constituents with honor. We can conclude that some current councilpersons are candidates for council, since a majority of current councilpersons represent their constituents with honor.

Which one of the following is a Loophole in the argument above?

Observed attempt 1: A What if this is bad Some & Most chaining?

Question 4

Many candidates for council will represent their constituents with honor. We can conclude that some current councilpersons are candidates for council, since a majority of current councilpersons represent their constituents with honor.

Which one of the following is a Loophole in the argument above?

Correct! You got it right on the first try!

Here is my diagram for this argument:

Current -m-> RCH <-s-> CC

Therefore: Current <-s-> CC

Notice how I wrote out the word “Current” in my diagram instead of abbreviating “current councilpersons” as “CC.” I made this choice to maintain clarity in my diagram, as it would be very easy to confuse “current councilpersons” with “candidates for council” as they would both be abbreviated “CC.”

If you did not diagram this argument, please consider expanding when you think diagramming is appropriate. It can be a valuable ally. Start practicing diagramming in the answer choices on the next page.

If you don’t understand exactly why this Some & Most chain is flawed, please review the Three Valid Some & Most Inferences on page 139 of The Loophole.

Question 5

Many candidates for council will represent their constituents with honor. We can conclude that some current councilpersons are candidates for council, since a majority of current councilpersons represent their constituents with honor.

The flawed reasoning in which one of the following arguments most closely parallels the flawed reasoning in the argument above?

Correct!

Here is the diagram for #1:

W -m-> LC

S <-s-> CA

Therefore: W <-s-> IC 

This answer choice does not exhibit bad Some & Most chaining because the two Some & Most statements in the premises do not chain together. There are no interlocking terms between the premises, so we cannot infer across the chain incorrectly.

Here is the diagram for #2: 

AS <-s-> EGO <-s-> PA

Therefore: AS <-s-> PA

This answer choice exhibits bad Some & Most chaining. It is the correct answer, even though this correct answer uses two Some statements in the premises while the stimulus used one Some statement and one Most statement in the premises. 

I know this may seem vexing at first, but this sort of maneuver happens in real Parallel Flaw correct answers, so you have to acclimate to it. This style of correct answer is exactly why we match the flaw on Parallel Flaw, rather than matching the Skeleton exactly as our first step

Here is the diagram for #3: 

PI → DPR <-s-> MDT

Therefore: PI <-s-> ~MDT

This answer choice does not exhibit bad Some & Most chaining because there is a Must Arrow mixed into the premise chain. We are not combining Some & Most statements in our premise chain, so we cannot infer across a Some & Most chain incorrectly, as the stimulus does. 

In addition, the conclusion changes the negation of one of the premise variables (MDT in the premises was changed to ~MDT in the conclusion). This sort of negation switch will never occur in a correct answer on Parallel Reasoning and Parallel Flaw.
