# Choose a practice protocol from the student's actual difficulty

This private instructor library compiles the operational details that were missing from the earlier [overview](index.md). Methods are attributed to Ellen Cassidy's captured Loophole Online lessons, not automatically adopted as mandatory Joshua curriculum. Existing topic pages and [Joshua's selected teaching standard](../../references/lsat-teaching-voice-standard.md) govern student-facing explanations. The annotated lecture editions linked below preserve the captured explanations and examples for deeper retrieval; they do not claim recovery of unprovided videos or images.

## Read, translate, react, then check the reasoning

1. Read one sentence or a smaller complete conceptual chunk. Avoid collecting several sentences before translating while learning.
2. Look away and retell its full conceptual content in ordinary language. Reorder ideas when useful, but retain qualifiers, dates, scope, comparisons and every list item. Reading again while paraphrasing is not a memory check.
3. React to this exact content. Give an example, make a connection, notice a possible problem, or ask a question and answer it. A generic verdict such as “that's interesting” does not show what was understood.
4. Repeat with the next chunk. Spontaneous micro-reactions may supplement the formal reaction stage; they do not replace translating the chunk.
5. At the end, produce the relevant analytical output: controversy, loophole, inference or resolution (CLIR). A final reaction can already be the CLIR, so another ceremonial step is unnecessary.
6. For an answer choice, translate the entire choice before judging it. Explain its relationship to the actual stimulus and question task. Start with evidence, then reach a verdict, rather than deciding first and looking for a justification.

For the source drill, speak the process aloud in one continuous recording. Do not pause recording between steps or stimuli. Cassidy prohibits skipping stimuli in this translation drill; that is not a general prohibition on test-day question triage. Verbalize exploratory thinking instead of silently editing it into a perfect reaction. Exploratory reactions may be wrong; an inference or final answer still requires logical checking.

When a student freezes, ask a concrete question about what they just read and require an attempted answer. Distinguish lack of understanding from fear that the reaction is not impressive enough. Do not impose the source's coercive rhetoric as a teaching interaction. Optimize for reaching an accurate insight rather than merely finishing the text quickly. Silent performance is a later transition after the process is reliable, not a reason to omit learning steps now.

## Repair missing words, qualifiers and list items

For an unfamiliar word, finish the thought, return to it and propose a meaning from the surrounding text. Mark it and collect it after the section into a vocabulary tracker. Verify the meaning and practice related roots and usages. The guess remains provisional: revise it when later evidence conflicts, and never turn confidence in a guess into proof that an answer is correct. The source's “90-plus-percent” guess accuracy and advice to lock in even a wrong guess are not established rules.

For lists, retain each member rather than substituting “several things.” Cassidy suggests distinct intonation or a brief reaction for each item. For qualifiers and dependent clauses, ask which group, time, degree or exception they constrain. Her example of missing “recent” illustrates accidentally applying an earlier time period to new findings. Her omitted dependent-clause example illustrates widening a conclusion beyond its stated group. A repair must name the dropped meaning, not merely say “pay closer attention.”

Ordinary vocabulary and context enable comprehension. External specialist facts do not replace what the question supplies. A learner can connect an oyster passage with ordinary experience without importing an external species count into the answer evidence.

## Check whether the stimulus survives analysis in memory

**Proto-Stimulus Rebound:** translate, react and produce the CLIR; cover the stimulus; retell it completely from memory, preferably with the argument parts in a different order; compare with the text and record each missing, added or changed concept. Answer the question afterward if that is part of the assignment. Previously seen stimuli can be mixed with fresh ones; use a count-up stopwatch if measuring the process. Skip the extra memory audit when the learner already reliably retains the stimulus.

For RC, follow the existing [cumulative-translation protocol](<../Curriculum/Reading Comprehension/Notes/cumulative-translation-review.md>): translate/react to paragraph 1 and recall it; do the same for paragraph 2, then recall 1+2; repeat cumulatively through the passage. These are full conceptual retellings, not shopping lists or main-point summaries. The source permits aloud recaps before silent ones and previously seen passages. Reliable stimulus RTQ is its prerequisite. Source goals of 12 minutes per passage with aloud recaps and 8 with silent recaps remain attributed training targets, not general test-day prescriptions.

## Turn opaque descriptions into concrete reasoning

Mark a difficult Method, Flaw or Argument Part choice during a section, without interrupting the section to do this drill. Afterward, make a three-column row: exact answer text; accurate own-words translation; an invented stimulus in which that description would be correct. Typing the full choice makes every word confrontable. Look up uncertain vocabulary or ask for help during review. Compare the invented case against every component of the description.

Cassidy's flower example describes drawing a distinction: two blue garden flowers appear alike but have different petal counts, so the speaker distinguishes them. That demonstrates the intended reasoning action; it does not prove that differing petal counts establish different species. The possible within-species variation is itself an objection to the illustrative argument.

For Method, identify the action the author performs across the whole argument. Do not substitute your judgment that the argument is bad. Practice unfamiliar descriptive vocabulary until it can be used accurately in a casual sentence. Cassidy's suggested five vocabulary usages per day is an optional source exercise. Distinguish **unjustified** from **false**: criticizing evidence can show that a conclusion has not been supported without proving its opposite. A counterexample must be a case that conflicts with the asserted general relationship.

## Practice conditional meaning without replacing the current standard

Paraphrase the complete statement into an ordinary if-then relationship before using its indicator to position labels. Read a proposed diagram back against the full sentence and look for a possible counterexample. The [current conditional reference](<../Curriculum/Conditional Logic/Notes/_megadoc_reference_conditionals.md>) remains the topic authority.

Cassidy's Unless routine rotates three of four forms: `~A, unless B`; `Unless B, A`; `Unless ~B, ~A`; `A, unless ~B`. Vary the letters and derive each relationship fresh. The captured answer-key heading contains no answers; this library does not invent a recovered key. Her proposed target is three diagrams in 15 seconds, then weekly maintenance, rather than a universal standard for every learner.

For conditional principle matching, determine the required conclusion, read the entire proposed principle, and test whether the stated premises establish its trigger. Keep the principle's full meaning, including contrapositively equivalent forms. Do not blindly reject an answer because a conclusion word is written on the left. A supplied fact plus an equivalent contrapositive can establish the required result. Cassidy's shortcut is an attributed heuristic, not a logical theorem.

## Keep assumption and weakening tasks precise

A necessary assumption is something the argument's claimed support requires. Negation tests the support, not whether the conclusion could independently be true. Some captured notes incorrectly equate conclusion truth with assumption truth; those formulations are excluded from the usable synthesis. The UFO/bones illustration in the source is available for comparing proposed bridges, not an approved answer-key exercise. Some alleged assumptions are broader than the sketch alone establishes. Use the current topic material and test each candidate independently.

For Weaken, put the actual conclusion into the task: does this whole choice damage the support for this specific claim? Delay the verdict until the full choice has been read and translated. Avoid rejecting a choice merely because it contains a familiar trap word or is not a surface topic match. A flaw prephrase helps but does not excuse ignoring a different way of weakening. The source's claim that only one answer can weaken at all is not adopted; question-relative strength and what the actual choices establish still matter.

For objection practice, hold the premises fixed and identify a possible missing connection rather than just declaring the conclusion false. The recovered kitten examples distinguish stress from hiding, mischief history from this specific act, and a two-cat sample from all felines. Their response-versus-understanding distinction prevents importing a stronger conclusion than the text asserts. Use the existing [Assumption versus Objection homework](<../Curriculum/Flaw and Famous Flaws/Homework/Pop Quiz - Assumption vs Objection.md>); the [recovered example edition](worked-examples.generated.md) preserves both the incomplete chapter and its five-question derivative without replacing that homework.

## Review without knowing which questions were wrong

**Camo:** preserve the initial answers without revealing the score or key. Reattempt a list containing misses and an unknown number of correct decoys. Restart each question completely: stimulus/passage, question task and every choice. Do not only compare the final two or preserve an initial answer out of loyalty. Then compare attempts and journal while the evidence is fresh.

| First attempt | Review | Source label | What to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wrong | Wrong | Conceptual gap | What remains misunderstood, including a repeated process error? |
| Wrong | Right | Misread | What changed in the reading or reasoning? |
| Right | Wrong | Self-doubt | Why was a defensible answer abandoned? Was the first answer genuinely understood? |
| Right | Right | Self-confidence | Can the learner explain the evidence, rather than repeating a lucky guess? |

These outcomes are clues, not proof of a single cause. Normally Cassidy waits at least 24 hours and puts Camo after new practice. The Failsafe explicitly uses immediate Camo after a five-minute break. Do not call this contradiction; it is a stated exception. Nor should a learner change an answer merely because the mixture must include misses: each change requires question-specific evidence.

**Wrong Answer Journal:** record date, question and chosen answer. In one or two sentences, name what the learner did that led to the wrong acceptance and right rejection, then a directly responsive action beginning “I will.” “I misread” is insufficient without identifying the dropped or changed meaning. Establish why choices are right/wrong first; journal the transferable repair rather than another long answer explanation. If a backlog prevents current journaling, Cassidy suggests starting fresh with recent sections rather than postponing all review.

**Island:** set aside questions that remain unclear after review. Revisit for five minutes per day. Move a question off only when its explanation genuinely makes sense, not merely because its answer letter is familiar. The Island is a queue for unresolved understanding, not a second answer key.

## Use a two-success accuracy progression

The captured **Accuracy Failsafe** sets a permitted-misses goal for a new section at natural pace. After a five-minute break, do Camo. If the goal is met, attempt a second new section of the same type and repeat the break/review. Two consecutive successes tighten the permitted-misses goal the following day. A first-section failure ends that day's Failsafe: journal and choose a recovery exercise instead of another same-type section. A second-section failure means journal both and keep tomorrow's goal unchanged.

Select recovery from the observed problem. The source names Answer Choice Primer or MemAC for answer-choice translation, and Highlight Reel for strategic mistakes. Their complete instructions were not captured here, so those names are pointers, not newly reconstructed drills. The source advises recovery without more timed question answering. A proposed target progression must be tailored to the actual student rather than automatically assigned.

Source: [Accuracy Failsafe](<../../raw/sources/old-obsidian-vault-2026-10-07/Clippings/loophole-online/workouts/Accuracy Failsafe • LO.md>).

## Preserve the source's routine targets without making them requirements

The Foundation routine orders consecutive translation/CLIR sections, extracurricular reading and vocabulary; it proposes at least three sections per day when possible, ideally six, plus a weekly eight-section marathon. Its described block is two to four hours. Supplemental suggestions are ScienceDaily for science, Aeon for general RC-like prose, Arts & Letters Daily for humanities, the NYT Guide to Essential Knowledge for background gaps, and selected Crash Course topics. These are historical source suggestions, not newly researched product recommendations.

The source advances to Translation + Decaf at the midpoint between initial and target translation time. Its example is 52 minutes initially, 20-minute target, midpoint 36. Targets are 20 minutes normally, 25 for 53-minute accommodations and 30 for 70-minute accommodations in the routine. The separate drill notes use 30 accommodated with 25 as a stretch target. Preserve that distinction rather than silently reconciling it. A qualifying time requires an aloud translation and reaction to every stimulus and a legitimate CLIR for each; the source suggests approximately five CLIR misses or fewer, adjustable for goals.

The Marathon uses six to eight fresh sections, records their order and individual count-up times, defers checking until after the batch, and permits medically or biologically required breaks. The captured 400-minute platform-timer setting is an old UI workaround, not verified current behavior. Claims that a single daily section cannot improve skill or that any other break guarantees failure are not accepted as established learning science. Fatigue is observable evidence to investigate, not an automatic instruction to push harder.

## Capture CLIRs and preserve blind review

Dictate the question number and CLIR into an existing document when producing the CLIR, then format the numbered entries without altering the student's words. For a full-session transcript, marking each CLIR aloud makes extraction easier. Check against the key after the drill, without inventing missing speech. The source's Whisper Flow/Superwhisper/Docs/Loom claims about pricing, privacy and capabilities are historical; use verified local settings before choosing tools.

Keep initial answers and timing data separate from revealed results when blind review is the purpose. The captured LawHub workaround uses self-paced tabs, avoids completion/show-answer controls and score-revealing hovers, and uses an external timer for custom durations. These exact buttons and persistence claims need a current interface check before operational use. This is not a blanket ban on exam-mode practice.

## Check a teaching reference before publishing it

Recovered conditional-plan review adds a useful artifact checklist: distinguish a quick reference from a complete lesson; make lookup labels contain the words a learner searches for; check mobile-width examples; avoid hiding every essential rule in collapsed panels; keep practice separate where it duplicates teaching; verify every diagram and its complete scope; distinguish contraposition from a second independent direction; preserve permission versus actual action; avoid inventing terms such as “good” weather; check causal tendencies before rendering them as guarantees; and distinguish beginner training requirements from test-day advice.

This checklist does not approve the historical artifact, old app path, left/right-only pedagogy or reviewer examples. The review's claim that a class explicitly taught “the only goes left” was rejected in its own triage; do not repeat the fabricated attribution. Current approved conditional sequence and voice standard control any implementation.
