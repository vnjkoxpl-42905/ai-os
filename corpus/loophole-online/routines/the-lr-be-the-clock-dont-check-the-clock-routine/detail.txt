Instructions

Choose an interval time goal.

Your interval time goal is your target time for each question.

For example, an interval time goal may be 90 seconds per question.

Follow the procedures of How to Set a Realistic Interval Time Goal below.

Set an interval timer to repeatedly countdown from that time goal 30 times in a row.

Do not incorporate rest intervals.

We choose 30 times so we can have some extra intervals in case our timer messes up.

Start your interval timer when you begin an LR or RC section*.

For RC, only use the interval timer on the questions, not during the passage.

You must select an answer on each question before the interval timer goes off.

You cannot stay on a question longer than the interval timer permits, no matter how badly you want to.

Ellen's Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine eases you into timing with minimal traumatic events. 

By doing this Routine before jumping into Test-Day Timed Sections, you enable yourself to more effectively transition your goal accuracy into a timed environment.

By studying and understanding timing on a deeper level, you train yourself to be fast as opposed to just expecting yourself to be fast.

How This Should Be Done

Gradually build up your Time Awareness Training as you progress through this Routine.

You have the option of overlapping your Time Awareness Training with other tasks as noted in the Time Awareness Training instructions, but make sure to go directly into the Cappuccino warmup and RC Pacing Practice as soon as the Time Awareness Training is completed.

This will enable you to more effectively and intuitively feel the time during your LR Pacing Practice.

How to Move On

You can move on from this Routine when you no longer feel the urge to check the clock to know how much time has passed.

I want you to trust yourself and your own internal clock so that you don't distract yourself on test day.
