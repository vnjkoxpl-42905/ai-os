### Step 2: Modified Inside Out

2

#### Modified Inside Out

##### Instructions

1. Read the first and last sentences of the passage* .

2. Read and translate each question stem and its answer choices.

3. Immediately after translating each question stem and its answer choices, write down one thing you learned from this question stem and its answer choices.

4. Repeat this pattern for every question of this passage.

5. Read and translate the passage.

6. Answer all the questions.

### Step 3: Decaf RC Translation Drill

3

#### Decaf RC Translation Drill

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Do your recaps out loud in the beginning to keep yourself honest about the quality and completeness of the recaps. Transition to silent recaps when you feel like you're ready.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

   1. This means all the details are included just like they would be on LR.

   2. Quip after *every* Translation.

   3. This standard applies to every paragraph you ever translate on RC.
2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

   1. The recap is not a summary, gist, or main point.

   2. The recap should not omit names, dates, and lists.

   3. The recap should not be a shopping list of information. It should be a story in your own words.

   4. It is encouraged to mix up the order of the sentences and ideas.

   5. This standard applies to every paragraph (or collection of paragraphs) you ever recap on RC.
3. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

4. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

5. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 back to back silently.

6. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 3. Do it just like LR.

7. Look away from paragraph 3 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 3 silently.

8. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 + 3 back to back silently.

9. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

10. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine will make RC far less traumatic.

Translating the question stems one-by-one without having to worry about getting the right answer is so soothing. And then moving into Modified Inside Out, which is essentially an RC section with a bunch of spoilers, keeps your blood pressure at a totally manageable level.

Specifying that the Modified Inside Out should be done with Decaf is not a way to punish you; it's a way to empower you.

When you finish your Decaf and can actually say out the full recap of the passage without looking, you're going to feel so confident heading into the questions.

That kind of confidence is addictive. You're working toward achieving that confidence with all your other RC practice after this.

#### How This Should Be Done

Slow and steady wins the race. Don't rush yourself through this Routine. If it takes whatever number of hours, it takes whatever number of hours. Who cares? You did it.

Pressuring yourself for RC speed before you feel comfortable with the section is the number one way students sabotage themselves before they get the chance to build any confidence. Take it at your pace. Don't rush. Speed comes after accuracy.

#### How to Move On

If you feel less fear (but still a little bit of fear) toward RC, move on to the RC Get to Know You Routine, the next Routine in the I'm Afraid of RC Routine Progression.

If you don't feel any fear toward RC, move on to any of the I Just Want to Focus on RC Routine Progressions.

### Identify and Translate the Question Stems, Know Whether They're Powerful or Provable, and Say Their Back Up Plans Without Hesitation

#### What problem are we solving?

Not being able to identify your question types and their associated qualities leaves you flying blind through the LSAT. Question types are exceedingly useful for training and predicting what you'll have to do in a given question. If you can't identify question types, you don't know what you're supposed to do, or you are sinking a ton of time deducing the nature of the task at hand from scratch every single time.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

##### If You Want to Win

- We want high information density in what we learn from the answer choices and question stems

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: Do your recaps out loud in the beginning to keep yourself honest about the quality and completeness of the recaps. Transition to silent recaps when you feel like you're ready.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

   1. This means all the details are included just like they would be on LR.

   2. Quip after *every* Translation.

   3. This standard applies to every paragraph you ever translate on RC.
2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

   1. The recap is not a summary, gist, or main point.

   2. The recap should not omit names, dates, and lists.

   3. The recap should not be a shopping list of information. It should be a story in your own words.

   4. It is encouraged to mix up the order of the sentences and ideas.

   5. This standard applies to every paragraph (or collection of paragraphs) you ever recap on RC.
3. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

4. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

5. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 back to back silently.

6. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 3. Do it just like LR.

7. Look away from paragraph 3 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 3 silently.

8. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 + 3 back to back silently.

9. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

10. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

## Stop Mental and Physical Self-Sabotage

### What problem are we solving?

Have you ever done a section when:

- you're falling asleep in the middle?

- someone else is taking a call a little too close to you?

- you're looking at the clock over and over again and saying to yourself "I have to speed up"?

- you're saying to yourself, "I can't get this wrong. Oh my god, I don't understand. I have to get it. I'll never get into Berkeley. My dad is right about me."?

- you just saw a previous score and you're going to cry?

- you have a stye on your left eye and also maybe COVID?

Doing the LSAT like this is mental and physical self-sabotage.

You are willfully engaging in behaviors that decrease your score.

I care about you and I care about your score, so I'm going to give you some real talk here.

Telling yourself you can't do something drastically increases the chances you won't be able to do that thing.

So stop it.

Doing LSAT in an environment or situation that pulls on your attention sets you up to fail.

So stop it.

Thinking about the past, the future, and what other people think while you're doing the LSAT ensures that you do not do your best.

So stop it.

This is your Olympics.

We need you in peak mental and physical condition while you're training to ensure that you're in peak condition on test day. This means that you have to advocate for yourself.

Create space in your life for LSAT is to be the #1 priority, not something you do after every high priority is done.

This Accuracy Fix teaches you how to be that your advocate.

THIS WILL HELP YOU IF

You&#x27;re intimidated by LSAT RC and need to find a way to approach the section that doesn&#x27;t freak you out.

SKIP FOR NOW IF

Instructions

NOTE: Do your recaps out loud in the beginning to keep yourself honest about the quality and completeness of the recaps. Transition to silent recaps when you feel like you&#x27;re ready.

Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1*. Do it just like LR.

This means all the details are included just like they would be on LR.

Quip after every Translation. 

This standard applies to every paragraph you ever translate on RC.

Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the FULL TRANSLATION of paragraph 1 silently.

The recap is not a summary, gist, or main point.

The recap should not omit names, dates, and lists.

The recap should not be a shopping list of information. It should be a story in your own words.

It is encouraged to mix up the order of the sentences and ideas.

This standard applies to every paragraph (or collection of paragraphs) you ever recap on RC.

Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the FULL TRANSLATION of paragraph 2 silently.

Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 back to back silently.

Read, translate, and quip paragraph 3. Do it just like LR.

Look away from paragraph 3 and recap the FULL TRANSLATION of paragraph 3 silently.

Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 + 3 back to back silently.

Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

SEE FULL WORKOUT DETAILS
Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine will make RC far less traumatic.

Translating the question stems one-by-one without having to worry about getting the right answer is so soothing. And then moving into Modified Inside Out, which is essentially an RC section with a bunch of spoilers, keeps your blood pressure at a totally manageable level. 

Specifying that the Modified Inside Out should be done with Decaf is not a way to punish you; it&#x27;s a way to empower you.

When you finish your Decaf and can actually say out the full recap of the passage without looking, you&#x27;re going to feel so confident heading into the questions.

That kind of confidence is addictive. You&#x27;re working toward achieving that confidence with all your other RC practice after this.

How This Should Be Done

Slow and steady wins the race. Don&#x27;t rush yourself through this Routine. If it takes whatever number of hours, it takes whatever number of hours. Who cares? You did it.

Pressuring yourself for RC speed before you feel comfortable with the section is the number one way students sabotage themselves before they get the chance to build any confidence. Take it at your pace. Don&#x27;t rush. Speed comes after accuracy.

How to Move On

If you feel less fear (but still a little bit of fear) toward RC, move on to the RC Get to Know You Routine, the next Routine in the I&#x27;m Afraid of RC Routine Progression.

If you don&#x27;t feel any fear toward RC, move on to any of the I Just Want to Focus on RC Routine Progressions.
