Instructions

Highlight any word you wouldn't bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

These highlighted words are your vocab words.

Harvest all the vocab words you've highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.

Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.

Ellen's Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This is the second Routine in the Non-Native English Speaker Routine Progression, and it replaces Translation Routine 2 in the Translation Core Routine Progression. 

If you haven't read the Why Are We Doing This section in the Non-Native Speaker Beginner Translation Routine, go read that now. This Routine is a continuation of that rationale.

How This Should Be Done

Keep up the volume of your Vocab Translation Drills and Vocab Training as you add Decaf to this Routine. Adding Decaf might make for longer days, but if you need to, you can decrease the number of Translation + CLIR Drills you're doing or the amount of Extracurricular Reading you're doing to account for the extra time.

How to Move On

When you hit the Decaf time goal silently, you can move to the Non-Native Speaker Intermediate Translation Routine.

I allow non-native English speakers to meet the Translation time goals silently, because English pronunciation tends to hold back speed. But do not let this silent standard decrease your adherence to the process. I

t's really important that you retain all of the conceptual details of each passage and each stimulus. Don't let that go just to move faster.

The whole point of this training is to train your English to LSAT level. We can meet that goal, but we can't meet it if you don't adhere to the Translation retention standard.
