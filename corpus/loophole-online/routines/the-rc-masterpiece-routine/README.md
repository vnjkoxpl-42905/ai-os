### Step 1: Cappuccino RC Translation Drill

1

#### Cappuccino RC Translation Drill

1 passage

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Cappuccino is similar to Decaf, except instead of recapping all of your Translations after finishing with each paragraph, you’ll be looking back at the text to confirm your Translations.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

3. Take a few seconds to confirm your Translation with the paragraph you just read.

   1. **In this step, you are not rereading from scratch.**

   2. You're confirming the Translation you already have is right.

   3. This style of confirming your Translation with the text applies to every confirmation you ever do on RC.
4. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

5. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

6. Take a few seconds to confirm your Translation with **all the text you've read of the passage so far.**

   1. This confirmation step will include all the paragraphs you've read so far.
7. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

8. **OPTIONAL**: Look away and recap the FULL TRANSLATION of the entire passage silently.

   1. This step is optional, but you should do it when you first begin Cappuccino RC Translation Drills and periodically afterward, just to confirm that you can.
9. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Step 2: Masterpiece

2

#### Masterpiece

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Masterpieces are multi-day workouts focused on a single section* . You can do a three-day Masterpiece or a four-day Masterpiece to start. Don't worry, you do less and less work as the Masterpiece progresses each day.

1. Complete the section in any workout style.

   1. Mark questions for hyperskip, but don't perform your hyperskip pass yet.

   2. When you're done with the section, put it aside without grading it.
2. The next day, redo each question in the section fully.

   1. When you return to the section, keep your answer selected at first, but uncross off all other answer choices.

   2. Do not just look at your previous answer without actually redoing the full question. Your objective is to decide whether you want to stick to your previous answer or choose a new answer.

   3. On each question you have three options:

      1. Lock in your previous answer.

         1. Once an answer is locked, you will not look at this question again until Camo Day. Locking is a way to signal to yourself that you know this answer is correct and do not need to look at it again.

         2. **Do not lock in more than 50% of your remaining answers on any lock-in pass** until you get to the last day of your Masterpiece.
      2. Keep your answer the same, but leave the question unlocked.

         1. Leave a question unlocked if you are not sure about whether the answer you've selected is correct.
      3. Change your answer.

         1. If you change your answer, you may not lock the question on this pass.
3. The next day, return only to your unlocked questions and redo them. Decide to lock in or change your answers, following the same procedure you did on day 2.

   1. This process repeats for however many days your Masterpiece will last.

   2. **Make sure all questions are locked in by the last day.**
4. **Optional**: On the last day, print out a fresh copy of the section and redo it straight-through.

   1. Compare the fresh section to your locked-in answers and see if there are any differences. If so, reconcile the two sections. Decide on your final answer before putting it into Camo.
5. After your last pass on the section, with all answers locked in, proceed with Camo and Wrong Answer Journaling.

### Step 3: Extracurricular Reading

3

#### Extracurricular Reading

##### Instructions

1. Every day, translate elevated reading material from outside the LSAT, using the following sources:

   1. **ScienceDaily.com**

      1. Ideal daily reading for those who feel alienated by scientific language or fear science passages in RC

      2. Be sure to choose the articles that challenge you the most, not articles that look familiar or interesting
   2. **Aeon.co**

      1. The most similar articles to LSAT RC on the internet

      2. Reading an Aeon.co article every day is a great habit if you have no specific topic-based weaknesses, but would like to make RC-style reading a habit.
   3. **ALDaily.com**

      1. Ideal daily reading for those who feel alienated by abstract language, art, humanities, and pretension.

      2. Be sure to choose the articles that look the most unfamiliar, not articles that speak to your pre-existing interests.
   4. **NYT Guide to Essential Knowledge**

      1. The single best way to fix gaps in common knowledge

      2. Here is a list of sections in the Guide that are relevant to LSAT. If any of these topics would trouble you as an RC passage, read over that section of the guide.

      3. **LSAT Relevant Guide Sections**: Architecture, Zoology, Botany, History, Art, Photography, Literature, Music (the serious parts about jazz and blues, especially), Film, Dance, Drama, Climate & Weather and Glossary of Weather Words (In Geography), Government, Law, Philosophy, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Astronomy, Geology
   5. **Crash Course**

      1. Not a written source, but Crash Course is too useful for solving common knowledge gaps to not include

      2. Watch Crash Course while you're in line, waiting for someone, on hold, brushing your teeth, etc. Any time you could be scrolling on your phone, watch Crash Course instead.

      3. **Top Five LSAT Relevant Crash Course Sections**: Intellectual Property (Ellen's favorite for LSAT students), Economics, US Government & Politics, Art History, and Philosophy

### Step 4: Vocab Training

4

#### Vocab Training

##### Instructions

1. Highlight any word you wouldn't bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

   1. These highlighted words are your vocab words.
2. Harvest all the vocab words you've highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

   1. Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template  or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

   2. Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

   3. To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.
3. Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

   1. To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

   2. 10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

There's a real-recognize-real quality to some students and the RC Masterpiece. Sometimes you just know that the RC Masterpiece's impulse control techniques were made for you (because they were ❤️‍🩹).

If nothing seems to improve your score, this Routine is for you.

#### How This Should Be Done

Since the RC Masterpiece takes different amounts of time on different days, you can contract or extend the Extracurricular Reading and Vocab Training time to fill your homework block.

For example, if you're on the third day of your RC Masterpiece and you don't actually have that many questions to complete, use that time as an opportunity to dive more deeply into your Extracurricular Reading and Vocab Training.

You can also layer this Routine with second RC Masterpiece section on a staggered start day. This means you would begin another RC Masterpiece section on day 2 or 3 of another section's RC Masterpiece. This would allow you to get more RC Masterpiece sections done throughout the week and potentially see a lot more improvement.

#### How to Move On

Move on from this Routine when your accuracy improves.

RC Masterpieces are great as temporary but intense Routines, but you don't need to stay on RC Masterpieces any longer than is necessary to move the accuracy.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: Masterpieces are multi-day workouts focused on a single section* . You can do a three-day Masterpiece or a four-day Masterpiece to start. Don't worry, you do less and less work as the Masterpiece progresses each day.

1. Complete the section in any workout style.

   1. Mark questions for hyperskip, but don't perform your hyperskip pass yet.

   2. When you're done with the section, put it aside without grading it.
2. The next day, redo each question in the section fully.

   1. When you return to the section, keep your answer selected at first, but uncross off all other answer choices.

   2. Do not just look at your previous answer without actually redoing the full question. Your objective is to decide whether you want to stick to your previous answer or choose a new answer.

   3. On each question you have three options:

      1. Lock in your previous answer.

         1. Once an answer is locked, you will not look at this question again until Camo Day. Locking is a way to signal to yourself that you know this answer is correct and do not need to look at it again.

         2. **Do not lock in more than 50% of your remaining answers on any lock-in pass** until you get to the last day of your Masterpiece.
      2. Keep your answer the same, but leave the question unlocked.

         1. Leave a question unlocked if you are not sure about whether the answer you've selected is correct.
      3. Change your answer.

         1. If you change your answer, you may not lock the question on this pass.
3. The next day, return only to your unlocked questions and redo them. Decide to lock in or change your answers, following the same procedure you did on day 2.

   1. This process repeats for however many days your Masterpiece will last.

   2. **Make sure all questions are locked in by the last day.**
4. **Optional**: On the last day, print out a fresh copy of the section and redo it straight-through.

   1. Compare the fresh section to your locked-in answers and see if there are any differences. If so, reconcile the two sections. Decide on your final answer before putting it into Camo.
5. After your last pass on the section, with all answers locked in, proceed with Camo and Wrong Answer Journaling.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

## Intuition, Not Impulse

### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

What Problem Are We Solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you&#x27;ve ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you&#x27;ve ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful. 

Impulse can&#x27;t. 

I know you think I don&#x27;t understand: &quot;Like come on, Ellen. Don&#x27;t you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?&quot; I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It&#x27;s not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem&#x27;s Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it&#x27;s also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you&#x27;re right, you can train to be fast. If you&#x27;re fast and wrong, you&#x27;ll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, &quot;Hey Ellen, why&#x27;d you do that?&quot; I&#x27;d be able to tell you.

Even if I&#x27;m not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I could tell you why I&#x27;m doing it. If you can&#x27;t say why you&#x27;re doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you&#x27;ll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

Instructions

Highlight any word you wouldn&#x27;t bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

These highlighted words are your vocab words.

Harvest all the vocab words you&#x27;ve highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.

Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

There&#x27;s a real-recognize-real quality to some students and the RC Masterpiece. Sometimes you just know that the RC Masterpiece&#x27;s impulse control techniques were made for you (because they were ❤️‍🩹).

If nothing seems to improve your score, this Routine is for you.

How This Should Be Done

Since the RC Masterpiece takes different amounts of time on different days, you can contract or extend the Extracurricular Reading and Vocab Training time to fill your homework block.

For example, if you&#x27;re on the third day of your RC Masterpiece and you don&#x27;t actually have that many questions to complete, use that time as an opportunity to dive more deeply into your Extracurricular Reading and Vocab Training.

You can also layer this Routine with second RC Masterpiece section on a staggered start day. This means you would begin another RC Masterpiece section on day 2 or 3 of another section&#x27;s RC Masterpiece. This would allow you to get more RC Masterpiece sections done throughout the week and potentially see a lot more improvement.

How to Move On

Move on from this Routine when your accuracy improves.

RC Masterpieces are great as temporary but intense Routines, but you don&#x27;t need to stay on RC Masterpieces any longer than is necessary to move the accuracy.
