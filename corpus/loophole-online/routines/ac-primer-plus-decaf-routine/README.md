## Ordered schedule, including every repeated step

### Step 1: Translation + CLIR Drill

1

#### Translation + CLIR Drill

1 section

##### Instructions

1. Start a stopwatch.

2. Read and translate the stimulus*  out loud in chunks.

3. Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

4. Continue this process on every stimulus chunk until you reach the end of stimulus.

5. Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

6. Stop the stopwatch and record your time.

### Step 2: Decaf RC Translation Drill

2

#### Decaf RC Translation Drill

≥ 1 section

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Do your recaps out loud in the beginning to keep yourself honest about the quality and completeness of the recaps. Transition to silent recaps when you feel like you're ready.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

   1. This means all the details are included just like they would be on LR.

   2. Quip after *every* Translation.

   3. This standard applies to every paragraph you ever translate on RC.
2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

   1. The recap is not a summary, gist, or main point.

   2. The recap should not omit names, dates, and lists.

   3. The recap should not be a shopping list of information. It should be a story in your own words.

   4. It is encouraged to mix up the order of the sentences and ideas.

   5. This standard applies to every paragraph (or collection of paragraphs) you ever recap on RC.
3. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

4. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

5. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 back to back silently.

6. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 3. Do it just like LR.

7. Look away from paragraph 3 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 3 silently.

8. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 + 3 back to back silently.

9. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

10. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Step 3: Answer Choice Translation Primer

3

#### Answer Choice Translation Primer

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: You should not break up the individual Answer Choice Translation sections over more than one day, but completing the Answer Choice Primer in its entirety will take 6 prep days if you do one workout per day.

1. For the first three days, complete new one section*  per day of the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill (defined below), for a total of three Written sections.

2. For the next three days, complete one new section per day of the Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drill (defined below), for a total of three Out Loud sections.

***Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drills***

1. Make a spreadsheet with two columns, or use this template :

   1. **Column 1**: Answer Choice Text

   2. **Column 2**: Translation
2. Read only the answer choices in the section.

   1. Do not read the stimulus.

   2. Do not read the question stem.
3. Write down the text of each individual answer choice in the Answer Choice Text column.

   1. Do not copy/paste this text. Type the text yourself.
4. Write down a Translation of each answer choice in the Translation column.

   1. This means read A, write the literal text of A, and write the Translation of A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

   2. Your Translation of the answer choice cannot be a copy/paste of the answer choice text nor can it be exactly the same as the answer choice text. Translation means putting the same meaning in your own words.

***Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drills***

1. Read only the answer choices in the section.

   1. Do not read the stimulus.

   2. Do not read the question stem.
2. Translate the answer choice out loud.

3. Quip the answer choice out loud.

   1. This means read A, translate A, and quip A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

### Step 4: Extracurricular Reading

4

#### Extracurricular Reading

##### Instructions

1. Every day, translate elevated reading material from outside the LSAT, using the following sources:

   1. **ScienceDaily.com**

      1. Ideal daily reading for those who feel alienated by scientific language or fear science passages in RC

      2. Be sure to choose the articles that challenge you the most, not articles that look familiar or interesting
   2. **Aeon.co**

      1. The most similar articles to LSAT RC on the internet

      2. Reading an Aeon.co article every day is a great habit if you have no specific topic-based weaknesses, but would like to make RC-style reading a habit.
   3. **ALDaily.com**

      1. Ideal daily reading for those who feel alienated by abstract language, art, humanities, and pretension.

      2. Be sure to choose the articles that look the most unfamiliar, not articles that speak to your pre-existing interests.
   4. **NYT Guide to Essential Knowledge**

      1. The single best way to fix gaps in common knowledge

      2. Here is a list of sections in the Guide that are relevant to LSAT. If any of these topics would trouble you as an RC passage, read over that section of the guide.

      3. **LSAT Relevant Guide Sections**: Architecture, Zoology, Botany, History, Art, Photography, Literature, Music (the serious parts about jazz and blues, especially), Film, Dance, Drama, Climate & Weather and Glossary of Weather Words (In Geography), Government, Law, Philosophy, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Astronomy, Geology
   5. **Crash Course**

      1. Not a written source, but Crash Course is too useful for solving common knowledge gaps to not include

      2. Watch Crash Course while you're in line, waiting for someone, on hold, brushing your teeth, etc. Any time you could be scrolling on your phone, watch Crash Course instead.

      3. **Top Five LSAT Relevant Crash Course Sections**: Intellectual Property (Ellen's favorite for LSAT students), Economics, US Government & Politics, Art History, and Philosophy

### Step 5: Vocab Training

5

#### Vocab Training

##### Instructions

1. Highlight any word you wouldn't bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

   1. These highlighted words are your vocab words.
2. Harvest all the vocab words you've highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

   1. Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template  or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

   2. Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

   3. To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.
3. Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

   1. To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

   2. 10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

The Answer Choice Primer arrives as soon as we hit the Translation + CLIR time goal to transfer our stimulus Translation skills into the answer choices.

The first half of the Primer is super tedious, so be ready. However, it's only tedious because it's stopping you from skipping over words in the answer choices.

The Primer is actually doing you a HUGE favor that you'll (hopefully) never recover from, even if it seems really annoying.

#### How This Should Be Done

Now that Translation + CLIR has moved to a warmup, lean into upping your Decaf RC Translation volume so that your RC Translation progression doesn't lag too far behind LR.

The more frequently you Decaf, the faster and easier it will become 🪄

Ideally, you are practicing 3 consecutive sections of Decaf RC Translation per day. If you don't practice Decaf every day or don't follow the Decaf instructions, it will stay difficult longer.

The Answer Choice Primer will take several days to complete.

It will feel very slow in the beginning, but make sure you're not trying to speed it up in the wrong ways. Write a real Translation in the Translation column, not just a near-copy/paste of the exact words of the answer choice.

The Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill is meant to slow you down and get you to acknowledge every word of every answer choice. That said, do not elongate filling in the spreadsheet longer than you have to.

#### How to Move On

First, complete 3 sections of the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill.

After that, complete 3 sections of the Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drill.

There is no time threshold on either drill; completing the sections is what moves you on.

Then move on to the Decaf + LR Translation Routine.

#### Instructions

1. Start a stopwatch.

2. Read and translate the stimulus*  out loud in chunks.

3. Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

4. Continue this process on every stimulus chunk until you reach the end of stimulus.

5. Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

6. Stop the stopwatch and record your time.

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

- Perform all steps of the drill out loud, and record your drills with a voice recorder.

  - You will eventually transition this process to silent, but the time goals are built around an out loud process.
- Press "record" at the very beginning, and do not end the recording until you have finished with the entire drill.

- Do not pause or stop the recording between steps or stimuli.

- No hyperskipping on Translation + CLIR Drills.

- Do not look at the clock during your Translation + CLIR Drills. Looking at the clock will make you significantly slower.

- After you're done with the whole day's Translation + CLIR Drills, **check your Translation + CLIR Drills against the CLIR Answer Key using Check Your Section**.

  - Aim for -5 or better matching the idea of your exact CLIR to the CLIR Answer Key (not just matching the stimulus type).

  - You'll find **Check Your Section** in the Review & Camo section of the sidebar. Just select your Workout as "Translation + CLIR Drill"; you can find it easily by typing "Translation" into the Workout field and selecting "Translation + CLIR Drill" in the dropdown. Then enter in the PT you used, the date, and the time. After you hit Submit, you'll get all the correct CLIRs for the section you just completed and you can compare them to the CLIRs you chose during your section.

  - The coolest part of this whole platform is that you can press X to mark the CLIR wrong and C to mark the CLIR right on each question. Then **all your wrong answers will be automatically sent to the Wrong Answer Journal and Analytics**!

### Disrupt Your Toxic Habits

#### What problem are we solving?

It's so easy to attribute a score you don't like to anything but your toxic habits. To say, "This one was just hard" or "I always have trouble with that question type." But none of those factors chose the wrong answer. You chose the wrong answer. And if you want your score to change, you have to change the toxic habits that made you choose that answer.

Toxic habits are like lifting weights with bad form – they feel like they're making lifting easier. But It's only a matter of time until you get injured. If you have bad form, you can't up your weight safely. Instead, you have to lower the weight and fix your form. (I would know... I had to have surgery to fix two hernias from bad weight-lighting form.)

The sad thing is that many toxic habits are recommended to well-meaning students who simply don't know any better. Just like the trainer who told me it was totally fine to up my weight way too fast.

If an "expert" tells you that you that only have to read the first word of most RC answers, who are you to know that's toxic?

You just started LSAT prep. You don't know any better. And you accidentally adopt a toxic habit that lowers your score.

That is the information asymmetry that keeps me grinding beyond the point of rationality. I hate that information asymmetry because it lets people take advantage of you.

Advice like that decreases your score and you have to feel the pain of score variation to figure out that it was toxic in the first place.

That's why I've created and tested a non-toxic, proven LSAT process for the past 30,000+ hours. The processes in Loophole Online are built to replace toxic habits from the ground up.

Implementing non-toxic habits will get you the answer 100 out of 100 times when you implement it as instructed. That's what this Accuracy Fix trains you to do.

### Don't Be Desperate in the Answers

#### What problem are we solving?

Sometimes you just want to get the question right so badly that every answer choice looks like an escape hatch.

"Is it right? Is it right!??

If it's right, then I can move on.

Then I won't waste any more time.

Then the stimulus must have meant this.

Then this must have been what they were looking for."

Notice the desperation dripping off this monologue.

You're looking for an answer choice to rescue you, but actually the roles need to be reversed. You have to be the strong one.

Be for real. You are better than 80% wrong.

Answer choices are designed to exploit your desire for them to be right and to prey on moments of inattention.

But you can channel your desire to get the question right into building an invulnerable fortress of analysis throughout every stage of the question. This fortress (Translation, CLIR, Mission, Back Up Plan) will allow you to see exactly why these answers are wrong without. any. drama.

The answer choices only gain manipulative power over you when you look to them to save you. But you are not the freaking damsel that answer C is here to rescue. You're the hero.

You're here to judge answer C, and you're going to judge it according to your vetted, bulletproof standards.

The answers need to impress *you*, not the other way around.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to build the fortress.

Because you are smart enough. You have the tools, the time, and the resources to never need any answer choice to save you again.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

Instructions

Start a stopwatch.

Read and translate the stimulus* out loud in chunks.

Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

Continue this process on every stimulus chunk until you reach the end of stimulus.

Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

Stop the stopwatch and record your time.

SECTIONS TO USE

Mix in Previously Seen Sections

HOW TO TIME

Count-Up Stopwatch

TIME GOAL TO MOVE ON

20 min

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Perform all steps of the drill out loud, and record your drills with a voice recorder.

You will eventually transition this process to silent, but the time goals are built around an out loud process.

Press &quot;record&quot; at the very beginning, and do not end the recording until you have finished with the entire drill.

Do not pause or stop the recording between steps or stimuli.

No hyperskipping on Translation + CLIR Drills.

Do not look at the clock during your Translation + CLIR Drills. Looking at the clock will make you significantly slower.

After you&#x27;re done with the whole day&#x27;s Translation + CLIR Drills, check your Translation + CLIR Drills against the CLIR Answer Key using Check Your Section.

Aim for -5 or better matching the idea of your exact CLIR to the CLIR Answer Key (not just matching the stimulus type).

You&#x27;ll find Check Your Section in the Review &amp; Camo section of the sidebar. Just select your Workout as &quot;Translation + CLIR Drill&quot;; you can find it easily by typing &quot;Translation&quot; into the Workout field and selecting &quot;Translation + CLIR Drill&quot; in the dropdown. Then enter in the PT you used, the date, and the time. After you hit Submit, you&#x27;ll get all the correct CLIRs for the section you just completed and you can compare them to the CLIRs you chose during your section.

The coolest part of this whole platform is that you can press X to mark the CLIR wrong and C to mark the CLIR right on each question. Then all your wrong answers will be automatically sent to the Wrong Answer Journal and Analytics!

The accommodated time goals for Translation + CLIR Drills are 25 minutes for 53-minute accommodations and 30 minutes for 70-minute accommodations.

You have a CLIR for every stimulus.

You have translated and quipped every stimulus in the section out loud.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: Do your recaps out loud in the beginning to keep yourself honest about the quality and completeness of the recaps. Transition to silent recaps when you feel like you're ready.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

   1. This means all the details are included just like they would be on LR.

   2. Quip after *every* Translation.

   3. This standard applies to every paragraph you ever translate on RC.
2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

   1. The recap is not a summary, gist, or main point.

   2. The recap should not omit names, dates, and lists.

   3. The recap should not be a shopping list of information. It should be a story in your own words.

   4. It is encouraged to mix up the order of the sentences and ideas.

   5. This standard applies to every paragraph (or collection of paragraphs) you ever recap on RC.
3. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

4. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

5. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 back to back silently.

6. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 3. Do it just like LR.

7. Look away from paragraph 3 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 3 silently.

8. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 + 3 back to back silently.

9. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

10. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: You should not break up the individual Answer Choice Translation sections over more than one day, but completing the Answer Choice Primer in its entirety will take 6 prep days if you do one workout per day.

1. For the first three days, complete new one section*  per day of the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill (defined below), for a total of three Written sections.

2. For the next three days, complete one new section per day of the Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drill (defined below), for a total of three Out Loud sections.

***Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drills***

1. Make a spreadsheet with two columns, or use this template :

   1. **Column 1**: Answer Choice Text

   2. **Column 2**: Translation
2. Read only the answer choices in the section.

   1. Do not read the stimulus.

   2. Do not read the question stem.
3. Write down the text of each individual answer choice in the Answer Choice Text column.

   1. Do not copy/paste this text. Type the text yourself.
4. Write down a Translation of each answer choice in the Translation column.

   1. This means read A, write the literal text of A, and write the Translation of A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

   2. Your Translation of the answer choice cannot be a copy/paste of the answer choice text nor can it be exactly the same as the answer choice text. Translation means putting the same meaning in your own words.

***Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drills***

1. Read only the answer choices in the section.

   1. Do not read the stimulus.

   2. Do not read the question stem.
2. Translate the answer choice out loud.

3. Quip the answer choice out loud.

   1. This means read A, translate A, and quip A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

Stop Oversimplifying
What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drills

Make a spreadsheet with two columns, or use this template:

Read only the answer choices in the section.

Do not read the stimulus.

Do not read the question stem.

Write down the text of each individual answer choice in the Answer Choice Text column.

Do not copy/paste this text. Type the text yourself.

Write down a Translation of each answer choice in the Translation column.

This means read A, write the literal text of A, and write the Translation of A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E for each question.

Your Translation of the answer choice cannot be a copy/paste of the answer choice text nor can it be exactly the same as the answer choice text. Translation means putting the same meaning in your own words.

Read only the answer choices in the section.

Do not read the stimulus.

Quip the answer choice out loud.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

### How to Pull This Off

Now that Translation + CLIR has moved to a warmup, lean into upping your Decaf RC Translation volume so that your RC Translation progression doesn't lag too far behind LR.

The more frequently you Decaf, the faster and easier it will become 🪄

Ideally, you are practicing 3 consecutive sections of Decaf RC Translation per day. If you don't practice Decaf every day or don't follow the Decaf instructions, it will stay difficult longer.

The Answer Choice Primer will take several days to complete.

It will feel very slow in the beginning, but make sure you're not trying to speed it up in the wrong ways. Write a real Translation in the Translation column, not just a near-copy/paste of the exact words of the answer choice.

The Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill is meant to slow you down and get you to acknowledge every word of every answer choice. That said, do not elongate filling in the spreadsheet longer than you have to.

### How to move on

First, complete 3 sections of the Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet Drill.

After that, complete 3 sections of the Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drill.

There is no time threshold on either drill; completing the sections is what moves you on.

Then move on to the Decaf + LR Translation Routine.

Instructions

Highlight any word you wouldn&#x27;t bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

These highlighted words are your vocab words.

Harvest all the vocab words you&#x27;ve highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.

Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.
