### Step 2: Masterpiece

2

#### Masterpiece

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Masterpieces are multi-day workouts focused on a single section* . You can do a three-day Masterpiece or a four-day Masterpiece to start. Don't worry, you do less and less work as the Masterpiece progresses each day.

1. Complete the section in any workout style.

   1. Mark questions for hyperskip, but don't perform your hyperskip pass yet.

   2. When you're done with the section, put it aside without grading it.
2. The next day, redo each question in the section fully.

   1. When you return to the section, keep your answer selected at first, but uncross off all other answer choices.

   2. Do not just look at your previous answer without actually redoing the full question. Your objective is to decide whether you want to stick to your previous answer or choose a new answer.

   3. On each question you have three options:

      1. Lock in your previous answer.

         1. Once an answer is locked, you will not look at this question again until Camo Day. Locking is a way to signal to yourself that you know this answer is correct and do not need to look at it again.

         2. **Do not lock in more than 50% of your remaining answers on any lock-in pass** until you get to the last day of your Masterpiece.
      2. Keep your answer the same, but leave the question unlocked.

         1. Leave a question unlocked if you are not sure about whether the answer you've selected is correct.
      3. Change your answer.

         1. If you change your answer, you may not lock the question on this pass.
3. The next day, return only to your unlocked questions and redo them. Decide to lock in or change your answers, following the same procedure you did on day 2.

   1. This process repeats for however many days your Masterpiece will last.

   2. **Make sure all questions are locked in by the last day.**
4. **Optional**: On the last day, print out a fresh copy of the section and redo it straight-through.

   1. Compare the fresh section to your locked-in answers and see if there are any differences. If so, reconcile the two sections. Decide on your final answer before putting it into Camo.
5. After your last pass on the section, with all answers locked in, proceed with Camo and Wrong Answer Journaling.

### Step 3: Method/Flaw/Argument Part Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet

3

#### Method/Flaw/Argument Part Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet

##### Instructions

1. Make a spreadsheet with three columns, or use this template :

   1. **Column 1**: Vexing Answer Choice

   2. **Column 2**: Answer Choice Translation

   3. **Column 3**: Corresponding Stimulus
2. Collect all the difficult answer choices to translate, focusing especially on Method/Flaw/Argument Part answer choices.

   1. This could include harvesting answer choices from your sections immediately after you're done or going back over old sections to harvest answer choices.
3. Write the difficult answers in the Vexing Answer Choice column of the spreadsheet, one answer choice per row.

4. Translate the answers in the Answer Choice Translation column of the spreadsheet.

5. In the Corresponding Stimulus column of the spreadsheet, write an example stimulus which would make the answer choice in that row correct.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

There's a real-recognize-real quality to some students and the Masterpiece. Sometimes you just know that the Masterpiece's impulse control techniques were made for you (because they were ❤️‍🩹).

If nothing seems to improve your score, this Routine is for you.

#### How This Should Be Done

Since the Masterpiece takes different amounts of time on different days, you can contract or extend the Method/Flaw/Argument Part Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet time to fill your study block.

For example, if you're on the third day of your Masterpiece and you don't have that many questions to complete, use that time as an opportunity to dive more deeply into your Method/Flaw/Argument Part Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet.

You can also layer this Routine with a second Masterpiece section on a staggered start day. This means you would begin another Masterpiece section on day 2 or 3 of the first section's Masterpiece. This would allow you to get more Masterpiece sections done throughout the week and potentially see a lot more improvement.

#### How to Move On

Move on from this Routine when your accuracy improves.

Masterpieces are great as temporary but intense Routines, but you don't need to stay on Masterpieces any longer than is necessary to move the accuracy.

### Practice Occurs Under Full Test Conditions

#### What problem are we solving?

You should not take an official LSAT until you have practiced regularly in test conditions. This means you're practicing in a quiet environment, taking exactly the allocated time you'll have on test day (no more no less), and you are not taking breaks to go on your phone or talk to people. Practicing in chaotic, unplanned conditions causes more score chaos than you'd think.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: Masterpieces are multi-day workouts focused on a single section* . You can do a three-day Masterpiece or a four-day Masterpiece to start. Don't worry, you do less and less work as the Masterpiece progresses each day.

1. Complete the section in any workout style.

   1. Mark questions for hyperskip, but don't perform your hyperskip pass yet.

   2. When you're done with the section, put it aside without grading it.
2. The next day, redo each question in the section fully.

   1. When you return to the section, keep your answer selected at first, but uncross off all other answer choices.

   2. Do not just look at your previous answer without actually redoing the full question. Your objective is to decide whether you want to stick to your previous answer or choose a new answer.

   3. On each question you have three options:

      1. Lock in your previous answer.

         1. Once an answer is locked, you will not look at this question again until Camo Day. Locking is a way to signal to yourself that you know this answer is correct and do not need to look at it again.

         2. **Do not lock in more than 50% of your remaining answers on any lock-in pass** until you get to the last day of your Masterpiece.
      2. Keep your answer the same, but leave the question unlocked.

         1. Leave a question unlocked if you are not sure about whether the answer you've selected is correct.
      3. Change your answer.

         1. If you change your answer, you may not lock the question on this pass.
3. The next day, return only to your unlocked questions and redo them. Decide to lock in or change your answers, following the same procedure you did on day 2.

   1. This process repeats for however many days your Masterpiece will last.

   2. **Make sure all questions are locked in by the last day.**
4. **Optional**: On the last day, print out a fresh copy of the section and redo it straight-through.

   1. Compare the fresh section to your locked-in answers and see if there are any differences. If so, reconcile the two sections. Decide on your final answer before putting it into Camo.
5. After your last pass on the section, with all answers locked in, proceed with Camo and Wrong Answer Journaling.

### Learn From Your Mistakes

#### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There's nothing interesting left anymore.

Now you "know where you are," but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights.

If you like the number, you'll walk away excited and think, "Okay, now I'm good."

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don't need to work *that* hard when you're good, right?

If you don't like the number, tears follow.

Now there's no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what's the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better.

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you're doing wrong.

## Intuition, Not Impulse

### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

Instructions

Make a spreadsheet with three columns, or use this template:

Column 1: Vexing Answer Choice

Column 2: Answer Choice Translation

Column 3: Corresponding Stimulus

Collect all the difficult answer choices to translate, focusing especially on Method/Flaw/Argument Part answer choices.

This could include harvesting answer choices from your sections immediately after you&#x27;re done or going back over old sections to harvest answer choices.

Write the difficult answers in the Vexing Answer Choice column of the spreadsheet, one answer choice per row.

Translate the answers in the Answer Choice Translation column of the spreadsheet.

In the Corresponding Stimulus column of the spreadsheet, write an example stimulus which would make the answer choice in that row correct.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

There&#x27;s a real-recognize-real quality to some students and the Masterpiece. Sometimes you just know that the Masterpiece&#x27;s impulse control techniques were made for you (because they were ❤️‍🩹).

If nothing seems to improve your score, this Routine is for you.

How This Should Be Done

Since the Masterpiece takes different amounts of time on different days, you can contract or extend the Method/Flaw/Argument Part Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet time to fill your study block.

For example, if you&#x27;re on the third day of your Masterpiece and you don&#x27;t have that many questions to complete, use that time as an opportunity to dive more deeply into your Method/Flaw/Argument Part Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet.

You can also layer this Routine with a second Masterpiece section on a staggered start day. This means you would begin another Masterpiece section on day 2 or 3 of the first section&#x27;s Masterpiece. This would allow you to get more Masterpiece sections done throughout the week and potentially see a lot more improvement.

How to Move On

Move on from this Routine when your accuracy improves.

Masterpieces are great as temporary but intense Routines, but you don&#x27;t need to stay on Masterpieces any longer than is necessary to move the accuracy.
