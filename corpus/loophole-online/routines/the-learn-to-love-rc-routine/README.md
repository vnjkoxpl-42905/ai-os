### Step 1: Decaf RC Translation Drill

1

#### Decaf RC Translation Drill

1 passage

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Do your recaps out loud in the beginning to keep yourself honest about the quality and completeness of the recaps. Transition to silent recaps when you feel like you're ready.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

   1. This means all the details are included just like they would be on LR.

   2. Quip after *every* Translation.

   3. This standard applies to every paragraph you ever translate on RC.
2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

   1. The recap is not a summary, gist, or main point.

   2. The recap should not omit names, dates, and lists.

   3. The recap should not be a shopping list of information. It should be a story in your own words.

   4. It is encouraged to mix up the order of the sentences and ideas.

   5. This standard applies to every paragraph (or collection of paragraphs) you ever recap on RC.
3. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

4. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

5. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 back to back silently.

6. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 3. Do it just like LR.

7. Look away from paragraph 3 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 3 silently.

8. Without looking back, recap the Translations of paragraphs 1 + 2 + 3 back to back silently.

9. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

10. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

##### Instructions

   1. Translate and quip these sentences. Add the sentences up to get an idea of what the passage is likely about.

### Step 3: Spoiled Read

3

#### Spoiled Read

##### Instructions

For each passage* :

1. Read and translate the first sentence of the passage.

2. Read and translate the last sentence of the passage.

3. Think for a second about the passage.

4. Translate the entire passage.

   1. Use whichever style of RC Translation you're currently using.
5. Answer the questions according to your typical RC question process.

***Spoiled Comparative Reads***

1. Read and translate the first and last sentences of Passage A.

2. Read and translate the first and last sentences of Passage B.

3. Think for a second about the relationships between the passages.

4. Translate both passages.

   1. Use whichever style of RC Translation you're currently using.
5. Answer the questions according to your typical RC question process.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine can hopefully make you love RC as much as I do.

To me, there is nothing more fun than Party Tricking the questions before you read the passage. (Why can't I find a way to fit Inside Out into 35 minutes? RC would be so much better if I could figure out a way to fit Inside Out into 35 minutes!)

I know that you have to get used to Party Tricking first, but hopefully, all of your work in Modified Inside Out and Light Inside Out have demystified the RC answer choices to the point where you are ready to take on this challenge.

If you really don't want a Party Trick, you can skip the Inside Out in this Routine, but try the Spoiled Read! It's a really cool Workout that drastically decreases the intimidation you feel when you read the passage and it's 35-minute compatible.

#### How This Should Be Done

You're allowed to choose whatever RC Translation Drill you want for the Inside Out and the Spoiled Read. Just make sure you're still not rushing through the section.

There are a ton of RC Party Tricking resources on this platform, so I won't belabor how to Party Trick here.

But Party Tricking takes a minute to get used to; you have to learn how to be a detective when you're first getting started with it.

Don't rush the Party Trick to the point where you're just saying "I don't know" and guessing. Take it as an opportunity to learn how to play the answers against one another while you're doing it.

Watch the RC Party Tricking demos in the Loophole Online course for inspiration.

#### How to Move On

Hopefully we've cured your RC fear because this is the last I'm Afraid of RC Routine!

If we haven't, feel free to return to the two earlier Routines in this Routine Progression.

Otherwise, move on to any of the I Just Want to Focus on RC Routine Progressions.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

### Stop Mental and Physical Self-Sabotage

#### What problem are we solving?

Have you ever done a section when:

- you're falling asleep in the middle?

- someone else is taking a call a little too close to you?

- you're looking at the clock over and over again and saying to yourself "I have to speed up"?

- you're saying to yourself, "I can't get this wrong. Oh my god, I don't understand. I have to get it. I'll never get into Berkeley. My dad is right about me."?

- you just saw a previous score and you're going to cry?

- you have a stye on your left eye and also maybe COVID?

Doing the LSAT like this is mental and physical self-sabotage.

You are willfully engaging in behaviors that decrease your score.

I care about you and I care about your score, so I'm going to give you some real talk here.

Telling yourself you can't do something drastically increases the chances you won't be able to do that thing.

So stop it.

Doing LSAT in an environment or situation that pulls on your attention sets you up to fail.

So stop it.

Thinking about the past, the future, and what other people think while you're doing the LSAT ensures that you do not do your best.

So stop it.

This is your Olympics.

We need you in peak mental and physical condition while you're training to ensure that you're in peak condition on test day. This means that you have to advocate for yourself.

Create space in your life for LSAT is to be the #1 priority, not something you do after every high priority is done.

This Accuracy Fix teaches you how to be that your advocate.

### Don't Be Desperate in the Answers

#### What problem are we solving?

Sometimes you just want to get the question right so badly that every answer choice looks like an escape hatch.

"Is it right? Is it right!??

If it's right, then I can move on.

Then I won't waste any more time.

Then the stimulus must have meant this.

Then this must have been what they were looking for."

Notice the desperation dripping off this monologue.

You're looking for an answer choice to rescue you, but actually the roles need to be reversed. You have to be the strong one.

Be for real. You are better than 80% wrong.

Answer choices are designed to exploit your desire for them to be right and to prey on moments of inattention.

But you can channel your desire to get the question right into building an invulnerable fortress of analysis throughout every stage of the question. This fortress (Translation, CLIR, Mission, Back Up Plan) will allow you to see exactly why these answers are wrong without. any. drama.

The answer choices only gain manipulative power over you when you look to them to save you. But you are not the freaking damsel that answer C is here to rescue. You're the hero.

You're here to judge answer C, and you're going to judge it according to your vetted, bulletproof standards.

The answers need to impress *you*, not the other way around.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to build the fortress.

Because you are smart enough. You have the tools, the time, and the resources to never need any answer choice to save you again.

## Stop Mental and Physical Self-Sabotage

### What problem are we solving?

Have you ever done a section when:

- you're falling asleep in the middle?

- someone else is taking a call a little too close to you?

- you're looking at the clock over and over again and saying to yourself "I have to speed up"?

- you're saying to yourself, "I can't get this wrong. Oh my god, I don't understand. I have to get it. I'll never get into Berkeley. My dad is right about me."?

- you just saw a previous score and you're going to cry?

- you have a stye on your left eye and also maybe COVID?

Doing the LSAT like this is mental and physical self-sabotage.

You are willfully engaging in behaviors that decrease your score.

I care about you and I care about your score, so I'm going to give you some real talk here.

Telling yourself you can't do something drastically increases the chances you won't be able to do that thing.

So stop it.

Doing LSAT in an environment or situation that pulls on your attention sets you up to fail.

So stop it.

Thinking about the past, the future, and what other people think while you're doing the LSAT ensures that you do not do your best.

So stop it.

This is your Olympics.

We need you in peak mental and physical condition while you're training to ensure that you're in peak condition on test day. This means that you have to advocate for yourself.

Create space in your life for LSAT is to be the #1 priority, not something you do after every high priority is done.

This Accuracy Fix teaches you how to be that your advocate.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine can hopefully make you love RC as much as I do.

To me, there is nothing more fun than Party Tricking the questions before you read the passage. (Why can&#x27;t I find a way to fit Inside Out into 35 minutes? RC would be so much better if I could figure out a way to fit Inside Out into 35 minutes!)

I know that you have to get used to Party Tricking first, but hopefully, all of your work in Modified Inside Out and Light Inside Out have demystified the RC answer choices to the point where you are ready to take on this challenge.

If you really don&#x27;t want a Party Trick, you can skip the Inside Out in this Routine, but try the Spoiled Read! It&#x27;s a really cool Workout that drastically decreases the intimidation you feel when you read the passage and it&#x27;s 35-minute compatible.

How This Should Be Done

You&#x27;re allowed to choose whatever RC Translation Drill you want for the Inside Out and the Spoiled Read. Just make sure you&#x27;re still not rushing through the section.

There are a ton of RC Party Tricking resources on this platform, so I won&#x27;t belabor how to Party Trick here.

But Party Tricking takes a minute to get used to; you have to learn how to be a detective when you&#x27;re first getting started with it.

Don&#x27;t rush the Party Trick to the point where you&#x27;re just saying &quot;I don&#x27;t know&quot; and guessing. Take it as an opportunity to learn how to play the answers against one another while you&#x27;re doing it.

Watch the RC Party Tricking demos in the Loophole Online course for inspiration.

How to Move On

Hopefully we&#x27;ve cured your RC fear because this is the last I&#x27;m Afraid of RC Routine!

If we haven&#x27;t, feel free to return to the two earlier Routines in this Routine Progression. 

Otherwise, move on to any of the I Just Want to Focus on RC Routine Progressions.
