#### Caffeinated RC Translation Drill

##### Instructions

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

2. Take a few seconds to confirm your Translation with the paragraph you just read.

   1. **In this step, you are not rereading from scratch.**

   2. You're confirming the Translation you already have is right.

   3. This style of confirming your Translation with the text applies to every confirmation you ever do on RC.
3. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

4. At the end of the passage, confirm your Translation with the entire passage. This should take 15-30 seconds.

5. **OPTIONAL**: Look away and recap the FULL TRANSLATION of the entire passage silently.

   1. This step is optional, but you should do it when you first begin Caffeinated RC Translation Drills and periodically afterward, just to confirm that you can.
6. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Step 2: Pacing Practice

2

#### Pacing Practice

1 section

##### Instructions

1. Choose an interval time goal.

   1. Your interval time goal is your target time for each question.

   2. For example, an interval time goal may be 90 seconds per question.

   3. Follow the procedures of How to Set a Realistic Interval Time Goal below.
2. Set an interval timer to repeatedly countdown from that time goal 30 times in a row.

   1. Do not incorporate rest intervals.

   2. We choose 30 times so we can have some extra intervals in case our timer messes up.
3. Start your interval timer when you begin an LR or RC section* .

   1. For RC, only use the interval timer on the questions, not during the passage.
4. You must select an answer on each question before the interval timer goes off.

   1. You cannot stay on a question longer than the interval timer permits, no matter how badly you want to.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

Thunderdomes and Pacing Practice go well together because Thunderdomes give you depth, breadth, and agility while Pacing Practice keeps you moving. For maintaining accuracy, it's helpful to mix Workouts that naturally run at different paces. If you always go fast, you can start to get sloppy and the accuracy drops. If you always go slow, you'll get distracted and lazy, which will also cause the accuracy to drop. This Routine Progression balances those tendencies.

#### How This Should Be Done

Approach this Routine like contrast therapy. Each workout exercises two distinct LSAT skillsets, so you want to put your full attention into growing your skills as much as you can in each Workout, even though you aren't getting a score on the Thunderdome.

You can switch the Thunderdome between LR and RC. Ideally, you're completing a full section of the Thunderdome, but I wouldn't do less than 10 Thunderdome questions at a time in this Routine, since you're completing ~50 questions in the Pacing Practice sections, you don't want the Thunderdome practice to be imbalanced.

#### How to Move On

If the Pacing Practice is working to reduce the time gradually while building accuracy with the Thunderdome, move to The Fast and Slow Routine 2 after you feel comfortable taking on the challenge of completing each Thunderdome output within 30 seconds. The Pacing Practice should help with this goal.

If the Pacing Practice is not reducing time as quickly as you would like, you can always switch the Pacing Practice to Light Failsafes or just move to the Speed Core Routines. They will bring the time down more dramatically.

If you're within two weeks of your target test date, you shouldn't spend too long on this Routine. It works as a way to introduce you to the timed environment, while still building accuracy skills with the Thunderdome, but you'll need the blunt force of Marathon Routines to bring the section time down by your test date.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

#### Instructions

1. Choose an interval time goal.

   1. Your interval time goal is your target time for each question.

   2. For example, an interval time goal may be 90 seconds per question.

   3. Follow the procedures of How to Set a Realistic Interval Time Goal below.
2. Set an interval timer to repeatedly countdown from that time goal 30 times in a row.

   1. Do not incorporate rest intervals.

   2. We choose 30 times so we can have some extra intervals in case our timer messes up.
3. Start your interval timer when you begin an LR or RC section* .

   1. For RC, only use the interval timer on the questions, not during the passage.
4. You must select an answer on each question before the interval timer goes off.

   1. You cannot stay on a question longer than the interval timer permits, no matter how badly you want to.

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

- Pacing Practice trains you to pace yourself.

  - It is an ideal workout if you find yourself over-investing in a few questions throughout the section and, in doing so, causing yourself to run out of time.
- You can use Pacing Practice within a test-day timed section just to ensure that you're moving on when you should, or you can use Pacing Practice on stopwatch-timed sections to gradually introduce timing in a controlled environment.

  - It's a versatile workout with many applications, so enjoy adapting it to your needs.

##### Timing Tips

***To Set a Realistic Interval Time Goal***

- Start with your natural pace and divide that natural pace by the number of questions in a section. This result is your interval time per question.

  - Your natural pace is how long it takes you to complete a section at goal accuracy without worrying about the time.

  - For instance, if your natural pace is 50 minutes per section and you divide that by the number of questions in a given LR section (~25 questions), you would have an interval time goal of 2 minutes per question.
- If your natural pace time is above your test-day time, gradually lower your interval time by 3-10 seconds after every Pacing Practice that meets your accuracy goal

  - Keep lowering the interval time until your interval time goes below your test-day timing.

#### How to Pull This Off

Failsafe Routines work best when combined with other Routines to bring time down while maintaining accuracy.

Essentially, you have to address the reasons why the Failsafe breaks in order to get it to progress, so you'll want to consider alternating Failsafe Routines with the Accuracy Fix Routines that pertain to your mistakes.

#### How to move on

Timing Failsafe Routines can get you to test-day goal time and accuracy without progressing to any other Speed Routine.

However, if you have lost every Timing Failsafe you've attempted for three consecutive prep days, here are a few options for what you can try next:

1. Set a higher baseline natural pace to start your Timing Failsafe

2. Replace the Timing Failsafe in this Routine with a Light Failsafe

3. Replace the Timing Failsafe in this Routine with Pacing Practice

4. Return to the Accuracy Fix Routines

If you're hitting your goal time and accuracy in the majority of your sections, take a Practice Test (PT).

You can move to any of the other Speed Routines, if your Timing Failsafe Routine went well. You do not have to go in order.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

### Don't Be Desperate in the Answers

#### What problem are we solving?

Sometimes you just want to get the question right so badly that every answer choice looks like an escape hatch.

"Is it right? Is it right!??

If it's right, then I can move on.

Then I won't waste any more time.

Then the stimulus must have meant this.

Then this must have been what they were looking for."

Notice the desperation dripping off this monologue.

You're looking for an answer choice to rescue you, but actually the roles need to be reversed. You have to be the strong one.

Be for real. You are better than 80% wrong.

Answer choices are designed to exploit your desire for them to be right and to prey on moments of inattention.

But you can channel your desire to get the question right into building an invulnerable fortress of analysis throughout every stage of the question. This fortress (Translation, CLIR, Mission, Back Up Plan) will allow you to see exactly why these answers are wrong without. any. drama.

The answer choices only gain manipulative power over you when you look to them to save you. But you are not the freaking damsel that answer C is here to rescue. You're the hero.

You're here to judge answer C, and you're going to judge it according to your vetted, bulletproof standards.

The answers need to impress *you*, not the other way around.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to build the fortress.

Because you are smart enough. You have the tools, the time, and the resources to never need any answer choice to save you again.

Thunderdome Answer Key
PT7.S1
Q1
1. Weaken: In the first years after the invention of the printing press, people who knew how to read but couldn’t afford expensive manuscript copies began stocking up on printed books
2. Strengthen: The affordability of printed books encouraged people who were previously unable read to learn to do so
3. NA: Increased demand for printed books wasn’t the result of a single rich family hoarding copies for their personal libraries
4. SA: A dramatic increase in demand for reading materials always results from increased literacy in a population
5. Method: Assumes the correlation between two phenomena means that one of those phenomena was the result of another
6. Argument Part: The public’s demand for printed books in the first years after the invention of the printing press was many times greater than demand had been for manuscript copies. – premise that the author claims as evidence for a certain phenomenon
7. Parallel Skeleton: Before X, few purchased the expensive versions of Y. Now, Y are cheaper, and more people buy them. Therefore, more people learned how to use Y.
8. Parallel Correct Answer: Before Fluffy went to Esther the dog trainer, he rarely sat on command. Esther has a reputation for inducing obedience in even the most defiant canines. Now, Fluffy is a model pup who sits whenever he’s told. This change in Fluffy’s behavior demonstrates that he must have had wax in his ears which prevented him from hearing commands, and Esther cured it.


Q2
1. Weaken: Bevex-sweetened soft drinks are now sold in liters which contain the equivalent of six cans of the substance, and die-hard consumers usually guzzle at least five liters a day
2. Strengthen: Humans process Bevex with a unique enzyme that inhibits its carcinogenic effects, which mice lack
3. NA: People drink fewer than 25 cans of Bevex-sweetened soda a day
4. SA: No consumer of Bevex-sweetened soda drinks more than five cans a day, and all artificial sweeteners have the same effect on humans as they do on mice
5. Method: Takes for granted that nobody drinks more than a certain amount of something a day. Assumes two different species process a substance in the same way
6. Argument Part: Bevex is in fact safe for people – conclusion the argument is designed to support
7. Parallel Skeleton: X is only bad for Y in large amounts. Z would have to drink a large quantity of X-containing substance to experience the same toxic effects. Thus, X is safe for Z to consume
8. Parallel Correct Answer: Hershey’s chocolate contains a chemical compound that is poisonous to dogs, but only if it is consumed in excess. A recent study demonstrated that Dachshunds can consume up to 10 grams of the chemical before its toxic effects kick in. Great Danes would have to consume five king-size Hershey’s bars to ingest an equivalent amount. Thus, we can say confidently that Great Danes are safe to indulge in Hershey’s chocolate
Q3
1. Controversy: Whether absolutely anyone can travel around the world
2. Agreement: Airlines make it possible to travel to different places
3. Loophole for Harry: What if most people can’t afford to pay for expensive airline tickets?
4. Loophole for Judith: What if the flights that everyone can afford enable globe-trotting?
Q4
1. Weaken: The new pills contain less than a quarter of the fiber that nutritionists recommend people consume daily
2. Strengthen: The same group of nutritionists claim that supplementing one’s diet with any quantity of additional fiber will yield health benefits
3. NA: The nutritionists’ recommendation does not apply exclusively to naturally occurring fiber in foods like lentils and chia seeds
4. SA: If a dietary supplement contains at least some fiber, it’s guaranteed to satisfy the nutritionists’ recommendation
5. Method: Presupposes that a supplement containing any amount of fiber will meet the nutritionists’ recommendation
6. Argument Part: Nutritionists have recommended that people eat more fiber – premise on which the advertising campaign hinges on
7. Parallel Skeleton: Experts say consuming more X is good for you. You should buy Y because it contains some amount of X
8. Parallel Correct Answer: 9/10 veterinarians say that feeding your dog fish oil will increase the shine and thickness of its coat. The nutrition label on Kelpy Kibble indicates that it contains 2 milligrams of fish oil
Q5
1. Inference 1: One cannot have a moral duty that is usually impossible to fulfill
2. Inference 2: Moral duties apply only to actions that individuals can consciously control
3. Inference 3: For a moral duty to exist, there must be a reliable way for individuals to determine how to act in accordance with it

## Stop Oversimplifying

### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

Thunderdomes and Pacing Practice go well together because Thunderdomes give you depth, breadth, and agility while Pacing Practice keeps you moving. For maintaining accuracy, it&#x27;s helpful to mix Workouts that naturally run at different paces. If you always go fast, you can start to get sloppy and the accuracy drops. If you always go slow, you&#x27;ll get distracted and lazy, which will also cause the accuracy to drop. This Routine Progression balances those tendencies.

How This Should Be Done

Approach this Routine like contrast therapy. Each workout exercises two distinct LSAT skillsets, so you want to put your full attention into growing your skills as much as you can in each Workout, even though you aren&#x27;t getting a score on the Thunderdome.

You can switch the Thunderdome between LR and RC. Ideally, you&#x27;re completing a full section of the Thunderdome, but I wouldn&#x27;t do less than 10 Thunderdome questions at a time in this Routine, since you&#x27;re completing ~50 questions in the Pacing Practice sections, you don&#x27;t want the Thunderdome practice to be imbalanced.

How to Move On

If the Pacing Practice is working to reduce the time gradually while building accuracy with the Thunderdome, move to The Fast and Slow Routine 2 after you feel comfortable taking on the challenge of completing each Thunderdome output within 30 seconds. The Pacing Practice should help with this goal.

If the Pacing Practice is not reducing time as quickly as you would like, you can always switch the Pacing Practice to Light Failsafes or just move to the Speed Core Routines. They will bring the time down more dramatically.

If you&#x27;re within two weeks of your target test date, you shouldn&#x27;t spend too long on this Routine. It works as a way to introduce you to the timed environment, while still building accuracy skills with the Thunderdome, but you&#x27;ll need the blunt force of Marathon Routines to bring the section time down by your test date.
