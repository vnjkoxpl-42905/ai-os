Ellen's Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

The first step in raising your score is training each of your skills in isolation.

This is what the Mini-Cycle is good at because it breaks the process of answering an LR question down into its component pieces.

We need to train each of your skills in isolation to overcome bad LSAT habits that decrease the score, like skimming, scanning, and gisting. Until we get rid of those habits, the score will stay stuck.

How This Should Be Done

This Routine is intended to be paired with Beginner Accuracy Routine (Day 2) to allow for an alternating every-other-day schedule between the Mini-Cycle and the Acronym. 

This Routine is a tough one to do in 75 minutes, given that the Mini-Cycle can run long. But if you've drilled your Translation + CLIR down to 20 minutes or less, it should be possible. 

Do not check your answers the end of this Routine. Camo on a separate Camo Day.

How to Move On

When the Mini-Cycle breaks the bad habits you may have brought into prep, you can move on from this Routine.

Once you start to see improved accuracy on the Mini-Cycle and you can match that accuracy on Beginner Accuracy Routine (Day 2)'s Acronym LR Sections, you can focus on improving the accuracy through other Accuracy Drills or you can start working on bringing your time down. 

If you see decreased accuracy on the Mini-Cycle, you likely have a stamina issue and can't maintain focus all the way through the workout. If you're seeing better accuracy on the Acronym LR Section in Beginner Accuracy Routine (Day 2), stick with Beginner Accuracy Routine (Day 2) and abandon this Routine. 

If you have 53-minute accommodations or 70-minute accommodations, you do not have to move on from Mini-Cycles as your preferred LR modality since you can do a Mini-Cycle on test day.
