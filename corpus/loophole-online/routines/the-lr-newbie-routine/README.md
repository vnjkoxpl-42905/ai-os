### Step 2: Question Stem Training

2

#### Question Stem Training

##### Instructions

1. Identify all the LR question stem types and their associated Back Up Plans in a section* .

   1. For instance, after reading a Weaken question stem, say, "Weaken. Does this make the conclusion less likely to be true?"

   2. Do not look at the stimulus or answer choices.

   3. This is not the time to attempt to answer the question.
2. Go to the LSAT Question Database to check your question stem identifications.

3. On the stems you get wrong, **write down**(on your scratch paper or using this template ):

   1. The exact text of the stem you misidentified

   2. Your wrong question stem identification

   3. The correct question stem identification

   4. How you could know the correct identification next time
4. Review the stems you misidentified and how you could identify them correctly before every Question Stem Training workout.

5. Only stop Question Stem Training once you can reliably hit 100% question type and Back Up Plan identification accuracy on three sections in a row.

### Step 3: LR Straight Through

3

#### LR Straight Through

1 section

##### Instructions

1. Read and translate the stimulus in chunks* .

2. Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

3. Continue until the end of the stimulus.

4. Mark the conclusion.

5. Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

6. Read the question stem, identify the question type and whether the question is Powerful-Provable.

7. Make a Mission.

   1. Making a Mission means answering the question yourself, except on Strengthen and Weaken questions. On these questions, your Mission should be a stimulus-specific goal for the answer choice to hit.

   2. You are allowed to go back to the stimulus when you make a Mission.

   3. Don't try to match your Mission in the answer choices.
8. On every individual answer choice:

   1. Read, translate, and quip the answer choice.

   2. Investigate whether this answer choice meets the question type's Back Up Plan.

      1. If you think this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, highlight the first word of the answer.

      2. If you are not sure whether this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, leave it open.

      3. If you know this answer doesn't meet the Back-Up Plan question, cross it out.
9. Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

10. Hyperskip, if needed.

    1. Aim to hyperskip 5-7 questions per section.
11. Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

### Step 4: Method Vocab Remix

4

#### Method Vocab Remix

##### Instructions

1. Write 10 Method/Flaw/Argument Part answer choices of your own design using two random words from the Uncut Method Vocab List.

   1. Each of your answer choices should use different Uncut Method Vocab List words. No repeats.

   2. Prioritize words you know the least well.

   3. Use the hardest words.

   4. You are allowed to change the parts of speech of words on the Uncut Method Vocab List.

### Step 5: Camo Review

5

#### Camo Review

##### Instructions

1. When you finish an LR or RC section in LawHub, do not press Complete Section. Instead, write down your answers.

   1. Use Self-Paced Mode on all LR and RC sections. Never use Exam Mode.
2. At least 24 hours later, enter your answers into Camo Review.

   1. **Do not check your answers against the answer key before entering your answers into Camo Review.**
3. Camo Review will give you a list of questions to return to.

   1. This list includes all of your wrong answers and an unknown number of decoy correct answers.
4. Reattempt all of the questions in your Camo Review from scratch. Attempt to do a better job on the question now than you did the first time.

   1. Doing a better job means you should retranslate, requip, and re-CLIR the stimulus on LR or retranslate and requip the passage on RC. Retranslate every part of the question, consider every answer choice, and try to correct your process mistakes before choosing a new answer.
5. Enter all your new answers into Camo Review.

6. Camo Review will identify all your Misreads, Conceptual Gaps, Self-Doubt, and Self-Confidence answers.

7. Your Misreads, Conceptual Gaps, and Self-Doubt answers have been automatically added to your Wrong Answer Journal. Go to your Wrong Answer Journal now to immediately begin the Wrong Answer Journal process.

### Step 6: Wrong Answer Journal

6

#### Wrong Answer Journal

##### Instructions

1. After you've Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

2. For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

   1. This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.
3. Fill in your Reason Missed column

   1. In this column, write down **what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question**.

      1. Ask yourself, "Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?" Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.
   2. The first word of every entry in this column should be "I."

      1. Example: "I misidentified the question stem."

      2. This will force you to reflect on **what you did** that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.
4. Fill in your Solution column

   1. In this column, write down **what you will do differently to ensure you don't make this same mistake again**.

      1. Ask yourself, "What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?"
   2. The first words of every entry in this column should be "I will..."

      1. Example: "I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types."
   3. Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine serves as a light basic introduction to LR accuracy.

It allows you to review some objectives from the Learn and Translation Prep Stages, while solidifying your question stem identifications and making sure you're solid on Method vocab.

But this Routine will still push you forward with LR full sections to iron out your LR Full Question Process and gradually improve your performance through review.

#### How This Should Be Done

Make sure you do this Routine exactly in the order provided.

Don't skip the Translation warmup and don't put the Question Stem Training any later in the Routine.

It's important to do Question Stem Training right before your LR Straight Through Section because we want to reinforce accurate question stem identification in that full section.

#### How to Move On

Once you beat Question Stem Training and the Method Vocab Remix, you can graduate from this Routine to the other Accuracy Fix Routines.

### Practice Occurs Under Full Test Conditions

#### What problem are we solving?

You should not take an official LSAT until you have practiced regularly in test conditions. This means you're practicing in a quiet environment, taking exactly the allocated time you'll have on test day (no more no less), and you are not taking breaks to go on your phone or talk to people. Practicing in chaotic, unplanned conditions causes more score chaos than you'd think.

#### Instructions

   3. The correct question stem identification

   4. How you could know the correct identification next time

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

##### If You Want to Win

- Never Camo before doing your sections for the day. Always Camo as the last LSAT thing you do for the day.

  - If you got more than two questions back in the Camo Review, some of the questions are definitely wrong, so you must change at least some of them.

### Learn From Your Mistakes

#### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

#### Instructions

1. After you've Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

2. For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

   1. This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.
3. Fill in your Reason Missed column

   1. In this column, write down **what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question**.

      1. Ask yourself, "Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?" Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.
   2. The first word of every entry in this column should be "I."

      1. Example: "I misidentified the question stem."

      2. This will force you to reflect on **what you did** that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.
4. Fill in your Solution column

   1. In this column, write down **what you will do differently to ensure you don't make this same mistake again**.

      1. Ask yourself, "What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?"
   2. The first words of every entry in this column should be "I will..."

      1. Example: "I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types."
   3. Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

- Keep your Wrong Answer Journal entries short and sweet. One to two sentences is enough for each column.

- **Do not waste time in your Wrong Answer Journal writing down detailed explanations of why the answer you chose was wrong, and why the right answer is right in the exact context of the question.**

  - If you're writing anything that cannot be ported directly to subsequent sections, you are wasting your Wrong Answer Journal time.
- The Wrong Answer Journal gives you a chance to identify the exact **actions you took** that made you make the wrong decision. It then gives you the opportunity to decide which actions you will take in the future that will enable you to make correct decisions. This is the essence of improvement on the LSAT.

  - I want you to know why an answer is right and another is wrong, but I don't want you to stop there. I want you to start there.

  - After you know why the correct answer is correct and why the wrong answer is wrong, look more deeply at your actions and mindset in relation to the question.

  - What did you do that caused this result? And what will you do to ensure a different result next time?
- Do not rely on cheap explanations in your Reason Missed column like "silly mistake," "misread," or "rushing."

  - Dive into why you made this silly mistake, why you misread, and why you were rushing.

  - This is how you can identify the problematic actions that lead to problematic judgments and change them for the better.

### Learn From Your Mistakes

#### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There's nothing interesting left anymore.

Now you "know where you are," but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights.

If you like the number, you'll walk away excited and think, "Okay, now I'm good."

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don't need to work *that* hard when you're good, right?

If you don't like the number, tears follow.

Now there's no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what's the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better.

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you're doing wrong.

Instructions

After you&#x27;ve Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.

Fill in your Reason Missed column

In this column, write down what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question.

Ask yourself, &quot;Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?&quot; Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.

The first word of every entry in this column should be &quot;I.&quot; 

Example: &quot;I misidentified the question stem.&quot;

This will force you to reflect on what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.

Fill in your Solution column

In this column, write down what you will do differently to ensure you don&#x27;t make this same mistake again.

Ask yourself, &quot;What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?&quot;

The first words of every entry in this column should be &quot;I will...&quot; 

Example: &quot;I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types.&quot;

Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

Click here to go to your Wrong Answer Journal

VIDEO WALKTHROUGH

How to Use the Wrong Answer Journal

SECTIONS TO USE

Previously Seen Sections Only

HOW TO TIME

Untimed

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Keep your Wrong Answer Journal entries short and sweet. One to two sentences is enough for each column.

Do not waste time in your Wrong Answer Journal writing down detailed explanations of why the answer you chose was wrong, and why the right answer is right in the exact context of the question.

If you&#x27;re writing anything that cannot be ported directly to subsequent sections, you are wasting your Wrong Answer Journal time. 

The Wrong Answer Journal gives you a chance to identify the exact actions you took that made you make the wrong decision. It then gives you the opportunity to decide which actions you will take in the future that will enable you to make correct decisions. This is the essence of improvement on the LSAT.

I want you to know why an answer is right and another is wrong, but I don&#x27;t want you to stop there. I want you to start there.

After you know why the correct answer is correct and why the wrong answer is wrong, look more deeply at your actions and mindset in relation to the question.

What did you do that caused this result? And what will you do to ensure a different result next time?

Do not rely on cheap explanations in your Reason Missed column like &quot;silly mistake,&quot; &quot;misread,&quot; or &quot;rushing.&quot;

Dive into why you made this silly mistake, why you misread, and why you were rushing.

This is how you can identify the problematic actions that lead to problematic judgments and change them for the better.

Instructions

After you&#x27;ve Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.

Fill in your Reason Missed column

In this column, write down what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question.

Ask yourself, &quot;Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?&quot; Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.

The first word of every entry in this column should be &quot;I.&quot; 

Example: &quot;I misidentified the question stem.&quot;

This will force you to reflect on what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.

Fill in your Solution column

In this column, write down what you will do differently to ensure you don&#x27;t make this same mistake again.

Ask yourself, &quot;What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?&quot;

The first words of every entry in this column should be &quot;I will...&quot; 

Example: &quot;I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types.&quot;

Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine serves as a light basic introduction to LR accuracy. 

It allows you to review some objectives from the Learn and Translation Prep Stages, while solidifying your question stem identifications and making sure you&#x27;re solid on Method vocab.

But this Routine will still push you forward with LR full sections to iron out your LR Full Question Process and gradually improve your performance through review.

How This Should Be Done

Make sure you do this Routine exactly in the order provided.

Don&#x27;t skip the Translation warmup and don&#x27;t put the Question Stem Training any later in the Routine.

It&#x27;s important to do Question Stem Training right before your LR Straight Through Section because we want to reinforce accurate question stem identification in that full section.

How to Move On

Once you beat Question Stem Training and the Method Vocab Remix, you can graduate from this Routine to the other Accuracy Fix Routines.
