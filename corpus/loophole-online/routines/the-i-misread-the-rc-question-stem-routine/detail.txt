Instructions

Translate the passage*.

Proceed to the first question and translate the question stem.

Make a Mission.

Making a Mission means answering the question yourself.

Go back to the passage to enable yourself to answer the question, if needed. Only look back at the passage in the Mission stage of the question.

Don't try to match your Mission in the answer choices.

On every individual answer choice:

Read, translate and quip the answer choice.

Investigate whether this answer choice meets the standard of what the question stem is asking.

If you think this answer meets the standard of the question stem, highlight the first word of the answer.

If you are not sure whether this answer meets the standard of the question stem, leave it open.

If you know this answer does not meets the standard of the question stem, cross it out.

Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

Hyperskip, if needed.

Aim to hyperskip a total of 5-7 questions per section.

Only go back to your hyperskips at the end of the entire section, not at the end of the passage they're associated with.

You can go back to the passage during the hyperskip.

Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

Ellen's Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This is a great Intro RC Routine to improve question stem Translation and tone identification skills.

How This Should Be Done

Don't combine the RC Question Stem Translation Drill with the Tone Training Drill.

This Routine is intended to train the targeted skills one at a time before allowing you to put those skills to use in a Straight Through RC section.

Translate the question stems on one section, then go to a new section for your Tone Training, and another new section for your RC Straight Through. You can reuse the sections you used for RC Question Stem Translation and the Tone Training later in your prep.

After you've done your Tone Training, do RC Straight Through without the highlighting you practiced in Tone Training. Our goal is to get you to start noticing the author's tone without the highlighting, since we don't want you to be reliant on highlighting tonal indicators on test day.

I tend to not assign Tone Training for very long just because I don't want students to become overly reliant on keywords to sense the tone.

You can think of Tone Training as a step-up toward naturally feeling the author's mood in the passage, but we pair it with the RC Straight Through section so that you can put those skills into action fast without getting married to the highlighting.

How to Move On

You can move on when you no longer feel any hesitation or doubt about the author's tone and are no longer missing questions due to question stem misreads.
