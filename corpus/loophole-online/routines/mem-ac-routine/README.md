### Step 1: Translation + CLIR Drill

#### Translation + CLIR Drill

1 section

##### Instructions

1. Start a stopwatch.

2. Read and translate the stimulus*  out loud in chunks.

3. Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

4. Continue this process on every stimulus chunk until you reach the end of stimulus.

5. Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

6. Stop the stopwatch and record your time.

#### MemAC (Memory Answer Choice Translation)

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: MemAC is a multi-day series of workouts using previously-seen LR and RC sections that progress through five levels as you meet certain benchmarks. Each level and benchmark is defined below. MemAC can be completed as a full section*  or by completing as much MemAC as possible in 1-3 hour blocks.

1. Start MemAC at Level 1, or from wherever you left off previously.

   1. Level 1 MemAC means that you will be performing the answer choice Translation process defined in the Level 1 instructions below. If you are on a later Level, follow the corresponding instructions below.
2. **To Level Up**: Complete the full MemAC process defined in your Level instructions without having to peek at the answer choices to accurately remember them on 10 questions in a row.

   1. For example, if you correctly recap all the answer choices on 10 questions in a row without having to peek on Level 1, move to Level 2 on the next question.
3. Each time you complete one full section of MemAC, complete a **MemAC Live Check-In Question**(defined below)**.**

***MemAC Live Check-In Question***

1. Complete a full LR question according to the Full LR Process.

   1. Just translate and quip the answers normally. Do not do MemAC.
2. After choosing your answer, attempt to translate all the answer choices from memory.

3. Write down how many answers you're able to translate from memory on each Live Check-In Question.

4. Compare the number of answers you're able to translate from memory across all your Live Check-In Questions to measure your progress.

***MemAC Level 1: One answer at a time***

***When you beat Level 1, progress to Level 2.***

1. Read, translate, and quip answer choice A.

2. Repeat the Translation of A from memory.

6. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C from memory.

9. Read, translate, and quip answer choice E.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

Besides Translation + CLIR Drills, MemAC is responsible for more score improvement amongst my tutoring students than any other Workout.

When you ping-pong between two answers, trying to decide between them, you will often reread each answer, hoping something clicks this time.

Then you'll reread the other answer, hoping something clicks again. And then back to the answer you just left. And then back to the other answer. This sucks, right?

You only cycle read like this because you can't keep both answers in your working memory.

Think of your working memory like a basket.

Most people have a basket the size of an apple. This means that if they want to place something the size of a watermelon in their basket, they must compress the watermelon to make it fit in the basket.

This working memory scarcity makes you want to cut information down to just its barest "most important" components. Because that's what you have room for.

It is the impulse behind the gist. Because if you don't cut that watermelon down, you don't see how it will fit in your basket.

But if your basket were the size of a car, you wouldn't care about the size of the watermelon, right?

MemAC takes your working memory basket from the size of an apple to the size of a car. That's why we do it.

#### How This Should Be Done

Now that you've beaten Decaf, you have the skills to take on MemAC, if you're up for the challenge.

You can choose to take on MemAC now or later in your prep if the need arises; it can be a game-changer for persistent accuracy problems.

If you have less than a month left until a non-negotiable test date, you do not have time to do MemAC right now.

Since you are suspending all other workouts besides the Translation + CLIR warmup and MemAC, you are expected to devote 100% of your time to MemAC to accelerate your progress through the workout.

MemAC is by far the most challenging in the beginning, so if you extend out the beginning stage of MemAC by only doing it intermittently, you are predisposing yourself to unnecessarily crashing out.

MemAC will become significantly easier within one week, if you do it with full focus for at least an hour each day.

If you want to make MemAC easier faster, do more than one hour at a time without breaks. It's not worth doing one MemAC question here or there.

#### How to Move On

Once you beat MemAC Level 5 (🤩🏆🤯), move on to the Cappuccino + LR Translation Routine. You have the option of moving MemAC to a warm up to keep yourself sharp.

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

- Perform all steps of the drill out loud, and record your drills with a voice recorder.

  - You will eventually transition this process to silent, but the time goals are built around an out loud process.
- Press "record" at the very beginning, and do not end the recording until you have finished with the entire drill.

- Do not pause or stop the recording between steps or stimuli.

- No hyperskipping on Translation + CLIR Drills.

- Do not look at the clock during your Translation + CLIR Drills. Looking at the clock will make you significantly slower.

- After you're done with the whole day's Translation + CLIR Drills, **check your Translation + CLIR Drills against the CLIR Answer Key using Check Your Section**.

  - Aim for -5 or better matching the idea of your exact CLIR to the CLIR Answer Key (not just matching the stimulus type).

  - You'll find **Check Your Section** in the Review & Camo section of the sidebar. Just select your Workout as "Translation + CLIR Drill"; you can find it easily by typing "Translation" into the Workout field and selecting "Translation + CLIR Drill" in the dropdown. Then enter in the PT you used, the date, and the time. After you hit Submit, you'll get all the correct CLIRs for the section you just completed and you can compare them to the CLIRs you chose during your section.

  - The coolest part of this whole platform is that you can press X to mark the CLIR wrong and C to mark the CLIR right on each question. Then **all your wrong answers will be automatically sent to the Wrong Answer Journal and Analytics**!

- **The time goal for Translation + CLIR Drills is 20 minutes.**

- The accommodated time goals for Translation + CLIR Drills are 25 minutes for 53-minute accommodations and 30 minutes for 70-minute accommodations.

  - You have a CLIR for every stimulus.

### Disrupt Your Toxic Habits

#### What problem are we solving?

It's so easy to attribute a score you don't like to anything but your toxic habits. To say, "This one was just hard" or "I always have trouble with that question type." But none of those factors chose the wrong answer. You chose the wrong answer. And if you want your score to change, you have to change the toxic habits that made you choose that answer.

Toxic habits are like lifting weights with bad form – they feel like they're making lifting easier. But It's only a matter of time until you get injured. If you have bad form, you can't up your weight safely. Instead, you have to lower the weight and fix your form. (I would know... I had to have surgery to fix two hernias from bad weight-lighting form.)

The sad thing is that many toxic habits are recommended to well-meaning students who simply don't know any better. Just like the trainer who told me it was totally fine to up my weight way too fast.

If an "expert" tells you that you that only have to read the first word of most RC answers, who are you to know that's toxic?

You just started LSAT prep. You don't know any better. And you accidentally adopt a toxic habit that lowers your score.

That is the information asymmetry that keeps me grinding beyond the point of rationality. I hate that information asymmetry because it lets people take advantage of you.

Advice like that decreases your score and you have to feel the pain of score variation to figure out that it was toxic in the first place.

That's why I've created and tested a non-toxic, proven LSAT process for the past 30,000+ hours. The processes in Loophole Online are built to replace toxic habits from the ground up.

Implementing non-toxic habits will get you the answer 100 out of 100 times when you implement it as instructed. That's what this Accuracy Fix trains you to do.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

What Problem Are We Solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you&#x27;ve ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you&#x27;ve ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful. 

Impulse can&#x27;t. 

I know you think I don&#x27;t understand: &quot;Like come on, Ellen. Don&#x27;t you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?&quot; I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It&#x27;s not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem&#x27;s Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it&#x27;s also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you&#x27;re right, you can train to be fast. If you&#x27;re fast and wrong, you&#x27;ll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, &quot;Hey Ellen, why&#x27;d you do that?&quot; I&#x27;d be able to tell you.

Even if I&#x27;m not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I could tell you why I&#x27;m doing it. If you can&#x27;t say why you&#x27;re doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you&#x27;ll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

Yeah, that&#x27;s not supposed to happen.

That&#x27;s something to train out of you.

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you &quot;just know&quot; the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can&#x27;t imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

That&#x27;s the problem what we&#x27;re here to solve.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: MemAC is a multi-day series of workouts using previously-seen LR and RC sections that progress through five levels as you meet certain benchmarks. Each level and benchmark is defined below. MemAC can be completed as a full section*  or by completing as much MemAC as possible in 1-3 hour blocks.

1. Start MemAC at Level 1, or from wherever you left off previously.

   1. Level 1 MemAC means that you will be performing the answer choice Translation process defined in the Level 1 instructions below. If you are on a later Level, follow the corresponding instructions below.
2. **To Level Up**: Complete the full MemAC process defined in your Level instructions without having to peek at the answer choices to accurately remember them on 10 questions in a row.

   1. For example, if you correctly recap all the answer choices on 10 questions in a row without having to peek on Level 1, move to Level 2 on the next question.
3. Each time you complete one full section of MemAC, complete a **MemAC Live Check-In Question**(defined below)**.**

***MemAC Live Check-In Question***

1. Complete a full LR question according to the Full LR Process.

   1. Just translate and quip the answers normally. Do not do MemAC.
2. After choosing your answer, attempt to translate all the answer choices from memory.

3. Write down how many answers you're able to translate from memory on each Live Check-In Question.

4. Compare the number of answers you're able to translate from memory across all your Live Check-In Questions to measure your progress.

***MemAC Level 1: One answer at a time***

***When you beat Level 1, progress to Level 2.***

1. Read, translate, and quip answer choice A.

2. Repeat the Translation of A from memory.

3. Read, translate, and quip answer choice B.

4. Repeat the Translation of A and B from memory.

5. Read, translate, and quip answer choice C.

6. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C from memory.

7. Read, translate, and quip answer choice D.

8. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D from memory.

9. Read, translate, and quip answer choice E.

10. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D, E from memory.

***MemAC Level 2: Two answers at a time***

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

## MemAC Routine

### Why are we doing this?

Besides Translation + CLIR Drills, MemAC is responsible for more score improvement amongst my tutoring students than any other Workout.

When you ping-pong between two answers, trying to decide between them, you will often reread each answer, hoping something clicks this time.

Then you'll reread the other answer, hoping something clicks again. And then back to the answer you just left. And then back to the other answer. This sucks, right?

You only cycle read like this because you can't keep both answers in your working memory.

Think of your working memory like a basket.

Most people have a basket the size of an apple. This means that if they want to place something the size of a watermelon in their basket, they must compress the watermelon to make it fit in the basket.

This working memory scarcity makes you want to cut information down to just its barest "most important" components. Because that's what you have room for.

It is the impulse behind the gist. Because if you don't cut that watermelon down, you don't see how it will fit in your basket.

But if your basket were the size of a car, you wouldn't care about the size of the watermelon, right?

MemAC takes your working memory basket from the size of an apple to the size of a car. That's why we do it.

### How to Pull This Off

Now that you've beaten Decaf, you have the skills to take on MemAC, if you're up for the challenge.

You can choose to take on MemAC now or later in your prep if the need arises; it can be a game-changer for persistent accuracy problems.

If you have less than a month left until a non-negotiable test date, you do not have time to do MemAC right now.

Since you are suspending all other workouts besides the Translation + CLIR warmup and MemAC, you are expected to devote 100% of your time to MemAC to accelerate your progress through the workout.

MemAC is by far the most challenging in the beginning, so if you extend out the beginning stage of MemAC by only doing it intermittently, you are predisposing yourself to unnecessarily crashing out.

Think of your working memory like a basket.

This working memory scarcity makes you want to cut information down to just its barest &quot;most important&quot; components. Because that&#x27;s what you have room for.

It is the impulse behind the gist. Because if you don&#x27;t cut that watermelon down, you don&#x27;t see how it will fit in your basket.

But if your basket were the size of a car, you wouldn&#x27;t care about the size of the watermelon, right?

MemAC takes your working memory basket from the size of an apple to the size of a car. That&#x27;s why we do it.

Instructions

NOTE: MemAC is a multi-day series of workouts using previously-seen LR and RC sections that progress through five levels as you meet certain benchmarks. Each level and benchmark is defined below. MemAC can be completed as a full section* or by completing as much MemAC as possible in 1-3 hour blocks.

Start MemAC at Level 1, or from wherever you left off previously.

Level 1 MemAC means that you will be performing the answer choice Translation process defined in the Level 1 instructions below. If you are on a later Level, follow the corresponding instructions below.

To Level Up: Complete the full MemAC process defined in your Level instructions without having to peek at the answer choices to accurately remember them on 10 questions in a row.

For example, if you correctly recap all the answer choices on 10 questions in a row without having to peek on Level 1, move to Level 2 on the next question.

Each time you complete one full section of MemAC, complete a MemAC Live Check-In Question(defined below).

MemAC Live Check-In Question

Complete a full LR question according to the Full LR Process. 

Just translate and quip the answers normally. Do not do MemAC.

After choosing your answer, attempt to translate all the answer choices from memory. 

Write down how many answers you&#x27;re able to translate from memory on each Live Check-In Question.

Compare the number of answers you&#x27;re able to translate from memory across all your Live Check-In Questions to measure your progress.
