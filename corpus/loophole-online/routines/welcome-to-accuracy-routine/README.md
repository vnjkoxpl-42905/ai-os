### Step 2: RC Straight Through

2

#### RC Straight Through

#### Inside Out

1 section

Alternate

##### Instructions

RC Straight Through Inside Out

1. Translate the passage* .

2. Proceed to the first question and translate the question stem.

3. Make a Mission.

   1. Making a Mission means answering the question yourself.

   2. Go back to the passage to enable yourself to answer the question, if needed. Only look back at the passage in the Mission stage of the question.

   3. Don't try to match your Mission in the answer choices.
4. On every individual answer choice:

   1. Read, translate and quip the answer choice.

   2. Investigate whether this answer choice meets the standard of what the question stem is asking.

      1. If you think this answer meets the standard of the question stem, highlight the first word of the answer.

      2. If you are not sure whether this answer meets the standard of the question stem, leave it open.

      3. If you know this answer does not meets the standard of the question stem, cross it out.
5. Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

6. Hyperskip, if needed.

   1. Aim to hyperskip a total of 5-7 questions per section.

   2. Only go back to your hyperskips at the end of the entire section, not at the end of the passage they're associated with.

   3. You can go back to the passage during the hyperskip.
7. Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

### Step 4: Extracurricular Reading

4

#### Extracurricular Reading

##### Instructions

1. Every day, translate elevated reading material from outside the LSAT, using the following sources:

   1. **ScienceDaily.com**

      1. Ideal daily reading for those who feel alienated by scientific language or fear science passages in RC

      2. Be sure to choose the articles that challenge you the most, not articles that look familiar or interesting
   2. **Aeon.co**

      1. The most similar articles to LSAT RC on the internet

      2. Reading an Aeon.co article every day is a great habit if you have no specific topic-based weaknesses, but would like to make RC-style reading a habit.
   3. **ALDaily.com**

      1. Ideal daily reading for those who feel alienated by abstract language, art, humanities, and pretension.

      2. Be sure to choose the articles that look the most unfamiliar, not articles that speak to your pre-existing interests.
   4. **NYT Guide to Essential Knowledge**

      1. The single best way to fix gaps in common knowledge

      2. Here is a list of sections in the Guide that are relevant to LSAT. If any of these topics would trouble you as an RC passage, read over that section of the guide.

      3. **LSAT Relevant Guide Sections**: Architecture, Zoology, Botany, History, Art, Photography, Literature, Music (the serious parts about jazz and blues, especially), Film, Dance, Drama, Climate & Weather and Glossary of Weather Words (In Geography), Government, Law, Philosophy, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Astronomy, Geology
   5. **Crash Course**

      1. Not a written source, but Crash Course is too useful for solving common knowledge gaps to not include

      2. Watch Crash Course while you're in line, waiting for someone, on hold, brushing your teeth, etc. Any time you could be scrolling on your phone, watch Crash Course instead.

      3. **Top Five LSAT Relevant Crash Course Sections**: Intellectual Property (Ellen's favorite for LSAT students), Economics, US Government & Politics, Art History, and Philosophy

### Step 5: Vocab Training

5

#### Vocab Training

##### Instructions

1. Highlight any word you wouldn't bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

   1. These highlighted words are your vocab words.
2. Harvest all the vocab words you've highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

   1. Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template  or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

   2. Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

   3. To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.
3. Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

   1. To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

   2. 10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

Your goal in this Routine (and every Accuracy Routine) is to improve your score.

This first Accuracy Routine should help you diagnose which Accuracy Fixes are relevant to you and proceed to the Routines associated with each Accuracy Fix.

Remember, the reason your score is not already at your goal is because you are making bad decisions at various points throughout the section. The question type didn't choose the wrong answer. The difficulty level didn't choose the wrong answer. You chose the wrong answer.

When you look at your wrong answers throughout the Accuracy Prep Stage, only analyze them through the lens of what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer.

Once you isolate the cause of your bad decision-making, you can choose the appropriate Accuracy Fix and begin Routines designed to address the areas causing your wrong answers, which will improve your score.

#### How This Should Be Done

Change the order of your sections each day so you do LR first one day and RC first the next.

If you want to try to fit this Routine into a 2-hour study block, be sure you pair Inside Out with the Acronym and the Mini-Cycle with Straight Through RC.

Whenever I say to "alternate" workouts in a Routine, switch which Workout you do each day. For example, if the Routine says, "Alternate: X // Y (1:1)," that means that I should do X on day 1 and then switch to doing Y the next time I do the Routine. The (1:1) numbers signal how many times you should do each Workout before switching to other Workout.

#### How to Move On

If Inside Out consistently outscores Straight Through RC, slow down your Straight Through RC and inspect exactly what Inside Out is giving you that Straight Through isn't. Your goal is to port that Inside Out accuracy to Straight Through RC, unless you have 70-minute accommodations and can stick with Inside Out on test day.

If Straight Through RC consistently outscores Inside Out, lean into Straight Through RC and omit the Inside Out from the Routine.

If the Mini-Cycles consistently outscores the Acronym, slow down your Acronym and inspect exactly what the Mini-Cycle is forcing you to do that you are omitting in the Acronym. Check off every step of the Acronym on paper until you're confident you're committing to every single step of the process.

If you see decreased accuracy on the Mini-Cycle, you likely have a stamina issue and can't maintain focus all the way through the Workout. If you're seeing better accuracy on the Acronym LR Section, lean into the Acronym and omit the Mini-Cycle from this Routine.

Use your performance on this Routine to judge which Accuracy Fixes are relevant to you. Allow those Accuracy Fixes to direct which Workouts and Routines you practice next.

### Practice Occurs Under Full Test Conditions

#### What problem are we solving?

You should not take an official LSAT until you have practiced regularly in test conditions. This means you're practicing in a quiet environment, taking exactly the allocated time you'll have on test day (no more no less), and you are not taking breaks to go on your phone or talk to people. Practicing in chaotic, unplanned conditions causes more score chaos than you'd think.

#### Instructions

1. Translate the passage* .

2. Proceed to the first question and translate the question stem.

3. Make a Mission.

   1. Making a Mission means answering the question yourself.

   2. Go back to the passage to enable yourself to answer the question, if needed. Only look back at the passage in the Mission stage of the question.

   3. Don't try to match your Mission in the answer choices.
4. On every individual answer choice:

   1. Read, translate and quip the answer choice.

   2. Investigate whether this answer choice meets the standard of what the question stem is asking.

      1. If you think this answer meets the standard of the question stem, highlight the first word of the answer.

      2. If you are not sure whether this answer meets the standard of the question stem, leave it open.

      3. If you know this answer does not meets the standard of the question stem, cross it out.
5. Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

6. Hyperskip, if needed.

   1. Aim to hyperskip a total of 5-7 questions per section.

   2. Only go back to your hyperskips at the end of the entire section, not at the end of the passage they're associated with.

   3. You can go back to the passage during the hyperskip.
7. Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: The Acronym is a way to hold yourself accountable to following the LR Full Question Process (outlined below).

1. Write the Acronym on a piece of scratch paper to keep next to you during LR sections* .

2. For each question, check off each letter as you do that step in the process.

***The Acronym***

- R - Read

- T - Translate

- Q - Quip

- C - CLIR

- Q - Question Type

- P - Powerful-Provable

- M - Mission

  - Just say what the correct answer would be according to this stimulus specifically

  - For Strengthen/Weaken, set a goal for the answer to hit
- A - Answer Choice Translation and quipping

- B - Back Up Plan

  - **How to Apply the Back Up Plan**: Talk through **why and how** the Back Up Plan standard applies to this answer using specific nouns and verbs from the stimulus

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

**Remember the LR Full Question Process:**

1. Read and translate the stimulus in chunks.

2. Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

3. Continue until end of stimulus.

4. Mark the conclusion.

5. Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

6. Read the question stem, identify the question type and whether the question is Powerful-Provable.

7. Make a Mission.

   1. Making a Mission means answering the question yourself, except on Strengthen and Weaken questions. On these questions, your Mission should be a stimulus-specific goal for the answer choice to hit.

   2. You are allowed to go back to the stimulus when you make a Mission.

   3. Don't try to match your Mission in the answer choices.
8. On every individual answer choice:

   1. Read, translate, and quip the answer choice.

   2. Investigate whether this answer choice meets the question type's Back Up Plan.

      1. If you think this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, highlight the first word of the answer.

      2. If you are not sure whether this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, leave it open.

      3. If you know this answer doesn't meet the Back-Up Plan question, cross it out.
9. Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

10. Hyperskip, if needed.

    1. Aim to hyperskip 5-7 questions per section.
11. Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

Remember the LR Full Question Process:

Read and translate the stimulus in chunks. 

Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

Continue until end of stimulus.

Mark the conclusion.

Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

Read the question stem, identify the question type and whether the question is Powerful-Provable.

Make a Mission.

Making a Mission means answering the question yourself, except on Strengthen and Weaken questions. On these questions, your Mission should be a stimulus-specific goal for the answer choice to hit.

You are allowed to go back to the stimulus when you make a Mission.

Don&#x27;t try to match your Mission in the answer choices.

On every individual answer choice:

Read, translate, and quip the answer choice.

Investigate whether this answer choice meets the question type&#x27;s Back Up Plan.

If you think this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, highlight the first word of the answer.

If you are not sure whether this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, leave it open.

If you know this answer doesn&#x27;t meet the Back-Up Plan question, cross it out.

Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

Hyperskip, if needed.

Aim to hyperskip 5-7 questions per section.

Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

Our goal is threefold:

- Expand your vocabulary

  - Harvest challenging words from your extracurricular reading and add it to them to your Vocab Tracker.

  - Practice these new words daily with or without AI.
- Familiarize you with new academic topics

  - Many student have trouble locking in on topics that they find boring or unfamiliar. Expand your common knowledge so there are fewer topics that shake you.

  - Always choose topics that you are least familiar with and least interested in.
- Improve your Translation skills

  - Your Translation skill set is one of the primary determinants of your test-day performance, but real LSAT material is limited.

  - By translating outside sources, you get the chance to improve your Translation skill without only relying on non-renewable LSAT materials.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

## Disrupt Your Toxic Habits

### What problem are we solving?

It's so easy to attribute a score you don't like to anything but your toxic habits. To say, "This one was just hard" or "I always have trouble with that question type." But none of those factors chose the wrong answer. You chose the wrong answer. And if you want your score to change, you have to change the toxic habits that made you choose that answer.

Toxic habits are like lifting weights with bad form – they feel like they're making lifting easier. But It's only a matter of time until you get injured. If you have bad form, you can't up your weight safely. Instead, you have to lower the weight and fix your form. (I would know... I had to have surgery to fix two hernias from bad weight-lighting form.)

The sad thing is that many toxic habits are recommended to well-meaning students who simply don't know any better. Just like the trainer who told me it was totally fine to up my weight way too fast.

If an "expert" tells you that you that only have to read the first word of most RC answers, who are you to know that's toxic?

You just started LSAT prep. You don't know any better. And you accidentally adopt a toxic habit that lowers your score.

That is the information asymmetry that keeps me grinding beyond the point of rationality. I hate that information asymmetry because it lets people take advantage of you.

Advice like that decreases your score and you have to feel the pain of score variation to figure out that it was toxic in the first place.

That's why I've created and tested a non-toxic, proven LSAT process for the past 30,000+ hours. The processes in Loophole Online are built to replace toxic habits from the ground up.

Implementing non-toxic habits will get you the answer 100 out of 100 times when you implement it as instructed. That's what this Accuracy Fix trains you to do.

## Intuition, Not Impulse

### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

Your goal in this Routine (and every Accuracy Routine) is to improve your score.

This first Accuracy Routine should help you diagnose which Accuracy Fixes are relevant to you and proceed to the Routines associated with each Accuracy Fix.

Remember, the reason your score is not already at your goal is because you are making bad decisions at various points throughout the section. The question type didn&#x27;t choose the wrong answer. The difficulty level didn&#x27;t choose the wrong answer. You chose the wrong answer.

When you look at your wrong answers throughout the Accuracy Prep Stage, only analyze them through the lens of what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer.

Once you isolate the cause of your bad decision-making, you can choose the appropriate Accuracy Fix and begin Routines designed to address the areas causing your wrong answers, which will improve your score.

How This Should Be Done

Change the order of your sections each day so you do LR first one day and RC first the next.

If you want to try to fit this Routine into a 2-hour study block, be sure you pair Inside Out with the Acronym and the Mini-Cycle with Straight Through RC.

Whenever I say to &quot;alternate&quot; workouts in a Routine, switch which Workout you do each day. For example, if the Routine says, &quot;Alternate: X // Y (1:1),&quot; that means that I should do X on day 1 and then switch to doing Y the next time I do the Routine. The (1:1) numbers signal how many times you should do each Workout before switching to other Workout.

How to Move On

If Inside Out consistently outscores Straight Through RC, slow down your Straight Through RC and inspect exactly what Inside Out is giving you that Straight Through isn&#x27;t. Your goal is to port that Inside Out accuracy to Straight Through RC, unless you have 70-minute accommodations and can stick with Inside Out on test day. 

If Straight Through RC consistently outscores Inside Out, lean into Straight Through RC and omit the Inside Out from the Routine. 

If the Mini-Cycles consistently outscores the Acronym, slow down your Acronym and inspect exactly what the Mini-Cycle is forcing you to do that you are omitting in the Acronym. Check off every step of the Acronym on paper until you&#x27;re confident you&#x27;re committing to every single step of the process. 

If you see decreased accuracy on the Mini-Cycle, you likely have a stamina issue and can&#x27;t maintain focus all the way through the Workout. If you&#x27;re seeing better accuracy on the Acronym LR Section, lean into the Acronym and omit the Mini-Cycle from this Routine. 

Use your performance on this Routine to judge which Accuracy Fixes are relevant to you. Allow those Accuracy Fixes to direct which Workouts and Routines you practice next.
