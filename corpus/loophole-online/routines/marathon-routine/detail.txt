Instructions

After you've Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.

Fill in your Reason Missed column

In this column, write down what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question.

Ask yourself, "Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?" Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.

The first word of every entry in this column should be "I." 

Example: "I misidentified the question stem."

This will force you to reflect on what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.

Fill in your Solution column

In this column, write down what you will do differently to ensure you don't make this same mistake again.

Ask yourself, "What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?"

The first words of every entry in this column should be "I will..." 

Example: "I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types."

Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

Ellen's Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

The Marathon Routine is the most effective Routine in the Prep Map for pure speed improvement.

No other Routine or workout will increase speed like this Routine.

The purpose of the Marathon Routine is to decrease the time it takes to get questions right by exhausting you to the point where your subconscious intuition will have to kick in to complete the questions.

This Routine will eventually enable you to finish the section in time at goal accuracy.

How This Should Be Done

Marathons have a ton of specific, crucial instructions, so make sure you internalize the Marathon timing procedures, section data to track, and advice on how to win contained in the Marathon Workout instructions.

When adding Marathon Routines to your prep, be strategic about section usage.

Since one Marathon uses several PTs, you have to stay mindful about your consumption of these non-renewable resources. It can be advisable to mix previously-seen material into some Marathons to minimize material usage.

Make sure you don't Marathon all the most recent PTs; this will leave you with no fresh exams to use as full test-conditions practice tests.

Marathon Routine Protocols

Warm up before any Marathon-style Routine so you can hit the first section with a running start.

If you don't warm up first, the Marathon will not speed you up as much because it will take you longer to build momentum.

Always Camo + Wrong Answer Journal at the end of a Marathon Routine day to avoid building up too much Camo debt.

The normal "wait at least 24 hours before you Camo" rule does not apply to Marathons.

Never interrupt a Marathon to Camo in between sections.

Camo the Marathon sections in the order of completion.

This means you should Camo the first section of your Marathon first, the second section next, and so on.

Complete the entire Camo + Wrong Answer Journal process for each Marathon section before checking any Marathon scores and before starting the Camo process on another Marathon section. Camo and Wrong Answer Journal each section one at a time.

How to Move On

Marathon Routines can get you to test-day goal time and accuracy without progressing to any other Speed Routine.

However, you should change your Speed Routine, if you notice any of the following issues in your Marathon Routine:

Your speed and accuracy on new sections have plateaued or gotten worse for three Marathons in a row

Try a Timing Failsafe Routine or return to the Accuracy Fix Routines

You don't have uninterrupted blocks of time on your calendar for Marathons and you are unwilling to adjust your calendar

Move to a Timing Failsafe Routine

Your score decreases when you switch from LR to RC or RC to LR

Move to a Mixed Section Marathon Routine

If you're still not hitting your time and accuracy goals, or you're just starting to reach your time goals while maintaining accuracy on a few sections, you probably need to do more Marathon Routines.

If you're hitting your goal time and accuracy in the majority of your sections, take a Practice Test (PT).

You can move to any of the other Speed Routines, if your Marathon Routine went well. You do not have to go in order.
