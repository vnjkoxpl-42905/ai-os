Instructions

After you've Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.

Fill in your Reason Missed column

In this column, write down what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question.

Ask yourself, "Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?" Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.

The first word of every entry in this column should be "I." 

Example: "I misidentified the question stem."

This will force you to reflect on what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.

Fill in your Solution column

In this column, write down what you will do differently to ensure you don't make this same mistake again.

Ask yourself, "What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?"

The first words of every entry in this column should be "I will..." 

Example: "I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types."

Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

Ellen's Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine is an alternate Welcome to Accuracy Routine for those who feel like they want to throw up when they think about Party Tricking.

However, this Routine has its own unique benefits too beyond the fact that it doesn't include any Party Tricking.

Starting with a Write In LR section allows us to hone our Mission skills immediately, which is a huge value-add. Write Ins force you to manually identify the question type and make a Mission rather than gisting through the question stem.

This immediately improves accuracy or gets you to confront the fact that you don't really understand what the question is asking, which is another benefit since it tells us where we need to focus our training.

Modified Inside Out allows us smoothly transition into RC. If RC intimidates you, this Routine is a great choice.

I haven't found a way to make RC more accessible than Modified Inside Out, but you can always sub Light Inside Out into this Routine to sample another attempt I've made to solve the RC intimidation problem.

How This Should Be Done

Don't try to match your Write In answer in the answer choices.

You're doing the Write In to force yourself to answer the question on your own and build analytical autonomy, not to play a cheap matching game in the answer choices.

Change the order of your sections each day so you do LR first one day and RC first the next.

Whenever I say to "alternate" workouts in a Routine, switch which Workout you do each day. For example, if the Routine says, "Alternate: X // Y (1:1)," that means that I should do X on day 1 and then switch to doing Y the next time I do the Routine. The (1:1) numbers signal how many times you should do each Workout before switching to other Workout.

How to Move On

If Modified Inside Out consistently outscores Straight Through RC, slow down your Straight Through RC and inspect exactly what Modified Inside Out is giving you that Straight Through isn't. Your goal is to port that Modified Inside Out accuracy to Straight Through RC, unless you have 70-minute accommodations and can stick with Modified Inside Out on test day. 

If Straight Through RC consistently outscores Modified Inside Out, lean into Straight Through RC and omit the Modified Inside Out from the Routine. 

Use your performance on this Routine to judge which Accuracy Fixes are relevant to you. Allow those Accuracy Fixes to direct which Workouts and Routines you practice next.
