### Step 1: Answer Choice Warmup

1

#### Answer Choice Warmup

##### Instructions

1. Open up a previously-seen LR or RC section* .

2. Translate the first four questions' answer choices in the style of a Written Answer Choice Translation Spreadsheet.

   1. Read only the answer choices for these four questions.

      1. Do not read the stimulus or the question stem.
   2. Write down each individual answer choice.

   3. Write down a Translation of each answer choice.

      1. This means read A, write the literal text of A, and write the Translation of A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E.
   4. Use the same spreadsheet and same style as you used when you completed the Answer Choice Translation Primer.
3. Translate the next four questions' answer choices in the style of an Out Loud Answer Choice Translation Drill.

   1. Read only the answer choices in the section.

      1. Do not read the stimulus.

      2. Do not read the question stem.
   2. Translate the answer choice out loud.

   3. Quip the answer choice out loud.

      1. This means read A, translate A, and quip A. Then repeat the same process on B, C, D, and E.

### Step 2: Mini-Cycle

2

#### Mini-Cycle

1 section

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Mini-Cycles involve working through full LR sections in batches of 9 questions at a time, without breaks between batches. **Do not do less than a full section*** .

1. For questions #1-3, translate and CLIR the stimulus only.

   1. Do not look at the question stem or answer choices yet.
2. For questions #4-6, Party Trick the answer choices only.

   1. Do not read the stimulus yet.
3. For questions #7-9, complete the full question according to the straight-through LR Full Question Process.

4. Return to questions #1-3 and Party Trick them.

   1. After Party Tricking #1-3, relook at the stimulus and change your answer if needed.
5. Return to questions #4-6 and translate and CLIR.

   1. Choose an answer based on your new understanding of the stimulus.

   2. Note if the answer choice differs from your original Party-Tricked choice.
6. Repeat this process with the next 9 questions, and then again with the remaining questions in the section. It’s expected that the last batch of questions is less than 9, so you won’t do as many full questions in that batch.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine purposefully messes with the answer choices to get you to wake up and stop auto-piloting your way through them.

We have some Answer Choice Translation in the warmup and some Party Tricking in the Mini-Cycle. But since the E Blackout removes one of the answers, it forces you to individually evaluate each answer choice's unique relation to the stimulus rather than acting comparatively and choosing the "best" answer.

There's always just one right answer, and its correctness is determined by its individual relationship to the stimulus, not its relationship to any other answer choice.

This Routine is built to break down these bad answer choice habits.

#### How This Should Be Done

Students are often desperate to break a plateau, but they aren't desperate to change their habits.

Take the answer choice disruption in this Routine as an opportunity to get you out of any funks you've been in.

This Routine is designed to make you re-examine the answer choices you've taken for granted.

Don't be put off by the uncertainty inherent in the E Blackout or in Party Tricking in the Mini-Cycle. This level of uncertainty is a gift to help you build your intuition.

#### How to Move On

This is a good Routine to return to intermittently when your answer choices start to get sloppy.

There's no need to move on from it permanently since it can be useful even in the week of your test to offer an answer choice tune-up.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: Mini-Cycles involve working through full LR sections in batches of 9 questions at a time, without breaks between batches. **Do not do less than a full section*** .

1. For questions #1-3, translate and CLIR the stimulus only.

   1. Do not look at the question stem or answer choices yet.
2. For questions #4-6, Party Trick the answer choices only.

   1. Do not read the stimulus yet.
3. For questions #7-9, complete the full question according to the straight-through LR Full Question Process.

4. Return to questions #1-3 and Party Trick them.

   1. After Party Tricking #1-3, relook at the stimulus and change your answer if needed.
5. Return to questions #4-6 and translate and CLIR.

   1. Choose an answer based on your new understanding of the stimulus.

   2. Note if the answer choice differs from your original Party-Tricked choice.
6. Repeat this process with the next 9 questions, and then again with the remaining questions in the section. It’s expected that the last batch of questions is less than 9, so you won’t do as many full questions in that batch.

##### If You Want to Win

- Mini-Cycles are great workouts for students with substantial previous prep experience.

  - The whole point of Party Tricking is that it makes you hone your intuition, which has discomfort attached to it in the beginning.

  - Choose an answer to the best of your Party-Tricking ability.

### Disrupt Your Toxic Habits

#### What problem are we solving?

It's so easy to attribute a score you don't like to anything but your toxic habits. To say, "This one was just hard" or "I always have trouble with that question type." But none of those factors chose the wrong answer. You chose the wrong answer. And if you want your score to change, you have to change the toxic habits that made you choose that answer.

Toxic habits are like lifting weights with bad form – they feel like they're making lifting easier. But It's only a matter of time until you get injured. If you have bad form, you can't up your weight safely. Instead, you have to lower the weight and fix your form. (I would know... I had to have surgery to fix two hernias from bad weight-lighting form.)

The sad thing is that many toxic habits are recommended to well-meaning students who simply don't know any better. Just like the trainer who told me it was totally fine to up my weight way too fast.

If an "expert" tells you that you that only have to read the first word of most RC answers, who are you to know that's toxic?

You just started LSAT prep. You don't know any better. And you accidentally adopt a toxic habit that lowers your score.

That is the information asymmetry that keeps me grinding beyond the point of rationality. I hate that information asymmetry because it lets people take advantage of you.

Advice like that decreases your score and you have to feel the pain of score variation to figure out that it was toxic in the first place.

That's why I've created and tested a non-toxic, proven LSAT process for the past 30,000+ hours. The processes in Loophole Online are built to replace toxic habits from the ground up.

Implementing non-toxic habits will get you the answer 100 out of 100 times when you implement it as instructed. That's what this Accuracy Fix trains you to do.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

What Problem Are We Solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you&#x27;ve ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you&#x27;ve ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful. 

Impulse can&#x27;t. 

I know you think I don&#x27;t understand: &quot;Like come on, Ellen. Don&#x27;t you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?&quot; I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It&#x27;s not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem&#x27;s Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it&#x27;s also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you&#x27;re right, you can train to be fast. If you&#x27;re fast and wrong, you&#x27;ll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, &quot;Hey Ellen, why&#x27;d you do that?&quot; I&#x27;d be able to tell you.

Even if I&#x27;m not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I could tell you why I&#x27;m doing it. If you can&#x27;t say why you&#x27;re doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you&#x27;ll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

Instructions

NOTE: Mini-Cycles involve working through full LR sections in batches of 9 questions at a time, without breaks between batches. Do not do less than a full section*.

For questions #1-3, translate and CLIR the stimulus only.

Do not look at the question stem or answer choices yet.

For questions #4-6, Party Trick the answer choices only.

Do not read the stimulus yet.

For questions #7-9, complete the full question according to the straight-through LR Full Question Process.

Return to questions #1-3 and Party Trick them.

After Party Tricking #1-3, relook at the stimulus and change your answer if needed.

Return to questions #4-6 and translate and CLIR.

Choose an answer based on your new understanding of the stimulus.

Note if the answer choice differs from your original Party-Tricked choice.

Repeat this process with the next 9 questions, and then again with the remaining questions in the section. It’s expected that the last batch of questions is less than 9, so you won’t do as many full questions in that batch.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Mini-Cycles are great workouts for students with substantial previous prep experience.

They will force you to ditch toxic habits because they are so intentionally disruptive.

By slowing you down with radically different processes, Mini-Cycles force you to act with intention, rather than replaying old impulses just to go fast.

This often results in a significant accuracy gain for previously plateaued test takers.

Mini-Cycles are not meant to be done in 35 minutes, so do not rush this Workout to accommodate test-day timing. 50 minutes is considered a fast Mini-Cycle.

Don&#x27;t let the fact that you are not confident in your ability to Party Trick ruin your Mini-Cycles.

No one is confident in their ability to Party Trick – it&#x27;s not a Workout where you&#x27;ll initially be sure of yourself.

The whole point of Party Tricking is that it makes you hone your intuition, which has discomfort attached to it in the beginning.

Choose an answer to the best of your Party-Tricking ability.

##### If You Want to Win

- E Blackouts are the single best answer choice analysis training workout around.

- E Blackouts one of the only effective ways I've found to stop Early Answer Lock In and E Bias. No other workout even comes close.

- E Blackouts force you to stop choosing answer comparatively.

  - On an E Blackout, you can't scan every answer and select the "best answer" by process of elimination.

  - You are forced to gauge each answer's individual relationship to the stimulus using the Back Up Plan, which results in much higher accuracy.
- E Blackouts look a lot scarier than they actually are. Give them a chance, and you'll see that they make you a better test taker.

  - Weird but a lot of students improve their score when they E Blackout.
- I know you might not believe me, but a tutoring student did E Blackout their real LSAT and got a 180. I have the receipts.

### Disrupt Your Toxic Habits

#### What problem are we solving?

It's so easy to attribute a score you don't like to anything but your toxic habits. To say, "This one was just hard" or "I always have trouble with that question type." But none of those factors chose the wrong answer. You chose the wrong answer. And if you want your score to change, you have to change the toxic habits that made you choose that answer.

Toxic habits are like lifting weights with bad form – they feel like they're making lifting easier. But It's only a matter of time until you get injured. If you have bad form, you can't up your weight safely. Instead, you have to lower the weight and fix your form. (I would know... I had to have surgery to fix two hernias from bad weight-lighting form.)

The sad thing is that many toxic habits are recommended to well-meaning students who simply don't know any better. Just like the trainer who told me it was totally fine to up my weight way too fast.

If an "expert" tells you that you that only have to read the first word of most RC answers, who are you to know that's toxic?

You just started LSAT prep. You don't know any better. And you accidentally adopt a toxic habit that lowers your score.

That is the information asymmetry that keeps me grinding beyond the point of rationality. I hate that information asymmetry because it lets people take advantage of you.

Advice like that decreases your score and you have to feel the pain of score variation to figure out that it was toxic in the first place.

That's why I've created and tested a non-toxic, proven LSAT process for the past 30,000+ hours. The processes in Loophole Online are built to replace toxic habits from the ground up.

Implementing non-toxic habits will get you the answer 100 out of 100 times when you implement it as instructed. That's what this Accuracy Fix trains you to do.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine purposefully messes with the answer choices to get you to wake up and stop auto-piloting your way through them.

We have some Answer Choice Translation in the warmup and some Party Tricking in the Mini-Cycle. But since the E Blackout removes one of the answers, it forces you to individually evaluate each answer choice&#x27;s unique relation to the stimulus rather than acting comparatively and choosing the &quot;best&quot; answer. 

There&#x27;s always just one right answer, and its correctness is determined by its individual relationship to the stimulus, not its relationship to any other answer choice.

This Routine is built to break down these bad answer choice habits.

How This Should Be Done

Students are often desperate to break a plateau, but they aren&#x27;t desperate to change their habits.

Take the answer choice disruption in this Routine as an opportunity to get you out of any funks you&#x27;ve been in.

This Routine is designed to make you re-examine the answer choices you&#x27;ve taken for granted.

Don&#x27;t be put off by the uncertainty inherent in the E Blackout or in Party Tricking in the Mini-Cycle. This level of uncertainty is a gift to help you build your intuition.

How to Move On

This is a good Routine to return to intermittently when your answer choices start to get sloppy.

There&#x27;s no need to move on from it permanently since it can be useful even in the week of your test to offer an answer choice tune-up.
