### Step 1: Unless Practice Routine

1

#### Unless Practice Routine

##### Instructions

1. Diagram three out of these four Unless statements:

   1. ~A, unless B.

   2. Unless B, A.

   3. Unless ~B, ~A.

   4. A, unless ~B.
2. Switch up which three statements you diagram every time.

3. Start every LSAT study session with this quick workout until you can diagram Unless accurately in under 5 seconds.

### Step 2: Question Stem Training

2

#### Question Stem Training

3+ sections

##### Instructions

1. Identify all the LR question stem types and their associated Back Up Plans in a section* .

   1. For instance, after reading a Weaken question stem, say, "Weaken. Does this make the conclusion less likely to be true?"

   2. Do not look at the stimulus or answer choices.

   3. This is not the time to attempt to answer the question.
2. Go to the LSAT Question Database to check your question stem identifications.

3. On the stems you get wrong, **write down**(on your scratch paper or using this template ):

   1. The exact text of the stem you misidentified

   2. Your wrong question stem identification

   3. The correct question stem identification

   4. How you could know the correct identification next time
4. Review the stems you misidentified and how you could identify them correctly before every Question Stem Training workout.

5. Only stop Question Stem Training once you can reliably hit 100% question type and Back Up Plan identification accuracy on three sections in a row.

### Step 3: Acronym LR Section

3

#### Acronym LR Section

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: The Acronym is a way to hold yourself accountable to following the LR Full Question Process (outlined below).

1. Write the Acronym on a piece of scratch paper to keep next to you during LR sections* .

2. For each question, check off each letter as you do that step in the process.

***The Acronym***

- R - Read

- T - Translate

- Q - Quip

- C - CLIR

- Q - Question Type

- P - Powerful-Provable

- M - Mission

  - Just say what the correct answer would be according to this stimulus specifically

  - For Strengthen/Weaken, set a goal for the answer to hit
- A - Answer Choice Translation and quipping

- B - Back Up Plan

  - **How to Apply the Back Up Plan**: Talk through **why and how** the Back Up Plan standard applies to this answer using specific nouns and verbs from the stimulus

### Step 4: Unofficial Diagramming Marathon

4

#### Unofficial Diagramming Marathon

##### Instructions

1. Look back at your most recently used LR sections to identify the questions that allow an opportunity for diagramming.

2. Reattempt these questions with a new mindset, one that embraces diagramming as a useful tool.

3. Try diagramming a few borderline stimuli, even if you aren't sure they should be diagrammed.

   1. This is how you learn the line between where diagramming is helpful and where it's not helpful.
4. The Unofficial Diagramming Marathon only ends when you no longer feel any hesitation to diagram, there is no fear of diagramming left, and diagramming seems boring and rote.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

This is a great Intro LR Routine to improve conditional diagramming skills.

#### How This Should Be Done

The star of this Routine is the Unofficial Diagramming Marathon, so definitely prioritize it in how you structure your time during this Routine.

If you're new to the Accuracy Prep Stage when you start this Routine, you can use your old Translation + CLIR sections for the Unofficial Diagramming Marathon. If you've never seen the answer choices on these sections, don't spoil them during the Unofficial Diagramming Marathon. Just stick to the stimuli when you look for opportunities to diagram.

If you have plenty of question-answering sections to review in the Unofficial Diagramming Marathon, that's great! Make sure to look at both the stimuli and the answer choices for opportunities to diagram on those.

#### How to Move On

You can move on when you no longer feel any hesitation or doubt in diagramming a conditional statement. And I mean that literally. Zero hesitation. And zero doubt.

If you need to do this Routine more than once to hit that standard, that's totally fine. Just keep on going with this Routine until you hit the standard.

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

- Don't simply memorize the correct diagrams. Diagram each statement fresh every time.

- Change the letters in the statements.

- You're working too hard to lose points on your real LSAT because you didn't really get Unless.

##### Timing Tips

- It shouldn't take you more than five seconds to diagram an individual Unless statement once you really understand how Unless works.

- The entire Routine shouldn't take you more than 15 seconds total once you're trained

- If the Unless Practice Routine takes you much longer than 15 seconds, it just shows how much you need to do it

- Once you hit the point where you can diagram three Unless statements in 15 seconds, you can lower your Unless Practice Routine frequency to once per week.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

##### If You Want to Win

**Remember the LR Full Question Process:**

1. Read and translate the stimulus in chunks.

2. Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

3. Continue until end of stimulus.

4. Mark the conclusion.

5. Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

6. Read the question stem, identify the question type and whether the question is Powerful-Provable.

7. Make a Mission.

   1. Making a Mission means answering the question yourself, except on Strengthen and Weaken questions. On these questions, your Mission should be a stimulus-specific goal for the answer choice to hit.

   2. You are allowed to go back to the stimulus when you make a Mission.

   3. Don't try to match your Mission in the answer choices.
8. On every individual answer choice:

   1. Read, translate, and quip the answer choice.

   2. Investigate whether this answer choice meets the question type's Back Up Plan.

      1. If you think this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, highlight the first word of the answer.

      2. If you are not sure whether this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, leave it open.

      3. If you know this answer doesn't meet the Back-Up Plan question, cross it out.
9. Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

10. Hyperskip, if needed.

    1. Aim to hyperskip 5-7 questions per section.
11. Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

### Intuition, Not Impulse

#### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

But I want you to be right more than I want you to be fast. If you're right, you can train to be fast. If you're fast and wrong, you'll have to slow down to train to be right.

No matter how quickly or intuitively I ever choose an answer, if you were to freeze time and say, "Hey Ellen, why'd you do that?" I'd be able to tell you.

Even if I'm not consciously thinking out all the words behind my decision, I *could* tell you why I'm doing it. If you can't say why you're doing something, it creates score variation, the hallmark of acting on impulse rather than purpose.

There will be a time when you'll approach the LSAT intuitively, but that intuition will be rooted in strategy. This Accuracy Fix is meant to ingrain that reliable, process-driven strategy that will mature into intuition.

You take care of the process, and the process will take care of the test.

NOTE: The Acronym is a way to hold yourself accountable to following the LR Full Question Process (outlined below).

Write the Acronym on a piece of scratch paper to keep next to you during LR sections*.

For each question, check off each letter as you do that step in the process.

The Acronym

R - Read

T - Translate

Q - Quip

C - CLIR

Q - Question Type

P - Powerful-Provable 

M - Mission

Just say what the correct answer would be according to this stimulus specifically

For Strengthen/Weaken, set a goal for the answer to hit

How to Apply the Back Up Plan: Talk through why and how the Back Up Plan standard applies to this answer using specific nouns and verbs from the stimulus

How and Why to Do an Acronym

Countdown Timer

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Remember the LR Full Question Process:

Read and translate the stimulus in chunks. 

Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

Continue until end of stimulus.

Mark the conclusion.

Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

Read the question stem, identify the question type and whether the question is Powerful-Provable.

Make a Mission.

Making a Mission means answering the question yourself, except on Strengthen and Weaken questions. On these questions, your Mission should be a stimulus-specific goal for the answer choice to hit.

You are allowed to go back to the stimulus when you make a Mission.

Don&#x27;t try to match your Mission in the answer choices.

On every individual answer choice:

Read, translate, and quip the answer choice.

Investigate whether this answer choice meets the question type&#x27;s Back Up Plan.

If you think this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, highlight the first word of the answer.

If you are not sure whether this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, leave it open.

If you know this answer doesn&#x27;t meet the Back-Up Plan question, cross it out.

Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

Hyperskip, if needed.

Aim to hyperskip 5-7 questions per section.

Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

### Learn From Your Mistakes

#### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There's nothing interesting left anymore.

Now you "know where you are," but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights.

If you like the number, you'll walk away excited and think, "Okay, now I'm good."

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don't need to work *that* hard when you're good, right?

If you don't like the number, tears follow.

Now there's no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what's the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better.

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you're doing wrong.
