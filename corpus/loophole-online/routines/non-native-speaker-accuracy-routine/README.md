### Step 1: MemAC (Memory Answer Choice Translation)

1

#### MemAC (Memory Answer Choice Translation)

5 questions

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: MemAC is a multi-day series of workouts using previously-seen LR and RC sections that progress through five levels as you meet certain benchmarks. Each level and benchmark is defined below. MemAC can be completed as a full section*  or by completing as much MemAC as possible in 1-3 hour blocks.

1. Start MemAC at Level 1, or from wherever you left off previously.

   1. Level 1 MemAC means that you will be performing the answer choice Translation process defined in the Level 1 instructions below. If you are on a later Level, follow the corresponding instructions below.
2. **To Level Up**: Complete the full MemAC process defined in your Level instructions without having to peek at the answer choices to accurately remember them on 10 questions in a row.

   1. For example, if you correctly recap all the answer choices on 10 questions in a row without having to peek on Level 1, move to Level 2 on the next question.
3. Each time you complete one full section of MemAC, complete a **MemAC Live Check-In Question**(defined below)**.**

***MemAC Live Check-In Question***

1. Complete a full LR question according to the Full LR Process.

   1. Just translate and quip the answers normally. Do not do MemAC.
2. After choosing your answer, attempt to translate all the answer choices from memory.

3. Write down how many answers you're able to translate from memory on each Live Check-In Question.

4. Compare the number of answers you're able to translate from memory across all your Live Check-In Questions to measure your progress.

***MemAC Level 1: One answer at a time***

***When you beat Level 1, progress to Level 2.***

1. Read, translate, and quip answer choice A.

2. Repeat the Translation of A from memory.

3. Read, translate, and quip answer choice B.

4. Repeat the Translation of A and B from memory.

5. Read, translate, and quip answer choice C.

6. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C from memory.

7. Read, translate, and quip answer choice D.

8. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D from memory.

9. Read, translate, and quip answer choice E.

10. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D, E from memory.

***MemAC Level 2: Two answers at a time***

***When you beat Level 2, progress to Level 3.***

1. Read, translate, and quip answer choice A.

2. Read, translate, and quip answer choice B.

3. Repeat the Translation of A and B from memory.

4. Read, translate, and quip answer choice C.

5. Read, translate, and quip answer choice D.

6. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D from memory.

7. Read, translate, and quip answer choice E.

8. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D, E from memory.

***MemAC Level 3: Three answers at a time***

***When you beat Level 3, progress to Level 4.***

1. Read, translate, and quip answer choice A.

2. Read, translate, and quip answer choice B.

3. Read, translate, and quip answer choice C.

4. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C from memory.

5. Read, translate, and quip answer choice D.

6. Read, translate, and quip answer choice E.

7. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D, E from memory.

***MemAC Level 4: Four answers at a time***

***When you beat Level 4, progress to Level 5.***

1. Read, translate, and quip answer choice A.

2. Read, translate, and quip answer choice B.

3. Read, translate, and quip answer choice C.

4. Read, translate, and quip answer choice D.

5. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D from memory.

6. Read, translate, and quip answer choice E.

7. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D, E from memory.

***MemAC Level 5: Five answers at a time***

***When you beat Level 5, congratulations! You've beaten MemAC!***

1. Read, translate, and quip answer choice A.

2. Read, translate, and quip answer choice B.

3. Read, translate, and quip answer choice C.

4. Read, translate, and quip answer choice D.

5. Read, translate, and quip answer choice E.

6. Repeat the Translation of A, B, C, D, E from memory.

### Step 2: Vocabulary Translation Drill

2

#### Vocabulary Translation Drill

1 section

##### Instructions

1. Translate and quip the passage or stimulus* .

   1. If you encounter a word you are unsure of, highlight it.
2. Finish the passage or stimulus.

   1. Be sure to **highlight ALL the words you're unsure of**.

   2. If you wouldn't bet money on the definition of the word, that means you are unsure of the definition of that word.
3. Add the highlighted words to your Vocab Tracker and write in the definitions according to the instructions outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video.

4. Restart the Vocabulary Translation Drill on the same stimulus or passage now that you know what the words mean.

5. Continue this pattern for the entire section.

### Step 3: Cappuccino RC Translation Drill

3

#### Cappuccino RC Translation Drill

1 section

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Cappuccino is similar to Decaf, except instead of recapping all of your Translations after finishing with each paragraph, you’ll be looking back at the text to confirm your Translations.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

3. Take a few seconds to confirm your Translation with the paragraph you just read.

   1. **In this step, you are not rereading from scratch.**

   2. You're confirming the Translation you already have is right.

   3. This style of confirming your Translation with the text applies to every confirmation you ever do on RC.
4. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

5. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

6. Take a few seconds to confirm your Translation with **all the text you've read of the passage so far.**

   1. This confirmation step will include all the paragraphs you've read so far.
7. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

8. **OPTIONAL**: Look away and recap the FULL TRANSLATION of the entire passage silently.

   1. This step is optional, but you should do it when you first begin Cappuccino RC Translation Drills and periodically afterward, just to confirm that you can.
9. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Step 4: Write In

4

#### Write In

1 section

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: The Write In replaces the Mission step of the Full LR Process.

1. Read and translate the stimulus in chunks* .

2. Quip each chunk of the stimulus after you translate.

3. Continue until the end of the stimulus.

4. Mark the conclusion.

5. Immediately proceed to the CLIR after your final quip of the stimulus (or the final quip is the CLIR).

6. Read the question stem, identify the question type and whether the question is Powerful-Provable.

7. **Write in a correct answer to the question.**

   1. **Make the Write In answer sound like a real answer choice, not a messy note.**
8. On every individual answer choice:

   1. Read, translate, and quip the answer choice.

   2. Investigate whether this answer choice meets the question type's Back Up Plan.

      1. If you think this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, highlight the first word of the answer.

      2. If you are not sure whether this answer meets the Back-Up Plan question, leave it open.

      3. If you know this answer doesn't meet the Back-Up Plan question, cross it out.
9. Choose amongst the open and highlighted answer choices.

10. Hyperskip, if needed.

    1. Aim to hyperskip 5-7 questions per section.
11. Use any remaining time to return to your hyperskips.

### Step 6: Vocab Training

6

#### Vocab Training

##### Instructions

1. Highlight any word you wouldn't bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

   1. These highlighted words are your vocab words.
2. Harvest all the vocab words you've highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

   1. Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template  or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

   2. Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

   3. To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.
3. Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

   1. To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

   2. 10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.

### Step 7: Camo Review

7

#### Camo Review

##### Instructions

1. When you finish an LR or RC section in LawHub, do not press Complete Section. Instead, write down your answers.

   1. Use Self-Paced Mode on all LR and RC sections. Never use Exam Mode.
2. At least 24 hours later, enter your answers into Camo Review.

   1. **Do not check your answers against the answer key before entering your answers into Camo Review.**
3. Camo Review will give you a list of questions to return to.

   1. This list includes all of your wrong answers and an unknown number of decoy correct answers.
4. Reattempt all of the questions in your Camo Review from scratch. Attempt to do a better job on the question now than you did the first time.

   1. Doing a better job means you should retranslate, requip, and re-CLIR the stimulus on LR or retranslate and requip the passage on RC. Retranslate every part of the question, consider every answer choice, and try to correct your process mistakes before choosing a new answer.
5. Enter all your new answers into Camo Review.

6. Camo Review will identify all your Misreads, Conceptual Gaps, Self-Doubt, and Self-Confidence answers.

7. Your Misreads, Conceptual Gaps, and Self-Doubt answers have been automatically added to your Wrong Answer Journal. Go to your Wrong Answer Journal now to immediately begin the Wrong Answer Journal process.

### Step 8: Wrong Answer Journal

8

#### Wrong Answer Journal

##### Instructions

1. After you've Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

2. For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

   1. This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.
3. Fill in your Reason Missed column

   1. In this column, write down **what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question**.

      1. Ask yourself, "Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?" Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.
   2. The first word of every entry in this column should be "I."

      1. Example: "I misidentified the question stem."

      2. This will force you to reflect on **what you did** that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.
4. Fill in your Solution column

   1. In this column, write down **what you will do differently to ensure you don't make this same mistake again**.

      1. Ask yourself, "What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?"
   2. The first words of every entry in this column should be "I will..."

      1. Example: "I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types."
   3. Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

We've finally beaten Translation, which means we're finally in the Accuracy Prep Stage.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

Stop Oversimplifying
What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: Cappuccino is similar to Decaf, except instead of recapping all of your Translations after finishing with each paragraph, you’ll be looking back at the text to confirm your Translations.

1. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 1* . Do it just like LR.

2. Look away from paragraph 1 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 1 silently.

3. Take a few seconds to confirm your Translation with the paragraph you just read.

   1. **In this step, you are not rereading from scratch.**

   2. You're confirming the Translation you already have is right.

   3. This style of confirming your Translation with the text applies to every confirmation you ever do on RC.
4. Read, translate, and quip paragraph 2. Do it just like LR.

5. Look away from paragraph 2 and recap the **FULL TRANSLATION** of paragraph 2 silently.

6. Take a few seconds to confirm your Translation with **all the text you've read of the passage so far.**

   1. This confirmation step will include all the paragraphs you've read so far.
7. Repeat this pattern for all remaining paragraphs.

8. **OPTIONAL**: Look away and recap the FULL TRANSLATION of the entire passage silently.

   1. This step is optional, but you should do it when you first begin Cappuccino RC Translation Drills and periodically afterward, just to confirm that you can.
9. Complete this same process on all remaining passages in the section.

### Strengthen Your Working Memory

#### What problem are we solving?

You know that feeling when your memory just goes blank in the middle of a question? And you kinda have no idea what you're supposed to do?

Yeah, that's not supposed to happen.

That's something to train out of you.

And this whole LSAT thing is going to feel so much better when you have the evidence you need to make the correct decisions in the moment.

I never knew memory was such privilege and advantage.

I used to think, "Of course *everyone* is **subconsciously and instantaneously interrelating every answer choice with the compete conceptual idea behind the stimulus.** That's the only way *anyone* would ever make a decision about an answer choice, right?"

WRONG.

This is the blindspot you have when you "just know" the answer to questions.

Choosing answers the way I just described feels so good that you can't imagine anyone would ever want to do it a different way.

But if you don't remember the complete conceptual idea behind the stimulus/passage while you're in the answer choices, you don't have the option of choosing answers this way.

That's the problem what we're here to solve.

This Accuracy Fix will train your memory to be able to enact this (much more fun and accurate) process in the answer choices.

Yes, the training is strenuous, but all good training is. And on the other side, you won't be able to believe you really chose answers without keeping the whole Translation in your working memory.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

Stop Oversimplifying
What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

### Don't Be Desperate in the Answers

#### What problem are we solving?

Sometimes you just want to get the question right so badly that every answer choice looks like an escape hatch.

"Is it right? Is it right!??

If it's right, then I can move on.

Then I won't waste any more time.

Then the stimulus must have meant this.

Then this must have been what they were looking for."

Notice the desperation dripping off this monologue.

You're looking for an answer choice to rescue you, but actually the roles need to be reversed. You have to be the strong one.

Be for real. You are better than 80% wrong.

Answer choices are designed to exploit your desire for them to be right and to prey on moments of inattention.

But you can channel your desire to get the question right into building an invulnerable fortress of analysis throughout every stage of the question. This fortress (Translation, CLIR, Mission, Back Up Plan) will allow you to see exactly why these answers are wrong without. any. drama.

The answer choices only gain manipulative power over you when you look to them to save you. But you are not the freaking damsel that answer C is here to rescue. You're the hero.

You're here to judge answer C, and you're going to judge it according to your vetted, bulletproof standards.

The answers need to impress *you*, not the other way around.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to build the fortress.

Because you are smart enough. You have the tools, the time, and the resources to never need any answer choice to save you again.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Write Ins are great workouts for forcing you to process the question stem&#x27;s ask on a deeper level.

The point is to force you to actually answer the question yourself, proving that you know the answer to the question without any suggestion.

The point of a Write In is not to match your Write In answer to the answer choices.

You don&#x27;t really have any business vetting someone else&#x27;s answers to a question if you can&#x27;t answer the question on your own, right?

Write Ins force you to hone this vital skill.

If you can design a correct answer yourself, it proves you really know what the question is asking, what the stimulus/passage entails, and how answer choices move. All of these are necessary conditions of LSAT mastery.

Writing down one word in reaction to the question stem is not a Write In.

If you struggle with answer choice language, writing your own correct answer during the Write In is a great opportunity to improve.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

Stop Oversimplifying
What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

##### If You Want to Win

- Never Camo before doing your sections for the day. Always Camo as the last LSAT thing you do for the day.

  - If you got more than two questions back in the Camo Review, some of the questions are definitely wrong, so you must change at least some of them.

  - If you Camo a section immediately after completing it, the Camo score will be worse and you will lose a valuable opportunity to improve.

### Learn From Your Mistakes

#### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There's nothing interesting left anymore.

Now you "know where you are," but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights.

If you like the number, you'll walk away excited and think, "Okay, now I'm good."

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don't need to work *that* hard when you're good, right?

If you don't like the number, tears follow.

Now there's no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what's the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better.

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you're doing wrong.

What Problem Are We Solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon. 

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to &quot;see how you&#x27;re doing,&quot; &quot;understand where you are,&quot; &quot;stay motivated,&quot; and &quot;know what to work on.&quot;

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you&#x27;re done with the section.  But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There&#x27;s nothing interesting left anymore. 

Now you &quot;know where you are,&quot; but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights. 

If you like the number, you&#x27;ll walk away excited and think, &quot;Okay, now I&#x27;m good.&quot;

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don&#x27;t need to work that hard when you&#x27;re good, right? 

If you don&#x27;t like the number, tears follow.

Now there&#x27;s no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what&#x27;s the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better. 

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you&#x27;re doing wrong.

Instructions

When you finish an LR or RC section in LawHub, do not press Complete Section. Instead, write down your answers.

Use Self-Paced Mode on all LR and RC sections. Never use Exam Mode.

At least 24 hours later, enter your answers into Camo Review.

Do not check your answers against the answer key before entering your answers into Camo Review.

Camo Review will give you a list of questions to return to.

This list includes all of your wrong answers and an unknown number of decoy correct answers.

Reattempt all of the questions in your Camo Review from scratch. Attempt to do a better job on the question now than you did the first time.

Doing a better job means you should retranslate, requip, and re-CLIR the stimulus on LR or retranslate and requip the passage on RC. Retranslate every part of the question, consider every answer choice, and try to correct your process mistakes before choosing a new answer.

Enter all your new answers into Camo Review.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Never Camo before doing your sections for the day. Always Camo as the last LSAT thing you do for the day.

If you Camo immediately before completing another section, you will be overly reactive to the result.

If the previous day&#x27;s section went well, this can make you lazy.

If it went poorly, this can make you discouraged.

It&#x27;s not worth taking the risk of either of these reactions affecting your performance on the next section.

The only point of Camo Review is to get a -0.

Do not phone in the Camo Review or rechoose the answer you originally chose out of loyalty because you aren&#x27;t sure it&#x27;s wrong.

If you got more than two questions back in the Camo Review, some of the questions are definitely wrong, so you must change at least some of them.

Do not Camo a section immediately after completing it, unless you are directed explicitly to do so as part of another Workout or Routine.

If you Camo a section immediately after completing it, the Camo score will be worse and you will lose a valuable opportunity to improve.

#### Instructions

1. After you've Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

2. For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

   1. This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.
3. Fill in your Reason Missed column

   1. In this column, write down **what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question**.

      1. Ask yourself, "Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?" Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.
   2. The first word of every entry in this column should be "I."

      1. Example: "I misidentified the question stem."

      2. This will force you to reflect on **what you did** that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.
4. Fill in your Solution column

   1. In this column, write down **what you will do differently to ensure you don't make this same mistake again**.

      1. Ask yourself, "What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?"
   2. The first words of every entry in this column should be "I will..."

      1. Example: "I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types."
   3. Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

#### Ellen's Advice

##### If You Want to Win

- Keep your Wrong Answer Journal entries short and sweet. One to two sentences is enough for each column.

- **Do not waste time in your Wrong Answer Journal writing down detailed explanations of why the answer you chose was wrong, and why the right answer is right in the exact context of the question.**

  - If you're writing anything that cannot be ported directly to subsequent sections, you are wasting your Wrong Answer Journal time.
- The Wrong Answer Journal gives you a chance to identify the exact **actions you took** that made you make the wrong decision. It then gives you the opportunity to decide which actions you will take in the future that will enable you to make correct decisions. This is the essence of improvement on the LSAT.

  - I want you to know why an answer is right and another is wrong, but I don't want you to stop there. I want you to start there.

  - After you know why the correct answer is correct and why the wrong answer is wrong, look more deeply at your actions and mindset in relation to the question.

  - What did you do that caused this result? And what will you do to ensure a different result next time?
- Do not rely on cheap explanations in your Reason Missed column like "silly mistake," "misread," or "rushing."

  - Dive into why you made this silly mistake, why you misread, and why you were rushing.

  - This is how you can identify the problematic actions that lead to problematic judgments and change them for the better.

### Learn From Your Mistakes

#### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There's nothing interesting left anymore.

Now you "know where you are," but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights.

If you like the number, you'll walk away excited and think, "Okay, now I'm good."

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don't need to work *that* hard when you're good, right?

If you don't like the number, tears follow.

Now there's no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what's the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better.

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you're doing wrong.

Instructions

After you&#x27;ve Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.

Fill in your Reason Missed column

In this column, write down what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question.

Ask yourself, &quot;Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?&quot; Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.

The first word of every entry in this column should be &quot;I.&quot; 

Example: &quot;I misidentified the question stem.&quot;

This will force you to reflect on what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.

Fill in your Solution column

In this column, write down what you will do differently to ensure you don&#x27;t make this same mistake again.

Ask yourself, &quot;What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?&quot;

The first words of every entry in this column should be &quot;I will...&quot; 

Example: &quot;I will do Question Stem Training to improve my ability to correctly identify question types.&quot;

Frame your Solution Column as a direct response to the Reason Missed column.

Previously Seen Sections Only

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
If You Want to Win

Keep your Wrong Answer Journal entries short and sweet. One to two sentences is enough for each column.

Do not waste time in your Wrong Answer Journal writing down detailed explanations of why the answer you chose was wrong, and why the right answer is right in the exact context of the question.

If you&#x27;re writing anything that cannot be ported directly to subsequent sections, you are wasting your Wrong Answer Journal time. 

The Wrong Answer Journal gives you a chance to identify the exact actions you took that made you make the wrong decision. It then gives you the opportunity to decide which actions you will take in the future that will enable you to make correct decisions. This is the essence of improvement on the LSAT.

I want you to know why an answer is right and another is wrong, but I don&#x27;t want you to stop there. I want you to start there.

After you know why the correct answer is correct and why the wrong answer is wrong, look more deeply at your actions and mindset in relation to the question.

What did you do that caused this result? And what will you do to ensure a different result next time?

Do not rely on cheap explanations in your Reason Missed column like &quot;silly mistake,&quot; &quot;misread,&quot; or &quot;rushing.&quot;

Dive into why you made this silly mistake, why you misread, and why you were rushing.

This is how you can identify the problematic actions that lead to problematic judgments and change them for the better.

## Intuition, Not Impulse

### What problem are we solving?

Impulse is great until you have no idea why you chose a wrong answer.

Acting on impulse is like the evil twin of acting on intuition. It happens when you try to be fast before you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level.

Intuition takes over after you've ingrained the process at a subconscious level. Intuition can be reliable and purposeful.

Impulse can't.

I know you think I don't understand: "Like come on, Ellen. Don't you know I have to do this in 35 minutes?" I get you.

This advice is coming from someone who could -0 Logical Reasoning sections in 27 minutes one month after meeting the LSAT for the first time.

It's not that I love to be slow or want you to be slow; I actually love speed. My Spotify history of Eminem's Rap God has the receipts (yes that song is so problematic it's also so fast and it just soothes my brain so much).

Instructions

After you&#x27;ve Camoed your LR or RC section, begin the Wrong Answer Journaling process.

For each wrong answer, fill in the date you did the section, the question number that was wrong, and the answer you chose.

This information will already be filled in for you if you Camo using Loophole Online.

Fill in your Reason Missed column

In this column, write down what you did wrong that caused you to miss this question.

Ask yourself, &quot;Why did I think that this wrong answer was right? Why did I think that this right answer was wrong?&quot; Focus on changing the actions that enabled these mistaken judgments.

The first word of every entry in this column should be &quot;I.&quot; 

Example: &quot;I misidentified the question stem.&quot;

This will force you to reflect on what you did that caused you to choose the wrong answer and overlook the right answer.

Fill in your Solution column

In this column, write down what you will do differently to ensure you don&#x27;t make this same mistake again.

Ask yourself, &quot;What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the wrong answer is wrong? What could I have done that would have made me realize in the moment that the right answer is right?&quot;
