Instructions

Highlight any word you wouldn't bet your LSAT score on during all LSAT sections and during all Extracurricular Reading.

These highlighted words are your vocab words.

Harvest all the vocab words you've highlighted after your section or reading is complete.

Harvesting your words means writing down each of your vocab words in a tracker. You can use my template or create a simple Google Sheet for this.

Always harvest your vocab words the same day that you highlight them.

To further optimize your vocab harvesting, watch the How to Harvest Vocab Words video.

Practice your vocab words daily for at least 10 minutes.

To practice, follow the procedures outlined in the How to Learn Vocab Without AI video or the How to Learn Vocab: AI Supercharged Edition video.

10 minutes of practice is a minimum, not a maximum. If you have a 100+ number of vocab words to learn, do not be afraid to practice for a longer period of time.

Ellen's Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

Here is a secret: My non-native English speaker students are my absolute favorites. Y'all inspire me. And I do all my best work for you. 

That's why I made a whole parallel Routine path just for non-native speakers, catering to your particular strengths and weaknesses. I love you. Let's do this. 

This Routine can replace the Translation Foundation Routine in the Translation Prep Stage if you are a non-native English speaker. Feel free to integrate the Non-Native English Speaker Routine Progression in with both the Translation Core Routines and the Accuracy Fix Routines. 

As a non-native speaker, you know that there is one big glaring problem for you when it comes to LSAT: vocab. 

And you may have been told by various naysayers that it's hopeless. That you'll never learn all the words you have to know for the LSAT. And there's no guarantee that any word you study will ever come up on test day. 

As someone who has guided many non-native English speakers who live outside the US to 170+ scores, I will tell you that these naysayers are WRONG. 

You can change your vocab level, and it does matter. You can increase the chance that you will know the words you see on test day.

It'll be difficult. It'll be time-consuming, but you can do it. This Routine and the subsequent Routine Progressions will show you how to achieve that goal.

How This Should Be Done

Note: this Routine is a modification of the Translation Foundation Routine. Go check out the instructions and notes there to get a full picture before you get started on the Non-Native Speaker Routine.

When you first start doing this Routine, you should spend as much time as humanly possible on Vocab Training.

I put the three Vocabulary Translation Drills and the Extracurricular Reading before the Vocab Training in this Routine to give you the opportunity to harvest words every day. That way you'll always have new, highly relevant words to fuel your practice.

Make sure you've watched the How to Read Hard Passages module of the Loophole Online Course before attempting this Routine or any other non-native speaker Routines. 

Feel free to expand the number of Translation + CLIR Drills to enable you to meet the Translation time goal more quickly. Just make sure that you keep the stable foundation of Vocabulary Translation Drills.

One of the most important things you can do for your LSAT prep is to attempt to create an English immersion environment for as much of the day as possible.

I recognize that this can be challenging if you don't live in an English-speaking nation, but please ask your family to help you with this goal. Seek out native English speaker friends and English-language environments. In addition, ask your family if they would try to speak English with you as much as possible.
