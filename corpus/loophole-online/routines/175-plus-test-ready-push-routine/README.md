### Step 4: Party Tricking Jail

4

#### Party Tricking Jail

1 section

##### Instructions

**NOTE**: Party Tricking Jail is an advanced workout for those who want to push themselves to become true answer choice experts. Beginners can make good progress with other workouts before entering Party Tricking Jail.

1. Party Trick every question in an LR or RC section* .

   1. This means you will only read the question stem and answer choices on each question before you select an answer on that question.

   2. Do not look at the stimulus or passage.
2. Camo the section immediately after completing it.

   1. The Camo will display all the questions that are wrong plus an unknown number of decoy correct answers.

   2. Do not look at the stimulus when you Camo.

   3. Party Trick the returned questions again. Doubt your answers on the questions Camo displays and try to correct your answer.
3. Get as close to -0 on the Camo as you can.

4. **OPTIONAL**: Do not exit Party Tricking Jail until you reach -5 or better pre-Camo.

   1. You can customize this accuracy goal to your personal needs.

### Step 5: Scavenger Hunt

5

#### Scavenger Hunt

##### Instructions

1. At the end of the week, have a friend grade all your sections.

   1. Ask your friend to only tell you how many you got wrong in each section.

   2. This means your friend will only tell you that a given section was a -5. They will not tell you anything else about the section.
2. Go back to each section to find every wrong answer.

3. The goal is to correct every section to a -0 without using the answer key.

4. Send your new answers to your friend to see how much you've improved.

5. **Optional**: Repeat this cycle until you get to -0 on each section.

### Step 6: Hit List

6

#### Hit List

##### Instructions

1. Make a spreadsheet with three columns, or use this template :

   1. **Column 1**: Top 15 Question

   2. **Column 2**: How This Question Works

   3. **Column 3**: What I Can Take From This Question
2. Keep a list of the 15 hardest questions you've ever completed in your prep.

3. Each day, study two questions from the Hit List.

4. Dissect exactly how each question is working.

   1. How does the question both camouflage and justify the correct answer?

   2. What makes this question hard?
5. Figure out what you can take from this question.

   1. And what can you work on in your process to improve your performance on questions that are difficult for the same reason going forward?
6. Continually add questions to and remove questions from your Hit List as some questions no longer feel difficult and others feel more challenging.

### Ellen's Advice

#### Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine forces you to look at the section from so many different perspectives all within one intense Routine.

It mixes in stimulus-only practice, answer-choice-only practice, test-day timed practice, and deep review – this refines your process, even when you're already scoring at a high level.

#### How This Should Be Done

If you need to cap your Thunderdome at 1 hour to complete this Routine within the amount of time you have that's fine. But the deeper you go, the more you'll get out of it.

The Test-Day Timed Sections should cut off when time is called, so they will take up the minority of your time in this Routine, but moving into Party Tricking directly afterwards offers a unique benefit.

Party Tricking is intuition-training and will provide a different perspective on the intuition-based approach that you just utilized in your test-day timed sections.

#### How to Move On

You can intersperse this Routine with PTs in the last month before your exam.

There's no pressing reason to move on from this Routine since it will always provide a valuable challenge.

### Stop Oversimplifying

#### What problem are we solving?

There is no such thing as "*just* a detail" on the LSAT.

Ignoring "details" leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you're bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on "just one word." That doesn't make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you'd to have when you're a lawyer, right? You don't want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed "*just* one word."

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you're not conceptually translating that answer. You're oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer's fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus.

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won't.

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices.

That's how we stop you from missing so many questions.

Stop Oversimplifying
What Problem Are We Solving?

There is no such thing as &quot;just a detail&quot; on the LSAT. 

Ignoring &quot;details&quot; leads to missing question through oversimplifying. If you run with an inaccurate, oversimplified version of the text, you&#x27;re bound to miss questions.

Answers are right and wrong based on &quot;just one word.&quot; That doesn&#x27;t make the LSAT mean, evil, or unfair. It makes the LSAT a test of precision.

And precision is something you&#x27;d to have when you&#x27;re a lawyer, right? You don&#x27;t want to be the attorney who harms their client because you missed &quot;just one word.&quot; 

Every word on the LSAT deserves equal levels of respect, as it works with every other word to create a conceptual Translation of each sentence.

Conceptual Translation can be the single biggest differentiator between high and lower scorers. It is the opposite of oversimplifying. When you ignore some words in an answer choice and focus solely on other words (keywording), you&#x27;re not conceptually translating that answer. You&#x27;re oversimplifying and massively increasing your risk of missing the question.

Keywording reduces an answer&#x27;s fate solely to whether it has certain words from the stimulus.

However, there are many correct answers that contain no words from the stimulus. There are many wrong answers that contain tons of words from the stimulus. 

You cannot oversimplify answer choice selection to a shallow matching game and expect the LSAT to tolerate it. It won&#x27;t. 

But this is just one way oversimplification decreases your score. There are approximately one million other ways oversimplification decreases your score. 

This Accuracy Fix trains you to embrace complexity because your mind is strong enough to handle it. Luckily, this is something you were trained to do in the Translation Prep Stage. Now is the time to put that training into action, especially in the answer choices. 

That&#x27;s how we stop you from missing so many questions.

### Don't Be Desperate in the Answers

#### What problem are we solving?

Sometimes you just want to get the question right so badly that every answer choice looks like an escape hatch.

"Is it right? Is it right!??

If it's right, then I can move on.

Then I won't waste any more time.

Then the stimulus must have meant this.

Then this must have been what they were looking for."

Notice the desperation dripping off this monologue.

You're looking for an answer choice to rescue you, but actually the roles need to be reversed. You have to be the strong one.

Be for real. You are better than 80% wrong.

Answer choices are designed to exploit your desire for them to be right and to prey on moments of inattention.

But you can channel your desire to get the question right into building an invulnerable fortress of analysis throughout every stage of the question. This fortress (Translation, CLIR, Mission, Back Up Plan) will allow you to see exactly why these answers are wrong without. any. drama.

The answer choices only gain manipulative power over you when you look to them to save you. But you are not the freaking damsel that answer C is here to rescue. You're the hero.

You're here to judge answer C, and you're going to judge it according to your vetted, bulletproof standards.

The answers need to impress *you*, not the other way around.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to build the fortress.

Because you are smart enough. You have the tools, the time, and the resources to never need any answer choice to save you again.

PT7.S1
Q1
1. Weaken: In the first years after the invention of the printing press, people who knew how to read but couldn’t afford expensive manuscript copies began stocking up on printed books
2. Strengthen: The affordability of printed books encouraged people who were previously unable read to learn to do so
3. NA: Increased demand for printed books wasn’t the result of a single rich family hoarding copies for their personal libraries
4. SA: A dramatic increase in demand for reading materials always results from increased literacy in a population
5. Method: Assumes the correlation between two phenomena means that one of those phenomena was the result of another
6. Argument Part: The public’s demand for printed books in the first years after the invention of the printing press was many times greater than demand had been for manuscript copies. – premise that the author claims as evidence for a certain phenomenon
7. Parallel Skeleton: Before X, few purchased the expensive versions of Y. Now, Y are cheaper, and more people buy them. Therefore, more people learned how to use Y.
8. Parallel Correct Answer: Before Fluffy went to Esther the dog trainer, he rarely sat on command. Esther has a reputation for inducing obedience in even the most defiant canines. Now, Fluffy is a model pup who sits whenever he’s told. This change in Fluffy’s behavior demonstrates that he must have had wax in his ears which prevented him from hearing commands, and Esther cured it.


Q2
1. Weaken: Bevex-sweetened soft drinks are now sold in liters which contain the equivalent of six cans of the substance, and die-hard consumers usually guzzle at least five liters a day
2. Strengthen: Humans process Bevex with a unique enzyme that inhibits its carcinogenic effects, which mice lack
3. NA: People drink fewer than 25 cans of Bevex-sweetened soda a day
4. SA: No consumer of Bevex-sweetened soda drinks more than five cans a day, and all artificial sweeteners have the same effect on humans as they do on mice
5. Method: Takes for granted that nobody drinks more than a certain amount of something a day. Assumes two different species process a substance in the same way
6. Argument Part: Bevex is in fact safe for people – conclusion the argument is designed to support
7. Parallel Skeleton: X is only bad for Y in large amounts. Z would have to drink a large quantity of X-containing substance to experience the same toxic effects. Thus, X is safe for Z to consume
8. Parallel Correct Answer: Hershey’s chocolate contains a chemical compound that is poisonous to dogs, but only if it is consumed in excess. A recent study demonstrated that Dachshunds can consume up to 10 grams of the chemical before its toxic effects kick in. Great Danes would have to consume five king-size Hershey’s bars to ingest an equivalent amount. Thus, we can say confidently that Great Danes are safe to indulge in Hershey’s chocolate
Q3
1. Controversy: Whether absolutely anyone can travel around the world
2. Agreement: Airlines make it possible to travel to different places
3. Loophole for Harry: What if most people can’t afford to pay for expensive airline tickets?
4. Loophole for Judith: What if the flights that everyone can afford enable globe-trotting?
Q4
1. Weaken: The new pills contain less than a quarter of the fiber that nutritionists recommend people consume daily
2. Strengthen: The same group of nutritionists claim that supplementing one’s diet with any quantity of additional fiber will yield health benefits
3. NA: The nutritionists’ recommendation does not apply exclusively to naturally occurring fiber in foods like lentils and chia seeds
4. SA: If a dietary supplement contains at least some fiber, it’s guaranteed to satisfy the nutritionists’ recommendation
5. Method: Presupposes that a supplement containing any amount of fiber will meet the nutritionists’ recommendation
6. Argument Part: Nutritionists have recommended that people eat more fiber – premise on which the advertising campaign hinges on
7. Parallel Skeleton: Experts say consuming more X is good for you. You should buy Y because it contains some amount of X
8. Parallel Correct Answer: 9/10 veterinarians say that feeding your dog fish oil will increase the shine and thickness of its coat. The nutrition label on Kelpy Kibble indicates that it contains 2 milligrams of fish oil
Q5
1. Inference 1: One cannot have a moral duty that is usually impossible to fulfill
2. Inference 2: Moral duties apply only to actions that individuals can consciously control
3. Inference 3: For a moral duty to exist, there must be a reliable way for individuals to determine how to act in accordance with it
4. Contradiction: Consumers have a moral obligation to comply with all recommendations made by environmentalists, regardless of whether they are practically feasible
Q6
1. Weaken: People want to boost the performance of their heart and lungs to achieve their fitness goals, and enhanced brain function isn’t correlated with such outcomes
2. Strengthen: A group of neurologists recently published a study affirming the efficacy of magazines like Stimulus in improving brain performance
3. NA: Brain performance can improve through methods that improve performance in other physical organs
4. SA: Everybody wants to improve brain performance, and subscribing to any magazine that claims to exercise your brain is guaranteed to achieve that goal
5. Method: Takes for granted that everyone wants to accomplish a certain goal. Implies that two physical organs are similar in one respect because they are similar in another. Claims that one technique for achieving an outcome is effective without offering evidence
6. Argument Part: Anyone who exercises knows from firsthand experience that exercise leads to better performance of such physical organs as the heart and the lungs, as well as to improvement in muscle tone. – cites an analogy intended to demonstrate the veracity of a claim
7. Parallel Skeleton: X and Y share a defining characteristic. Because you can improve the performance of X through Z, Z will have the same effect on Y. Buy this product, it does Z!
8. Parallel Correct Answer: It is widely accepted that giving your cat a scratching post will alleviate stress and stress-related behavioral issues. Just like cats, dogs are domesticated mammals that can experience stress. Order a Scratchinator today: the post that keeps your pooch pacified!
Q7
1. Weaken: Major reforms to the health-care system enacted in the 1990s increased the incentive for providers and insurers to assume the costs of care
2. Strengthen: The state of the health-care system hasn’t changed significantly since the 1980s
3. NA: Health-care providers and insurers still have an incentive to transfer the cost burden of care to other parties
4. SA: Health-care providers and insurers always capitalize on any opportunity to increase profits, and passing off costs is still an opportunity in the current health-care system
5. Method: Uses an analogy to demonstrate the challenges of addressing a societal issue
6. Argument Part: The reason is that this system gives health-care providers and insurers every incentive to shift, wherever possible, the costs of treating illness onto each other or any other party, including the patient – premise the author substantiates by way of example in the last sentence
7. Parallel Skeleton: There’s no feasible solution to the problem of X within systemic constraints. Y, a critical stakeholder in X, has no practical reason to decrease X, as demonstrated by this action taken 30 years ago.
8. Parallel Correct Answer: There’s no effective way to address the issue of inbreeding in the doodle population. That’s because doodle breeders get a reputational boost by producing puppies that are both trainable and aesthetically pleasing, and that happens when their parents are related. Just consider Pierre, the last championship Labradoodle, whose parents were first cousins!

Q10
1. Weaken: The group of hyperactive children in the experiment started behavioral modification therapy immediately before switching to a low-additive diet
2. Strengthen: A separate study showed that if hyperactive children consume less nitrates, the most common food additive, they exhibit fewer behavior problems
3. NA: The researchers trained to assess the presence of behavior problems did so accurately during the study in question
4. SA: The physiological processes that influence behavior are themselves exclusively influenced by changes in the quantity of additives a person consumes
5. Method: Presupposes that the correlation between two variables resulted from a decrease in one of those variables
6. Argument Part: Originally nearly 60 percent of the children exhibited behavior problems; after the change in diet, only 30 percent did so. – A finding from a study that constitutes the sole support for a conclusion drawn by the author
7. Parallel Skeleton: X causally contributes to Y, as evidenced by the fact that this study showed a reduction in Y when consumption of X decreased
8. Parallel Correct Answer: A study of 4,000 adult dogs found they were less fearful of fireworks if they were exposed to loud noises as puppies. This conclusively demonstrates that your dog will be more tolerant of fireworks on July 4th if you bang pots and set off explosives near them at a young age

2. Inference: National Motor Company cars from decades other than the 1960s are more likely to receive major engine repairs
3. Contradiction: The National Motor Company manufactured cars in the 1960s that were likely to receive more major engine repairs compared to those produced in other decades
Q12
1. Inference 1: Theorems derived through enormous computations are more likely to be accepted as legitimate today compared to 1976
2. Inference 2: Mathematical norms have evolved
3. Inference 3: At least some mathematicians in 1976 would reject a theorem based on evidence considered adequate by today’s standards
4. Contradiction: Most mathematicians today abide by the same standards upheld by some mathematicians in 1976
Q13
1. Weaken: The rules of conditional reasoning DO NOT apply
2. Strengthen: The rules of conditional reasoning DO apply
3. NA: The physical benefits of climbing mountains do not guarantee you will live to a ripe old age
4. SA: The only activity that can stave off boredom is climbing mountains
5. Method: Uses conditional reasoning to arrive at a valid conclusion
6. Argument Part: But you will be bored unless you climb mountains. – A premise used by the author to directly support the main conclusion
7. Parallel Skeleton: If you do X, you will not Y. But if you don’t do X, you will be Z. Thus, if you Y, you will also have been Z at some point
8. Parallel Correct Answer: If you feed Dubai chocolate to your dog, they will refuse to eat dry kibble. However, unless you feed your puppy this delicious Middle Eastern delicacy, they will feel uncultured. Therefore, if your dog refuses to eat their kibble, they will have felt uncultured
Q14
1. Weaken: The lobsters under observation feasted on detritus that floated into the traps
2. Strengthen: The marine biologists accurately gauged the lobsters’ appetites throughout the study
3. NA: Lobsters can experience hunger if they go without food for two months
4. SA: If the lobster subjects in this study didn’t resort to cannibalism, trapped lobsters will never eat each other in response to hunger
5. Method: Fails to consider whether the conditions outlined in a hypothesis have been satisfied before dismissing the claim. Rejects a general hypothesis based on a single study
6. Argument Part: Eight lobsters even shared one trap together for two months without eating one another – A specific finding which purportedly disproves a hypothesis
7. Parallel Skeleton: Scientists theorized that removing X’s access to Y would result in Z, if X was craving Y. In an experiment, X had no access to Y, but they didn’t Z. We can therefore dismiss this theory
8. Parallel Correct Answer: A group of veterinarians posited that French Bulldogs will actually start speaking French when they feel anxious. After ten appointments with Frenchie patients, not one reverted to speaking their native tongue. We can safely conclude the naïve veterinarians’ theory is false
Q15
1. Weaken: After the ban was enacted, there was a precipitous decline in the population of deer-eating Grizzly bears in Greenfield County
2. Strengthen: In surrounding counties, similar restrictions on hunting caused a massive spike in the deer population
3. NA: Residents of Greenfield County abided by the hunting ban
4. SA: Any policies that limit the activity of hunters are superfluous and lead to dangerous side effects
5. Method: Assumes a causal relationship between two factors that are correlated. Fails to consider the circumstances that may have necessitated a particular policy
6. Argument Part: Eight years ago, hunting was banned in Greenfield County on the grounds that hunting endangers public safety. – premise the author refutes to support a conclusion
7. Parallel Skeleton: X was implemented to address the problem of Y. However, X is correlated with Z, a form of Y. Moreover, there’s no evidence this specific kind of Y occurred. Therefore, implementing X was unnecessary and is responsible for the presence of Z.
8. Parallel Correct Answer: In Los Angeles, a campaign led by animal rights activists resulted in a citywide ban on feeding dogs processed kibble. The activists claim that kibble is indigestible and harmful to the health of Angeleno pooches. After the ban was enacted, however, the number of vet visits in the city spiked dramatically, with many patients exhibiting signs of gastrointestinal distress and dehydration. Therefore, the ban was both unwarranted and the cause of rather than the solution to health issues in the canine community

4. Contradiction: Previous estimates of the mass of Halley’s comet are accurate
Q17
1. Weaken: Letters printed on recycled paper will not make a good impression on clients, even if the paper is of high quality
2. Strengthen: If recycled paper was regarded as high quality before the 1850s, recycled paper today is of similar caliber
3. NA: The office manager considers the materials that make up recycled paper to be a relevant factor in judging its quality
4. SA: All letters printed on recycled paper are guaranteed to impress clients and cannot be deemed inferior
5. Method: Justifies a conclusion through facts that are not relevant to the office manager’s argument
6. Argument Part: … so we cannot print them on inferior paper – intermediate conclusion the office manager uses to support his stance on ordering recycle paper
7. Parallel Skeleton: Person A: I won’t buy X. I need X for this purpose, and it isn’t of sufficient quality to accomplish that goal. Person B: But X can be of sufficient quality. Just consider this irrelevant historical example
8. Parallel Correct Answer: Person A: I refuse to adopt a dachshund. I want a dog I can go jogging with, so an injury-prone dachshund is poorly suited for my lifestyle. Person B: There are at least some examples of hearty, athletic dachshunds. After all, dachshunds were bred in the 17th century to hunt badgers, and it wasn’t until the 1990s that improper breeding practices introduced chronic back issues into the bloodline
Q18
1. Weaken: Unlike Alicia, Peter has been cited for automobile theft twice in the past year, and the police abide by a strict “three strikes” policy
2. Strengthen: Individuals who commit the same offense should face the same legal consequences
3. NA: Blameworthiness has a bearing on whether someone should be charged for a crime
4. SA: Anyone who borrows a car without the owner’s permission should be charged with automobile theft
5. Method: Summarizes a circumstance which triggered a certain punishment to argue that same punishment should apply in a similar circumstance
6. Argument Part: However, when Peter Foster did the same thing, he was charged with automobile theft. – cites a consequence that the author argues should apply to anyone who commits a similar crime
7. Parallel Skeleton: Person A committed this crime and faced legal consequences. Person B committed the same crime, but didn’t face the same consequences. ### Test-Day Timing Marathon Routine

#### Why are we doing this?

Test-Day Timing Marathon Routines are the best way to push yourself to improve stamina and test-day readiness at the same time.

The first few sections of a Test-Day Timing Marathon may be rocky, but you should gradually acclimate to how a test-day timed section feels.

Don't attempt Test-Day Timing Marathon Routines if your Marathon-timed sections are 10+ minutes from test-day timing. Test-Day Timing Marathons are more appropriate when you're within 5 minutes of test-day timing.

Test-Day Timing Marathons don't allow for natural time progression in the same way Marathon Routines do, so they risk compromising your accuracy and process far more than the other Speed Routines.

#### How to Pull This Off

To maximize the value of Test-Day Timing Marathons, treat this Routine just like test day (except for no breaks after section 2 😅).

This Routine is basically test day on steroids. If you can get your goal section-level accuracy in this Routine, you are already getting ahead on our Perform Prep Stage goals.

**Marathon Routine Protocols**

- Warm up before any Marathon-style Routine so you can hit the first section with a running start.

  - If you don't warm up first, the Marathon will not speed you up as much because it will take you longer to build momentum.
- Always Camo + Wrong Answer Journal at the end of a Marathon Routine day to avoid building up too much Camo debt.

  - The normal "wait at least 24 hours before you Camo" rule does not apply to Marathons.
- Never interrupt a Marathon to Camo in between sections.

- Camo the Marathon sections in the order of completion.

  - This means you should Camo the first section of your Marathon first, the second section next, and so on.
- Complete the entire Camo + Wrong Answer Journal process for each Marathon section before checking any Marathon scores and before starting the Camo process on another Marathon section. Camo and Wrong Answer Journal each section one at a time.

#### How to move on

If your Test-Day Timing Marathons crash and burn even though you've seen goal time and accuracy on Marathon-timed sections before, you may just be anxious about the countdown timer and need experience to acclimate.

Mixed Timing Marathon Routines are the best remedy for that situation because they ground you in your accurate, controlled process between test-day timed sections.

If you're hitting your goal time and accuracy in your Test-Day Timing Marathon Routine, take a Practice Test (PT).

#### Instructions

**NOTE**: Party Tricking Jail is an advanced workout for those who want to push themselves to become true answer choice experts. Beginners can make good progress with other workouts before entering Party Tricking Jail.

1. Party Trick every question in an LR or RC section* .

   1. This means you will only read the question stem and answer choices on each question before you select an answer on that question.

   2. Do not look at the stimulus or passage.
2. Camo the section immediately after completing it.

   1. The Camo will display all the questions that are wrong plus an unknown number of decoy correct answers.

   2. Do not look at the stimulus when you Camo.

   3. Party Trick the returned questions again. Doubt your answers on the questions Camo displays and try to correct your answer.
3. Get as close to -0 on the Camo as you can.

4. **OPTIONAL**: Do not exit Party Tricking Jail until you reach -5 or better pre-Camo.

   1. You can customize this accuracy goal to your personal needs.

##### If You Want to Win

- Traditionally, Party Tricking Jail has been assigned when an advanced student reaches a score plateau around -3 to -6,

  - I'll assign Party Tricking Jail when the plateau results from inadequate answer choice processing.

  - When I assign Party Tricking Jail this way, I suspend all the student's other homework so their entire Routine is *only* Party Tricking Jail.

  - I find it effective to suspend all the other homework so the student is more able to break the old habits that have left them plateaued.

- Party Tricking Jail should not be undertaken unless you can pass Level 5 of MemAC or have a true appetite for danger.

- It's key to push past the first few questions of Party Tricking Jail where you will undoubtedly feel uncertain and potentially uncomfortable.

  - Feeling uncertain is the whole point.

  - This is a Workout that is designed to hone your intuition. Like Arya learning to fight with a blindfold on.

### Don't Be Desperate in the Answers

#### What problem are we solving?

Sometimes you just want to get the question right so badly that every answer choice looks like an escape hatch.

"Is it right? Is it right!??

If it's right, then I can move on.

Then I won't waste any more time.

Then the stimulus must have meant this.

Then this must have been what they were looking for."

Notice the desperation dripping off this monologue.

You're looking for an answer choice to rescue you, but actually the roles need to be reversed. You have to be the strong one.

Be for real. You are better than 80% wrong.

Answer choices are designed to exploit your desire for them to be right and to prey on moments of inattention.

But you can channel your desire to get the question right into building an invulnerable fortress of analysis throughout every stage of the question. This fortress (Translation, CLIR, Mission, Back Up Plan) will allow you to see exactly why these answers are wrong without. any. drama.

The answer choices only gain manipulative power over you when you look to them to save you. But you are not the freaking damsel that answer C is here to rescue. You're the hero.

You're here to judge answer C, and you're going to judge it according to your vetted, bulletproof standards.

The answers need to impress *you*, not the other way around.

This Accuracy Fix trains you to build the fortress.

Because you are smart enough. You have the tools, the time, and the resources to never need any answer choice to save you again.

NOTE: Party Tricking Jail is an advanced workout for those who want to push themselves to become true answer choice experts. Beginners can make good progress with other workouts before entering Party Tricking Jail.

Party Trick every question in an LR or RC section*.

This means you will only read the question stem and answer choices on each question before you select an answer on that question.

### Learn From Your Mistakes

#### What problem are we solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon.

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to "see how you're doing," "understand where you are," "stay motivated," and "know what to work on."

So, of course, you need to check the score as soon as you're done with the section. But suddenly, as soon as you see that number.

There's nothing interesting left anymore.

Now you "know where you are," but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights.

If you like the number, you'll walk away excited and think, "Okay, now I'm good."

And then the next section is likely to crash and burn from laziness and complacency. You don't need to work *that* hard when you're good, right?

If you don't like the number, tears follow.

Now there's no motivation to continue because nothing ever works. So what's the point? Why try? I should just give up and save the effort I would waste on this. I never get better.

When you do this, you actively decline to change the behaviors that caused your wrong answers. This causes you to not improve.

Failing to look inward to understand what you need to change will cause you to leave points on the table.

This Accuracy Fix stops that rollercoaster of pain and plateaus.

You can get better. Give yourself the chance to really understand what you're doing wrong.

What Problem Are We Solving?

Seeing the score is like popping a balloon. 

You want to see the score so bad. Because you need to &quot;see how you&#x27;re doing,&quot; &quot;understand where you are,&quot; &quot;stay motivated,&quot; and &quot;know what to work on.&quot;

There&#x27;s nothing interesting left anymore. 

Now you &quot;know where you are,&quot; but the emotional reaction to where you are overwhelms any hope of strategic insights. 

If you like the number, you&#x27;ll walk away excited and think, &quot;Okay, now I&#x27;m good.&quot;

Instructions

Make a spreadsheet with three columns, or use this template:

Column 1: Top 15 Question

Column 2: How This Question Works

Column 3: What I Can Take From This Question

Keep a list of the 15 hardest questions you&#x27;ve ever completed in your prep.

Each day, study two questions from the Hit List.

Dissect exactly how each question is working.

How does the question both camouflage and justify the correct answer?

What makes this question hard?

Figure out what you can take from this question.

And what can you work on in your process to improve your performance on questions that are difficult for the same reason going forward?

Continually add questions to and remove questions from your Hit List as some questions no longer feel difficult and others feel more challenging.

Ellen&#x27;s Advice
Why Are We Doing This?

This Routine forces you to look at the section from so many different perspectives all within one intense Routine.

It mixes in stimulus-only practice, answer-choice-only practice, test-day timed practice, and deep review – this refines your process, even when you&#x27;re already scoring at a high level.

How This Should Be Done

If you need to cap your Thunderdome at 1 hour to complete this Routine within the amount of time you have that&#x27;s fine. But the deeper you go, the more you&#x27;ll get out of it.

The Test-Day Timed Sections should cut off when time is called, so they will take up the minority of your time in this Routine, but moving into Party Tricking directly afterwards offers a unique benefit.

Party Tricking is intuition-training and will provide a different perspective on the intuition-based approach that you just utilized in your test-day timed sections.

How to Move On

You can intersperse this Routine with PTs in the last month before your exam.

There&#x27;s no pressing reason to move on from this Routine since it will always provide a valuable challenge.
