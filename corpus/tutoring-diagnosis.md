# Diagnose the cause of a miss before assigning work

Private teacher reference recovered from Scott Milam and Christopher Nguyen's 7Sage tutoring webinar, dated 2022-01-11 in the preserved clip. This is an attributed coaching framework, not a new LSAT U scoring rule or a claim about a particular student. [Full source](../raw/sources/old-obsidian-vault-2026-10-07/Clippings/7Sage%20LSAT%20Tutoring%20Webinar.md); [comparison and limitations](../research/obsidian-vault-comparison/2026-10-07/research-review.md).

## A wrong answer can come from four different problems

| Working hypothesis | What to investigate | What practice would address it |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | Can the student restate the relevant sentence, quantifier and relationship without changing them? | Accurate translation and a check against the exact wording. |
| Logical fundamentals | Can the student explain what follows, what does not follow, and why? | Targeted concept work and short worked examples. |
| Question process | Does the student know what this question asks an answer to do? | Contrast question tasks and explain why an answer meets this task. |
| Test-taking strategy | Does the student understand the reasoning after review but lose time or mismanage confidence under the clock? | Timed section review of skips, flags, revisits and opportunity cost. |

These labels are hypotheses tested through the student's reasoning. An answer marked wrong does not reveal its cause on its own. A task that permits new information, such as Strengthen or Weaken, should not be judged by an inference task's requirement that the answer already follow from the stimulus.

The recovered webinar also groups Strengthen, Weaken, Evaluate, Resolve and Sufficient Assumption as tasks where an answer can introduce information. That shared feature does not make the tasks interchangeable: sufficient assumption must establish the conclusion, whereas strengthening need only improve its support; necessary assumption concerns what the argument requires. Use the actual question task rather than rejecting every answer that adds a fact. This is a diagnostic distinction from the [preserved webinar](../raw/sources/old-obsidian-vault-2026-10-07/Clippings/7Sage%20LSAT%20Tutoring%20Webinar.md), not a replacement taxonomy.

## Inspect the whole timing sequence

For a diagnosed reading-retention problem, the [RC cumulative-translation protocol](<Curriculum/Reading Comprehension/Notes/cumulative-translation-review.md>) supplies a concrete observation exercise. For a conditional meaning/diagram mismatch, the [existing conditional reference](<Curriculum/Conditional Logic/Notes/_megadoc_reference_conditionals.md>) routes to Joshua's approved checks. [Student-record conventions](../students/README.md) keep the individual evidence and resulting judgment in the person's folder.

A final-passage miss may begin with earlier questions consuming the available time. Review correct answers too: a correct but costly question can explain why the student never reached another question. Compare what the student did in the moment with what they can explain afterward before calling the final passage the problem.

Flagging and second-pass review are different from later untimed blind review. On a timed return, inspect whether the student actually reconsidered the conclusion, scope or relevant relationship, or simply repeated the first reading. Rebuilding every flagged question from scratch is the speakers' suggestion; its opportunity cost still needs judgment.

Check both overconfidence and underconfidence. A student may dismiss an unresolved two-choice decision too quickly, or spend remaining time reconfirming answers they already understand. Compare those choices with the attainable questions they left unfinished; no universal confidence percentage is adopted here.

In RC, distinguish a claim explicitly stated in the passage from one supported by implication. Ask what exact passage evidence supports the answer and whether the answer adds an unsupported commitment. The webinar's comparative-passage reading order is a speaker-specific strategy to evaluate, not a universal requirement for every student. Its captured advice remains accessible through the full source; current individual teaching judgments belong in student records.

## Turn review into a change the student can test

After review, name the specific failure and one behavior to test in the next timed attempt. “I found the right answer after unlimited rereading” is different evidence from “I can explain what I missed and how I will catch it under timed conditions.” Use the [Loophole method reference](loophole-online/index.md) when accurate translation or review practice addresses the diagnosed problem; keep the existing curriculum's logical standards.

The webinar's numeric confidence thresholds, timing ranges, old difficulty ratings, historical score estimates and logic-games examples are source-specific historical material. They were not adopted as current policy. This page synthesizes the useful diagnostic distinctions, without publishing or assigning anything.
