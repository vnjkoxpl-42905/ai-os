# Answer Key: Advanced Parsing

## Part I: The Vocabulary

*Use these definitions to calibrate your understanding of LSAT-specific terminology.*

**1. "Explanation"**

- **Strict Definition:** **Causation** (Cause & Effect).
- *Why?* On the LSAT, to "explain" something is to identify the cause that produced the effect. It does not mean simply describing a situation.

**2. "Defend"**

- **Strict Definition:** Advocating for or justifying a position as "right".
- *Why?* Merely stating a person's motivation (e.g., "they did it for money") is not a defense; a defense implies the author agrees with or supports the action.

**3. "Aesthetic Principle"**

- **Strict Definition:** A broad normative rule or "rule of thumb" applying to multiple scenarios.
- *Why?* If a rule applies only to one specific situation (e.g., "This painting is bad"), it is just a judgment. A principle must be broad enough to apply to many situations.

**4. "Supported Premise"**

- **Also known as:** **Intermediate Conclusion** (or Sub-Conclusion).
- *Why?* It functions as a conclusion because it is supported by evidence, but it also functions as a premise because it supports the main conclusion.

## Part II: Advanced Structural Mapping

*Key Strategy: Identify the directional word. Words like "From," "By," and "Basis" usually point backward to the Evidence.*

| Abstract Phrase | Breakdown & Logic Flow |
|---|---|
| **1. challenges the plausibility of X by offering Y as an alternative explanation** | **Conclusion:** X (is implausible) · **Evidence:** Y · *Note:* "By offering" introduces the evidence used to make the attack. |
| **2. substantiates the claim that X is false by demonstrating that Y is an inevitable consequence of X** | **Conclusion:** X is false · **Evidence:** Y (is a bad consequence) · *Note:* "Substantiates" means "supports." The author supports the idea that X is false using Y. |
| **3. undermines the support for X by drawing a distinction between Y and Z** | **Conclusion:** Support for X (is weak) · **Evidence:** Distinction between Y and Z · *Note:* The goal is to undermine X. The method is the distinction. |
| **4. rejects the hypothesis that X caused Y on the basis of Z** | **Conclusion:** Rejects "X caused Y" · **Evidence:** Z · *Note:* "On the basis of" is a classic premise indicator. |
| **5. derives the necessity of X from the impossibility of Y** | **Conclusion:** X (is necessary) · **Evidence:** Y (is impossible) · *Note:* "From" introduces the evidence source. |
| **6. treats the occurrence of X as corroborating evidence for Y** | **Conclusion:** Y · **Evidence:** X · *Note:* If X is "evidence for" Y, then X supports Y. |
| **7. X is presented in order to support the claim that Y** | **Conclusion:** Y · **Evidence:** X · *Note:* "In order to support" tells you that X is the support holding up Y. |

## Part III: Reverse Engineering High-Level Flaws

*This section confirms if you can translate abstract logic into concrete examples.*

**8. The Flaw:** *"Presumes, without providing justification, that a condition necessary for an outcome is also sufficient to produce that outcome."*

- **The Logic:** Confusing a **Requirement** with a **Guarantee**.
- **Correct Example:**
  - Premise (True): "To win the lottery, it is necessary to buy a ticket."
  - Flawed Conclusion: "I bought a ticket; therefore, I am guaranteed to win."

**9. The Flaw:** *"Rejects a position on the grounds that the proponents of the position have a vested interest in its acceptance."*

- **The Logic:** This is a **Source Argument** (Ad Hominem). Attacking the person's motive (money/power) instead of their facts.
- **Correct Example:**
  - Opposing View: "The mechanic says my brakes are dangerous and need replacing."
  - Flawed Conclusion: "He just wants to make money, so my brakes are probably fine."

**10. The Flaw:** *"Mistakes a temporal relationship between two events for a causal relationship."*

- **The Logic:** **Correlation/Temporal vs. Causation**. Just because Y happened *after* X, doesn't mean X *caused* Y.
- **Correct Example:**
  - Premise (Timeline): "The rooster crowed (X), and then the sun came up (Y)."
  - Flawed Conclusion: "Therefore, the rooster's crowing caused the sun to rise."
