## The must arrow shows a direction of guarantee

Compiled on 2026-10-11 from [LSAT Lab's conditional lesson](../../../../wiki/sources/lsat-lab-conditional-logic-clip.md), particularly 0:31–2:56 and 8:19–10:18. This is teaching synthesis, not a verbatim transcript or a replacement for Joshua's selected lesson order.

**If the condition on the left is true, the condition on the right must be true.** Read the arrow from the sufficient condition toward the necessary condition. The left guarantees the right; the left requires the right. The rule itself does not guarantee that the left condition has occurred.

For the existing law-school example:

**Enrolled in law school → Is a student**

Given enrollment, being a student must follow. Being a student alone does not establish law-school enrollment. Both labels describe complete conditions. The arrow represents the connection between them, not an indicator word that belongs inside either condition. Left-to-right is the chosen notation convention; the underlying relationship is sufficient-to-necessary, not a claim that sentence order decides the roles.

The source's New York City/USA example shows why the arrow does not by itself assert cause or later timing. Its requirement readback at 8:19 is useful after the concrete example: the left side requires the right side. Start with the guarantee, then name the two roles, then let the learner interpret a new rule before revealing the answer.

### Preserve what a single rule leaves open

| Given A → B and this fact | What follows from those alone |
|---|---|
| A | B must be true. |
| Not A | B is unsettled. |
| B | A is unsettled. |
| Not B | Not A. |

The latter row motivates later contraposition. Do not front-load compound negation, chains, exception words or biconditionals into the first arrow explanation just because the source covers them in one video.

The source's 1:26 metaphor that a rule disappears when A is absent is loose. The rule remains true; not A simply supplies no conclusion about B. Existing corpus quiz wording that says to ignore the conditional in this case is inference shorthand with this limit, not permission to erase a rule from later combined reasoning. No raw source is altered by this clarification.
