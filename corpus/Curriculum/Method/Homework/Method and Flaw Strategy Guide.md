# LSAT Logical Reasoning: Method of Reasoning & Flaw Strategy Guide

## I. Core Philosophy: The Approach

To master Logical Reasoning, you must move from simply understanding the passage (stimulus) to mastering the answer choices.

- **Think Out Loud:** Verbalize your thought process as you read. This helps process complex information, though you can stop if it interferes with your flow.
- **The "Travel" Analogy:** Treat arguments like travel destinations. The "method of transportation" is the argument's reasoning. Understanding *how* the author gets from Point A (Premise) to Point B (Conclusion) reveals where things can go wrong (the Flaw).
- **Literal Interpretation:** You must interpret LSAT claims literally. If an argument claims "cooler is better," you must test extreme literal possibilities (e.g., Would -1,000 degrees be ideal, or would it destroy the object?).

## II. Analyzing the Stimulus: Categorization

You cannot evaluate an argument until you categorize its components. As you read, label the text:

- **Opposing Viewpoints:** Often introduced early. Treat the author as a "narcissist" who will almost certainly push back against any generalized viewpoints or "what people say."
- **Pivot Words:** Words like "But" indicate a shift from background/opposing views to the author's argument.
- **Indicator Words:**
  - **Premise Indicators:** "For," "Because," "Since," "After all." These guarantee that what follows is evidence.
  - **Conclusion Placement:** A premise indicator implies an attached conclusion, which usually immediately precedes the premise.

## III. Mastering Answer Choices: "De-stractification"

Answer choices in Method and Flaw questions are often abstract. Your goal is to "de-stractify" them, that is, make them concrete.

### 1. The Two-Part Answer Check

To validate an answer choice, you must map its abstract terms onto the specific text of the stimulus.

- **Descriptive Accuracy:** The answer choice must be descriptively accurate. Every noun, adverb, and adjective in the answer choice must map to a real part of the argument.
- **Least Wrong > Most Right:** Do not look for the "perfect" answer. Look for the "least wrong" answer. If you cannot explicitly falsify a word (prove it is effectively wrong), you cannot eliminate it.

### 2. Disqualification Strategy

- Move from "qualifying" answers (looking for why they are right) to "disqualifying" them (looking for why they are explicitly wrong).
- Eliminate answers until only one remains that cannot be explicitly disqualified.

## IV. LSAT Glossary: Translating "Test Speak" to English

The LSAT uses common words with strictly specific definitions. Misinterpreting these leads to incorrect answers.

| Term | LSAT Definition & Usage |
|------|-------------------------|
| **"Explanation" / "Explain"** | Strictly means **Causation** (Cause and Effect). It does not mean "providing details" or "elaborating." |
| **"Defend" / "Defense"** | Implies advocating for a side or justifying it as "right." Stating a motivation is *not* the same as defending it. |
| **"Aesthetic Principle"** | A broad, normative rule (a rule of thumb) that applies to multiple scenarios, not just one specific instance. |
| **"From" / "By"** | Keywords that introduce evidence/premises (e.g., "extrapolated *from*..." means the following text is the premise). |

## V. Question Type Deep Dives

### A. Method of Reasoning (Argument Procedure)

- **Goal:** Identify logical and rhetorical moves.
- **Strategy:** Map the answer choice components to the argument parts.
  - *Example:* If an answer says "appealing to a historical fact," you must find a premise that functions as a historical fact.
  - *Trap:* Watch for answers that reverse the order (e.g., saying the argument uses X to support Y, when it actually uses Y to support X).

### B. Flaw Questions

- **Reverse Engineering:** Before reading the stimulus, scan the answer choices to see what each *would* require to be correct.
  - *Example:* If an answer says the argument "generalized from fruits it tested to fruits it did not," the stimulus *must* contain a specific test group and a conclusion about a different group.
- **Common Flaw — Extrapolation:** Taking a narrow data set (e.g., temperatures of 10°, 20°, 30°) and assuming the trend continues infinitely (e.g., "the cooler the better" implies -100° is best).
- **Controlled Experiments:** If a study cites "similar conditions," you can usually eliminate answer choices suggesting "other factors" (like sunlight or humidity) skewed the results, as those factors would cancel out across groups.

## VI. Timing and Next Steps

### The Accuracy-Efficiency Paradox

- **Disregard Speed:** Do not focus on speed right now. Prioritizing speed over accuracy leads to mistakes.
- **The Fix:** Complete accuracy leads to intuition. Once you are accurate, efficiency naturally follows. Speed is a byproduct of mastery.

### Homework & Drills

1. **Reverse Engineering:** For the next set of questions, look at the answer choices *first*. Identify exactly what would need to be in the stimulus for that answer to be correct.
2. **Keyword Mapping:** Practice extracting abstract terms from answer choices and mapping them directly to the evidence and conclusion in the text.
