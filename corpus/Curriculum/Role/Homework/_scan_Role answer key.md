**Question 1\**
**Description:** Infers that a practice is undesirable solely because it results in negative consequences that are difficult to quantify.

1.  **Argument A:** Implementing a new training program in the workplace has uncertain costs, but it may lead to improved employee performance and satisfaction. Therefore, it should not be considered a priority.

2.  **Argument B:** Noise pollution from urban construction projects affects psychological well-being, yet its impact is hard to measure accurately. For this reason, these projects should be limited.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument B** – Matches the description because it concludes a practice should be limited solely due to hard-to-measure negative impacts.

**Question 2\**
**Description:** Discounts a viewpoint based on the perceived improbability of the conditions being met rather than on evidence negating the viewpoint.

1.  **Argument A:** The theory that the universe is teeming with undiscovered life is dismissed due to the sheer vastness of space and slim chance of such life making contact with Earth.

2.  **Argument B:** The belief in ancient aliens is often refuted through historical records and ongoing scientific research that find no credible artifacts or evidence to support such claims.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument A** – Matches the description because it rejects the theory solely due to perceived improbability, not evidence disproving it.

**Question 3\**
**Description:** Reasons that because a certain phenomenon leads to a known positive outcome, it must be actively pursued despite unknown variables.

1.  **Argument A:** Investing in space exploration can result in technological advances and inspire generations, so it should be pursued aggressively even though the financial risks are unpredictable.

2.  **Argument B:** Introducing universal basic income could alleviate poverty, yet the economic implications remain uncertain, so careful assessments are necessary before any implementation.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument A** – Matches the description because it promotes aggressive pursuit despite unknown risks, based on known benefits.

**Question 4\**
**Description:** Relies on an analogy between two things that are not relevantly similar.

1.  **Argument A:** Anthropologists argue that cultures change much like languages do. Since languages evolve by adopting new expressions and phrases, new cultural norms can be integrated similarly over time.

2.  **Argument B:** Some claim that managing a country is like driving a car; both require adjustment to conditions. However, the complexities of geopolitics transcend the straightforward mechanics of driving.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument B** – Matches the description because it draws an analogy between unrelated domains (geopolitics vs. driving).

**Question 5\**
**Description:** Derives its conclusion based solely on an event that occurred once, assuming it will happen again in the same manner.

1.  **Argument A:** In the 1800s, a major city overcame economic recession by focusing on industrial growth. Therefore, any city facing a recession today should also turn to industrial development as the solution.

2.  **Argument B:** Because a town hosted a successful annual fair last year, it’s expected that the same fair will experience success each year.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument B** – Matches the description because it assumes future success based on a single prior occurrence.

**Question 6\**
**Description:** Improperly shifts the burden of proof by assuming that a claim is true unless it is proven false.

1.  **Argument A:** Critics of the new diet fad say it is unhealthy. Yet, there is minimal research showing definitive harm, so it must be safe for consumption.

2.  **Argument B:** A hypothesis has been proposed that energy drinks increase concentration. Until research proves otherwise, it should be assumed true.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument A** – Matches the description because it assumes safety until harm is proven, shifting the burden of proof.

**Question 7\**
**Description:** Confuses necessary conditions with sufficient conditions.

1.  **Argument A:** To graduate, you must complete a thesis. Completing your thesis is enough to ensure graduation.

2.  **Argument B:** She believes that to be healthy, one must exercise. Thus, exercising guarantees health.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument A** – Matches the description because it treats a necessary requirement (thesis) as if it guarantees graduation.

**Question 8\**
**Description:** Bases its generalization on a sample that is known to be biased.

1.  **Argument A:** A survey at the local yoga studio found 95% of participants believe yoga is the best form of exercise. Therefore, yoga must be universally considered the best exercise.

2.  **Argument B:** By only interviewing tech company CEOs, the study concludes that all industries are rapidly advancing in AI technology.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument A** – Matches the description because it generalizes from a biased sample (yoga enthusiasts).

**Question 9\**
**Description:** Fails to recognize that evidence for the occurrence of one event is not enough to conclude a causation with another.

1.  **Argument A:** Ice cream sales spike in summer, and more people swim during this time. Thus, eating ice cream causes people to swim.

2.  **Argument B:** As daylight hours increase in spring, people tend to spend more time outdoors, hence proving long days lead to more outdoor activities.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument A** – Matches the description because it mistakes correlation (ice cream sales and swimming) for causation.

**Question 10\**
**Description:** Presents a potentially false dilemma, suggesting there are only two possible choices.

1.  **Argument A:** We must either increase taxes or face economic collapse; there is no other option.

2.  **Argument B:** The city can either build a new park or focus on maintaining current recreational areas to improve community well-being.

✅ **Correct Answer:** **Argument A** – Matches the description because it presents only two options while ignoring other possible solutions.
