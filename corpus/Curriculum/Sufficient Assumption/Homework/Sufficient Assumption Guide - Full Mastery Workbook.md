Tutorial Answer Key

1. Conditional
Gap: Certification this quarter follows from clearing final safety validation.
Bridge: Any device that clears final safety validation is certified for commercial sale this quarter.
Proof Check: Prem1 gives: Passes stress -> Sent to validation. Bridge adds: Clears validation -> Certified this
quarter. Together we can derive the conclusion about the T9 after it clears validation.
Coaching Note: When the conclusion predicts a next step, look for a rule that makes the predicted step
guaranteed.

2. Conditional
Gap: From not-enforceable to not-published (denying the consequent).
Bridge: Any policy that was published in the employee handbook before the quarter began is enforceable this
quarter.
Proof Check: Bridge allows contrapositive: Not enforceable -> Not published. Prem2 then guarantees the
conclusion. The intranet detail is irrelevant.
Coaching Note: Spot fluff: events like 'intranet refresh' rarely change the logic. Filter them out before building
the bridge.

3. Conditional
Gap: Being 'considered' guarantees only the time requirement, not the full set of eligibility requirements.
Bridge: Any bid that is considered necessarily met all eligibility requirements.
Proof Check: Prem2 + bridge delivers the conclusion directly. Prem1 explains one known requirement but does
not alone cover 'all'.
Coaching Note: When a conclusion asserts 'all requirements', your bridge must upgrade from a partial condition
to a full guarantee.

4. Conditional
Gap: Processing implies valid ID, but the conclusion asserts 'every prerequisite'.
Bridge: A valid ID is the only prerequisite for processing.
Proof Check: From Prem1, Processed -> Valid ID. With the bridge, Valid ID -> all prerequisites satisfied; hence
the conclusion.
Coaching Note: When a conclusion quantifies over 'every' condition, a minimal bridge often reduces the
prerequisite set.

5. Quantifier & Scope
Gap: We only know 'some' GED analysts are fluent; nothing yet about 'most' GED analysts.
Bridge: Most analysts on the Global Equities desk are fluent in Mandarin.
Proof Check: Bridge makes most GED analysts fluent; Prem2 makes all fluent Level 3 passers; therefore most
GED analysts are L3 passers.
Coaching Note: Watch for upgrades in quantity (some -> most). Your bridge must explicitly perform the
upgrade on the correct set.

6. Quantifier & Scope
Gap: Premises justify 'most' assigned to litigation, not 'every'.
Bridge: All associates hired last year graduated from a top tier law school.
Proof Check: Bridge + Prem2 assign all last year's hires to litigation. The volunteering detail is fluff.
Coaching Note: If the conclusion says 'every', make sure the bridge closes any quantity slack left by 'most'
premises.

7. Quantifier & Scope
Gap: We do not know that any bus-only lanes city reduced commute times.
Bridge: At least one city that implemented bus-only lanes reduced average commute times.
Proof Check: Bridge ensures a bus-lane city reduced commutes; Prem2 then gives higher satisfaction for that
city; conclusion follows.
Coaching Note: Tie the specific subgroup in Prem1 to the trigger condition used in Prem2.

8. Quantifier & Scope
Gap: Premises speak about finalists; the conclusion is about the set of promoted employees.
Bridge: All promoted employees are finalists who exceeded the communication bar.
Proof Check: Bridge gives Promoted -> Finalist AND Exceeded-Comm. Prem1 adds Finalist -> Met-Tech.
Therefore Promoted -> Met-Tech AND Exceeded-Comm.
Coaching Note: Quantifier traps: a conclusion about 'all promoted' requires a bridge that puts every promoted
person inside the controlled subgroup.

9. Causal Mechanisms
Gap: Temporal correlation plus a partial control is not enough to prove causation.
Bridge: When crashes decline immediately after the patch and no other relevant changes occur, the patch is the
cause of the decline.
Proof Check: Bridge converts a post hoc correlation under a clean hold-constant into a sufficient causal claim.
Coaching Note: Causal bridges typically assert ruling out alternatives plus a mechanism or controlled setting.

10. Causal Mechanisms
Gap: Generalization is probabilistic ('usually'), and the vendor UI change is irrelevant noise.
Bridge: Our firm is sufficiently similar to the firms in the observed set such that adopting continuous training
will reduce violations next quarter.
Proof Check: Bridge transfers the observed tendency to our firm; the UI detail is fluff and does not affect the
causal link.
Coaching Note: When premises are statistical, the bridge must justify external validity for your specific case.

11. Causal Mechanisms
Gap: Observed difference may be due to confounds (size/staffing/automation).
Bridge: The only relevant difference between the plants was the type of filter used.
Proof Check: Bridge removes confounds, isolating filter type as the cause of the lower defect rate.
Coaching Note: Look for language that neutralizes alternative causes.

12. Causal Mechanisms
Gap: We need the usual relationship to apply to Acme's context this quarter.
Bridge: Acme Co.'s operations are typical of the cases in which real-time tracking reduces late deliveries.
Proof Check: Bridge allows us to apply the general relationship to Acme now; thus late deliveries will decline.
Coaching Note: Bridging a general rule to a new instance requires a representativeness claim.

13. Definition and Term Match
Gap: Access alone is not enough; exfiltration is also required.
Bridge: Sensitive personal data from the staging environment was exfiltrated by the attackers.
Proof Check: With the bridge, both conditions (access + exfiltration) are met, satisfying the definition of breach.
Coaching Note: With biconditional-style definitions, supply the missing half explicitly.

14. Definition and Term Match
Gap: We have not established the recognized exception.
Bridge: The witness statement falls within a recognized exception to the oath requirement.
Proof Check: Bridge supplies the alternative path in the disjunctive necessary condition.
Coaching Note: Watch for 'only if A or B': if one disjunct is missing, your bridge must assert the other.

15. Definition and Term Match
Gap: Definition requires three years; Jordan has only two. Praise is irrelevant fluff.
Bridge: Relevant internship experience from last year counts toward the three-year requirement for qualification.
Proof Check: Bridge upgrades Jordan's experience to meet the threshold; combined with license he qualifies.
Coaching Note: Definitions with thresholds invite 'upgrade' bridges that close numeric gaps.

16. Definition and Term Match
Gap: Registration before data collection has not been established.
Bridge: The protocol was registered before any data were collected.
Proof Check: Bridge adds the missing definitional component; both conditions satisfied -> admissible.
Coaching Note: Supply the exact missing limb of the definition, nothing more.

17. Comparative
Gap: The comparison metric is undefined; retention might not equal overall workplace quality.
Bridge: Employee retention is the deciding metric for which company is a better place to work.
Proof Check: Bridge equates the metric with the conclusion's standard, making the retention comparison
dispositive.
Coaching Note: When a conclusion uses 'better', your bridge must fix the standard that makes one dimension
decisive.

18. Comparative
Gap: We need the test to reflect typical use, and colors are irrelevant fluff.
Bridge: The standardized battery test accurately reflects typical use conditions.
Proof Check: With the bridge, the test result becomes probative for the typical-use claim; color options are noise.
Coaching Note: Bridge lab metrics to real-world performance by asserting validity of the test as a proxy.

19. Comparative
Gap: We must ensure 'shortest median wait' is the governing and stable criterion.
Bridge: Median wait time is the sole and decisive criterion for ranking emergency care quality in the region this
year.
Proof Check: Bridge pins the conclusion's standard to the metric; Prem2 satisfies it, yielding the conclusion.
Coaching Note: Comparatives require a named baseline or criterion; your bridge should assert it explicitly.

20. Comparative
Gap: We need the composite index to validly represent overall improvement.
Bridge: The composite index fully captures overall institutional improvement for ranked law schools.
Proof Check: Bridge makes the index an adequate proxy for improvement; Prem2 then proves the conclusion.
Coaching Note: Translate technical indices into the qualitative claim by asserting the index's coverage.

21. Prescriptive
Gap: We jump from 'unethical' to a policy prescription about investigation.
Bridge: Any corporation that behaves unethically should be subject to federal investigation.
Proof Check: Bridge supplies the policy rule linking moral status to regulatory action.
Coaching Note: Prescriptive conclusions need a rule connecting status to action; supply it.

22. Prescriptive
Gap: Confusion does not equal misleading without a link; the launch timing is fluff.
Bridge: If customer complaints show confusion about a product's advertised performance metrics, that product's
advertising is misleading.
Proof Check: Bridge converts the complaints into misleading advertising, which triggers the regulatory
requirement.
Coaching Note: Tie real-world indicators to the legal standard that triggers the remedy.

23. Prescriptive
Gap: We must connect discovery to knowledge at the company level.
Bridge: Discovery of a safety defect by the safety team counts as the company knowingly shipping an unsafe
product.
Proof Check: Bridge turns the discovery into company knowledge; shipping then triggers the recall rule.
Coaching Note: When rules use 'knowingly', bridges often equate internal discovery with corporate knowledge.

24. Prescriptive
Gap: We must treat the sibling's stake as a financial gain from this case.
Bridge: A sibling's significant stake in a company that will benefit from the ruling counts as the judge's close
relative standing to gain financially.
Proof Check: Bridge instantiates the rule's condition using the sibling scenario; conclusion follows.
Coaching Note: Make the real-world scenario fit the policy rule by spelling out the definitional mapping.
