# Naming Part and Telling Part

This framework is a way to break down the conclusion of an argument to better understand its structure and identify what needs to be proven.

- **Naming Part:** This is the subject or entity being discussed in the conclusion. It's the central topic of the statement.
- **Telling Part:** This is the attribute, requirement, or definition being attached to the Naming Part. It's what the author is claiming about the subject.

The primary goal is to prove that the "Naming Part" meets the criteria of the "Telling Part" to validate the conclusion.

## Applying the Framework to Arguments

According to your notes, this framework is especially useful for a specific type of question:

- **Sufficient Assumption Questions:** Your role is like a prosecutor trying to prove that the "defendant" (the Naming Part) is "guilty" of the "crime" (the Telling Part). To do this, you must find an answer choice that, when added to the argument, guarantees the conclusion is true.

Your notes outline two main ways to establish this link:

1. **Appeal to a Given Rule:** If the argument's premises already provide a clear rule or definition, you can use that rule to show that the Naming Part fits the Telling Part.
2. **Create a Rule:** If no explicit rule is given, you must construct one yourself. This new rule must logically and conclusively connect the evidence in the argument to the conclusion.

The correct answer for a sufficient assumption question will be the one that provides or supports the rule needed to guarantee the conclusion. It must explicitly bridge the gap between the Naming Part and the Telling Part.
