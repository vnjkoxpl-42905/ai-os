### Details

- **Session Preview and Question Types** Joshua Fisseha's Presentation outlined the plan for the session, which focuses on "must be true" and "most strongly supported" inference questions. He distinguished between the two types, noting that "must be true" questions often involve conditional reasoning, which is rigid and binary, while "most strongly supported" questions frequently involve causal reasoning, which is more flexible ([<u>00:00:48</u>](#section-1)). Joshua Fisseha's Presentation also highlighted the importance of recognizing "pivot words" like *but*, *yet*, and *however* in "most strongly supported" questions, as answers will often reconcile the ideas bridged by these words ([<u>00:02:20</u>](#section-3)).

- **Approach to Inference Questions** Joshua Fisseha's Presentation instructed participants to distinguish between rules (conditional statements) and descriptive facts, noting that a fact often triggers the rule or its contrapositive. He also emphasized classifying the types of reasoning (conditional, causal, normative, quantity) within the statements to anticipate the necessary inference, such as connecting conditional statements or addressing causation ([<u>00:03:32</u>](#section-4)). Eliza Kuznetcova and Glenn Gonzales confirmed the plan and readiness to begin ([<u>00:04:41</u>](#section-5)).

The group identified the statement about subcontracting some manufacturing as a situational fact ([<u>00:11:41</u>](#section-9)), which should be applied to the given rule: if manufacturing is subcontracted, some control over quality is lost ([<u>00:13:34</u>](#section-11)).

- **Inference and Conclusion for the First Question** The rule was established: if a company subcontracts manufacturing, they lose some control ([<u>00:13:34</u>](#section-11)). The fact is that the company subcontracts with other companies that "maintain complete control" over the quality of products ([<u>00:12:41</u>](#section-10)) ([<u>00:14:26</u>](#section-12)). By using the contrapositive of the rule (if they have complete control, they do not subcontract manufacturing), the group correctly inferred that the companies with complete control cannot themselves subcontract their product manufacturing ([<u>00:14:26</u>](#section-12)) ([<u>00:16:47</u>](#section-14)). The answer choice supporting this inference was A ([<u>00:17:41</u>](#section-15)).
