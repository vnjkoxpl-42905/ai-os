Synopsis. Our most straightforward task find the conclusion in the argument and then just pick the answer that best matches that claim. So they need to complicate that simple task by hiding the conclusion. They very rarely put it as the final claim on this question type. Instead we usually hear the Conclusion before we hear its Support. (aka an Upside Down Argument). If the first sentence sounds like an Author Opinion then its almost definitely going to be the conclusion (verify that it gets supported by the rest of the paragraph). If the first sentence sounds like an Attribution then expect some Pivot/Pushback from the author. That sentiment that sounds like theyre wrong will be the conclusion. The conclusion often uses referential language like pronouns e.g. This position is misguided so we will often have to import specific wording from elsewhere into the explicit Conclusion. Keywords. Main conclusion, main point. Examples. Which of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the argument? Which of the following is Eddies main point? Stimulus Tendencies. LSAC is seeking to make it harder than usual to find the Conclusion. So its very rare to see the Conclusion be the final claim prefaced by anything obvious like Therefore / Thus / So though sometimes it will be there. Rather than making the conclusion the last claim in more than 90% of Main Conclusion questions the conclusion appears earlier than evidence comes after. These are the two most common places they stash the conclusion: First sentence. After a But / Yet / However pivot. Other times the Conclusion will be indirectly revealed by Support language that may after all not be true. This usually wouldnt be on an obvious Premise indicator like since or because. Sometimes our best clue about where the conclusion is will be an Opinion Indicator like probably apparently evidently. Be aware that there may be Intermediate Conclusions. They will have the support of a sub-premise but they are there to support the Main Conclusion. Be aware that there are classic Conclusion-indicator words that look like Conclusion-indicator words. You can use thus therefore hence so. Pre-phrase Goal. This is the most salvageable question type ever. We just have to find the conclusion and reiterate to ourselves Okay dont over-complicate this. I just got that claim was identified as the conclusion. Many conclusions will be some form of rebuttal or other claim that uses referential language (pronouns like this that they such as it such). If so we want to take the time to replace the pronoun with its reference language. Example Some people propose laws that would require mandatory background checks on people owning a gun. However such laws wont affect the homicide rate. Almost all homicides are committed by people who would pass the background check proposed in that law. If we correctly identified the 2nd sentence as the conclusion our job is to convince ourselves what such laws refers to and use that language in its place when were saying the conclusion back to ourselves. Okay the conclusion is that laws requiring mandatory background checks wont affect the homicide rate. Reading Goal. 1. Find the conclusion. 2. Make sure that it a. Sounds like Authors Opinion (facts are not conclusions) b. Is Supported by at least one explicit claim. Correct Answer. The correct answer just reproduces the meaning of the Conclusion. Sometimes its a verbatim regurgitation. More commonly they use synonyms or inverted syntax to present an equivalent meaning that still looks a little different. F.e. If the conclusion appeared in the argument as not all tornadoes should be avoided then an equivalent claim would be some tornadoes should not be avoided. If you didnt already do the 2-part Conclusion Check when you were first reading the argument and determining which claim is the conclusion you can do it now (or again). Is this answer expressing an Opinion from our Author? (facts are not conclusions). Was this answer Supported by at least one explicit claim? Incorrect Answers. Premise Its tempting because it was in the paragraph. But it wasnt supported. The conclusion has to be supported by at least one other claim. Last Claim Trap They know that we wish for the last claim to be the Conclusion. Intermediate Conclusion.

Rebuttal Conclusions. All 9 arguments in this set feature a Rebuttal conclusion. Rebuttal arguments occur on all question types but they dominate two question types: Main Conclusion and Role. All nine questions in this set are Main Conclusion questions. Your goal is to learn the feel and the signposts of this type of argument. Rebuttal style arguments are often tricky for students to navigate because they tend to go in this order: a. Counterpoint / Attribution (i.e. someone elses view). b. Authors Conclusion (i.e. but/yet/however I disagree). c. Authors Support (which may start with some sort of concession to the opposition; although youre right insofar as X reasons Y and Z are why I think your original position was bad.). Tips. Look for an early attribution a claim or point of view that does not belong to the author. Look for a pivot into the Conclusion. Put the Conclusion into specific words.
