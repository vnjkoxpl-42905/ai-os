# First Sentence Conclusions — (Reading Only)

These eleven questions all have a Conclusion in the **first sentence**, which of course is not the most obvious place to find a conclusion.

Flip through the eleven arguments and **just read them**, thinking about Conclusion vs. Supporting Ideas.

How would we recognize that the first sentence is the author's main conclusion? Are there any Opinion indicators or Support indicators that would help us recognize?

## Just Read through each of these nine arguments.

Think about how we would have figured out that the first sentence was the conclusion.

This exercise is about getting our brains used to the feel of a pattern. Focus on noticing how the first claim is an **opinion**, and then **feeling** how the subsequent claims go on to defend that initial opinion.

- Are there any Support Indicator words that follow it? (e.g., FABS = for, after all, because, since)
- Are there any Opinion Indicator words in it? (e.g., should, ought, probably, better)

If we look at the claim in the first sentence and ask ourselves, "Why should we believe that?", does the rest of an argument seem like an answer to that question? (It should.)

You don't need to attempt these questions; use these as a quick reading exercise.
