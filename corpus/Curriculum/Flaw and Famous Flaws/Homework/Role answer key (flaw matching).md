# "Role answer key" — Flaw Description Matching (Answer Key)

> Despite the filename, the actual content is a **flaw-description matching drill**: each item gives an abstract flaw description, then two candidate arguments (A vs. B), and asks which one fits. This is the same skill set tested by Famous Flaws / Method-of-Reasoning Flaw answer choices, not Role.

---

### Question 1
**Description:** Infers that a practice is undesirable solely because it results in negative consequences that are difficult to quantify.

- **Argument A:** Implementing a new training program in the workplace has uncertain costs, but it may lead to improved employee performance and satisfaction. Therefore, it should not be considered a priority.
- **Argument B:** Noise pollution from urban construction projects affects psychological well-being, yet its impact is hard to measure accurately. For this reason, these projects should be limited.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument B — concludes a practice should be limited solely due to hard-to-measure negative impacts.

### Question 2
**Description:** Discounts a viewpoint based on the perceived improbability of the conditions being met rather than on evidence negating the viewpoint.

- **A:** The theory that the universe is teeming with undiscovered life is dismissed due to the sheer vastness of space and slim chance of such life making contact with Earth.
- **B:** The belief in ancient aliens is often refuted through historical records and ongoing scientific research that find no credible artifacts or evidence to support such claims.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument A — rejects the theory solely due to perceived improbability, not evidence disproving it.

### Question 3
**Description:** Reasons that because a certain phenomenon leads to a known positive outcome, it must be actively pursued despite unknown variables.

- **A:** Investing in space exploration can result in technological advances and inspire generations, so it should be pursued aggressively even though the financial risks are unpredictable.
- **B:** Introducing universal basic income could alleviate poverty, yet the economic implications remain uncertain, so careful assessments are necessary before any implementation.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument A — promotes aggressive pursuit despite unknown risks, based on known benefits.

### Question 4
**Description:** Relies on an analogy between two things that are not relevantly similar.

- **A:** Anthropologists argue that cultures change much like languages do. Since languages evolve by adopting new expressions and phrases, new cultural norms can be integrated similarly over time.
- **B:** Some claim that managing a country is like driving a car; both require adjustment to conditions. However, the complexities of geopolitics transcend the straightforward mechanics of driving.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument B — draws an analogy between unrelated domains (geopolitics vs. driving).

### Question 5
**Description:** Derives its conclusion based solely on an event that occurred once, assuming it will happen again in the same manner.

- **A:** In the 1800s, a major city overcame economic recession by focusing on industrial growth. Therefore, any city facing a recession today should also turn to industrial development as the solution.
- **B:** Because a town hosted a successful annual fair last year, it's expected that the same fair will experience success each year.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument B — assumes future success based on a single prior occurrence.

### Question 6
**Description:** Improperly shifts the burden of proof by assuming that a claim is true unless it is proven false.

- **A:** Critics of the new diet fad say it is unhealthy. Yet, there is minimal research showing definitive harm, so it must be safe for consumption.
- **B:** A hypothesis has been proposed that energy drinks increase concentration. Until research proves otherwise, it should be assumed true.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument A — assumes safety until harm is proven, shifting the burden of proof.

### Question 7
**Description:** Confuses necessary conditions with sufficient conditions.

- **A:** To graduate, you must complete a thesis. Completing your thesis is enough to ensure graduation.
- **B:** She believes that to be healthy, one must exercise. Thus, exercising guarantees health.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument A — treats a necessary requirement (thesis) as if it guarantees graduation.

### Question 8
**Description:** Bases its generalization on a sample that is known to be biased.

- **A:** A survey at the local yoga studio found 95% of participants believe yoga is the best form of exercise. Therefore, yoga must be universally considered the best exercise.
- **B:** By only interviewing tech company CEOs, the study concludes that all industries are rapidly advancing in AI technology.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument A — generalizes from a biased sample (yoga enthusiasts).

### Question 9
**Description:** Fails to recognize that evidence for the occurrence of one event is not enough to conclude a causation with another.

- **A:** Ice cream sales spike in summer, and more people swim during this time. Thus, eating ice cream causes people to swim.
- **B:** As daylight hours increase in spring, people tend to spend more time outdoors, hence proving long days lead to more outdoor activities.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument A — mistakes correlation (ice cream sales and swimming) for causation.

### Question 10
**Description:** Presents a potentially false dilemma, suggesting there are only two possible choices.

- **A:** We must either increase taxes or face economic collapse; there is no other option.
- **B:** The city can either build a new park or focus on maintaining current recreational areas to improve community well-being.

✅ **Correct Answer:** Argument A — presents only two options while ignoring other possible solutions.
